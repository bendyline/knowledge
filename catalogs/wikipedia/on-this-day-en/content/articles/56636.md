**Colombo** ([/kəˈlʌmboʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*kə-LUM-boh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-2] [Sinhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language): කොළඹ, romanised: *Koḷam̆ba*, IPA: [\[ˈkoləᵐbə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA); [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language): கொழும்பு, romanised: *Koḻumpu*, IPA: [\[koɻumbɯ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Tamil)) is [Sri Lanka](26750.md)'s [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Sri_Lanka) and [judicial](2830384.md) capital, as well as the country's most populous city. It is also the financial centre of the island and a tourist destination.[^cite_note-\:0-3] The Colombo metropolitan area is estimated to have a population of 5.6 million,[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] and 752,993[^cite_note-census2011-1] within the municipal limits. It is located on the west coast of the island. It is also the administrative capital of the [Western Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Province,_Sri_Lanka) and the district capital of [Colombo District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_District).[^cite_note-rweb1-8]

It was made the capital of the island when Sri Lanka was ceded to the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) in 1815,[^cite_note-HoC-9] retaining its capital status when Sri Lanka gained [independence in 1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_independence_movement). In 1978, when administrative functions were moved to [Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Jayawardenepura_Kotte), Colombo was designated as the commercial capital of Sri Lanka.

## Etymology



Coat of arms of Colombo from the [Dutch Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Ceylon) era, depicting a [mango tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango_tree)

The name 'Colombo', first introduced by the [Portuguese explorers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_explorers) in 1505, is believed to be derived from the classical [Sinhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language) name කොලොන් තොට, கொல்லம் துறைமுகம் *Kolon thota*, meaning "port on the river [Kelani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelani_River)".[^cite_note-so1-10]

Another belief is that the name is derived from the Sinhala name කොල-අඹ-තොට, பெருங்குடல் துறைமுகம் *Kola-amba-thota* which means 'Harbour with leafy/green mango trees'.[^cite_note-we1-11]

The author of the oldest Sinhala grammar, *Sidatsangarava,* written in the 13th century wrote about a category of words that exclusively belonged to early Sinhala. It lists *naramba* (to see) and *kolamba* (fort or harbour) as deriving from the indigenous [Vedda language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedda_language). *Kolamba* may also be the source of the name of the commercial capital Colombo.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

## History

Traveller [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta), who visited the island in the 14th century, referred to it as *Kalanpu*.[^cite_note-14] Arabs, whose primary interests were trade, began to settle in Colombo around the eighth century AD mostly because the port helped their business by the way of controlling much of the trade between the [Sinhalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_people) kingdoms and the outside world. It was popularly believed that their descendants comprised the local [Sri Lankan Moor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Moors) community, but their genetics are predominantly South Indian.[^cite_note-HoC-9][^cite_note-15]

### Portuguese era

[Portuguese](23033.md) explorers led by Dom [Lourenço de Almeida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louren%C3%A7o_de_Almeida) first arrived in Sri Lanka in 1505. During their initial visit they made a treaty with the King of [Kotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kotte), [Parakramabahu VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parakramabahu_VIII) (1484–1518), which enabled them to trade in the island's crop of [cinnamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinnamon), which lay along with the coastal areas of the island, including in Colombo. As part of the treaty, the Portuguese were given full authority over the coastline in exchange for the promise of guarding the coast against invaders. They were allowed to establish a [trading post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_post) in Colombo.[^cite_note-cs1-16] Within a short time, however, they expelled the [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Moors) inhabitants of Colombo and began to build a [fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort) in 1517.

The Portuguese soon realised that control of Sri Lanka was necessary for the protection of their coastal establishments in India, and they began to manipulate the rulers of the Kotte kingdom to gain control of the area. After skilfully exploiting rivalries within the royal family, they took control of a large area of the kingdom and the Sinhalese King Mayadunne established a new kingdom at Sitawaka, a domain in the Kotte kingdom.[^cite_note-cs1-16] Before long he annexed much of the Kotte kingdom and forced the Portuguese to retreat to Colombo, which was repeatedly besieged by Mayadunne and the later kings of Sitawaka, forcing them to seek reinforcement from their major base in [Goa](40010153.md), India. Following the fall of the kingdom in 1593, the Portuguese were able to establish complete control over the coastal area, with Colombo as their capital.[^cite_note-cs1-16][^cite_note-cs2-17] This part of Colombo is still known as [Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_\(Colombo\)) and houses the presidential palace and the majority of Colombo's [five star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_rating) hotels. The area immediately outside Fort is known as [Pettah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pettah,_Sri_Lanka) ([Sinhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language): පිට කොටුව,[Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language): புறக் கோட்டை *piṭa koṭuva*, "outer fort") and is a commercial hub.

### Dutch era



Dutch engraving of Colombo in about 1680

In 1638 the [Dutch](21148.md) signed a treaty with King Rajasinha II of Kandy which assured the king assistance in his war against the Portuguese in exchange for a monopoly of the island's major trade goods. The Portuguese resisted the Dutch and the Kandyans but were gradually defeated in their strongholds beginning in 1639.[^cite_note-cs4-18] The Dutch captured Colombo in 1656 after an epic siege, at the end of which a mere 93 Portuguese survivors were given [safe conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe-conduct) out of the fort. Although the Dutch (e.g., [Rijcklof van Goens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijcklof_van_Goens)) initially restored the captured area back to the Sinhalese kings, they later refused to turn them over and gained control over the island's richest cinnamon lands including Colombo which then served as the capital of the Dutch maritime provinces under the control of the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) until 1796.[^cite_note-cs4-18][^cite_note-cs3-19]

### British era



Map of Colombo, c. 1914

Although the British captured Colombo in 1796, it remained a [British military](31730.md) outpost until the Kandyan Kingdom was ceded to them in 1815 and they made Colombo the capital of their newly created [crown colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_overseas_territories) of [British Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Ceylon). Unlike the Portuguese and Dutch before them, whose primary use of Colombo was as a military fort, the British began constructing houses and other civilian structures around the fort, giving rise to the current City of Colombo.[^cite_note-HoC-9]

Initially, they placed the administration of the city under a "[Collector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_collector)", and John Macdowell of the [Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras_Presidency) Service was the first to hold the office. Then, in 1833, the [Government Agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Agent_\(Sri_Lanka\)) of the Western Province was charged with the administration of the city. Centuries of [colonial rule](7299.md) had meant a decline of indigenous administration of Colombo and in 1865 the British conceived a Municipal Council as a means of training the local population in [self-governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance). The [Legislative Council of Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Council_of_Ceylon) constituted the Colombo Municipal Council in 1865 and the Council met for the first time on 16 January 1866. At the time, the population of the region was around 80,000.[^cite_note-HoC-9]

During the time they were in control of Colombo, the British were responsible for much of the planning of the present city. In some parts of the city, [tram](30733.md) car tracks and granite flooring laid during the era are still visible today.[^cite_note-cs3-19][^cite_note-cs5-20]

### After independence



The formal ceremony marking the start of self-rule at [Independence Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Square,_Colombo).

This era of colonialism ended peacefully in 1948 when Ceylon gained independence from Britain. Due to the tremendous impact this caused on the city's inhabitants and on the country as a whole, the changes that resulted at the end of the colonial period were drastic. An entire new culture took root. Changes in laws and customs, clothing styles, religions and [proper names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_names) were a significant result of the colonial era. These cultural changes were followed by the strengthening of the island's economy. Even today, the influence of the Portuguese, the Dutch and the British is visible in Colombo's architecture, names, clothing, food, language and attitudes. Buildings from all three eras stand as reminders of the turbulent past of Colombo. The city and its people show an interesting mix of European clothing and lifestyles together with local customs.[^cite_note-PostI-21]

Historically, Colombo referred to the area around the *[Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_\(Colombo\))* and *[Pettah Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pettah_Market)* which is known for the variety of products available as well as the [Khan Clock Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Clock_Tower), a local landmark. At present, it refers to the city limits of the [Colombo Municipal Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Municipal_Council).[^cite_note-22] More often, the name is used for the [Conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conurbation) known as [Greater Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Colombo), which encompasses several [Municipal councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_council) including [Kotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Jayawardenepura_Kotte), Dehiwela and Colombo.

Although Colombo lost its status as the capital of Sri Lanka in the 1980s to Sri Jayawardanapura, it continues to be the island's commercial centre. Despite the official capital of Sri Lanka moving to the adjacent Sri Jayawardanapura Kotte, most countries still maintain their [diplomatic missions](8970.md) in Colombo.[^cite_note-emb1-23]

## Geography



Trizen (apartment complex) overlooking [Beira Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beira_Lake) at night.

The geography of Colombo consists of both land and water. The city has many canals and, in the heart of the city, the 65-hectare (160-acre) [Beira Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beira_Lake). The lake is one of the most distinctive landmarks of Colombo and was used for centuries by colonists to defend the city.[^cite_note-limc-24] It remains a tourist attraction, hosting [regattas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regattas),[^cite_note-25] and [theatrical events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater) on its shores. The northern and north-eastern border of the city of Colombo is formed by the [Kelani River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelani_River), which meets the sea in a part of the city known as the Modera (*mōdara* in Sinhala) which means [river delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta).

### Climate

Colombo features a [tropical rainforest climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate) (*Af*). Colombo's climate is hot throughout the year. From March to April the average high temperature is around 31 °C (87.8 °F).[^cite_note-26] The only major change in the Colombo weather occurs during the [monsoon](57630.md) seasons from April to June and September to November, when heavy rains occur. Colombo sees little relative diurnal range of temperature, although this is more marked in the drier winter months, where minimum temperatures average 22 °C (71.6 °F). Rainfall in the city averages around 2,500 millimetres (98 in) a year.[^cite_note-WMO-27]

## Attractions



The *Seema Malakaya* of the [Gangarama Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangaramaya_Temple) in the [*Beira* Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beira_Lake) in the *[Company Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_Roads)* area is one of many religious structures in Colombo



A picture of Colombo's skyline from the [Lotus tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_Tower), showing the [ITC Ratnadipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITC_Ratnadipa), [One Galle Face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Galle_Face), [The Shangri-La](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shangri-La_Colombo) apartments and [Port City Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_City_Colombo) in the background.

[Galle Face Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galle_Face_Green) is located in the heart of the city along the Indian Ocean coast and is a destination for tourists and residents alike. The [Galle Face Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galle_Face_Hotel) is a historic landmark on the southern edge of this promenade.

[Gangaramaya Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangaramaya_Temple) is one of the most important temples in Colombo. The temple's architecture demonstrates an eclectic mix of Sri Lankan, Thai, Indian and Chinese architecture.[^cite_note-John_Keells-31]

The [Viharamahadevi Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viharamahadevi_Park) (formerly Victoria Park) is an urban park located next to the [National Museum of Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Colombo) and the [Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_Hall,_Colombo). It is the oldest and largest park in Colombo and features a large Buddha statue.

As part of the Urban Regeneration Program of the [Government of Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Sri_Lanka), many old sites and buildings were revamped into modern public recreational spaces and shopping precincts. These include [Independence Memorial Hall Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Memorial_Hall), [Pettah Floating Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pettah_Floating_Market) and [Old Dutch Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Colombo_Dutch_Hospital), among others.

The population of Colombo is a mix of numerous ethnic groups, mainly [Sinhalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_people), [Sri Lankan Moor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Moor) and [Sri Lankan Tamils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Tamils), . Colombo is the most populous city in Sri Lanka, with 642,163 people living within the city limits.[^cite_note-cen01-33] In 1866 the city had a population of around 80,000.[^cite_note-34]

## Government and politics



Colombo Municipal Council

### Local government

Colombo is a [charter city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_city), with a [mayor-council government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor-council_government).[^cite_note-36] The mayor and council members are elected through local government elections held every five years. The city had historically been ruled by the [United National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_National_Party) (UNP), a [right](56522.md) leaning party, whose business-friendly policies resonate with the population of Colombo. However in 2025, the [National People's Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_People's_Power) won the local elections and [Vraîe Cally Balthazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vra%C3%AEe_Cally_Balthazaar) became mayor. She is the second woman to hold the position, after her predecessor [Rosy Senanayake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosy_Senanayake) (2018–2023), and leads Colombo under the NPP-[JVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janatha_Vimukthi_Peramuna) alliance, marking the city's first left-wing governance since [N.M. Perera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N.M._Perera) (1954–1956).

The city government provides sewer, road and waste management services to the residents. In the case of water, electricity and telephone utility services, the council liaises with the [National Water Supply and Drainage Board (NWSDB)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Water_Supply_and_Drainage_Board), the [Ceylon Electricity Board (CEB)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceylon_Electricity_Board) and [telephone service providers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_in_Sri_Lanka) operating in the country respectively.

### National capital status

Colombo was the capital of the coastal areas controlled by the Portuguese, the Dutch and the British from the 1700s to 1815 when the British gained control of the entire island following the [Kandyan convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandyan_convention). From then until the 1980s the national capital of the island was Colombo.

During the 1980s plans were made to move the administrative capital to [Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Jayawardenepura_Kotte) and thus move all governmental institutions out of Colombo to make way for commercial activities. As a primary step, the [Parliament](571660.md) was moved to a new complex in Kotte, with several ministries and departments also relocated. However, the move was never completed.

Today, many governmental institutions still remain in Colombo. These include the [President's House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President's_House,_Colombo), [Presidential Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Secretariat_\(Sri_Lanka\)), [Prime Minister's House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister's_House_\(Colombo\)) (Temple Trees), [Prime Minister's Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister's_Office_\(Colombo\)), the [Supreme Court of Sri Lanka](2830384.md), [Central Bank of Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Sri_Lanka), important government ministries and departments; such as [Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_and_Planning_\(Sri_Lanka\)) (Treasury), [Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Sri_Lanka\)), Public Administration & Home affairs, [Foreign affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Sri_Lanka\)), Justice and the [Military headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Headquarters_\(Sri_Lanka\)), Naval headquarters ([SLNS Parakrama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLNS_Parakrama)), Air Force headquarters ([SLAF Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLAF_Colombo)) and [Police](13613956.md) national and field force headquarters.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

## Economy



Colombo City is the hub of Sri Lanka's economic activity

The great majority of Sri Lankan corporations have their head offices in Colombo including [Aitken Spence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aitken_Spence), [Ceylinco Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceylinco_Consolidated), Stassen group of companies, [John Keells Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Keells_Holdings), [Cargills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargills_\(Ceylon\)), [Hemas Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemas_Holdings), SenzMate and Akbar Brothers. Some of the industries include chemicals, textiles, glass, cement, leather goods, furniture and jewellery. In the city centre is the [World Trade Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_Colombo). The 40-story Twin Tower complex is the centre of important commercial establishments, in the [Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_\(Colombo\)) district, the city's nerve centre. Right outside the Fort area is [Pettah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pettah,_Sri_Lanka) which is derived from the Sinhala word *pita* which means 'out' or 'outside'.[^cite_note-onlineColombo-40]



The [Old Parliament Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Parliament_Building,_Colombo), [Bank of Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Ceylon) building and [WTC twin towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_Colombo) in the Colombo financial district of [Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_\(Colombo\))

The Colombo Metropolitan area has a GDP (PPP) of \$122 billion or 40% of the GDP, making it the most important aspect of the Sri Lankan economy.  The per capita income of the Colombo Metro area stood at US\$8623 and purchasing power per capita of \$25,117, making it one of the most prosperous regions in South Asia.[^cite_note-cbsl.gov.lk-41] The Colombo Metropolitan (CM) area is the most important industrial, commercial and administrative centre in Sri Lanka. A major share of the country's export-oriented manufacturing takes place in the CM area, which is the engine of growth for Sri Lanka.



[Galle Face Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galle_Face_Green), where many major events take place, is a favourite location for many. It is in close proximity to many of the major hotels. Formerly, it was the site of the city's racecourse, golf course and the cricket field

&#x20;The Western province contributes less than 40% to the GDP and about 80% of industrial value additions although it accounts for only 5.7% of the country's geographic area and 25% of the national population. Given its importance as the primary international gateway for Sri Lanka and as the main economic driver of the country, the government of Sri Lanka (GoSL) has launched an ambitious program to transform Colombo and its area into a metropolis of international standards. Bottlenecks are preventing the Colombo metropolitan area from realizing its full economic potential. To facilitate the transformation of Colombo, the government has to address these bottlenecks which have for long been obstructing economic and physical urban regeneration.[^cite_note-ES-42]

Pettah is more crowded than the Fort area. Pettah's roads are always packed and [pavements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidewalk) are full of small stalls selling items from delicious [sharbat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharbat_\(beverage\)) to [shirts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirt). Main Street consists mostly of clothes shops and the crossroads, which are known as Cross-Streets where each of the five streets specialises in a specific business. For example, First Cross Street is mostly [electronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics) goods shops, the Second [cellular phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_phones) and fancy goods. Most of these businesses are dominated by Muslim traders. At the end of Main Street further away from Fort is Sea Street – Sri Lanka's gold market – dominated by Tamil interests. This mile-long street is full of jewellery shops,[^cite_note-onlineColombo-40] including the former head office of [SriLankan Airlines](483603.md).[^cite_note-43]

## Law enforcement and crime



The [Supreme Court of Sri Lanka](2830384.md) is located in Colombo

The [Sri Lanka Police](13613956.md), the main law enforcement agency of the island, liaise with the municipal council but is under the control of the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Sri_Lanka\)) of the central government.[^cite_note-44] Policing in Colombo and its suburbs falls within the *Metropolitan Range* headed by the Deputy Inspector General of Police (Metropolitan), this also includes the Colombo Crime Division.[^cite_note-45] As with most Sri Lankan cities, the magistrate court handles felony crimes while the district court handles civil cases.&#x20;

As in other large cities around the world, Colombo experiences certain levels of [street crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_crime) and [bribery](260979.md). Indeed, the corruption extends to the very top, US reports show. In addition, in the period from the 1980s to 2009, there have been a number of major terrorist attacks.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] The [LTTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTTE) has been linked to most of the bombings and assassinations in the city.[^cite_note-48] [Welikada Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welikada_Prison) is situated in Colombo and it is one of the largest maximum-security prisons in the country.[^cite_note-49]

Colombo Galle face road in the evening

### Harbour



Container handling at Colombo Port.

[Colombo Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Colombo) is the largest and one of the busiest [ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) in Sri Lanka. Colombo was established primarily as a port city during the colonial era, with an artificial harbour that has been expanded over the years. The [Sri Lanka Navy](6186841.md) maintains a [naval base](291782.md), *SLNS Rangalla*, within the harbour.

The Port of Colombo handled 3.75 million [twenty-foot equivalent units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-foot_equivalent_unit) in 2008, 10.6% up on 2007 (which itself was 9.7% up on 2006), bucking the global economic trend. Of those, 817,000 were local shipments with the rest transshipments. With a capacity of 5.7 million TEUs and a dredged depth of over 15 m (49 ft), the Colombo Harbour is one of the busiest ports in the world and ranks among the top 25 ports (23rd). [Sri Lanka's Port of Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Colombo) is said to be the busiest, largest port in the Indian Ocean.[^cite_note-51]

Colombo is part of the [21st Century Maritime Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Century_Maritime_Silk_Road) that runs from the Chinese coast to the Upper Adriatic region with its rail connections to [Central](5188.md) and [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe).[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

### Transport

#### Bus

Colombo has an extensive public transport system based on buses operated both by private operators and the government-owned [Sri Lanka Transport Board (SLTB)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Transport_Board). The three primary bus terminals – Bastian Mawatha, Central and the Gunasinghapura Bus Terminals – are in Pettah.[^cite_note-57] Bastian Mawatha handles long-distance services whereas Gunasinghapura and Central handle local services.

The state-owned [Lanka Metro Transit (PVT) LTD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanka_Metro_Transit) is in charge of running low-floor fully air-conditioned [accessible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accessibility) buses (named "*Metro Buses*") throughout the [Western Region Megapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Region_Megapolis) area, with buses connecting Colombo itself and its immediate suburbs. The Makumbura Multimodal Transport Centre acts as an important connection hub with regional buses, long-distance buses, rail as well as [Katunayake Bandaranaike airport](2870845.md) shuttles.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

## Education



[Royal College Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_Colombo), the oldest public school in the city.

See also: [Education in Sri Lanka](4560910.md)

Education institutions in Colombo have a long history. Colombo has many of the prominent [public schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_school_\(UK\)) in the country, some of them government-owned and others private. Most of the prominent schools in the city date back to the 1800s when they were established during the British colonial rule,[^cite_note-64] such as the [Royal College Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_Colombo) established in 1835. Certain urban schools of Sri Lanka have some religious alignment; this is partly due to the influence of the British, who established Christian missionary schools.[^cite_note-edu-65][^cite_note-edu2-66] These include [Anglican schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism): [Bishop's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop's_College_\(Sri_Lanka\)) (1875); [Methodist schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist): [Wesley College Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesley_College_Colombo) (1874); [Buddhist schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist): [Ananda College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ananda_College) (1886); Nalanda College (1925) [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) schools: [Zahira College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahira_College,_Colombo) (1892); [Catholic schools](606848.md): [St Benedict's College, Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Benedict's_College,_Colombo) (1865), [St Joseph's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Joseph's_College,_Colombo) (1896). The religious alignments do not affect the curriculum of the school except for the demographics of the student population.[^cite_note-edu-65] The secular schools [Mahanama College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahanama_College) (1954), [D. S. Senanayake College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._S._Senanayake_College) (1967), and [Sirimavo Bandaranaike Vidyalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirimavo_Bandaranaike_Vidyalaya) (1973), [Thurstan College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurstan_College) (1950) and [Isipathana College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isipathana_College) (1952) have been established in the post independence era. Colombo has many International Schools that have come up in recent years.

Higher education in the city has a long history, beginning with the establishment of the [Colombo Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Medical_School) (1870), the [Colombo Law College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Law_College) (1875), the School of Agriculture (1884) and the Government Technical College (1893). The first step in the creation of a university in Colombo was taken in 1913 with the establishment of the [University College Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Colombo) which prepared students for the [external examinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London_External_System) of the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London). This was followed by the establishment of the [University of Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ceylon) in Colombo.[^cite_note-cmb.ac.lk-67] Today the [University of Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colombo) and the [University of the Visual & Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Visual_&_Performing_Arts) are state universities in the city. The [Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Institute_of_Information_Technology) has a metropolitan campus in the city centre. There are several private higher education institutions in the city.

## Architecture



The Red Mosque

Colombo has widely varying architecture that spans centuries and depicts many styles. [Colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_architecture) buildings influenced by the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Portugal), [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_the_Netherlands) and [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_the_United_Kingdom) exist alongside structures built in [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_architecture), [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_architecture), [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_architecture), [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_India) and [Contemporary](3970948.md) architectural styles. No other place is this more evident than in the Fort area. Here, one may find new, towering skyscrapers as well as historic buildings dating far back as the 1700s.[^cite_note-oldCMB1-68][^cite_note-oldCMB2-69]

### Colombo Fort

The Portuguese were the first colonists to settle in Colombo. Establishing a small trading post, they had laid the foundations for a small fort which in time became the largest colonial fort on the island. The Dutch expanded the fort, thus creating a well fortified harbour. This came into the possession of the British in the late 1700s, and by the late 19th century, seeing no threat to the [Colombo Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Harbour), began demolishing the ramparts to make way for the development of the city. Although now there is nothing left of the fortifications, the area which was once the fort is still referred to as Fort. The area outside is [Pettah, Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pettah,_Sri_Lanka) or පිටකොටුව ('*Pitakotuwa')* in Sinhala, which means outer fort.[^cite_note-oldCMB1-68][^cite_note-oldCMB2-69]



&#x20;The VOC (Verenigde Oostindische Compagnie) logo of [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) on the gates of [Wolvendaal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolvendaal_Church)

### Dutch-era buildings

There are none of the buildings of the Portuguese era and only a few from the Dutch period. These include the oldest building in the fort area, the [former Dutch Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Colombo_Dutch_Hospital), the Dutch House which is now the [Colombo Dutch Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Dutch_Museum) and several churches. The [President's House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President's_House,_Colombo) (formerly the Queen's House) was originally the Dutch governor's house and successive British governors made it their office and residence. However, it has undergone much change since the Dutch period. Adjoining the President's House are the Gordon Gardens, now off-limits to the public.[^cite_note-oldCMB1-68][^cite_note-oldCMB2-69][^cite_note-70]

### British-era buildings

Much of the old buildings of the fort area and in other parts of the city date back to British times; these include governmental, commercial buildings, and private houses. Some of the notable government building of British colonial architecture includes the old Parliament building, which is now the [Presidential Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Secretariat_\(Sri_Lanka\)); the [Republic Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Building,_Colombo), which houses the [Ministry of Foreign affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Sri_Lanka\)) but once housed the Ceylon Legislative council; the [General Treasury Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Treasury_Building); the old [General Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Post_Office,_Colombo), an Edwardian-style building opposite the President's House; the [Prime Minister's Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister's_Office_\(Colombo\)); the [Central Telegraph Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Telegraph_Office,_Colombo); and the [Mathematics department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Royal_College_Building,_Colombo) of the [University of Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colombo) (formally the [Royal College, Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College,_Colombo)).[^cite_note-cmb.ac.lk-67] Notable commercial buildings of the British era include the [Galle Face Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galle_Face_Hotel), Cargills and Millers' complex, and the [Grand Oriental Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Oriental_Hotel).[^cite_note-oldCMB1-68][^cite_note-oldCMB2-69]

## Culture

### Annual cultural events and fairs



Vesak *Thorana* in Colombo



Christmas Celebrations in Colombo

Colombo's most popular festival is the celebration of [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha)'s birth, enlightenment and death all falling on the same day. In Sinhala this is known as *[Vesak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesak)*.[^cite_note-vesak-71] During this festival, much of the city is decorated with lanterns, lights and special displays of light (known as *thoran*). The festival falls in mid-May and lasts a week. Many Sri Lankans visit the city to see the lantern competitions and decorations. During this week people distribute, rice, drinks and other food items for free in *dunsal* which means charity place. These *dunsal* are popular amongst visitors from the suburbs.

Since there is a large number of Muslims in Colombo, Eid Ul Fitr and Eid Ul Adha are two Islamic festivals that are celebrated in Colombo. Many businesses flourish during the eventual countdown for Eid Ul Fitr which is a major Islamic festival celebrated by Muslims after a month-long fasting. Colombo is generally very busy on the eve of the festivals as people do their last-minute shopping.

Christmas is another major festival. Although Sri Lanka's Christians make up only just over 7% of the population, Christmas is one of the island's biggest festivals. Most streets and commercial buildings light up from the beginning of December and festive sales begin at all shopping centres and department stores. Carolling and nativity plays are frequent sights during the season.

The [Sinhalese and Tamil New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_and_Tamil_New_Year) is a cultural event that takes place on 13 and 14 April. The festivities include many events and traditions that display a great deal of Sri Lankan culture. Several old clubs of the city give a glimpse of the British equestrian lifestyle; these include the [Colombo Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Club), [Orient Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orient_Club), the 80 Club, and the [Colombo Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Cricket_Club).

### Performing arts



The [Nelum Pokuna Mahinda Rajapaksa Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelum_Pokuna_Mahinda_Rajapaksa_Theatre) is a major venue for the performing arts

Colombo has several [performing arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performing_arts) centres, which are popular for their musical and theatrical performances, including the [Lionel Wendt Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Wendt_Art_Centre), the Elphinstone, and Tower Hall, all of which were made for western-style productions. The [Navarangahala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navarangahala) found in the city is the country's first [national](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation) [theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_\(structure\)) designed and built for Asian and local style musical and theatrical productions.

The [Nelum Pokuna Mahinda Rajapaksa Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelum_Pokuna_Mahinda_Rajapaksa_Theatre) is a world-class theatre that opened in December 2011.[^cite_note-Pokuna-72] Designed in the form of the Lotus Pond in [Polonnaruwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonnaruwa),[^cite_note-Treasure-73] the theatre is a major theatre destination.

### Museums and art collections

The [National Museum of Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_National_Museum), established on 1 January 1877 during the tenure of the British Colonial Governor [Sir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir) [William Henry Gregory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Gregory), is in the Cinnamon Gardens area.[^cite_note-74] The museum houses the [crown jewels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_jewels) and throne of the last king of the kingdom of Kandy, [Sri Vikrama Rajasinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Vikrama_Rajasinha).[^cite_note-75]

There is also the [Colombo Dutch Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Dutch_Museum) detailing the Dutch colonial history of the country. Colombo does not boast a very big art gallery. There is a small collection of random Sri Lankan paintings at the Art Gallery in Green Path; next to it is the Natural History Museum.

### Sports



[Colombo Racecourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Racecourse) ground

One of the most popular sports in Sri Lanka is [cricket](25675557.md). The country emerged as champions of the 1996 [Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_World_Cup) and became runners up in 2007 and 2011. In the [ICC World Twenty20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_World_Twenty20) they became runners up in 2009 and 2012 and winners in 2014. The sport is played in parks, playgrounds, beaches and even in the streets. Colombo is the home for two of the country's most popular [international cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_structure_of_cricket) stadiums, [Singhalese Sports Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singhalese_Sports_Club)'s [Cricket Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_Stadium) and [R. Premadasa Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Premadasa_Stadium) (named after late president [Premadasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premadasa)). [Colombo Kaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Kaps) represents the city in [Lanka Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanka_Premier_League).

Colombo has the distinction of being the only city in the world to have four cricket test venues in the past: [Paikiasothy Saravanamuttu Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paikiasothy_Saravanamuttu_Stadium), [Singhalese Sports Club Cricket Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singhalese_Sports_Club_Cricket_Ground), [Colombo Cricket Club Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Cricket_Club_Ground) and [Ranasinghe Premadasa Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranasinghe_Premadasa_Stadium). The [Sugathadasa Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugathadasa_Stadium) is an international standard stadium for [athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport), [swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_\(sport\)) and [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_\(soccer\)), also held the [South Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Games) in 1991 and 2006. Situated in Colombo the [Royal Colombo Golf Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Colombo_Golf_Club) is one of the oldest in Asia. Other sporting clubs in Colombo include [Colombo Swimming Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Swimming_Club), [Colombo Rowing Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Rowing_Club) and the [Yachting Association of Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yachting_Association_of_Sri_Lanka).

[Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_Union) is also a popular sport at the club and school levels. Colombo has its local football team [Colombo FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_FC) and the sport is being developed as a part of the FIFA Goal program.

The [Colombo Port City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Port_City) is to include a new Formula One track, constructed in the vicinity of the Colombo Harbour. According to Dr Priyath Wickrama, the Chairman of the [Sri Lanka Ports Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Ports_Authority), an eight-lane F1 track will "definitely" be a part of the New Port City. This would host the Sri Lankan Grand Prix.

[Colombo Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Marathon) is an internationally recognised [marathon](49132.md) established in 1998.

### Media

See also: [Television in Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_Sri_Lanka)

Almost all major media businesses in Sri Lanka operate from Colombo. The state media has its offices in Bullers Road and carries out regional transmissions from there. These include the [Sri Lanka Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Broadcasting_Corporation) (SLBC), formerly known as [Radio Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Ceylon), and the [Sri Lanka Rupavahini Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Rupavahini_Corporation). The SLBC is the oldest radio station in [South Asia](21566765.md) and the second oldest in the world. Many private broadcasting companies have their offices and transmission stations in or around Colombo. As with most metro areas, radio bands are highly utilised for radio communications. Some of the prominent radio stations broadcasting in the Colombo area are [Sirasa FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirasa_FM), [FM Derana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FM_Derana), [Hiru FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiru_FM), [Shakthi FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakthi_FM), [Vettri FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vettri_FM), [Sooriyan FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sooriyan_FM), [Kiss FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_FM_\(Sri_Lanka\)), Lite FM, [Yes FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yes_FM), [Gold FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_FM_\(Sri_Lanka\)), Sith FM, Y FM, E FM and many more.

Television networks operating in the Colombo metro area include the state-owned television broadcasting networks which are broadcast by the [Rupavahini Corporation of Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Rupavahini_Corporation), broadcasting television in the official languages Sinhala and Tamil. English language television is also broadcast, more targeted to the demographics of the English speaking Sri Lankans, expatriate communities and tourists. There are as well several private operators. Many of the privately run television station networks were often based upon operational expansions of pre-existing commercial radio networks and broadcast infrastructure.
