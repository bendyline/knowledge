**Niigata Prefecture** (新潟県, *Niigata-ken*; Japanese pronunciation: [\[ɲiː.ɡa.ta, -taꜜ.keɴ, ɲiː.ŋa-\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)[^cite_note-2]) is a [prefecture](74517.md) in the [Chūbu region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%ABbu_region) of [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu) of [Japan](15573.md).[^cite_note-3] Niigata Prefecture has a population of 2,071,066 (as of 1 October 2025) and is the [fifth-largest prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Japanese_prefectures_by_area) of Japan by geographic area at 12,584.18 km<sup>2</sup> (4,858.78 sq mi). Niigata Prefecture borders [Toyama Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyama_Prefecture) and [Nagano Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagano_Prefecture) to the southwest, [Gunma Prefecture](212632.md) to the south, [Fukushima Prefecture](211914.md) to the east, and [Yamagata Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamagata_Prefecture) to the northeast.

[Niigata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niigata,_Niigata) is the capital and largest city of Niigata Prefecture, with other major cities including [Nagaoka](6793151.md), [Jōetsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Detsu,_Niigata), and [Sanjō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanj%C5%8D,_Niigata).[^cite_note-4] Niigata Prefecture contains the Niigata Major Metropolitan Area centered on Niigata with a population of 1,395,612, the largest [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) on the [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan) coast and the twelfth-largest in Japan. Niigata Prefecture is part of the historic [Hokuriku region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokuriku_region) and features [Sado Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sado_Island), the sixth largest island of Japan in area following the [four main islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Japan) and [Okinawa Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island).

## History

See also: [Historic Sites of Niigata Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Sites_of_Niigata_Prefecture)



Rinsenji Temple, the family temple of Uesugi

Until after the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration), the area that is now Niigata Prefecture was divided into [Echigo Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echigo_Province) (on the mainland) and [Sado Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sado_Province).[^cite_note-5] During the [Sengoku period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sengoku_period), the [Nagao clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagao_clan), who were at times vassals to the Uesugi, ruled a fief in the western part of modern Niigata from Kasugayama Castle. The most notable member of the Nagao clan was Nagao Kagetora, later and better known as [Uesugi Kenshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uesugi_Kenshin). He unified the leaders of Echigo Province and became its sole ruler. By taking the surname Uesugi, he also became the head of the Uesugi clan and effectively brought their realm under his control.

The city of Niigata is now the third largest Japanese city facing the Sea of Japan, after [Fukuoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka) and [Kitakyushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitakyushu). It was the first Japanese port on the Sea of Japan to be opened to foreign trade following the opening of Japan by [Matthew Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Perry_\(naval_officer\)). It has since played an important role in trade with [Russia](25391.md) and [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea). A freighter from [North Korea](21255.md) visits Niigata once a month, in one of the few forms of direct contact between Japan and that country.

The [Etsuzankai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etsuzankai) organization, led by the politician [Kakuei Tanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakuei_Tanaka), was highly influential in bringing infrastructure improvements to Niigata Prefecture in the 1960s and 1970s. These included the [Jōetsu Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Detsu_Shinkansen) [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) line and the [Kanetsu Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanetsu_Expressway) to Tokyo.

On October 23, 2004, the [Chūetsu earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Ch%C5%ABetsu_earthquake) struck Niigata Prefecture and was measured at [Shindo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Meteorological_Agency_seismic_intensity_scale) 6+ at [Ojiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojiya,_Niigata).

On January 9, 2006, a heavy [winter storm](244878.md) struck the prefecture and its neighbors. At least 71 people died and more than 1,000 were injured. Also in 2006, a massive tsunami and earthquake damaged homes and caused casualties in the maritime areas of Niigata Prefecture, especially near [Sado Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sado,_Niigata).

On July 16, 2007, another [earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Niigata_earthquake) hit the area.

Niigata Prefecture hosts the [Fuji Rock Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuji_Rock_Festival), an annual event held at the Naeba ski resort. The three-day event, organized by Smash Japan, features more than 200 Japanese and international musicians. It is one of the largest outdoor music events in Japan, with more than 100,000 people attending in 2005.

## Geography

Niigata Prefecture stretches about 240 km (149 mi) along the [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan), from the southwest to the northeast, with a coastal plain between the mountains and the sea. It also includes [Sado Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sado,_Niigata). Niigata Prefecture could be placed in either the [Hokuriku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokuriku_region) or the [Kōshinetsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dshin'etsu_region), both of which are considered parts of the [Chūbu region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%ABbu_region). The prefecture is generally divided into four geographical areas: [Jōetsu region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Detsu_region) (上越) in the south, [Chūetsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%ABetsu) (中越) in the center, [Kaetsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaetsu?action=edit\&redlink=1) (下越) in the north, and [Sado Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sado,_Niigata). The mouth of the [Shinano River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinano_River), the longest river in Japan, is located in Niigata Prefecture.

As of 1 April 2014, 25% of the total land area of the prefecture was designated as [Natural Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Japan#History), namely [Bandai-Asahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandai-Asahi_National_Park), [Chūbu-Sangaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%ABbu-Sangaku_National_Park), [Nikkō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikk%C5%8D_National_Park), and [Oze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oze_National_Park) National Parks; [Echigo Sanzan-Tadami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echigo_Sanzan-Tadami_Quasi-National_Park) and [Sado-Yahiko-Yoneyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sado-Yahiko-Yoneyama_Quasi-National_Park) Quasi-National Parks; and thirteen Prefectural Natural Parks.[^cite_note-6]

## Economy

### Agriculture, forestry and fishing



Paddy fields in Minamiuonuma

The major industry in Niigata Prefecture is agriculture. Rice is the principal product, and among the prefectures of Japan Niigata ranks first in rice output, followed by [Hokkaido](58092.md) and [Akita](61729.md).[^cite_note-7] The area around [Uonuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uonuma,_Niigata) is known for producing the [Koshihikari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koshihikari) variety, widely considered to be the highest-quality rice produced in Japan.

Rice-related industries are also very important to the prefectural economy. Niigata Prefecture is known throughout Japan for its high-quality [sake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sake), [senbei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senbei), [mochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mochi), and [arare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arare_\(food\)). In [sake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sake) production, the prefecture comes third after [Gunma](212632.md) and [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Prefecture) prefectures.

The prefecture was also the place of origin of the ornamental [carp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carp) known as [koi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koi).

Niigata Prefecture produces the highest volume of [azaleas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azalea) and cut [lilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily) in Japan, and is increasing the production of cut flowers and flower bulbs. Along with [Toyama Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyama_Prefecture), it produces the highest volume of [tulips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulip) in the country.

### Mining and manufacturing



Ruins of Kitazawa Flotation Plant, [Sado gold mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sado_mine)

[Crude oil](23195.md) is produced in Niigata Prefecture, although Japan relies heavily on petroleum imported from other countries. [Kerosene heaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerosene_heater) are also produced for use in the cold Niigata winters.

Kinzan, on [Sado Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sado,_Niigata), was an active [gold mine](1291393.md) until it was closed in 1989.

[Sanjō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanj%C5%8D,_Niigata) and [Tsubame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsubame,_Niigata) produce 90 percent of all the silverware made in Japan. The two cities are second after [Osaka](52230.md) in the production of [scissors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scissors), [kitchen knives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_kitchen_knives), nail clippers and [wrenches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrench).

Niigata Prefecture may have been the first area in Japan to produce [knitted textiles](2162735.md), although the earliest products may have been imported from China. A [nuclear power plant](207347.md), which formerly had the highest energy output in the world, is located in the tiny village of [Kariwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kariwa,_Niigata). It has been closed since the [Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukushima_Daiichi_nuclear_disaster).[^cite_note-8]

- *Hegi-soba* ([soba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soba) from the [Uonuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uonuma,_Niigata) and [Ojiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojiya,_Niigata) areas, flavored with a special kind of seaweed)

- "[Tsubame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsubame,_Niigata)-[Sanjō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanj%C5%8D,_Niigata) [ramen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramen)" (ramen made using thick [udon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udon)-style noodles)

- [Tochio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochio,_Niigata) *[aburage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aburage)* (aburaage is called "aburage" in [Tochio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochio,_Niigata))

- *Kirazu* (dishes using *[okara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okara_\(food\))*)

- *Kakinomoto* (edible [chrysanthemums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysanthemums))

- *[Kanzuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanzuri)* (a fermented paste from [Myōkō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My%C5%8Dk%C5%8D,_Niigata) made by leaving chili peppers exposed on snow, then adding salt, [yuzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuzu), and [kōji mold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dji_mold))

## Tourism and sports

Much of the tourism in Niigata centers around [skiing](28478.md), especially in the alpine areas of [Myōkō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My%C5%8Dk%C5%8D,_Niigata) and [Yuzawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuzawa,_Niigata), and going to [onsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onsen). [Sado Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sado,_Niigata) off the west coast of Niigata is accessible via ferry (taking one to two and a half hours) from [Naoetsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naoetsu?action=edit\&redlink=1) or [Niigata City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niigata,_Niigata).

Professional sports clubs include [Albirex Niigata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albirex_Niigata), a [J-League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J-League) Division 1 Football Club, and [Niigata Albirex BB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niigata_Albirex_BB), a BJ (Basketball Japan) League team.
