The **list of weather records** includes the most extreme occurrences of [weather phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_severe_weather_phenomena) for various categories. Many weather records are measured under specific conditions—such as surface temperature and wind speed—to keep consistency among measurements around the Earth. Each of these records is understood to be the record value officially observed, as these records may have been exceeded before modern [weather instrumentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_weather_instruments) was invented, or in remote areas without an official [weather station](206018.md). This list does not include [remotely sensed observations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_sensing) such as satellite measurements, since those values are not considered official records.[^cite_note-2]

### Measuring conditions

The standard measuring conditions for temperature are in the air, 1.25 metres (4.1 ft) to 2.00 metres (6.6 ft) above the ground,[^cite_note-978-92-63-10008-5-4] and shielded from direct sunlight intensity (hence the term *x* degrees "in the shade").[^cite_note-running2011-5] The following lists include all officially confirmed claims measured by those methods.

Temperatures measured directly on the ground may exceed air temperatures by 30 to 50 °C (54 to 90 °F).[^cite_note-running2011-5] The highest natural ground surface temperature ever recorded may have been an alleged reading of 93.9 °C (201.0 °F) at [Furnace Creek, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furnace_Creek,_California), United States, on 15 July 1972.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-6] In 2011, a ground temperature of 84 °C (183.2 °F) was recorded in [Port Sudan](213572.md), Sudan.[^cite_note-7] The theoretical maximum possible ground surface temperature has been estimated to be between 90 and 100 °C (194 and 212 °F) for dry, darkish soils of low thermal conductivity.[^cite_note-8]

Satellite measurements of ground temperature taken between 2003 and 2009, taken with the [MODIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderate_Resolution_Imaging_Spectroradiometer) infrared spectroradiometer on the [Aqua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqua_\(satellite\)) satellite, found a maximum temperature of 70.7 °C (159.3 °F), which was recorded in 2005 in the [Lut Desert, Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lut_Desert,_Iran). The Lut Desert was also found to have the highest maximum temperature in five of the seven years measured (2004, 2005, 2006, 2007 and 2009). These measurements reflect averages over a large region and so are lower than the maximum point surface temperature.[^cite_note-running2011-5]

Satellite measurements of the surface temperature of Antarctica, taken between 1982 and 2013, found a coldest temperature of −93.2 °C (−135.8 °F) on 10 August 2010, at [81°48′S 59°18′E  /  81.8°S 59.3°E  / -81.8; 59.3](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=List_of_weather_records\&params=81.8_S_59.3_E_). Although this is not comparable to an air temperature, it is believed that the air temperature at this location would have been lower than the official record lowest air temperature of −89.2 °C (−128.6 °F).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

### Hottest

#### Highest temperatures ever recorded

Main articles: [Highest temperature recorded on Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest_temperature_recorded_on_Earth) and [Heat wave](293270.md)



A world map showing areas with [Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *B* classification (dry climates). The temperatures of the hot variants *(BWh, BSh)* of these climates have the potential to exceed 50 °C (122 °F) during the hottest seasons.

According to the [World Meteorological Organization](33584.md) (WMO), the highest temperature ever recorded globally is 56.7 °C (134.1 °F) on 10 July 1913 in [Furnace Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furnace_Creek,_California) (Greenland Ranch), California[^cite_note-WMOhighesttemp-11], but the validity of this record is challenged as possible problems with the reading have since been discovered. Christopher C. Burt, a weather historian writing for [Weather Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_Underground_\(weather_service\)), believes that the 1913 [Death Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Valley) reading is "a myth", and is at least 2.2 or 2.8 °C (4 or 5 °F) too high.[^cite_note-KPBS-report-12] Burt proposes that the highest reliably recorded temperature on Earth could still be at Death Valley, but is instead 54.0 °C (129.2 °F) recorded on 30 June 2013.[^cite_note-Burt-13] This is lower than a 1931 measurement of 55 °C (131 °F) recorded in Kebili, Tunisia, which has been verified by the WMO, although it has been disputed by most meteorologists.[^cite_note-AMS2013-14] The temperature of 54 °C (129 °F) is also matched by a 1942 record from [Tirat Zvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirat_Zvi), Israel.[^cite_note-ims-15] In 2016 and 2017, readings in [Kuwait](7515890.md) and [Iran](14653.md) also matched the 2013 Death Valley record, while readings in 2020 and 2021 also at Furnace Creek went even higher, up to 54.4 °C (130 °F), however, they have not yet been validated by WMO.[^cite_note-16] The WMO has stated they stand by the 1913 record pending any future investigations.

The former highest official temperature on Earth, 58.0 °C (136.4 °F), measured in [ʽAziziya, Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CA%BDAziziya) on 13 September 1922, was reassessed in July 2012 by the WMO which published a report that invalidated the record.[^cite_note-AMS2013-14] Another record hottest temperature was reportedly reached during a severe [heat burst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_burst) that occurred in [Kopperl, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopperl,_Texas) on 15 June 1960. Also nicknamed "Satan's Storm", the temperature is reported to have increased to around 60 °C (140 °F) in a matter of minutes during the decay phase of a thunderstorm.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] This event, however, is poorly known and is not commonly included on lists of the highest globally recorded temperatures. There have been other unconfirmed reports of high temperatures, but these temperatures have never been officially validated by national weather services/WMO, and are currently considered to have been recorder's errors,[^cite_note-19] thus not being recognised as world records.[^cite_note-20]

#### Highest global average temperature

The warmest day on record for the entire planet was 22 July 2024 when the highest *global average temperature* was recorded at 17.16 °C (62.89 °F).[^cite_note-\:2-21] The previous record was 17.09 °C (62.76 °F) set the day before on 21 July 2024.[^cite_note-\:2-21] The month of July 2023 was the hottest month on record globally.[^cite_note-22] September 2023 was the most anomalously warm month, averaging 1.75 °C (3.15 °F) above the preindustrial average for September.[^cite_note-23] The [Copernicus Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernicus_Programme) (begun 1940) had recorded 13 August 2016, as the hottest global temperature, but by July 2024, that date had been downgraded to the fourth hottest.[^cite_note-24]

The [World Meteorological Organization](33584.md) (WMO) stated in its *State of the Global Climate 2025* report that the period from 2015 to 2025 represented the warmest eleven-year span since global temperature records began. The report also indicated that 2025 ranked among the two or three hottest individual years on record globally, with the global mean temperature estimated at approximately 1.43 °C (2.57 °F) above the 1850-1900 preindustrial baseline.[^cite_note-25]

## Tornadoes

Main article: [Tornado records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_records)



The Tri-State Tornado holds many records, including the longest known tornado track, fastest speed for a significant tornado, and the deadliest tornado outside of [Bangladesh](3454.md).

### Deadliest in history

- **On Earth**: Approximately 1,300 deaths ([Daulatpur–Saturia tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daulatpur%E2%80%93Saturia_tornado)); [Manikganj District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manikganj_District), Bangladesh on 26 April 1989.

  [^cite_note-tornado_project_Bangladesh-309]

- **In North America:** 695 deaths ([Tri-State Tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tri-State_Tornado)); [Missouri](19571.md), [Illinois](14849.md), [Indiana](21883857.md), United States, 18 March 1925.

  [^cite_note-Handy_Weather_Answer_Book-204]

- **In Europe:** 600 or more deaths ([Grand Harbour Tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valletta,_Malta_Tornado)); [Valletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valletta), Malta, 23 September 1551 or 1556 (sources conflict).

  [^cite_note-TORRO_records-310]

- **In South America:** 300 deaths ([1926 Encarnación tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1926_Encarnaci%C3%B3n_tornado)), [Encarnacióm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encarnaci%C3%B3n,_Paraguay), [Paraguay](23420.md), 20 September 1926.

- **In Australia:** Three deaths, [Kin Kin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kin_Kin), [Queensland](59051.md) tornado, 14 August 1971.

  [^cite_note-311]

### Outbreaks

- **Largest**: The [2011 Super Outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Super_Outbreak): 207 confirmed tornadoes occurred in a span of 24 hours on April 27, 2011, with a total of 367 occurring throughout the duration of the outbreak. They affected six US states, and included 33 rated [EF2](3724479.md), 21 rated EF3, 11 rated EF4, and 4 rated EF5.

  [^cite_note-312]

- **Most severe**: The [1974 Super Outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Super_Outbreak) produced 7 F5, 23 F4, 34 F3, and 34 F2 tornadoes on April 3–4, 1974. It received a score on expert [Thomas P. Grazulis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_P._Grazulis)'s outbreak intensity score of 578, surpassing the 2011 outbreak's score of 378.

  [^cite_note-313]

## Tropical cyclones

Main article: [List of tropical cyclone records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tropical_cyclone_records)

### Most intense (by minimum surface air pressure)

- **Most intense ever recorded**: 870 hPa (25.69 [inHg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inches_of_mercury)); [eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_\(cyclone\)) of [Super Typhoon Tip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Tip) over the northwest Pacific Ocean, 12 October 1979.

  [^cite_note-Handy_Weather_Answer_Book-204]

- **Most intense in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md):** 872 hPa (25.75 inHg); eye of [Hurricane Patricia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Patricia) over the eastern Pacific Ocean, 23 October 2015.

  [^cite_note-strongest_hurricane-314]

- **Most intense ever recorded on land:** 892 hPa (26.35 inHg); [Craig Key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Key), [Florida](18933066.md), United States, eye of the [Labor Day Hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Day_Hurricane_of_1935), 2 September 1935. While other landfalling tropical cyclones potentially had lower pressures, data is vague from areas other than the [Atlantic basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_basin), especially before the invention of [weather satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_satellite).

  [^cite_note-315]

- **Most intense landfall (estimated):** 884 hPa (26.10 inHg); [Rakiraki District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakiraki_District,_Fiji), [Viti Levu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viti_Levu), [Fiji](10707.md), during [Cyclone Winston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Winston), 20 February 2016. Although no official land pressure readings were recorded at the [landfall](5016402.md) site, it is estimated that Winston made landfall with the aforementioned pressure.

  [^cite_note-SPEArTC-316]

  &#x20;A reading of 880hPa (26inHg) from [Bathurst Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathurst_Bay), [Queensland](59051.md), [Australia](4689264.md), during [Cyclone Mahina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Mahina), on 4 March 1899 is disputed to have been recorded by Captain William Porter, the same person who recorded the currently established mark of 914hPa (27inHg).

  [^cite_note-317]

  &#x20;Further evidence to back up this claim comes in the form of an eyewitness estimate for the storm surge at Ninian Bay, 30 km from Bathurst Bay, which was said to be approximately 13m. This figure would be consistent with the pressure of 880 hPa (26 inHg) allegedly recorded by Capt. Porter.

  [^cite_note-318]

This storm had a cloud top height of 68,000 ft (21 km; 12.9 mi). Lightning from this storm was detected as far as 50–60 mi (80–97 km) away from the center of the storm.
