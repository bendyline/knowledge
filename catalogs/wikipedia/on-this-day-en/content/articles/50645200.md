***Future Present Past*** is the second [EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play) by American band [the Strokes](148546.md), released on June 3, 2016 through [Cult Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_Records). The title represents each song: "Drag Queen" refers to the "future", "Oblivius"—the "present", and "Threat of Joy"—the "past".[^cite_note-Consequence_of_Sound-1][^cite_note-TripleJ-2]

## Promotion and release

The night before the announcement of *Future Present Past*, billboards in [London](17867.md) and [New York City](645042.md) began displaying promotional videos for the Strokes featuring a running figure and each word comprising the EP's title. Cult Records posted a video of one such billboard to its [Instagram](31591547.md) account.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] On May 26, 2016, the Strokes lead singer [Julian Casablancas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Casablancas) announced *Future Present Past* during the premiere of his [SiriusXMU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SiriusXMU) show *Culture Void*, and the album became available for pre-order.[^cite_note-billboard-oblivius-5][^cite_note-cult-press-release-6] Three tracks from the EP were premiered that same day: "Oblivius" during the *Culture Void* broadcast, "Drag Queen" on [Zane Lowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zane_Lowe)'s [Beats 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beats_1) show, and "Threat of Joy" on [Annie Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Mac)'s [BBC Radio 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_1) show.[^cite_note-billboard-oblivius-5] The Strokes performed each of the three songs on May 31 during a concert at the [Capitol Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Theatre_\(Port_Chester,_New_York\)) in [Port Chester, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Chester,_New_York).[^cite_note-7] Beginning [Memorial Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Day), the Strokes and Cult Records hosted a [pop-up shop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop-up_shop) in New York City for a week.[^cite_note-cult-press-release-6]

The EP was released on June 3 via [10-inch vinyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_inch_vinyl) and digital download, the same day as the Strokes' headline performance at the [Governors Ball Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governors_Ball_Music_Festival).[^cite_note-cult-press-release-6] It was their first EP since *The Observer* in 2003 and their first record released under Casablancas' label Cult Records.[^cite_note-Consequence_of_Sound-1]

## Composition

The opening track "Drag Queen" features [post-punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-punk) influences and pitch-shifted, compressed vocals,[^cite_note-Slate-8][^cite_note-AllMusic-9] and its bassline drew comparisons to [Joy Division](16579.md) and its successor [New Order](22146.md).[^cite_note-Drowned_in_Sound-10][^cite_note-Consequence_of_Sound-1] Critics viewed its [anti-capitalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-capitalist), dystopia-focused lyrics as an exploration of themes that had influenced Casablancas' songwriting outside of the Strokes, including for [The Voidz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Voidz)' 2014 album *[Tyranny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyranny_\(The_Voidz_album\))*.[^cite_note-Consequence_of_Sound-1][^cite_note-Drowned_in_Sound-10] "Oblivius" features falsetto vocals *[Drowned in Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drowned_in_Sound)*'s Derek Robertson described as maintaining the "very [Occupy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupy_movement) serious" tone of "Drag Queen"'s lyrics while introducing a more "joyous" sound.[^cite_note-Drowned_in_Sound-10] Critics found "Threat of Joy" to be reminiscent of the Strokes' musical style in their debut album *[Is This It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Is_This_It)*.[^cite_note-Boston_Globe-11][^cite_note-DIY-12] *[NME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NME)*'s Barry Nicolson noted the simplicity of the song's main riff and Casablancas' ad-libbing as influences from the Strokes' earlier work.[^cite_note-NME-13] The EP ends with a remix of "Oblivius" by the Strokes drummer [Fabrizio Moretti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrizio_Moretti). [*Slate*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_\(magazine\))'s Darian Alexander described the remix as a "space-y, synth-y alternative version" of the original track.[^cite_note-Slate-8]

## Packaging

The 10-inch vinyl release of *Future Present Past* features stickers featuring the Strokes' and Cult Records' logos, as well as digital download access for each track on the record. Cult Records sold limited edition red vinyl copies of the record on their website, in contrast with blue vinyl copies made available at retailers.[^cite_note-cult-press-release-6] The cover features the painting *Low Go* by Max Krance.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] Critics commented that the track listing corresponds sonically with the title—"Drag Queen" the future, "Oblivius" the present, and "Threat of Joy" the past.[^cite_note-Consequence_of_Sound-1][^cite_note-DIY-12]

## Music videos

On June 2, 2016, the band released a lyric video for "Drag Queen", featuring various [vaporwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaporwave)-themed [GIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GIF) animations created by the artist KidMograph/Gustavo Torres, and edited and arranged by Liz Hirsch.

Initially "Oblivius" was to have a music video, but Casablancas later stated that it was "shut down" by the band's publishers as it became "too political".[^cite_note-16] "Threat of Joy" was chosen instead, and was shot by the band's frequent collaborator [Warren Fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Fu). Fu stated that the plot had been written with two days' notice, and features a tongue-in-cheek look at the failed attempt to make an "Oblivius" video as its "[MacGuffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacGuffin)". It was released via [Noisey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noisey) on June 28, 2016.

A lyric video for "Oblivius" was the EP's final, and was released on July 12, 2016. Like "Drag Queen", it features vaporwave-themed GIF animations created by KidMograph/Gustavo Torres, edited and arranged by Liz Hirsch.

*Future Present Past* received positive reviews, and holds a score of 75/100 on Metacritic based on six reviews, indicating a "generally favorable" response.[^cite_note-MC-17] "Mystery has forever shaped the mythology behind the Strokes, and they're rarely so forthcoming. Which is why Casablancas' peculiar transparency is one of the more alluring accomplishments," said Michael Roffman of *[Consequence of Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consequence_of_Sound)*.[^cite_note-Consequence_of_Sound-1] Barry Nicolson of *[NME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NME)* added that, "Perhaps the highest compliment you could pay this EP is that if you didn't know who it was and had no preconceived notions about what it should—or shouldn't—sound like, you'd think you had stumbled across something very special indeed."[^cite_note-NME-13]
