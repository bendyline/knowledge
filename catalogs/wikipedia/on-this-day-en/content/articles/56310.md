**Douglas County** is a [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County) located in the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [Colorado](5399.md). As of the [2020 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the population was 357,978. The county is named in honor of U.S. Senator [Stephen A. Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_A._Douglas).[^cite_note-2020Census-2] The county seat is [Castle Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Rock,_Colorado).[^cite_note-GR6-3]

Douglas County is part of the [Denver metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_metropolitan_area). It is located midway between Colorado's two largest cities, Denver and [Colorado Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Springs,_Colorado), and contains a portion of Aurora, the state's third-largest city. Douglas County has the [highest median household income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-income_counties_in_the_United_States) of any Colorado county or statistical equivalent. It is ranked seventh nationally in that category.

Douglas County is one of Colorado's fastest growing counties.[^cite_note-4]

## Overview

Douglas County is lightly wooded, mostly with [ponderosa pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponderosa_pine), with broken terrain characterized by mesas, [foothills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foothills), and small streams. [Cherry Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_Creek_\(Colorado\)) and [Plum Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plum_Creek_\(Douglas_County,_Colorado\)) rise in Douglas County and flow north toward Denver and into the [South Platte River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Platte_River). Both were subject to flash flooding in the past, Plum Creek being partially responsible for the Denver flood of 1965. Cherry Creek and Plum Creek are now dammed.

Most residents commute to workplaces elsewhere in the metropolitan area outside of the county. Suburban development is supplementing the traditional [ranching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranching) economy of the county.

## History

Douglas County was one of the original 17 counties created in the Colorado Territory by the Colorado Territorial Legislature on November 1, 1861. The county was named in honor of [U.S. Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Senator) [Stephen A. Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_A._Douglas)[^cite_note-5] of [Illinois](14849.md), who died five months before the county was created. The county seat was originally [Franktown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franktown,_Colorado), but was moved to [California Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Ranch,_Colorado) in 1863, and then to Castle Rock in 1874. Although the county's boundaries originally extended eastward to the [Kansas](16716.md) state border, in 1874, most of the eastern portion of the county became part of [Elbert County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbert_County,_Colorado).

### Parks and recreational areas

Three state parks fall within Douglas County: [Castlewood Canyon State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlewood_Canyon_State_Park), [Chatfield State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatfield_State_Park) and [Roxborough State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roxborough_State_Park). Parts of the county lie within the [Pike National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pike_National_Forest) and were crossed by the historic [South Platte Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Platte_Trail).

Recreation trails in the county include:

- [American Discovery Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Discovery_Trail)

- [Colorado Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Trail)

- [Devils Head National Recreation Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devils_Head_National_Recreation_Trail?action=edit\&redlink=1)

- [Highline Canal National Recreation Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highline_Canal_National_Recreation_Trail)

- [Platte River Greenway National Recreation Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platte_River_Greenway_National_Recreation_Trail?action=edit\&redlink=1)

- [Ridgeline Open Space Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridgeline_Open_Space)

- [Bluffs Regional Park Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluffs_Regional_Park_Trail?action=edit\&redlink=1)

The [Rueter–Hess Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rueter%E2%80%93Hess_Reservoir), when filled, may provide significant recreation, including fishing, hiking, and nonmotorized boating. If filled to capacity, surface size would be 1,140 acres, making this a fairly significant reservoir in Colorado and Douglas County's largest body of water.

[Cherokee Ranch and Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee_Ranch) is a 3,400-acre privately owned property which forms a 12,000-acre [open space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_space_reserve) with the Highlands Ranch Backcountry and [Daniels Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniels_Park). The area is host to a variety of animals and the [Cherokee Ranch petrified forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee_Ranch_petrified_forest).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

&#x20;The **Prairie Canyon Ranch**, at 4620 [CO-83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_State_Highway_83), about 10 miles (16 km) south of [Franktown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franktown,_Colorado), is a Douglas County Open Space. It is a working cattle ranch on 978 acres (3.96 km<sup>2</sup>), open to the public on special events.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

### 2000 census

As of the [census](6889.md)[^cite_note-GR8-21] of 2000, 175,766 people, 60,924 households, and 49,835 families were residing in the county. The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) was 209 people per square mile (81 people/km<sup>2</sup>). The 63,333 housing units averaged 75 units per square mile (29 units/km<sup>2</sup>). The [racial makeup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)) of the county was 92.77% White, 2.51% Asian, 0.95% African American, 0.41% Native American, 1.49% from other races, and 1.88% from two or more races. Hispanics or Latinos of any race made up 5.06% of the population.

Of the 60,924 households, 47.2% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 73.8% were married couples living together, 5.7% had a female householder with no husband present, and 18.2% were not families. About 13.3% of all households were made up of individuals, and 1.9% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.88, and the average family size was 3.19.

In the county, the population distribution was 31.6% under the age of 18, 4.8% from 18 to 24, 37.9% from 25 to 44, 21.6% from 45 to 64, and 4.2% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 34 years. For every 100 females, there were 99.7 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 97.4 males.

The median income for a household in the county was \$82,929, and for a family was \$88,482 (these figures had risen to \$93,819 and \$102,767, respectively, as of a 2007 estimate[^cite_note-22]). Males had a median income of \$60,729 versus \$38,965 for females. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the county was \$34,848. About 1.6% of families and 2.1% of the population were below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line), including 1.9% of those under age 18 and 3.7% of those age 65 or over.

Douglas County had the highest median household income of any Colorado county or statistical equivalent in 2000. In 2008, it [ranked eighth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest-income_counties_in_the_United_States#25_highest-income_counties_by_median_household_income_\(2006%E2%80%9308\)) in the United States in that category; it was one of two in the top 15 not in the vicinity of [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area) or [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Metropolitan_Area).

### Health and longevity

In 2021, Douglas County was judged by the *[U.S. News & World Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._News_&_World_Report)* to be the second healthiest of 3,143 [counties and county-equivalents of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_and_county-equivalents_of_the_United_States) based on 84 different factors. Residents of the county lived 84.0 years on the average compared to the U.S. average of 77.5 years.[^cite_note-US_News-23]

### Political history

As a primarily suburban county, Douglas County has long been known as a Republican stronghold. In the 2012 election, Mitt Romney won 62% of the vote. However, Douglas County has become more competitive in recent years, with Donald Trump winning 55% of the county's vote in 2016 and only 52% of the vote in 2020 and 2024. In 2024, Kamala Harris achieved the highest vote share for a Democratic presidential nominee in the county since 1964.[^cite_note-26] In [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Colorado_gubernatorial_election), incumbent Democratic governor Jared Polis lost the county by a razor-thin margin, winning nearly 49% of the vote.[^cite_note-27] However, Democratic strength is mostly limited to northern Douglas County, including [Highlands Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highlands_Ranch,_Colorado), [Lone Tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lone_Tree,_Colorado), and [Meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian,_Colorado), while the rest of the county is still strongly Republican.[^cite_note-28]

## Education

Douglas County is served by [Douglas County School District RE-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_County_School_District_RE-1), which covers the entire county.[^cite_note-30] It is the third-largest school district in Colorado. In addition to traditional neighborhood schools, the district includes sixteen charter schools,[^cite_note-31] four option schools, and an online school. Schools are rated generally high in the area.

The University Center at Chaparral in Parker offers courses through [Arapahoe Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arapahoe_Community_College), the [University of Colorado Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Denver), University College of the [University of Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Denver), and the Douglas County School District. The [University of Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Phoenix) has a campus in Lone Tree.

The county was home to its own university, the [University of Colorado South Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_South_Denver) in Lone Tree, but it permanently closed in 2021 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and financial concerns.

### Libraries

The [Douglas County Libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_County_Libraries) system has seven branches throughout the county. The library also houses the Douglas County History Research Center, which collects and preserves the history of Douglas County, the High Plains, the Divide area of the Front Range and Colorado, to provide historical research resources to the public.[^cite_note-32]
