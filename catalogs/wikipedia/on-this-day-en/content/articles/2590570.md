**Suwayda** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): ٱلسُّوَيْدَاء, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *as-Suwaydāʾ*), also spelled **Sweida**,[^cite_note-4] is a city located in southern [Syria](7515849.md), close to the [border with Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan%E2%80%93Syria_border). It is a [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze)-majority city, with small [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian) and [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Muslim) [Bedouin](61770.md) minorities.[^cite_note-BDS-5]

It is the capital of the [Suwayda Governorate](3096933.md), one of Syria's 14 [governorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate), bordering Jordan in the South, [Daraa Governorate](5317030.md) in the West and [Rif Dimashq Governorate](4521946.md) in the north and east.&#x20;

The city is sometimes referred to as "Little Venezuela" due to the city's influx of affluent [Venezuelan Syrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelans_in_Syria) immigrants.[^cite_note-6] Many of them are descendants of Suwayda natives who emigrated to [Venezuela](32374.md) in the nineteenth century; upon returning, they brought with them the Spanish language and elements of South American culture.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

According to the [2004 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Syrian_census) conducted by Syria's [Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bureau_of_Statistics_\(Syria\)), Suwayda had a population of 73,641.[^cite_note-10]

## History

### Ancient and medieval eras

The city was founded by the [Nabataeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabataeans) as **Suada**. It became known as **Dionysias Soada** ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Διονῡσιάς) in the [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period) and the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), for the god [Dionysus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysus), patron of [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine) – the city is situated in a famous ancient wine-producing region.

The name *Dionysias* replaced the former Nabataean name in 149 AD after Nabataean influence decreased and then concentrated towards the south, as a result of the then accelerating [Hellenization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenization) of [Coele-Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coele-Syria).

Dionysias was a part of the Roman [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province) of [Arabia Petraea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia_Petraea), and received the rights of [civitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civitas) during the reign of [Commodus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodus) between 180 and 185.

[Dionysus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysus) was worshipped in the same Nabatean temple dedicated to [Dushara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dushara). This practice of associating the worship of local and Hellenic gods was common in Hellenistic Syria.

This name remained in use during the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), when the town was under the influence of the [Ghassanids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghassanids). Dionysias then was a [diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) with a [suffragan bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragan_bishop) from [Bosra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosra). It was mentioned in the *[Synecdemus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synecdemus)* of [Hierocles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierocles_\(author_of_Synecdemus\)). After the [early Muslim conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests), the city was conquered by the [Rashidun Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_Caliphate) of the [Arabs](2185.md) in 629 and became a [titular see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_see).[^cite_note-11]

[Yaqut al-Hamawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaqut_al-Hamawi) noted in the 1220s that Suwayda was "a village of the [Hauran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauran) Province".[^cite_note-12]

### Ottoman era

In 1516, the city and the adjoining region was conquered from the [Mamluk Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate) by the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md). In 1596 Suwayda appeared under the name of *Majdal Sawda* in the [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria) [tax registers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defter) as part of the *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* (subdistrict) of Bani Nasiyya of the [Hauran Sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauran_Sanjak). It had a population of 5 households and 5 bachelors, all [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim). The villagers paid a fixed tax rate of 20% on various agricultural products, including wheat, barley, summer crops, goats and/or beehives, in addition to "occasional revenues"; a total of 6,125 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e). 3/4 of the revenue went to a [waqf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waqf).[^cite_note-13]

In the late 17th and/or early 18th centuries, [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) from [Mount Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon) and other parts of greater Syria migrated in significant numbers to the [Jabal Hauran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Hauran),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFirro199236–37,_42-14] which was subsequently referred to as *Jabal al-Druze* ('the Druze Mountain'). The Jabal Hauran was full of deserted and ruined but once-prosperous and naturally defensible ancient villages which were utilized by the Druze newcomers. Initial settlement was concentrated in the northwestern area of the mountain due to the proximity to Druze villages on [Mount Hermon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hermon).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFirro199238–40-15] Settlement radiated across Jabal Hauran and Suwayda was among the villages settled by the Druze before 1812.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFirro1992150-16] By at least 1860, Suwayda had become the main Druze settlement in the region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFirro1992147-17]

In the 19th century, Dionysias was identified as Suwayda by French statesman and [antiquarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiquarian) [William Waddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Waddington). Visitors recorded the now-destroyed [Tomb of Hamrath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Hamrath), an elaborate late Hellenistic mausoleum dating from the 1st century BCE.[^cite_note-\:0-18]

### Civil war (2011–present)

Main article: [Syrian civil war](30741795.md)

On 28 October 2012, security forces launched a campaign of mass arrests in the city.[^cite_note-aljasalqin-19]

#### 2018 Suwayda attacks

Main article: [2018 Suwayda attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Suwayda_attacks)

On 25 July 2018, the city was rocked by a string of terrorist attacks. A group of at least 56 [ISIS](9087364.md)-affiliated attackers entered the city and initiated a series of gunfights and [suicide bombings](18835454.md) killing 246 people, the vast majority of them civilians. Many of the terrorists were reported killed during the attack, bringing the total death toll to at least 302 people.[^cite_note-20] Forty-two Druze residents between the ages of 7 and 60 were kidnapped by ISIS and held captive. One was executed bringing the total in captivity to 41.[^cite_note-21]

#### Protests

On 7 June 2020, anti-government protests erupted in the city due to the deteriorating economic situation. Protesters demanded the resignation of President [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md) for the first time since 2015.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] As a result of the protests, Prime Minister [Imad Khamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imad_Khamis) was sacked on 11 June and replaced by [Hussein Arnous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_Arnous).[^cite_note-24]

In February 2022, hundreds of protesters took to the streets in Suwayda to decry corruption and worsening living standards.[^cite_note-25]

In August 2023, thousands of protestors [took to the streets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Syrian_protests) to decry worsening economic conditions and demanding the departure of Syrian President Bashar al Assad.[^cite_note-26]

#### Southern Syria offensive

During the [Southern Syria offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Syria_offensive_\(2024\)), Suwayda was captured by the [Syrian opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition) on 7 December 2024.[^cite_note-20241207_AP-27] On 18 December 2024, a delegation of the [Syrian transitional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_caretaker_government) arrived at the city to meet with the spiritual leader of the Druze.[^cite_note-28]

#### 2025 clashes

Main article: [Southern Syria clashes (July 2025–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Syria_clashes_\(July_2025%E2%80%93present\))

In July 2025, heavy fighting broke out between [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) and [Bedouin](61770.md) in Suwayda, killing dozens.[^cite_note-\:1-29] As of July 19, 2025 over 600 Druze were reported dead. The Syrian government deployed regular soldiers and Interior Ministry units there. According to Israeli officials, this was done to crack down on the Druze minority.[^cite_note-\:2-30]

The [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) (IDF) attacked [Syrian Armed Forces](27479.md) vehicles from the air and with URVs in order to prevent their arrival to the area. In a response, Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) and Defense Minister [Israel Katz](19041378.md) cited the need to protect the Druze religious minority due to their close ties to [Israeli Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Druze) as well as assuring the security of Israel's borders by preventing the deployment of weaponry and Syrian military forces in the area.[^cite_note-\:3-31]

## Geography

### Climate

Suwayda's climate is warm, dry and temperate, with no rainfall in the summer months.This location is classified as [BSk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) by [Köppen and Geiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification). The average temperature is 15.5 °C. About 323 mm of precipitation falls annually.

## Demographics

The city had a population of 73,641 in the 2004 census.[^cite_note-auto-33]

The inhabitants of the city are mainly [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze), with small [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian) (mostly [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarchate_of_Antioch)) and [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Muslim) Bedouin minorities.&#x20;

The Sunni Bedouins primarily reside on the northern and eastern outskirts of the city, in the areas of al-Maqwas, al-Haroubi, al-Mashoureb, Rajem az-Zaytoun, ad-Diyatha, and ash-Shaqrawiyah.

In 2011, the [Melkite Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Greek_Catholic_Church) had approximately 2,300 believers in Suwayda.[^cite_note-34]

## Archaeology



[Diana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana_\(mythology\)) discovered at bath by [Actaeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actaeon); mosaic in Suwayda

Many archeological sites can be found in the old part of the city:

- **Temple of Dionysus-Dushara**: eight well-decorated columns are still standing from the temple.

- **Saint Sergius Basilica**: was built in the fifth century. It has [Byzantine architectural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_architecture) elements, with an [abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey) surrounding it. The basilica was dedicated to [Sergius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergius_and_Bacchus).

- **The arch of the lesser church**: the church itself is ruined. An arch is still standing there known locally as "The Gallows" ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): المشنقة *al-Mashnaqah*) with grape motif decorations.

- **The theater**: was recently discovered, south of the [Agora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agora).

The city has many ancient reservoirs, towers and old Roman houses that are still inhabited by locals today.

Many parts of the old city still await excavation, such as the [Roman aqueduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_aqueduct), a conical reservoir, and a larger [Roman theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_theatre_\(structure\)). There is also an old 7th century [mosque](19894.md) built during the time of the [Rashidun Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_Caliphate).
