# Minivan

Not to be confused with [Minibus](1094925.md) or [Midibus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midibus).

"Mini van" redirects here. For the van version of the Mini car, see [Mini Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini#Mini_Van).



The [Chrysler Pacifica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Pacifica_\(minivan\)), the best-selling minivan in the United States as of 2025

[^cite_note-1]



The [Toyota Previa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Previa), 3rd generation

A **minivan**, commonly known as a **van**, is a [car classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_classification) for vehicles designed to transport passengers in the rear seating row(s), with reconfigurable seats in two or three rows. The equivalent classification in Europe is **MPV** (**multi-purpose vehicle**), **people carrier**,[^cite_note-2] or **M-segment**.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-EEC_Merger_Procedure-4][^cite_note-CP-5][^cite_note-PF-6]

Compared with a [full-size van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-size_van), most minivans are based on a passenger [car platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_platform) and have a lower body. Early models, such as the [Ford Aerostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Aerostar) and [Chevrolet Astro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Astro), utilized a [compact pickup truck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_pickup_truck) platform.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

Minivans often feature a 'one-box' or 'two-box' [body configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_body_configurations), a higher roof, a flat floor, sliding doors for rear passengers, and high '[H-point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-point)' seating. The largest minivan size is also referred to as 'Large MPV' and became popular following the introduction of the 1984 [Dodge Caravan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_Caravan) and [Renault Espace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Espace). Typically, these are based on platforms derived from D-segment passenger cars or compact pickups. Since the 1990s, the smaller [compact MPV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_MPV) and [mini MPV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_MPV) sizes of minivans have also become popular.[^cite_note-9]

Although predecessors to the minivan date back to the 1930s, the contemporary minivan body style was developed concurrently by several companies in the early 1980s, most notably by [Chrysler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler) (producer of the [Chrysler minivans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_minivans)) and [Renault](162292.md) (the [Renault Espace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Espace)), both of which were first sold in model year 1984. Minivans cut into and eventually overshadowed the traditional [station wagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Station_wagon) market, growing in global popularity and diversity throughout the 1990s. Since the 2000s, their reception has varied across the world. In North America, for example, they have been largely eclipsed by [crossovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossover_\(automobile\)) and [SUVs](47696.md), while in Asia, they are often marketed as [luxury cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxury_car).

## Etymology

The term *minivan* originated in North America and the United Kingdom in 1959. In the UK, the [Mini Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini#Mini_Van_\(1960%E2%80%931983\)) was a small van manufactured by the [Austin Motor Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Motor_Company), based on the newly introduced Mini car. In the US, the term was used to differentiate the smaller passenger vehicles from [full-size vans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-size_van) (such as the Ford E-Series, Dodge Ram Van, and Chevrolet Van), which were then called 'vans'.[^cite_note-10]

The first known use of the term was in 1959, but it was not commonly used until the 1980s.[^cite_note-11]

## Characteristics

### Chassis

In contrast to larger vans, most modern minivans/MPVs use a front-engine, front-wheel drive layout, while some model lines offer all-wheel drive as an option (i.e. Toyota Sienna,[^cite_note-12] Toyota Previa,[^cite_note-13] Chrysler Pacifica [^cite_note-14]). Alongside adopting the form factor introduced by [Chrysler minivans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_minivans), the configuration allows for less engine intrusion and a lower floor in the passenger compartment. In line with larger full-size vans, unibody construction has been commonly used (the spaceframe design of the [Renault Espace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Espace) and the [General Motors APV minivans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Lumina_APV) being exceptions).

Minivans/MPVs are produced on distinct chassis architecture or share platforms with other vehicles such as sedans and [crossover SUVs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossover_SUV). Minivans do not have as much ground clearance, towing capacity, or off-road capability compared to SUVs.[^cite_note-Trompete-15] Minivans provide more space for passengers and cargo than sedans and SUVs.[^cite_note-Trompete-15]

### Body style



Side profile of a [Honda Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Odyssey_\(North_America\))

Minivans/MPVs use either a [two-box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_body_configurations#Two-box_design) or a [one-box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_body_configurations#One-box_design) body design with [A, B, C, and D pillars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillar_\(car\)). The cabin may be fitted with two, three, or four rows of seats, with the most common configurations being 2+3+2 or 2+3+3. Compared to other types of passenger vehicles, the body shape of minivans is designed to maximize interior space for both passengers and cargo. It is achieved by lengthening the [wheelbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheelbase), creating a flatter floor, taller roof, and more upright side profile, but not as prominent as commercial-oriented vans that are boxier in profile. Practicality and comfort for passengers are also enhanced with a larger rear cargo space opening and larger windows.[^cite_note-16]

Some minivans/MPVs may use sliding doors, while others offer conventional forward-hinged doors. Initially, a feature of the 1982 [Nissan Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Prairie), the 1996 [Chrysler minivans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_minivans_\(NS\)) introduced a driver-side sliding door; by 2002, all minivans were sold with doors on both sides of the body. Most minivans are configured with a rear [lift gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lift_gate); few minivans have used panel-style rear doors, for example, cargo versions of the [Chevrolet Astro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Astro), [Ford Aerostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Aerostar), and the [Mercedes-Benz V-Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_V-Class).

### Interior



A cutaway [Renault Espace V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Espace#5), showcasing its relatively flat floor and third-row seat space

Most minivans have a reconfigurable interior to carry passengers and their effects. The first examples were designed with removable rear seats unlatched from the floor for removal and storage (in line with larger vans); however, users gave poor reception to the design as many seats were heavy and hard to remove. In 1995, the [Honda Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Odyssey_\(North_America\)) was introduced with a third-row seat that folded flat into the floor, which was then adopted by many competitors, including [Chrysler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler) that introduced third-row and [fold-flat second-row seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Voyager#Stow'n_Go_seating) in 2005.

High-end minivans may include distinguished features such as captain seats or [Ottoman seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_\(furniture\)), as opposed to [bench seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bench_seat) for the second row.[^cite_note-17]

## Predecessors

Before the adoption of the *minivan* term, there is a long history of one-box passenger vehicles roughly approximating the body style,[^cite_note-18] with the 1936 [Stout Scarab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stout_Scarab) often cited as the first minivan.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] The passenger seats in the Scarab were moveable and could be configured for the passengers to sit around a table in the rear of the cabin. Passengers entered and exited the Scarab via a centrally mounted door.

The [DKW Schnellaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DKW_Schnellaster)—manufactured from 1949 until 1962—featured [front-wheel drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front-wheel_drive), a [transverse engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transverse_engine), a flat floor, and multi-configurable seating, all of which would later become characteristics of minivans.[^cite_note-23]

In 1950, the [Volkswagen Type 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Type_2) adapted a bus-shaped body to the chassis of a small passenger car (the Volkswagen Beetle). When Volkswagen introduced a sliding side door to the Type 2 in 1968, it then had the prominent features that would later come to define a minivan: compact length, three rows of forward-facing seats, station wagon-style top-hinged tailgate/liftgate, sliding side door, passenger car base.[^cite_note-24]

The 1956–1969 [Fiat Multipla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_600#Multipla) also had many features in common with modern minivans. The Multipla was based on the chassis of the [Fiat 600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_600) and had a [rear engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear-engine,_rear-wheel-drive_layout) and [cab forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cab_forward) layout.[^cite_note-25]

The early 1960s saw Ford and Chevrolet introduce "compact" vans for the North American market, the [Econoline Club Wagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_econoline) and [Greenbrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Greenbrier) respectively. The Ford version was marketed in the [Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Falcon_\(North_America\)) series, the Chevrolet in the [Corvair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Corvair) 95 series. The Econoline grew larger in the 1970s, while the Greenbrier was joined by (and later replaced by) the [Chevy Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevy_Sportvan).

## North America

Due to their larger footprint and engines, minivans developed for the North American market are distinct from most minivans/MPVs marketed in other regions, such as Europe and Asia. As of 2020, average exterior length for minivans in North America ranged around 200 inches (5.08 m), while many models use [V6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V6_engine) engines with more than 270 horsepower (201 kW; 274 PS) mainly to fulfill towing capacity requirements which North American customers demand.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

In 2021, sales of the segment totalled 310,630 units in the U.S. (2.1% of the overall car market), and 33,544 in Canada (2.0% of the overall car market).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] As of 2022, the passenger-oriented minivan segment consists of the [Toyota Sienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Sienna), [Chrysler Pacifica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Pacifica), [Chrysler Voyager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Voyager), [Honda Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Odyssey_\(North_America\)), and [Kia Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kia_Carnival).[^cite_note-30]

### History

#### 1970s and 1980s



1985 [Dodge Caravan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_Caravan)

In the late 1970s, Chrysler began a development program to design "a small affordable van that looked and handled more like a car."[^cite_note-Smithsonian2-31] The result of this program was the first American minivans based on the [S platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_minivans_\(S\)), the 1984 [Plymouth Voyager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth_Voyager) and [Dodge Caravan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_Caravan).[^cite_note-32] The S minivans debuted the minivan design features of front-wheel drive, a flat floor and a sliding door for rear passengers.[^cite_note-Smithsonian2-31][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

The term minivan came into use largely compared to size to [full-size vans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-size_van); at six feet tall or lower, 1980s minivans were intended to fit inside a typical garage door opening.[^cite_note-stepper-85-022-35] In 1984, *The New York Times* described minivans "the hot cars coming out of Detroit,"[^cite_note-memory2-36] noting that "analysts say the mini-van has created an entirely new market, one that may well overshadow the... [station wagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Station_wagon)."[^cite_note-memory2-36]

In response to the popularity of the Voyager/Caravan, [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors) released the 1985 [Chevrolet Astro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Astro) and [GMC Safari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMC_Safari) badge-engineered twins, and Ford released the 1986 [Ford Aerostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Aerostar). These vehicles used a traditional [rear-wheel drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear-wheel_drive) layout, unlike the Voyager/Caravan.[^cite_note-stepper-85-022-35][^cite_note-37]

To match the launch of minivans by American manufacturers, Japanese manufacturers introduced the [Toyota Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Van), [Nissan Vanette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Vanette), and [Mitsubishi Delica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Delica) to North America in 1984, 1986, and 1987, respectively. These vehicles were marketed with the generic "Van" and "Wagon" names (for cargo and passenger vans, respectively).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

In 1989, the [Mazda MPV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda_MPV) was released as the first Japanese-brand minivan developed from the ground up specifically for the North American market. Its larger chassis allowed an optional V6 engine and four-wheel drive to fit. In contrast to the sliding doors of American minivans, a hinged passenger-side door was used. A driver-side entry was added for 1996, as Mazda gradually remarketed the model line as an early crossover SUV.

By the end of the 1980s, demand for minivans as family vehicles had largely superseded full-size station wagons in the United States.[^cite_note-40]

#### 1990s



1998–2000 [Toyota Sienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Sienna)

During the 1990s, the minivan segment underwent several significant changes. Many models switched to the front-wheel drive layout used by the Voyager/Caravan minivans. For example, Ford replaced the Aerostar with the front-wheel drive [Mercury Villager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_Villager) for 1993 and the [Ford Windstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Windstar) for 1995. The models also increased in size due to the extended-wheelbase ("Grand") versions of the Voyager and Caravan, launched in 1987. An increase in luxury features and interior equipment was seen in the Eddie Bauer version of the 1988 Ford Aerostar, the 1990 [Chrysler Town & Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Town_&_Country), and the 1990 [Oldsmobile Silhouette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldsmobile_Silhouette). The third-generation Plymouth Voyager, Dodge Caravan, and Chrysler Town & Country – released for the 1996 model year – had an additional sliding door on the driver's side.

Following the 1990 discontinuation of the Nissan Vanette in the United States, Nissan also ended the sale of the second-generation [Nissan Axxess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Axxess). Nissan reentered the segment by forming a joint venture with Ford to develop and assemble a minivan that became the [Nissan Quest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Quest) and its Mercury Villager counterpart.

Toyota also introduced the [Toyota Previa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Previa) in 1990 to replace the Van/Wagon in North America. It was designed solely as a passenger vehicle sized to compete with American-market minivans. For 1998, the [Toyota Sienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Sienna) became the first Japanese-brand minivan assembled in North America, replacing the Toyota Previa in that market. For 1999, Honda introduced a separate version of the [Odyssey for North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Odyssey_\(North_America\)), with North America receiving a larger vehicle with sliding doors. These Japanese minivans targeted a higher price bracket as they were considerably larger and had better handling/horsepower over their domestic counterparts (i.e. Dodge Caravan).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

The highest selling year for minivans was in 2000, when 1.4 million units were sold. At that time, domestic minivans were produced in large volumes and often sold for cheaper than midsize cars, however Japanese minivans were more expensive than either.[^cite_note-44]

#### 2000s and 2010s



2017 [Dodge Grand Caravan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_Grand_Caravan)

In the 2000s, sales of minivans began to decrease. In 2013, the segment's sales reached approximately 500,000, one-third of its 2000 peak.[^cite_note-Mom_mobiles-CNBC2-45] Market share of minivans in 2019 reached around 2% after a steady decline from 2004, when the segment recorded above 6% of share.[^cite_note-46] It has been suggested that the falling popularity of minivans is due to the increasing popularity of SUVs and crossovers, as well as the minivan's increasingly undesirable image as a vehicle for older drivers or the [soccer mom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_mom) demographics.[^cite_note-Mom_mobiles-CNBC2-45][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] Manufacturers have responded by marketing minivans as premium vehicles as well as lowering production volumes, resulting in a long waiting list for minivans such as the Toyota Sienna.[^cite_note-agirlsguidetocars.com-49][^cite_note-Comparing_Toyota_Grand_Highlander_t-50]

Despite the increasing popularity of three-row crossover SUVs, they have not completely replaced minivans in form or function. The [Chrysler Pacifica crossover SUV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Pacifica_\(crossover\)) (the nameplate which was later reused for a minivan in 2017) which was released in 2004 had less passenger room with its third-row seats being considered too small, compared to that of the [Chrysler Town & Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Town_&_Country) minivan which was also less expensive and more fuel-efficient. Even twenty years later while crossover SUVs had improved, minivans still hold such advantages; compared to the similar-sized [Toyota Grand Highlander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Grand_Highlander) crossover SUV which is considerably more expensive when comparably equipped, the Toyota Sienna minivan has easier loading thanks to sliding doors and lower ground clearance, more cargo space behind the 3rd row, and better comfort for the 3rd-row passengers.[^cite_note-agirlsguidetocars.com-49][^cite_note-Comparing_Toyota_Grand_Highlander_t-50]

From 2000 onward, several minivan manufacturers adopted boxier square-based exterior designs. Compared to the 1990s, minivans have also been positioned as more upmarket vehicles with available advanced equipment, including power doors and liftgate; seating that folded flat into the cabin floor, rear-view camera; parking sensors; DVD/VCR entertainment systems; and in-dash navigation (entertainment and navigation only offered on higher-end trims). However, the Quest and Sedona only echo these design changes in their third and second respective generations. At the same time, Chrysler introduced fold-flat seating in 2005 (under the trademark “Stow-n’-go”). Mazda's MPV did not feature power doors and was discontinued in 2017.



2004 [Ford Freestar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Windstar)

Due to the market decline, North American sales of the [Volkswagen Eurovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Eurovan) ceased in 2003. Ford exited the segment in 2006 when the [Ford Freestar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Freestar) was canceled, Chrysler discontinued its short-wheelbase minivans in 2007, and General Motors left the market segment in 2009 with the cancellation of the [Chevrolet Uplander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Uplander). However, Volkswagen marketed the [Volkswagen Routan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Routan) (a rebadged [Chrysler RT minivans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_minivans_\(RT\))) between 2009 and 2013. In 2010, Ford started importing the commercial-oriented [Ford Transit Connect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Transit_Connect) Wagon from Turkey. A similar vehicle, the [Mercedes-Benz Metris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_Metris), entered the North American market in 2016.

The [Kia Sedona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kia_Sedona), which was introduced for the 2002 model year, is notable for being the first minivan from a South Korean manufacturer in the region.[^cite_note-51] For 2007, [Kia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kia) also introduced the three-row [Kia Rondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kia_Rondo) compact MPV, where it was prominently marketed as a crossover due to its small size and the use of hinged rear doors.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

Another compact MPV released to the market was the [Mazda5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda5) in 2004, a three-row vehicle with rear sliding doors. Mazda claimed the model "does not fit into any traditional (North American) segmentation."[^cite_note-54] The [Ford C-Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_C-Max) was released for 2013 as a [hybrid electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_electric_vehicle) and [battery electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_electric_vehicle) compact MPV with sliding doors. However, it did not offer third-row seating in North America.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

During the 2020s, however, minivans have been reported to be resurging in popularity. Commonly cited reasons for said resurgence include larger amounts of cargo space in comparison to SUVs; lighter weight; and more optimal fuel efficiency, due to many models having recently adopted hybrid, PHEV and electric powertrains.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

### History

#### 1980s

The 1984 [Renault Espace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Espace) was the first European-developed minivan developed primarily for passenger use (as the earlier DKW and Volkswagen used their commercial van platforms in a minibus variant). Beginning development in the 1970s under the European subsidiaries of Chrysler,[^cite_note-62] the Espace was intended as a successor for the [Matra Rancho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_Rancho), leading to its use of front-hinged doors. While slow-selling at the time of its release, the Espace would become the most successful European-brand minivan.[^cite_note-63]

Initially intending to market the Espace in North America through [American Motors Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Motors_Corporation) (AMC), the 1987 sale of AMC to Chrysler canceled the plans for Renault to do so. In the late 1980s, Chrysler and Ford commenced sales of American-designed minivans in Europe (categorized as full-size in the region), selling the [Chrysler Voyager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Voyager) and Ford Aerostar. General Motors imported the Oldsmobile Silhouette (branded as the Pontiac Trans Sport), later marketing the American-produced [Opel/Vauxhall Sintra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opel_Sintra).

#### 1990s

In the 1990s, several joint ventures produced long-running minivan designs. In 1994, [badge engineered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebadging) series of [Eurovans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovans) was introduced, produced by [Sevel Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevel_Nord) and marketed by Citroën, Fiat, Lancia, and Peugeot. The Eurovans were built with two sliding doors; the gearshift was located on the dashboard to increase interior space, and a petal-type handbrake was adopted. In 1995, Ford of Europe and Volkswagen entered a joint venture, producing the [Ford Galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Galaxy), [SEAT Alhambra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEAT_Alhambra), and [Volkswagen Sharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Sharan) minivans, featuring front-hinged rear side doors.

In 1996, Mercedes introduced the [Mercedes-Benz V-Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_V-Class) as a standard panel van for cargo (called Vito) or with passenger accommodations substituted for part or all of the load area (called V-Class or Viano). In 1998, the [Fiat Multipla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_Multipla) was released. A two-row, six-seater MPV with a 3+3 seat configuration borrowing its name from [an older minivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_600#Multipla), it is notable for its highly controversial design.[^cite_note-64]

Market reaction to these new full-size MPV models was mixed. Consumers perceived MPVs as large and truck-like despite boasting similar footprints as large sedans. Arguably, cultural reasons regarding vehicle size and high fuel prices were a factor. During 1996 and 1997, the Western European MPV market expanded from around 210,000 units to 350,000 units annually. However, the growth did not continue as expected, resulting in serious plant [overcapacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overcapacity).[^cite_note-SM-65]

Renault set a new "compact MPV" standard with the [Renault Scénic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Sc%C3%A9nic) in 1996, which became popular. Based on the C-segment [Mégane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_M%C3%A9gane) platform, it offered the same multi-use and flexibility aspects as the larger MPVs but with a much smaller footprint.[^cite_note-SM-65]

#### 2000s

&#x20;After the success of the Renault Scénic, other makers have developed similar European-focused products, such as the [Opel Zafira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opel_Zafira) that offered three-row seating, [Citroën Xsara Picasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn_Xsara_Picasso) and others.[^cite_note-SM-65]

In Japan, the classification is known as "minivan" ([Japanese](15606.md): ミニバン, [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization): *Miniban*) and defined by its three-row seating capacity.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

Before the birth of minivans with modern form factors, tall wagon-type vehicles with large seating capacity in Japan were known as light vans. They commonly adopted [mid-engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-engine_design), [cab over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cab_over) design, and rear-wheel drive layout with one-box form factor. Examples included the [Toyota TownAce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_TownAce), [Nissan Vanette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Vanette), [Mitsubishi Delica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Delica), and [Mazda Bongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda_Bongo). These vehicles were based on commercial vehicles, which created a gap compared to sedans regarding ride quality and luxury.[^cite_note-DM-68]

The [Nissan Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Prairie), released in 1982, is considered the first Japanese compact minivan.[^cite_note-69] Derived closely from a compact sedan, the Prairie was marketed as a "boxy sedan", configured with sliding doors, folding rear seats, and a lifting rear hatch.[^cite_note-DM-68] The [Mitsubishi Chariot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Chariot) adopted nearly the same form factor, instead using wagon-style front-hinged doors.

In 1990, Toyota introduced the [Toyota Estima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Estima) in Japan, which carried over the mid-engine configuration of the TownAce.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] Along with its highly rounded exterior, the Estima was distinguished by its nearly panoramic window glass.[^cite_note-72] The Estima was redesigned in 2000, adopting a front-wheel drive layout and offered with a [hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_electric_vehicle) powertrain since 2001.[^cite_note-73] In 2002, Toyota introduced the [Toyota Alphard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Alphard) which was developed as a luxury-oriented model. In 2020, Lexus introduced their first [luxury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxury_car) minivan, the [Lexus LM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexus_LM), produced with varying degrees of relation with the Toyota Alphard/Vellfire. The LM designation stands for "Luxury Mover".[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

Nissan introduced the [Nissan Serena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Serena) in 1991 and the [Nissan Elgrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Elgrand) in 1997.

In 1995, Honda entered the minivan segment by introducing the [Honda Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Odyssey).[^cite_note-76] The Odyssey was designed with front-hinged doors and as derived from the Honda Accord. It came with advantages such as sedan-like driving dynamics and a lower floor to allow for easy access.[^cite_note-DM-68] A design feature that would become adopted by other manufacturers, the Odyssey introduced a rear seat that folded flat into the floor (replacing a removable rear seat). The Odyssey evolved into a low-roof, estate-like minivan until 2013, when it adopted a high-roof body with rear sliding doors.[^cite_note-77] Honda also produced the [Honda Stepwgn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Stepwgn) mid-size MPV since 1996, which is designed with a higher cabin and narrow width, and the [Honda Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Stream) since 2000 to slot below the Odyssey.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

In 2020, minivans made up 20.8% of total automobile sales in Japan, behind SUVs and compact hatchbacks, making it one of the largest minivan markets in the world.[^cite_note-80]

### South Korea

In South Korea, both the terms "minivan" and "MPV" are used.[^cite_note-IC-81] The [Kia Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kia_Carnival) (also sold the Kia Sedona) was introduced in 1998 with dual sliding doors. Sharing its configuration with the Honda Odyssey, the [Hyundai Trajet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Trajet) was sold from 1999 to 2008. Introduced in 2004, the [SsangYong Rodius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SsangYong_Rodius) is the highest-capacity minivan, seating up to 11 passengers. It was discontinued in 2019.

Current minivans marketed in South Korea are the Kia Carnival and Hyundai Staria, along with imported options such as the Toyota Sienna (originally for North America) and later generations of Honda Odyssey.[^cite_note-IC-81]

## Size categories

### Mini MPV

Main article: [Mini MPV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_MPV)



[Opel Meriva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opel_Meriva) (2003–2017)&#x20;

Mini MPV – an abbreviation for Mini Multi-Purpose Vehicle – is a vehicle size class for the smallest size of minivans (MPVs). The Mini MPV size class sits below the compact MPV size class, and the vehicles are often built on the platforms of B-segment hatchback models.

Several minivans based on B-segment platforms have been marketed as 'leisure activity vehicles' in Europe.[^cite_note-Patrascu-106] These include the [Fiat Fiorino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_Fiorino) and [Ford Transit Courier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Transit_Courier).[^cite_note-107]

## Related categories

### Leisure activity vehicle



[Matra Rancho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_Rancho), one of the first LAVs



Peugeot Rifter, a LAV based on the [Peugeot Partner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot_Partner) commercial van

A **leisure activity vehicle** (abbreviated **LAV**), also known as van-based MPV[^cite_note-108] and *ludospace* in French,[^cite_note-109] is the passenger-oriented version of small commercial vans primarily marketed in Europe.[^cite_note-Patrascu-106] One of the first LAVs was the 1977 [Matra Rancho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matra_Rancho) (among the first crossover SUVs and a precursor to the Renault Espace), with European manufacturers expanding the segment in the late 1990s, following the introduction of the [Citroën Berlingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn_Berlingo) and [Renault Kangoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Kangoo).

Leisure activity vehicles are typically derived from [supermini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermini_car) or [subcompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subcompact_car) car platforms, differing from [mini MPVs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_MPV) in body design. To maximize interior space, LAVs feature a taller roof, more upright windshield, and longer hood/bonnet with either a [liftgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatchback#Characteristics) or barn doors to access the boot. Marketed as an alternative to sedan-derived [small family cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_family_car), LAVs have seating with a lower [H-point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-point) than MPVs or minivans, offering two (or three) rows of seating.

Though sharing underpinnings with superminis, subcompacts, and mini MPVs, using an extended wheelbase can make leisure activity vehicles longer than those from which they are derived. For example, the [Fiat Doblò](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_Dobl%C3%B2) is one of the longest LAVs with a total length of 4,255 mm (167.5 in), versus the 4,050 mm (159.4 in) of the [Opel Meriva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opel_Meriva) (a mini MPV) and the 4,030 mm (158.7 in) of the [Peugeot 206](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot_206) SW (a supermini).

### Asian utility vehicle

Main article: [Basic utility vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_utility_vehicle)



[Toyota Revo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Revo), also known as the Kijang in Indonesia

An **Asian utility vehicle** (abbreviated **AUV**) originates from the Philippines to describe basic and affordable vehicles with either large seating capacity or cargo designed to be sold in developing countries. These vehicles are usually available in minivan-like wagon body style with a seating capacity of 7 to 16 passengers. They are usually based on a compact pickup truck with [body-on-frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body-on-frame) chassis and [rear-wheel drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear-wheel_drive) to maximize its load capacity and durability while maintaining low manufacturing costs. Until the 2000s, AUVs were popular in Southeast Asia, particularly in Indonesia, the Philippines, Taiwan, and some African markets.[^cite_note-110]

The first AUV is the [Toyota Tamaraw/Kijang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Kijang), introduced in the Philippines and Indonesia in 1975 as a pickup truck with an optional rear cabin.[^cite_note-111] In the 1990s, other vehicles such as the [Isuzu Panther/Hi-Lander/Crosswind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isuzu_Panther) and [Mitsubishi Freeca/Adventure/Kuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Freeca) emerged in the AUV segment.[^cite_note-112] Modern equivalent of AUV is the [Toyota Innova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Innova), an MPV that is the direct successor to the Kijang which in its first two generations were built with body-on-frame construction. The vehicle's third generation switched to [unibody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unibody) construction.[^cite_note-113]

### Three-row SUV

See also: [Sport utility vehicle](47696.md) and [Crossover (automobile)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossover_\(automobile\))

With the decline of the minivan/MPV category in many regions, such as North America and Europe, in the mid-2010s, SUVs and crossovers with three rows of seating became popular alternatives. Compared to minivans, three-row SUVs lose sliding doors and generally offer less interior space due to the higher priorities on exterior styling and higher [ground clearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_clearance).[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116]
