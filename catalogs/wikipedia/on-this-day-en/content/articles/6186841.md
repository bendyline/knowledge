The **Sri Lanka Navy** (**SLN**) ([Sinhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language): ශ්‍රී ලංකා නාවික හමුදාව, romanised: *Śrī Laṃkā nāvika hamudāva*; [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language): இலங்கை கடற்படை, romanised: *Ilaṅkai kaṭaṟpaṭai*) is the [naval arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy) of the [Sri Lanka Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Armed_Forces) and is classed as the country's most vital defence force due to its island geography. It is responsible for defending the maritime borders and interests of the Sri Lankan nation. The role of the Sri Lanka Navy is to conduct operations at sea for the defence of the nation and its interests and conduct prompt and sustainable combat operations at sea in accordance with the national policies.[^cite_note-3]

[Sri Lanka](26750.md), situated in the middle of major [sea lanes](2018420.md) passing through the [Indian Ocean](14580.md), was always a magnet for seafarers and has a long history of naval campaigns. The current Sri Lankan Navy was established on 9 December 1950 when the Navy Act was passed for the formation of the **Royal Ceylon Navy**. The roots of the modern Sri Lankan Navy date back to 1938 when the **Ceylon Naval Volunteer Force** was established, which was renamed and absorbed into the [Royal Navy](26061.md) as the **Ceylon Royal Naval Volunteer Reserve** during World War II. The current name, Sri Lanka Navy, was constituted in 1972 when Sri Lanka became a republic and with the introduction of new constitution.

It played a key role in the [Sri Lankan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_civil_war), conducting surveillance and patrol, amphibious and supply operations. During the war, the navy moved from a small force focused on coastal patrols to a large combat force concentrating on [asymmetric naval warfare](46863.md) capable of amphibious and land operations in support of [counter-insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-insurgency) operations that progressed into engagements of a new form of [littoral zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_zone) warfare. It carried out expeditionary deployments in the Indian Ocean in order to intercept rogue arm shipments on the high seas. The navy has its own elite [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces) unit, the [Special Boat Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Boat_Squadron_\(Sri_Lanka\)).

The professional head of the navy is the [Commander of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_of_the_Navy_\(Sri_Lanka\)), currently [Vice Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_admiral) Damian Fernando. The [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief_of_the_Sri_Lankan_Armed_Forces) of the [Sri Lanka Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Armed_Forces) is the [President of Sri Lanka](359933.md), who heads the [National Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Council_\(Sri_Lanka\)); the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Sri_Lanka\)) is the organisation where ship buying policies are made for the navy.[^cite_note-4] The Sri Lanka Navy has five Advanced Offshore Patrol Vessels (equivalent to a traditional [patrol frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrol_frigate)), three [Offshore Patrol Vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_patrol_vessel), two [missile boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_boat), 40 [fast attack crafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_attack_craft), more than 200 [patrol boats](536773.md), seven landing ships/craft, along with six auxiliary vessels.[^cite_note-5]

## History

### The beginning and World War II

Main article: [Ceylon Royal Naval Volunteer Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceylon_Royal_Naval_Volunteer_Reserve)



*Eritrea*, 14 September 1943 entering Port of Colombo to surrender following interception by [HMS *Overdale Wyke*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Overdale_Wyke) of the Ceylon Naval Volunteer Force.

In January 1938 the Ceylon Naval Volunteer Force (CNVF) was created with Commander W\.G. Beauchamp as [Commanding Officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commanding_officer) under ordinance No I of 1937. On 31 August 1939 at the outset of [World War II](32927.md), the CNVF was mobilised for war duties. It took part in action during the [Easter Sunday Raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Sunday_Raid).[^cite_note-6] Three years later, the CNVF was offered to, and accepted by the [Royal Navy](26061.md) (RN) as a [Volunteer Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_reserve_force), the Ceylon Royal Naval Volunteer Reserve (CRNVR). It continued under Royal Navy operational and administrative command until March 1946. With the end of the war, it reverted to Ceylon Government control, though yet CRNVR in name. In the 1939–1946 period, the CRNVR carried out several operational duties, mainly at sea. Cutting its teeth on the Port Commission tugs Samson and Goliath, it later crewed and operated trawlers and Antarctic whalers converted as [minesweepers](217118.md) and fitted out with guns, submarine detection equipment and anti-submarine weaponry. They were HMS Overdale Wyke (the first ship to be purchased by the Government of Ceylon), HMS Okapi, HMS Semla, HMS Sambhur, HMS Hoxa, HMS Balta and HM Tugs Barnet and C 405. In addition the CRNVR crewed nned several Motor Fishing Vessels (MFV), [Harbour Defence Motor Launch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour_Defence_Motor_Launch) (HDML) and miscellaneous auxiliary vessels. All were crewed exclusively by CRNVR personnel. These ships were meant to sweep and guard the approaches the harbours but were often used on extended missions outside Ceylon waters. In the course of these operations, the ships came under enemy fire, recovered essential information from [Imperial Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) aircraft that were shot down, sailed to Akyab (modern [Sittwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sittwe)) after the Burma front was opened in two FMVs for harbour duties, and were called upon to accept the surrender of the Italian [sloop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloop_of_war) *[Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea_\(colonial_ship\))* and escort her to the Colombo port with a prize crew on board.

### Royal Ceylon Navy



Naval Ensign of the Royal Ceylon Navy (1950–1972).

#### Formation

With Ceylon gaining self-rule from the British in 1948, the [Parliament of Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Ceylon) passed the *Navy Act, No. 34 of 1950* which established the Royal Ceylon Navy (RCyN) on 9 December 1950. The CRNVR served as a source of officers and sailors for the newly established RCyN as one hundred were selected and transferred to the regular naval force. Under the Navy Act, the CRNVR became the volunteer naval force on 9 January 1951 as the Royal Ceylon Volunteer Naval Force (RCVNF). The first warship of the RCyN was commissioned in 1951 as [HMCyS *Vijaya*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMCyS_Vijaya), an [Algerine-class minesweeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerine-class_minesweeper), ex-HMS *Flying Fish* along with other [patrol boats](536773.md) and tugs. It was the policy of His Majesties Government of Ceylon to build a strong navy to be the first line of defence of the [island country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_country). As such the fleet was expanded with the addition of, [HMCyS *Parakrama*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Pickle_\(J293\)), another Algerine-class minesweeper (ex-HMS *Pickle*), two Canadian-built "River" class frigates [HMCyS *Mahasena*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMCS_Orkney_\(K448\)) (ex-HMCS *Orkney*, *Violetta* and ex-Israeli ship *Mivtach*), [HMCyS *Gajabahu*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLNS_Gajabahu_\(1972\)) (ex-HMCS *Hallowell*, ex-Israeli *Misnak*) and an oceangoing tug (ex-HMS *Adept*). The RCyN took part in several joint naval exercises and a goodwill missions. Commodore [Royce de Mel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royce_de_Mel) became the first Ceylonese to head the navy as he was appointed Captain of the RCyN in 1955. In 1959, the navy took over the strategic [Royal Naval Dockyard, Trincomalee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLN_Dockyard) as the last of the British forces in Ceylon withdrew. In 1960, flexing its blue water capability a naval fleet undertook a deployment to the far east. Its return resulted in scandal as a search for contraband took place. A Commission of Inquiry into the incident resulted in the dismissal of several officers (with commissions withdrawn and others retired) and the compulsory retirement of Rear Admiral de Mel, who was thereafter implicated in an [attempt military coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Ceylonese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt) in 1962.[^cite_note-Past-7][^cite_note-8]

#### Stagnation

In the aftermath of the attempted coup, the armed forces saw major budget cuts that dramatically halted the expansion it enjoyed in the 1950s. Under [N. Q. Dias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N._Q._Dias), the [Ministry of External Affairs and Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_External_Affairs_and_Defence) changed its defence policy taking steps to prevent a further coup attempts. Joint operations among armed services were stopped, with the army to focused on internal security and the role of the navy was scaled down. As a result, several of its ships were sold off and its size reduced by the stoppage of recruitment of officers cadets and sailors for over seven years, the loss of important bases and barracks and the stoppage of training in the United Kingdom. Two batches of 300 ratings were recruited in 1966 and 1969.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-COIN_operations-10][^cite_note-Moore-11]

### Insurrection

Main article: [1971 JVP Insurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_JVP_Insurrection)

As a result, in 1971 the navy was poorly equipped and short of personnel when the [1971 JVP Insurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_JVP_Insurrection) broke out. RCyN had only one warship, HMCyS *Gajabahu* which was not put to sea as its crew were dispatched with other naval personnel for shore duty. RCyN initially mounted the defence of ports and thereafter carryout offensive counterinsurgency operations against the insurgents. During the insurrection navy suffered its first combat casualties and went on to man detention centres to rehabilitate surrendered insurgents after it was crushed in a few months. Ceylon, however, had to rely on the [Indian Navy](604522.md) to established an exclusion zone around the island.[^cite_note-COIN_operations-10][^cite_note-Moore-11]

With the JVP insurrection, the Government felt the limitations of the RCyN and the need to strengthen the navy. The [People's Republic of China](5405.md) gifted two [Type 062-class gunboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_062-class_gunboat) and these were commissioned in February 1972, while three more were ordered.[^cite_note-12]

### Navy of the Republic



Sri Lanka Navy Ensign.

In July 1972 the "Dominion of Ceylon" became the "Democratic Socialist Republic of Sri Lanka" and the Royal Ceylon Navy became the **Sri Lanka Navy**. The [ensign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensign_\(flag\)), along with the Flag Officers' flags, were redesigned. The term "Captain of the Navy", introduced in the Navy Act, was changed to "Commander of the Navy", in keeping with the terminology adopted by the other two services. Finally, "Her Majesty's Ceylon Ships" (HMCyS) became "Sri Lankan Naval Ships" (SLNS).[^cite_note-DN01-13]

SLN received three more Type 062-class gunboats in December 1972, while the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) gifted a [Shershen-class torpedo boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shershen-class_torpedo_boat) in 1975. These gunboats allowed the SLN to carry out effective coastal patrolling and several cruises to regional ports. New bases were established to counter smuggling operations in the coastal areas. Five inshore patrol crafts were ordered from Cheverton, while six coastal patrol craft were built by the Colombo Dockyards.[^cite_note-DN01-13]

### Civil war

Main article: [Sri Lankan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Civil_War)



[SLNS *Samudura*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLNS_Samudura), fast gun boats and troopship *JetLiner* in the Colombo harbour.



Sri Lanka Navy personnel on [Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Sri_Lanka\)) parade.

At the begin of the civil war in the 1980s the navy found itself poorly equipped to face the new threats the [LTTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTTE) created. It found itself engaging in anti-smuggling operations to counter LTTE gun running between India and Sri Lanka. As the larger gun boats proved ineffective against faster small boats used by the LTTE with outboard motors, the navy began deploying small boats with [waterjets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pump-jet) and inshore patrol boats armed with machine guns to police its waters. The LTTE responded with mounting machine guns and attacking the navy boats. This began an [asymmetric war](46863.md) at sea, taking place primarily in the coastal waters among small boats of the navy and the LTTE naval arm the [Sea Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Tigers). The decades long conflict saw the escalation of the size, firepower and speed of the boats with new techniques deployed by both sides. The Sea Tigers mastered the art of using sophisticated [suicide crafts](18835454.md) against naval vessels both small and large. The navy acquired in the late 1980s Israeli [Dvora-class fast patrol boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dvora-class_fast_patrol_boat) which it designated as Fast Attack Crafts (FAC). The FACs of the [Fast Attack Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Attack_Flotilla) became the workhorses of the navy's offensive and defensive operations against the Sea Tigers. In the 1990s and 2000s [Super Dvora class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Dvora_Mk_III-class_patrol_boat) boats were added and a locally built [Colombo class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_Fast_Attack_Craft) was introduced in larger numbers. These proved highly successful in limiting the LTTE's use of the seas.[^cite_note-DN01-13]

During the war the navy increased its fleet of larger vessels by introducing two locally built [Jayasagara class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayasagara_class_patrol_craft) offshore patrol vessels and purchasing several more Type 062-class gunboats. The navy lost several ships in the 1990s to Sea Tiger attacks by suicide crafts such as in the sinking of [SLNS *Sagarawardena*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLNS_Sagarawardena) and [SLNS *Ranaviru*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLNS_Ranaviru) and the use of suicide frogmen such as in the [bombing of SLNS *Sooraya* and SLNS *Ranasuru*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_SLNS_Sooraya_and_SLNS_Ranasuru). Due to the threat posed by the Sea Tigers, the navy had to undertake [convoy duty](42990.md) to escort shipping to the Jaffna peninsula to which all land routes were controlled by the LTTE. In order keep supply lines open to Jaffna, the navy employed several auxiliary ships. It also deployed auxiliary ships to support FACs.[^cite_note-DN01-13]

In the early 1980s a land combat force named Naval Patrolmen was created which at first limited itself to base defence and, as its numbers increased, took part in offensive operations against the [LTTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTTE) along with the Sri Lankan Army. An elite naval special forces unit called the [Special Boat Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Boat_Squadron_\(Sri_Lanka\)) was created in the late 1980s based on the British [Special Boat Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Boat_Service).[^cite_note-DN02-14]

In order to support ground operations of the army, landing ships and boats were acquired. In early 1990s the SLN carried out in conjunction with the army its first [amphibious operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare) code named *[Operation Sea Breeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sea_Breeze_\(Sri_Lanka\))* followed by the larger *[Operation Balavegaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Balavegaya)* a year later and on the seas it began an aggressive clamp down on LTTE actives including gunrunning. In 1992, Admiral [W.W.E. Clancy Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clancy_Fernando), the commander of the navy was assassinated by a [suicide bomb attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_bomber) by the LTTE.[^cite_note-DN01-13]

The mid-1990s saw a slow expansion of larger fleet assets with addition of newer Type 062-class gun boats and a [Haiqing class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_037_corvette#Type_037I_Haiqing-class_submarine_chaser) [submarine chaser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_chaser) being added to the fleet to intercept arms shipments destined for the Tigers within Sri Lankan territorial waters. In 2000 the Navy started a [fleet air arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Air_Arm) (FAA) by acquiring a HAL Chetak from India to expand its surveillance capability by operating from newly acquired [Offshore Patrol Vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_patrol_vessel). During the same time [conventional warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_warfare) capability was increased by the addition of [Sa'ar 4-class missile boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa'ar_4-class_missile_boat). In 2004, the navy received a [Reliance-class cutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_endurance_cutter) from the United States Excess Defense Articles (EDA) program.[^cite_note-15]

Following the resumption of hostilities between the government of Sri Lanka and the LTTE since early 2006, the navy took up an active role in limiting the LTTE's use of the seas. This resulted in several major sea battles occurring during the course of 2006, 2007 and 2009. Most significant of the events during this time was the interception and sinking of several large cargo ships that were bringing [illegal arms shipments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunrunning) to the LTTE in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) in international waters ([Sri Lanka Navy anti arms smuggling operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Navy_anti_arms_smuggling_operations)). These naval operations have proven the [blue water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-water_navy) capability of the Sri Lankan Navy.

During the war the navy, along with the army, developed its own weapons development programmes to produce and maintain weapon systems suited for indigenous requirements in collaboration with [Colombo Dockyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Dockyard) which included the [Jayasagara class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayasagara_class_patrol_craft), [Colombo class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_Fast_Attack_Craft) and the [Ranavijaya class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranavijaya-class_landing_craft); while the navy designed and developed the [Arrow class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow-class_patrol_boat).

#### Major operations

### After the war



[SLNS Gajabahu (P626)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLNS_Gajabahu_\(P626\)), [SLNS Sayurala (P623)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLNS_Sayurala_\(2016\)) and [SLNS Sindurala (P624)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLNS_Sindurala_\(P624\)) during 2022 Colombo Naval Exercise. [SLNS Samudura (P261)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLNS_Samudura_\(P261\)) also visible in the distance.

With the end of the civil war, the navy has begun reorienting itself for the future defence of the island. This has led to force redeployment, training exercises and transfer of certain duties to the newly formed [Sri Lanka Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Coast_Guard). In the post war years the navy has expanded its maritime operations to fisheries control and to counter human trafficking. Operations to counter illegal poaching by Tamil Nadu fishermen have led to allegations that personnel from the Sri Lanka Navy have attacked more than twelve fishermen, two of whom have died, in a series of disputes.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] Australia transferred two [Bay-class patrol boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay-class_patrol_boat) to the Sri Lanka Navy, following its Prime Minister's visit to the island for the [Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Heads_of_Government_Meeting) in November 2013.[^cite_note-18] The first of these vessels was delivered in April 2014.

#### Blue water navy



The U.S. 7th Fleet flagship [USS Blue Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Blue_Ridge_\(LCC_19\)) manoeuvres into formation with SLNS Sayura and SLNS Samudura.

Expanding its blue water capability the navy began commissioning larger fleet assets equivalent to Patrol frigates, which it termed as *Advanced Offshore Patrol Vessels*. In April and August 2018 two 105m long vessels of the [Saryu-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saryu-class_patrol_vessel) were commissioned. Built by [Goa Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goa_Shipyard) on order to the Sri Lanka navy, these were the largest purpose built ships for the Sri Lanka Navy.[^cite_note-ft.lk-19]

In 2017, SLNS *Sayurala* took part in Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) International Fleet Review 2017 in [Thailand](30128.md). This is the longest foreign tour (21 days) an SLN Ship undertook after the year 1965 with 127 sailors including 18 officers.[^cite_note-20] This followed in 2018 by SLNS *Sagara* which sailed to [Indonesia](14579.md) to attend the Multilateral Naval Exercise “Komodo” and “International Fleet Review” (IFR) 2018,[^cite_note-navy-21] while SLNS *Samudura* and SLNS *Suranimala* sailed to India to take part in [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_\(naval_exercise\)).[^cite_note-defence-22] The navy participated in [Exercise RIMPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_RIMPAC) for the first time in 2018, sending a contingent of marines to the international maritime exercise.[^cite_note-navy2-23]

In August 2018, the navy took over a [Hamilton-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton-class_cutter) [high endurance cutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_endurance_cutter) which was transferred to the Navy from United States under the EDA program. Commissioned in June 2019 as an Advanced Offshore Patrol Vessel, it became the largest combat vessel in the Sri Lankan navy at 3250 tonnes and second former United States cutter in its service.[^cite_note-Secretary_Class_High_Endurance_cutter_offer_to_Sri_Lanka-24]

In June 2019, the navy took over a [Type 053H2G frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_053H2G_frigate) which was transferred to the Navy from China. It will be armed with [dual Type 79 100 mm naval guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_79_100_mm_naval_gun) and two [Type 76A dual-37 mm anti-aircraft guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_76_twin_37_mm_naval_gun) to function as an Advanced Offshore Patrol Vessel.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

In August 2021, the navy dispatched its [Landing Ship, Tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_Ship,_Tank) SLNS *Shakthi* to sail to the [Port of Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Chennai) to sealift urgently needed medical grade oxygen needed for the [COVID-19](63030231.md) situation in the island.[^cite_note-27] On 26 October 2021, the navy formally took over the second Hamilton-class high endurance cutter transferred from the United States at the [USCG Station Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Guard_District_13) under the EDA program. It reached its home port in 2022, following a seven-month refit in Seattle.[^cite_note-Douglas_Munro1-28]



Sri Lanka Navy Marines new locally made Combat All-Terrain Vehicles (CATVs)

In March 2022, the Government of Sri Lanka signed several defence agreements with the Government of India which included the establishment of a [Maritime Rescue Coordinating Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Maritime_Rescue_Coordinating_Centre) on an Indian grant as well as the acquisition of [Dornier 228](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornier_228) maritime reconnaissance aircraft to the Sri Lanka Air Force to operate with naval personnel. A 4,000 ton [floating dock](213739.md) constructed by [Goa Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goa_Shipyard) was also acquired as part of a grant from India, to facilitate repair and maintenance of larger fleet units based at Trincomalee instead of having to depend dry dock facilities in Colombo.[^cite_note-29]

In January 2024, the President of Sri Lanka announced plans to deploy a navy ship to the [Red Sea](26191.md) to join the maritime coalition defending shipping against increased attacks by [Houthi](17240332.md) rebels in Yemen.[^cite_note-30] The navy has deployed one of its five [Advanced Offshore Patrol Vessels](#Ships) to the Red Sea as part of [Operation Prosperity Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Prosperity_Guardian).[^cite_note-31]

The navy responded to the distress call issued in the [Sinking of IRIS Dena](82572206.md) off the Sri Lankan southern coast in the early hours of 4 March 2026, deploying two vessels rescuing 32 survivors.[^cite_note-32]

## Training



Sri Lankan Navy divers and their US counterparts during a joint diving exercise in the [Apra Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apra_Harbor) off the coast of Guam

Main article: [Military academies in Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_academies_in_Sri_Lanka)

At the formation of the Royal Ceylon Navy in 1950, it looked to the [Royal Navy](26061.md) to its training in the formational years. Naval rating training was initiated locally with Royal Navy instructors at [Royal Naval Dockyard, Trincomalee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_Dockyard,_Trincomalee) while officer cadets and specialist training took place in the United Kingdom. Initial batches of officer cadets were sent to [Britannia Royal Naval College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britannia_Royal_Naval_College), along with specialised training at trade schools of the Royal Navy, while senior officers were sent to the [Royal Naval College, Greenwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_College,_Greenwich) and to the [Royal College of Defence Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Defence_Studies). Following the resuspension of recruitment following the suspension from 1962 to 1967, the [Naval and Maritime Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_and_Maritime_Academy) was established for basic officer training. With the rapid expansion of the Sri Lankan armed forces in the 1980s and 1990s saw the establishment of local specialist and trade schools in the navy, along with staff colleges and a defence university. At present the Director General Training directs all naval training establishments.

All pre-commissioning training for officers are carried out at the [Naval and Maritime Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_and_Maritime_Academy) (NMA) at the SLN Dockyard in [Trincomalee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trincomalee) and short/specialised officer training is conducted at the SLNS Gemunu, Welisara. This training includes theoretical aspects covered at the training institute followed by a practical exposure on board the Sri Lanka Navy fleet at sea. The Naval & Maritime Academy also has specialist schools for training areas such as [ASW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare), [diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_diving), [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine), [combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_training), [NBCD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBRN_defense) and sniper. It also conducts the Sub Lieutenant Technical Course for newly [commissioned officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioned_officer), the Junior Naval Staff Course for [staff officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staff_officer) and the Long Logistics Management Course (LLMC) for [logistics officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics_officer) of the navy. The [General Sir John Kotelawala Defence University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Sir_John_Kotelawala_Defence_University) (KDU) formed in 1981 and situated in Ratmalana, fourteen kilometres south of Colombo, is Sri Lanka's only university specialising in defence studies. Apart from postgraduate defence studies each year, approximately fifty cadets from all three services are admitted to the university (aged 18–22) to participate in a three-year programme of under graduate studies.[^cite_note-35]

[Senior officers](150237.md#Field_or_senior_officers) of the ranks of [Lieutenant Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Commander) and [Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander) follow the [Command and Staff Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staff_College) at the [Defence Services Command and Staff College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Services_Command_and_Staff_College) (DSCSC) at Batalanda, Makola which allows officers to gain a Masters Of Science (Defence Studies) degree from the KDU. Senior officers destined for [flag rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_rank) attend the prestigious [National Defence College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_College,_Sri_Lanka) (NDC) in [Colombo](56636.md) which is the highest level of training leading to a Master of Philosophy from the KDU. The navy continuous to send its senior officers for overseas training.

Basic training for new recruits (approximately six months) are conducted at Advanced Naval Training Centre, SLNS 'Nipuna'; Naval Institute of Technology, SLNS 'Thakshila', Welisara; and at Naval Recruit Training Centres at several shore establishments. This basic training will be followed by *on-the-job* training on-board fleet units and at shore establishments. Combat Training School at SLNS 'Pandukabaya' conducts combat training for [Naval Patrolmen](#Naval_Patrolmen).

Additional training is carried out in [UK](31717.md), [India](14533.md), [Pakistan](23235.md) and [Australia](4689264.md).

## Marine battalion

Main article: [Sri Lanka Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Marine_Corps)



Sri Lankan Marines in an amphibious capabilities demonstration during the Sri Lanka Marine Corps Boot Camp graduation at Sri Lankan Naval Station Barana in Mullikulum, Sri Lanka, Feb. 27, 2017.

In 2016, the Sri Lanka Navy formed its first [battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion) of [Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marines) specialising in [amphibious warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare). The unit started training under the assistance of the [11th Marine Expeditionary Unit](5852328.md) of the [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md) in November 2016 and received further training from the [Commando Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commando_Regiment_\(Sri_Lanka\)) of [Sri Lanka Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Army).[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

The first group consisting of 164 Marines, consisting of 6 officers and 158 sailors, passed out on 27 February 2017 from Naval Base SLNS Barana in Mullikulam in a ceremony attended by the President [Maithripala Sirisena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maithripala_Sirisena), and the Commander of the Navy Vice Admiral [Ravindra Wijegunaratne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravindra_Wijegunaratne) alongside the tri-force Commanders and other senior officers. On July 29, 2017, Vice Admiral Wijegunarathna opened the new Marine Headquarters, SLNS Vidura in [Sampoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampoor), [Trincomalee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trincomalee_District).[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

## Women in the Sri Lanka Navy

Women in the Sri Lanka Navy were taken for the first time in 1985;[^cite_note-64] For women in the Sri Lanka Navy, there is no separate branch or department. Women can join both as officers and sailors.

Today women are recruited to both the regular and [volunteer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Volunteer_Naval_Force) forces. At first limited to the medical branch, currently females are able to join all branches of the navy. In 2007, the navy appointed its first-ever female [Commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(rank\)), Surgeon Commodore Indranee Y. Amarasinghe.[^cite_note-fe-como-65]

## Future of Sri Lanka Navy

The Sri Lanka Navy set a medium-term fleet expansion goal targeting ten new vessels in its 'Sri Lanka Navy 2025' plan as part of its expansion of [blue water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_water) operations.[^cite_note-67]

According to the Maritime Doctrine of Sri Lanka (MDSL) published in 2020, the establishment of [Naval Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_aviation) consisting of helicopters and drones have been proposed and initial steps have been taken.[^cite_note-68]

Naval variant of a locally developed [guided missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_missile) and 40-barrel and 20-barrel [MRLS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_Rocket_Launcher) to be soon installed on naval vessels.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

The Navy planned to purchase a locally constructed 110m OPV and 10 45m Fast Patrol Boats from the Colombo Dockyard PLC, alongside a 95.6m corvette from China. However, the plans were suspended in 2022 due to the lack of funds and instead the navy decided to obtain similar vessels for free from friendly countries.[^cite_note-71] In January 2024 the United States deputy secretary of state for management and resources [Richard R. Verma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_R._Verma) stated that the US will provide another Offshore Patrol Vessel to the Sri Lanka Navy.[^cite_note-72]
