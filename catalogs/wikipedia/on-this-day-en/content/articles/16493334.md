**Sarah Fredrika Sjöström** (Swedish: [\[ˈsɑ̂ːra ˈɧø̂ːstrœm\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Swedish); born 17 August 1993) is a Swedish competitive [swimmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_\(sport\)) specialising in the sprint [freestyle](23272431.md) and [butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butterfly_stroke) events. She is one of the most decorated swimmers of all time.

As of 2024, Sjöström has won a total of [23 individual medals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_World_Aquatics_Championships#Individual_events) at long course World Championships, more than any other swimmer in history. She was the first female swimmer to win five individual medals at a single [FINA World Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FINA_World_Aquatics_Championships), a feat she accomplished in 2019. In 2021, Sjöström achieved a career total of over 1000 [most valuable player](235000.md) points in the International Swimming League, becoming the first swimmer in history to do so. In 2022, she became the first swimmer from any country to have won a total of 28 medals at [LEN European Aquatics Championships](657021.md). She has won a total of 112 medals at [Swimming World Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FINA_Swimming_World_Cup).

Sjöström is the current [world record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_world_records_in_swimming) holder in the 50 metre butterfly ([long course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic-size_swimming_pool)). She is a former world record holder in the 50 metre freestyle (long course and [short course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_course_\(swimming\))), 100 metre butterfly (long course and short course), 100 metre freestyle (long course and short course), 200 metre freestyle (short course), and the 4×50 metre medley relay (short course). She is the first Swedish woman to win an [Olympic gold medal in swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] She won the [Overall Swimming World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FINA_Swimming_World_Cup) in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FINA_Swimming_World_Cup) and [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FINA_Swimming_World_Cup). In 2022, she became the first swimmer representing a country in Europe to win 10 individual World Championships gold medals.[^cite_note-CIIC26Jun2022-4]

Sjöström participated in five consecutive [Olympic Games](22576.md), from [Beijing 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics) through [Paris 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics). She has won six Olympic medals: three gold (100 metre butterfly, 100 metre freestyle and 50 metre freestyle), two silver (200 metre freestyle and 50 metre freestyle), and one bronze (100 metre freestyle).

## Career

### 2008–2009

#### 2008 European Championships

On 22 March 2008, at the age of 14, Sjöström won the gold medal in the 100 metre [butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butterfly_stroke) at the [2008 European Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_European_Aquatics_Championships) in [Eindhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven), [Netherlands](21148.md), with a time of 58.44 seconds.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] In the semi-finals the day before, she set a new [Swedish record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Swedish_records_in_swimming) of 58.38 seconds, breaking the previous record of 58.71 seconds by [Anna-Karin Kammerling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna-Karin_Kammerling).[^cite_note-LEN21Mar2008-7] Three days earlier, she won her first medal of the Championships in the 4×100 metre freestyle relay, contributing a split time of 56.17 for the third leg of the relay in the preliminary heats before being substituted out for the finals relay and winning a bronze medal for her efforts when the finals relay placed third in 3\:41.28.[^cite_note-LEN18Mar2008-8][^cite_note-TNYT18Mar2008-9] At the Swedish championships in 2008 in the same event, she finished in a time of 58.55 seconds.

#### 2008 Summer Olympics

See also: [Swimming at the 2008 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics)

Sjöström earned a spot on the [Swedish Swim Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics) for the [2008 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics) in [Beijing](18603746.md), [China](5405.md), where she finished 27th in the preliminary heats of the [100 metre butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_100_metre_butterfly) with a time of 59.08 and 29th in the preliminary heats of the [100 metre backstroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_100_metre_backstroke) with a time of 1\:02.38, failing to advance to the semi-finals in both races. She also swam the backstroke leg of the [4×100 metre medley relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_4_%C3%97_100_metre_medley_relay) in the preliminaries, in which the Swedish team qualified in seventh place for the finals, though the team went on to be disqualified in the final for a false start. The day of the 4×100 metre medley relay final coincided with Sjöström's 15th birthday.

#### 2009 World Championships

### 2010–2012

#### 2010 European Championships

At the [2010 European Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_European_Aquatics_Championships) in [Budapest](36787.md), [Hungary](13275.md), Sjöström defended her European title in the 100 metre butterfly by winning the event.[^cite_note-16] With a split time of 53.77 seconds for the third leg of the 4×100 metre freestyle relay in the final, she helped Sweden win the bronze medal in a time of 3\:38.81.[^cite_note-LEN9Aug2010-17] For the 4×100 metre medley relay, she anchored the relay in the final with a time of 53.73 seconds to help her country finish in 4\:01.18 and win the silver medal.[^cite_note-LEN15Aug2010-18] In December 2011, she broke the Swedish record when she won the 100 metre freestyle at the Open Dutch Championships in Eindhoven. However, she failed to win a medal at the [2011 World Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_World_Aquatics_Championships) in [Shanghai](27643.md).[^cite_note-19]

At the [2011 World Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_World_Aquatics_Championships) in [Shanghai](27643.md), China, Sjöström competed in [five events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2011_World_Aquatics_Championships), first placing fourth in the [100 metre medalist [Lu Ying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu_Ying) of China.[^cite_note-FINA25Jul2011f100fl-20] In her next event, the [200 metre freestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2011_World_Aquatics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_200_metre_freestyle), she placed fourth, finishing less than nine-tenths of a second behind world record holder in the event and gold medalist [Federica Pellegrini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federica_Pellegrini) of [Italy](14532.md) with a time of 1\:56.41.[^cite_note-FINA27Jul2011f200fr-21] The following day, she helped achieve a time of 8\:02.30 and overall placing of twelfth in the [4×200 metre freestyle relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2011_World_Aquatics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_4_%C3%97_200_metre_freestyle_relay), splitting a 1\:57.84 for the third leg of the relay in the #### 2012 European Championships

For Sjöström, 2012 was a year of both successes and setbacks. At the [2012 European Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_European_Aquatics_Championships), held in early summer in [Debrecen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debrecen), Hungary, she achieved double gold medals in her individual events, with one in the 50 metre butterfly and one in the 100 metre freestyle.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] In her relay event, the 4×100 metre freestyle relay, she helped her team win the silver medal in a final time of 3\:38.40, swimming the third leg of the relay in 54.04 seconds.[^cite_note-LEN21May2012-27]

#### 2012 Summer Olympics

See also: [Swimming at the 2012 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics)

At the [2012 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics) in [London](17867.md), Sjöström competed in four individual events,[^cite_note-28] reaching the semi-finals of the [50 metre freestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_50_metre_freestyle), [100 metre freestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_100_metre_freestyle) and [200 metre freestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_200_metre_freestyle), and placing fourth in the final of the [100 metre butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_100_metre_butterfly) with a time of 57.17 seconds. Sjöström's world record was broken in this race by [Dana Vollmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_Vollmer) of the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-29] In 2015, Sjöström regained the world record by improving it twice at the [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_World_Aquatics_Championships), held in [Kazan](202295.md), [Russia](25391.md).[^cite_note-30]

In 2015, and leading up to the World Championships, Sjöström received the Jerring Prize and a [Victoria Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Scholarship).[^cite_note-sarah-sjostrom-far-victoriastipendiet-2015-46] Approximately two months earlier, at the 2015 Eindhoven Swim Cup in April, she became the first female Swedish swimmer to achieve a time faster than 1\:00.00 in the 100 metre backstroke, setting a new Swedish record with a personal best time of 59.98 seconds.[^cite_note-Keith5Apr2015-47] At the [2015 World Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_World_Aquatics_Championships), held in July and August, she won a total of five medals, including gold medals in the [50 metre butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2015_World_Aquatics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_50_metre_butterfly) and [100 metre butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2015_World_Aquatics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_100_metre_butterfly) events, during the latter she broke the world record twice, first with a 55.74 in the semifinals then again with a 55.64 in the final.[^cite_note-BBCS2Aug2015-48][^cite_note-yahoo_145243484-49] Her gold medal in the 50 metre butterfly was the first gold medal she won at a [FINA World Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FINA_World_Aquatics_Championships). With both of the gold medals, she also became the first Swedish swimmer to win two World Championships gold medals during a single World Aquatics Championships. In the [100 metre freestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2015_World_Aquatics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_100_metre_freestyle), she won the silver medal with a time of 52.70 seconds, sharing the podium with Australian sisters [Bronte Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronte_Campbell), gold medalist, and Cate Campbell, bronze medalist.[^cite_note-FINA7Aug2015f100fr-50]

Thus she won medals in all four individual events, and in the three relay events there was a silver medal and two fourth-place finishes. In all, she won two gold medals, two silver medals and a bronze medal during world championship week; more medals than any Swede had previously won at the same world championships.[^cite_note-yahoo_145243484-49]

Sjöström at the 2015 European Short Pool Championships

At the [2015 European Short Course Swimming Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_European_Short_Course_Swimming_Championships), held in December in [Netanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netanya), [Israel](9282173.md), Sjöström won the gold medal in the 50 metre butterfly with a Championships record time of 24.58 seconds on day two.[^cite_note-SR3Dec2015-51] The following day, she placed first in the final of the 100 metre freestyle with a time of 51.37 seconds to win the gold medal.[^cite_note-Lepesant4Dec2015-52] In her first race of the fifth and final evening, the semifinals of the 50 metre freestyle, she finished in a time of 23.75 seconds to qualify for the final ranking second overall.[^cite_note-LEN6Dec2015sf50fr-53] Approximately, 28 minutes later, she won the gold medal in the 100 metre butterfly, achieving a Championships record time of 55.03 seconds in the final to finish 0.63 seconds ahead of silver medalist [Jeanette Ottesen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeanette_Ottesen) of Denmark.[^cite_note-Schultz6Dec2015f100fl-54] She won her second medal of the evening approximately 44 minutes later, swimming to a silver medal-finish in the final of the 50 metre freestyle with a time of 23.63 seconds, which was 0.07 seconds behind the first-place finisher and 0.41 seconds ahead of the third-place finisher.[^cite_note-Schultz6Dec2015f50fr-55] Concluding with the final of the 4×50 metre medley relay approximately 26 minutes later, she split a 24.33 for the butterfly leg of the relay to help finish in a time of 1\:45.34 and win the silver medal.[^cite_note-LEN6Dec2015f450mr-56]

### 2017–2019

In January 2017, Sjöström competed at the 19th Luxembourg Euro Meet and won the 50 metre freestyle with a time of 24.01 seconds, the 100 metre freestyle with a 53.21, the 50 metre butterfly with a time of 25.02 seconds, and the 100 metre butterfly with a 57.12.[^cite_note-69] Three months later, she competed in the Stockholm Swim Open and won the 50 metre butterfly with a time of 25.63 seconds.[^cite_note-70] She also won the 100 metre butterfly with a time of 56.26 seconds.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] She also won the 50 metre freestyle, setting a time of 23.83 seconds, the second fastest time ever.[^cite_note-73] In the 100 metre freestyle she took first-place with the time of 52.54 seconds, a new personal best and [Swedish record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Swedish_records_in_swimming).[^cite_note-74]

#### 2017 Swimming World Cup

For the whole summer of 2017, Sjöström broke six world records in less than one month (the two at the aforementioned World Championships and later at [2017 Swimming World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FINA_Swimming_World_Cup) [short course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_course_\(swimming\)) competitions in Moscow, 50 metre freestyle at 23.10 seconds and 100 metre freestyle at 50.77 seconds, and Eindhoven, 100 metre freestyle at 50.58 seconds and 200 metre freestyle at 1\:50.43).[^cite_note-Rieder2Aug2017-88][^cite_note-FINA3Aug2017-89][^cite_note-WR100fr11Aug2017-90][^cite_note-WR200fr12Aug2017-91] As a simultaneous holder of eight world records on the short and long course, she became the swimmer to hold the most current world records in international swimming. In early August, Sjöström improved her time in the short course 50 metre freestyle, at races in Berlin, from 23.10 to 23.00.[^cite_note-OT7Aug2017-92] In the race, however, she was second behind Ranomi Kromowidjojo, who with the time 22.93 took from Sjöström one of her eight records.

#### 2019 International Swimming League

In the fall of 2019, she was a member of the [inaugural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_International_Swimming_League) [International Swimming League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Swimming_League) competing for the [Energy Standard Swim Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Standard_Swim_Club) of which she was co-captain with [Chad le Clos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_le_Clos). The team won the overall title in the season finale in [Las Vegas](47737.md), United States, in December.[^cite_note-113] At the first stop of the regular season, in [Indianapolis](57707.md), United States, she won the Most Valuable Player title, amassing 55.5 points over the competition, and after the season finale in Las Vegas, she was named the overall Most Valuable Player for the entire season with 243.5 points. During the season, she won the 50 metre freestyle in three matches, the 100 metre freestyle in two matches, the 50 metre butterfly in three matches, and the 100 metre butterfly in one match. ### 2020–2021

#### 2020 International Swimming League

In the [2020 International Swimming League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_International_Swimming_League) season Sjöström placed 5th overall in points in 2020, due in part to a back injury that made her sit out of a full match.[^cite_note-114] She ranked second to [Caeleb Dressel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caeleb_Dressel) in points-per-match with 59.4, noted 16 individual wins, including three in the skins.[^cite_note-\:0-115] And while neither were official world records, Sjöström also featured on Energy Standard's women's and mixed 4×100 freestyle relays that were both the fastest of all time.[^cite_note-\:0-115]

#### 2020 Summer Olympics

See also: [Swimming at the 2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics)

Due to a broken elbow Sjöström accidentally obtained by falling on ice in February 2020, she did not compete at the [2020 European Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_European_Aquatics_Championships).[^cite_note-Dampf28Jun2021-116][^cite_note-Baldwin1Aug2021-117] She returned to training in the pool in March and to competitive swimming in early June ahead of the [2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-Baldwin1Aug2021-117][^cite_note-118] In the [50 metre freestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_50_metre_freestyle) at the 2020 Olympic Games in [Tokyo](30057.md), Japan, and held in 2021 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), Sjöström won the silver medal.[^cite_note-Baldwin1Aug2021-117] The persistence Sjöström demonstrated in working through her elbow injury and winning a medal at the Olympic Games earned her the "Olympic Resilience Award" for the 2020 Summer Olympics from *[Swimming World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_World)*.[^cite_note-Rieder14Sep2021-119] In her other events, Sjöström placed fifth in the [100 metre freestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_100_metre_freestyle), fifth in the [4×100 metre medley relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_4_%C3%97_100_metre_medley_relay), sixth in the [4×100 metre freestyle relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_4_%C3%97_100_metre_freestyle_relay), and seventh in the [100 metre butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_100_metre_butterfly).[^cite_note-IOC1Aug2021-120]

On the sixth and final day, Sjöström led-off the [4×50 metre freestyle relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FINA_World_Swimming_Championships_\(25_m\)_%E2%80%93_Women's_4_%C3%97_50_metre_freestyle_relay) with a 23.50 in the prelims, helping qualify the relay to the final ranking third.[^cite_note-FINA21Dec2021h450fr-139] In her first final of the evening, Sjöström helped set a new [Swedish record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Swedish_records_in_swimming) of 1\:34.54 and win the silver medal in the 4×50 metre freestyle relay with a lead-off split of 23.33 seconds.[^cite_note-FINA21Dec2021f450fr-140] For her next race, the 50 metre freestyle final, Sjöström won the gold medal and set a new [Championships record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Championships_records_in_swimming) of 23.08 seconds, breaking the former record of 23.19 set by [Ranomi Kromowidjojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranomi_Kromowidjojo) of the Netherlands in 2018 by over one-tenth of a second.[^cite_note-FINA21Dec2021f50fr-141][^cite_note-Sutherland21Dec2021-142] In her final event of the championships, Sjöström split a 54.65 for the butterfly leg of the [4×100 metre medley relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FINA_World_Swimming_Championships_\(25_m\)_%E2%80%93_Women's_4_%C3%97_100_metre_medley_relay), helping win the gold medal in a new [European record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_records_in_swimming) time of 3\:46.20.[^cite_note-FINA21Dec2021f4100mr-143]

At the end of 2021, Sjöström was the only swimmer to rank in the top ten internationally in two or more individual long course metre events for 11 or more consecutive years, 2021 inclusive, ranking in the top ten in the 100 metre freestyle for 12 years and the 50 metre butterfly and 100 metre butterfly for 13 years.[^cite_note-Takata3Jan2022-144] Additionally, Sjöström was tied with Katie Ledecky for number two in terms of total number of individual world records set in the 21st century, each having achieved 14 individual world records, and was six world records behind number one ranked [Katinka Hosszú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katinka_Hossz%C3%BA).[^cite_note-Takata14Jan2022-145]

#### 2022 European Championships

Sjöström was one of twelve Swedish female swimmers named to the official roster for the [2022 European Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_European_Aquatics_Championships), held in August in [Rome](25458.md), Italy.[^cite_note-R2022n2Aug2022-154] On the second day of competition, she ranked first in the prelims heats of the [50 metre butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2022_European_Aquatics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_50_metre_butterfly) with a time of 25.30 seconds, qualifying for the semifinals later in the day.[^cite_note-LEN12Aug2022p50fl-155] She lowered her time to a 25.10 in the semifinals and qualified for the final ranking first by over half a second.[^cite_note-LEN12Aug2022sf50fl-156] The following day, she won the gold medal with a time of 24.96 seconds.[^cite_note-LEN13Aug2022f50fl-157][^cite_note-Pérez13Aug2022SS-158] Her swim marked the fourteenth time she had swum the race faster than 25.00 seconds.[^cite_note-ST13Aug2022-159] Later in the session, she won a silver medal in the [4×100 metre freestyle relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2022_European_Aquatics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_4_%C3%97_100_metre_freestyle_relay), swimming a 53.12 for the lead-off leg of the relay in the final to help finish in 3\:37.29.[^cite_note-LEN13Aug2022f4100frr-160]

In the morning session on day five, Sjöström started off ranking first in the prelims heats of the [50 metre freestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2022_European_Aquatics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_50_metre_freestyle) with a 24.50, qualifying for the semifinals.[^cite_note-LEN15Aug2022p50fr-161][^cite_note-ST15Aug2022p-162] For her second event of the session, she helped qualify the [4×100 metre mixed freestyle relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2022_European_Aquatics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Mixed_4_%C3%97_100_metre_freestyle_relay) to the final ranking sixth, splitting a 54.28 for the fourth leg of the relay.[^cite_note-LEN15Aug2022p4100mfrr-163] With a 24.27 in the semifinals of the 50 metre freestyle, she qualified for the final ranking first, 0.09 seconds ahead of [Katarzyna Wasick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katarzyna_Wasick) of [Poland](22936.md).[^cite_note-LEN15Aug2022sf50fr-164][^cite_note-ST15Aug2022sf-165] Finishing off the day, she won bronze medal in the 4×100 metre mixed freestyle relay, splitting a 52.68 for the third leg of the relay to contribute to the final time, and Swedish record, of 3\:23.40.[^cite_note-LEN15Aug2022f4100mfrr-166] The bronze medal marked her 26th medal at [LEN European Aquatics Championships](657021.md) and set a new record for the most number of medals won at the Championships by a female swimmer from any country.[^cite_note-Sydsvenskan15Aug2022-167] She further increased the record to a total of 27 medals the following day when she won the gold medal in the 50 metre freestyle with a time of 23.91 seconds, this also marked a new overall record by a swimmer from any country, male or female.[^cite_note-LEN16Aug2022f50fr-168][^cite_note-ST16Aug2022-169] The previous record was set by male swimmer [Alexander Popov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Popov_\(swimmer\)) of Russia at 26 medals.[^cite_note-F24w16Aug2022-170]

The seventh and final morning, Sjöström split a 54.01 for the freestyle leg of the [4×100 metre medley relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2022_European_Aquatics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_4_%C3%97_100_metre_medley_relay), helping qualify for the final ranking first in 3\:59.19.[^cite_note-LEN17Aug2022p4100mrw-171] In the final, she lowered her split time to a 52.04, helping win the gold medal in a time of 3\:55.25, which marked her 17th gold medal at LEN Aquatics Championships and extended the all-time total medal record to 28 medals.[^cite_note-LEN17Aug2022f4100mrw-172][^cite_note-Martínez17Aug2022-173]

### 2023

At the 2023 Stockholm Swim Open in April, Sjöström won the long course 50 metre freestyle with the fastest time in the world for the 2023 year by a female at that point in the time, a [2023 World Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Aquatics_Championships) qualifying time of 23.92 seconds.[^cite_note-LT15Apr2023-174][^cite_note-Nuoto15Apr2023-175]

At the [2023 World Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Aquatics_Championships), Sjöström won the long course [50 metre freestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2023_World_Aquatics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_50_metre_freestyle), breaking her own [world record](8480088.md) in the semifinal. In doing so, she set the record for the longest period of time in between world-record setting performances by an individual swimmer, 14 years and 3 days after first breaking the [100 metre butterfly world record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_record_progression_100_metres_butterfly) in 2009.[^cite_note-176] She also won the [50 metre butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2023_World_Aquatics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_100_metre_butterfly) for the fifth time in a row at the World Aquatic Championships. These two medals led her to tie and then overtake [Michael Phelps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Phelps) as the swimmer with the most individual World Aquatic Championship medals at 21 in her career.[^cite_note-177] She additionally performed on Sweden's [400 metre freestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2023_World_Aquatics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_4_%C3%97_100_metre_freestyle_relay) and [400 metre medley relays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2023_World_Aquatics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_4_%C3%97_100_metre_medley_relay); notably, her lead-off split of 52.24 in finals for the freestyle relay would have earned her a silver medal in the 100 metre freestyle.[^cite_note-178]

The latter for a sixth time in a row at the World Aquatic Championships.[^cite_note-179]

#### 2024 Olympic Games

Sjöström, now focusing on the shortest distances, had only planned to swim [50 metre freestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_50_metre_freestyle) at the [Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics). However, she decided to join [100 m freestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_100_metre_freestyle) as it suited her schedule between the [4×100m freestyle relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_4_%C3%97_100_metre_freestyle_relay) and the 50 metre freestyle.[^cite_note-180] She first raced in the heats and the final of the [4x100m freestyle relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_4_%C3%97_100_metre_freestyle_relay), where the Swedish team finished 5th in the final setting a new national record.[^cite_note-181] She then went on to win the gold in 100 metre freestyle on 31 July with a time of 52.16 seconds. Despite being the World Record holder, Sjöström had never won a gold medal in this distance at the global level.[^cite_note-182] On the last day of competition she won another gold medal in the [50 metre freestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_50_metre_freestyle), with a time of 23.71 seconds.[^cite_note-183] She closed her Olympic Games with a 7th place finish in the [4x100m medley relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_4_%C3%97_100_metre_medley_relay) alongside her Swedish teammates.

## Accomplishments and awards

Sjöström won the gold in the 100 m freestyle at the 2024 Paris Olympics. She won the gold medal in the 100 m butterfly at the 2016 Rio Summer Olympics on 7 August 2016, setting an Olympic and World record with a time of 55.48. She also won a gold medal in 100 m butterfly in the World Championships in Rome 2009 and set the world record.
