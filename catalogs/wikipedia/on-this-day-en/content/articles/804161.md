**TotalEnergies SE** is a French [multinational](214491.md) integrated energy and [petroleum company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oil_exploration_and_production_companies) founded in 1924 and is one of the seven [supermajor oil companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Oil). Its businesses cover the entire oil and gas chain, from [crude oil](23195.md) and [natural gas](22131.md) exploration and production to [power generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_generation), transportation, [refining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refining), petroleum product marketing, and international crude oil and product trading. TotalEnergies is also a large-scale [chemicals](58721.md) manufacturer.

TotalEnergies has its head office in the [Tour Total](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_Total) in [La Défense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_D%C3%A9fense) district in [Courbevoie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courbevoie), west of [Paris](22989.md). The company is a component of the [Euro Stoxx 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_Stoxx_50) [stock market index](23408957.md).[^cite_note-2] In the 2023 [*Forbes* Global 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_Global_2000), TotalEnergies was ranked as the 21st largest company in the world.[^cite_note-3]

## History

### 1924–1985: Compagnie Française des Pétroles

The company was founded after World War I, when petrol was seen as vital in case of a new war with [Germany](11867.md). The then–French President [Raymond Poincaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Poincar%C3%A9) rejected the idea of forming a partnership with [Royal Dutch Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Shell) in favour of creating an entirely French oil company. At Poincaré's behest in 1924 Col. [Ernest Mercier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Mercier), with the support of 90 banks and companies, founded the Compagnie Française des Pétroles (CFP) (in English, the French Petroleum Company).

As per the agreement reached during the [San Remo conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Remo_conference) of 1920, the French state received the 25% share held by [Deutsche Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bank) in the [Turkish Petroleum Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Petroleum_Company) (TPC) as part of the compensation for war damages caused by Germany during [World War I](4764461.md). The French government's stake in TPC was transferred to CFP,[^cite_note-GT-DEX-1920-78-7] and the [Red Line agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Line_Agreement) in 1928 rearranged the shareholding of CFP in TPC (later renamed the [Iraq Petroleum Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_Petroleum_Company) in 1929) to 23.75%.[^cite_note-GT-DEX-1928-63-8] The company from the start was regarded as a [private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_sector) company in view of its listing on the [Paris Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Stock_Exchange) in 1929.

During the 1930s, the company was engaged in exploration and production, primarily from the Middle East. Its first refinery began operating in [Normandy](21724.md) in 1933. After World War II, CFP engaged in oil exploration in Venezuela, Canada, and Africa while pursuing energy sources within France. Exploration in [Algeria](358.md), then a French colony, began in 1946, with Algeria becoming a leading source of oil in the 1950s.[^cite_note-TOT-H-02-9]

In 1954 CFP branded its [downstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downstream_\(petroleum_industry\)) products as Total in [Africa](5334607.md) and [Europe](9239.md).[^cite_note-TOT-H-02-9][^cite_note-TOT-H-01-10]

Total entered the [United States](3434750.md) in 1971 by acquiring Leonard Petroleum of [Alma, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma,_Michigan) and several [Standard Oil of Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amoco) stations in [Metro Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Detroit).[^cite_note-11]

In 1980, Total Petroleum (North America) Ltd., a company controlled 50% by CFP, bought the American refining and marketing assets of [Vickers Petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickers_Petroleum) as part of a sell-off by [Esmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JBS_USA) of its energy holdings. This purchase gave Total refining capacity, transportation, and a network of 350 [service stations](61260.md) in 20 states.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-cobizhall-14]

### 1985–2003: Total CFP and rebranding to Total



[Total Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Plaza), the headquarters of the subsidiary [Total Petrochemicals USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Petrochemicals_USA), in [Downtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Houston) [Houston](13774.md)

The company renamed itself Total CFP in 1985, to build on the popularity of its gasoline brand.[^cite_note-TOT-H-01-10] Later in 1991, the name was changed to Total, when it became a public company listed on the [New York Stock Exchange](21560.md). In 1991, the French government held more than 30 percent of the company's stock but by 1996 had reduced its stake to less than 1 percent.[^cite_note-TOT-H-01-10][^cite_note-15] In the period between 1990 and 1994, foreign ownership of the firm increased from 23 percent to 44 percent.

Total continued to expand its retail presence in [North America](21139.md) under several brand names. In 1989, [Denver, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver,_Colorado)–based Total Petroleum, Total CFP's North American unit, purchased 125 Road Runner retail locations from [Texarkana, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texarkana,_Texas)–based Truman Arnold Companies.[^cite_note-rrstores-16] By 1993, Total Petroleum was operating 2,600 retail stores under the Vickers, [Apco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APCO_Oil_Corporation), Road Runner, and Total brands. That year, the company began remodeling and rebranding all of its North American gasoline and [convenience stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convenience_store) to use the Total name.[^cite_note-prnews-17] Four years later, Total sold its North American refining and retail operations to [Ultramar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultramar) [Diamond Shamrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_Shamrock) for \$400 million in stock and \$414 million in assumed debt.[^cite_note-18]

In 1996, the [Girassol oil field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girassol_oil_field) was discovered and operated by TotalEnergies SE.[^cite_note-19] After Total's [takeover](56119.md) of [Petrofina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrofina) of Belgium in 1999, it became known as Total Fina. Afterwards, it also acquired [Elf Aquitaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elf_Aquitaine). First named TotalFinaElf after the merger in 2000, its name reverted to Total in 2003. During that rebranding, the globe logo was unveiled.[^cite_note-20]

Total's leadership had been aware of the deleterious effects of [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) since at least 1971; The company nevertheless openly denied the findings of climate science until the 1990s; Total also pursued a number of strategies to cover up the threat and contribution to [climate change](5042951.md).[^cite_note-21]

### 2003–2021

In 2003, Total signed for a 30% stake in the gas exploration venture in the [Kingdom of Saudi Arabia](349303.md) (KSA) – South Rub' al-Khali joint venture along with [Royal Dutch Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Shell) and [Saudi Aramco](290527.md).[^cite_note-22] The stake was later bought out by its partners.

In 2006, Saudi Aramco and Total signed a [memorandum of understanding](1018279.md) to develop the Jubail Refinery and Petrochemical project in Saudi Arabia which targeted 400,000 barrels per day (bpd). Two years later, the two companies officially established a joint venture called Saudi Aramco Total Refining and Petrochemical Company (SATORP) in which a 62.5% stake was held by Saudi Aramco and the balance 37.5% held by Total.[^cite_note-GT-DEX-2008-09-23][^cite_note-GT-DEX-2008-08-24]

Total withdrew in 2006 from all Iranian development work because of [United Nations](31769.md) concerns that resulted in sanctions over possible weaponization of the [nuclear program of Iran](721807.md).[^cite_note-apgm-25]

During the [2009–2010 Iraqi oil services contracts tender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Iraq#2009_Oil_services_contracts), a consortium led by [CNPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNPC) (37.5%), which also included TOTAL (18.75%) and [Petronas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petronas) (18.75%) was awarded a production contract for the "[Halfaya field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halfaya_Field)" in the south of Iraq, which contains an estimated 4.1 billion barrels (650,000,000 m<sup>3</sup>) of oil.[^cite_note-GT-DEX-2010-62-26][^cite_note-GT-DEX-2010-63-27]

In 2010 Total and [Erg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erg_\(company\)) merged their respective subsidiaries [Total Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Italia) and Erg Petroli, forming [TotalErg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TotalErg), 49% controlled by the French group and 51% by the Italian one.[^cite_note-\:02-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] As of 2010, Total had over 96,000 employees and operated in more than 130 countries.[^cite_note-33] In 2010, Total announced plans to pull out of the forecourt market in the [United Kingdom](31717.md).[^cite_note-34]

In 2012, Total announced it was selling its 20% stake and operating mandate in its Nigerian offshore project to a unit of [China Petrochemical Corp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Petrochemical_Corporation) for \$2.5 billion.[^cite_note-35]

In 2013, Total started the operation at Kashagan with [North Caspian Operating Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caspian_Operating_Company).[^cite_note-36] It is the biggest discovery of oil reserves since 1968. In 2013, Total increased its stake in [Novatek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novatek) to 16.96%.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] In 2013, Total and its joint venture partner agreed to buy [Chevron Corporation](284749.md)'s retail distribution business in [Pakistan](23235.md) for an undisclosed amount.[^cite_note-39]

In January 2014, Total became the first major oil and gas firm to acquire exploration rights for [shale gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale_gas) in the UK after it bought a 40 percent interest in two licences in the [Gainsborough Trough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Midlands_Oil_Province#Gainsborough_based_fields) area of northern England for \$48 million.[^cite_note-40] In July 2014, the company disclosed it was in talks to sell its LPG distribution business in France to Pennsylvania-based [UGI Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UGI_Corporation) for €450 million (\$615 million).[^cite_note-UGItotalSA-41]

On 20 October 2014, at 23\:57 [MST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Standard_Time), a [Dassault Falcon 50](3098320.md) business jet heading to Paris caught fire and exploded during takeoff after colliding with a [snow removal vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_removal_vehicle) in [Vnukovo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vnukovo_International_Airport), killing four, including three crew members and CEO of Total S.A. [Christophe de Margerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christophe_de_Margerie) on board. Alcohol presence was confirmed in the blood of the driver of the vehicle on the ground. [Patrick Pouyanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Pouyanne), who was Total's Refining Chief at that time, was appointed as CEO,[^cite_note-42] and also as chairman of Total in 2015.

In 2015, Total unveiled plans to cut 180 jobs in the United Kingdom, reduce refinery capacity and slow spending on North Sea fields after it fell to a \$5.7bn final-quarter loss. The company said it would also sell off \$5bn worth of assets worldwide and cut exploration costs by 30%.[^cite_note-43] In 2016, Total signed a \$224M deal to buy Lampiris, the third-largest Belgian supplier of gas and renewable energy to expand its gas and power distribution activities.[^cite_note-44] In 2016, Total bought French battery maker [Saft Groupe S.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saft_Groupe_S.A.) in a \$1.1bn deal, to boost its development in renewable energy and electricity businesses.[^cite_note-45] In 2016, Total agreed to acquire \$2.2-billion in upstream and downstream assets from [Petrobras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrobras) as part of the firms' strategic alliance announced earlier that year.[^cite_note-46] For Total, these new partnerships with Petrobras reinforce Total's position in [Brazil](3383.md) through access to new fields in the [Santos Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_Basin) while entering the gas value chain. Between 2013 and 2017, Total organized the [ARGOS Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARGOS_Challenge), a robotic competition with the aim to develop robots for its oil and gas production sites.[^cite_note-47] It was won by an Austrian-German team using a variant of the [taurob tracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurob_tracker) robot.[^cite_note-48]

In 2017, Total signed a deal for a total amount of \$4.8b with Iran for the development and production of [South Pars](9037100.md), the world's largest gas field.[^cite_note-\:0-49][^cite_note-tair-50] The deal was the first foreign investment in Iran since in the 2015 sanctions over Iran's nuclear weaponisation were lifted by the [JCPOA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JCPOA).[^cite_note-tair-50] In 2017, Total announced the acquisition of [Maersk Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maersk_Oil) for \$7.45 billion in a share and debt transaction.[^cite_note-51] This deal positioned Total as the second operator in the North Sea.[^cite_note-52] In 2017, Total signed an agreement with EREN Renewable energy to acquire an interest of 23% in EREN RE for an amount of €237.5 million.[^cite_note-53] In November 2017, Total announced the launch on the French residential market of Total Spring, a natural gas and green power offering that is 10% cheaper than regulated tariffs. Total is thus pursuing its strategy of downstream integration in the gas and power value chain in Europe.[^cite_note-54]

On 10 January 2018 TotalErg was [acquired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_acquisition) by [Gruppo API](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruppo_API),[^cite_note-\:12-55][^cite_note-\:2-56] with the exception of the *Special Fluids* division, acquired by the newly formed [Total Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Italia_\(2018_company\)).[^cite_note-57] In 2018, Total officially withdrew from the Iranian South Pars gas field because of sanctions pressure from the US.[^cite_note-dwir-58]

In 2019, Total announced the sale of a 30% stake in the Trapil pipeline network to crude oil storage operator Pisto SAS for €260 million.[^cite_note-59] Later that year, Total signed deals to transfer 30% and 28.33% of its assets in [Namibia](21292.md)'s Block 2913B and Block 2912 respectively to [QatarEnergy](11292570.md). The company will also transfer 40% of its existing 25% interests in the Orinduik and Kanuku blocks of [Guyana](17238691.md) and 25% interest in Blocks L11A, L11B, and L12 of [Kenya](188171.md) to QatarEnergy.[^cite_note-60]

In July 2020 the company changed its name from Total SA to Total SE as part of registration as a European company.[^cite_note-61] In 2020, the company announced its intention to cut 500 voluntary jobs in France.[^cite_note-62]

In 2021, Total left the [American Petroleum Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Petroleum_Institute) lobby, due to differences in the common vision of how to tackle the fight against climate change.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] In 2021, Total said that it had registered an income of \$3 billion for the period of January–March, which is close to the levels registered before the pandemic.[^cite_note-65]

### 2021–present: Rebranding to TotalEnergies

In 2021, the company announced a name change to TotalEnergies as an intended illustration of its investments in the production of green electricity.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] At the Ordinary and Extraordinary Shareholders' Meeting in May of that year, shareholders approved the name change to TotalEnergies.[^cite_note-68]

In 2022, TotalEnergies announced it would end all operations in [Myanmar](19457.md), citing rampant human rights abuses and deteriorating rule of law since the [2021 Myanmar coup d'état](66567997.md) and has also called for international sanctions targeting the oil and gas sector in the country, which is one of the main sources of revenue for Myanmar's government.[^cite_note-69] As of 11 March 2022, Total was one of the only Western oil companies to continue operating in Russia after the [Russian Invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] In June 2022, TotalEnergies signed a partnership with [QatarEnergy](11292570.md) for the world's largest LNG expansion project, the North Field East (NFE). Holding the largest stake, 6.25%, TotalEnergies will hold the equivalent of one of the four trains.[^cite_note-72] In September 2022, an additional agreement was signed to include the North Field South (NFS) which is the second phase of the NFE. This gave TotalEnergies a stake of 9.375% of the 25% stakes available to international companies.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

On 30 March 2023, Total sold a shipment of LNG which it sourced from [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE) to [CNOOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNOOC) on the Shanghai Petroleum and Natural Gas Exchange. It was reportedly the first trade to be settled in the [renminbi](66958.md) (Chinese yuan) currency on the SHPGX.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] In July 2023, [Iraq](7515928.md) signed a \$27 billion energy agreement with TotalEnergies to develop the country's energy sector and boost output of oil, gas and renewables.[^cite_note-77] Additionally, [Indian Oil Corp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Oil_Corporation), has signed [liquefied natural gas](832128.md) (LNG) import deals with *[ADNOC LNG](7992255.md)* and *TotalEnergies* in the same month.[^cite_note-78] In October 2023, TotalEnergies sold its Canadian operations to [Suncor Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suncor_Energy) for C\$1.47 billion(\$1.07 billion).[^cite_note-79] TotalEnergies has agreed to buy liquefied natural gas from Qatar for 27 years, cementing the European nation's commitment to fossil fuels beyond 2050.[^cite_note-80] In 2023, Total invested \$300 million in a renewable energy joint venture with [Adani Green Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adani_Green_Energy). The joint venture's portfolio capacity is 1,050 [MW](21347693.md#Megawatt) - 300 MW of operating capacity, 500 MW of solar projects under construction and 250 MW of projects under development, as well as solar and wind power projects in [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-81] At the end of January 2024, TotalEnergies reached an agreement with [OMV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OMV) to purchase a 50% stake in its joint venture in [Malaysia](3607937.md) (SapuraOMV) for \$903 million. The deal includes the repayment of a \$350 million loan from OMV to the joint venture.[^cite_note-82]

On 21 February 2024, TotalEnergies and [Airbus](26220236.md) entered a strategic partnership to meet emission-reduction goals through the use of sustainable aviation fuels (SAF). TotalEnergies will provide more than 50% of Airbus' European fuel requirements. Compared to fossil fuels, SAF can reduce CO<sub>2</sub> emissions by up to 90%.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] TotalEnergies and QatarEnergy entered an agreement on 6 March 2024 to purchase participating interests in [South Africa](17416221.md)'s Orange Basin offshore oil field. Under the agreement, TotalEnergies will have the exclusive right to operate its wells in Block 3B/4B with a 33% interest holding, while QatarEnergy will receive a 24% interest in the same block.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] On 22 April 2024, OmanLNG and TotalEnergies signed a deal in which OmanLNG will provide 800,000 metric tons of liquefied natural gas.[^cite_note-87] On 14 November 2024, TotalEnergies announced that it will fill all of its upstream assets with real-time methane leak detection equipment by 2025 to help minimize the emissions. This is expected to help the company in its target of slashing methane emissions to nearly zero percent by 2030.[^cite_note-88] On 15 November 2024, TotalEnergies, [BP](18998720.md), [Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_plc) and [Equinor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equinor) promised to invest \$500 million to increase the access to affordable energy, focusing primarily in sub-Saharan Africa, south and southeast Asia. This would include domestic solar energy systems, micro-electricity grids, energy production, transport, logistics and storage, e-mobility technologies, and modern cooking fuels such as liquefied petroleum gas (LPG).[^cite_note-89] On 3 December 2024 TotalEnergies announced its plans to build a 0.3 gigawatt (GW) solar park in Saudi Arabia, while another leading company from France [EDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lectricit%C3%A9_de_France) has been assigned to build two solar parks with a total of 1.4 GW.[^cite_note-90]

In January 2025, TotalEnergies sold all of its service stations in Mali to Coly Energy Mali, a subsidiary managed by the Beninese company Bénin Petro.[^cite_note-91] On 18 February 2025, TotalEnergies confirmed its departure from Burkina Faso, after 70 years of presence, in Burkina Faso.[^cite_note-92]

TotalEnergies was accused by the French court in October 2025 of having misled consumers with its environmental claims. They were told to remove messages of wanting to reach carbon neutrality by 2050 from the company website. It is the first time that the french "greenwashing" law has been imposed on a fossil fuel company.[^cite_note-93]

In December 2025, the company terminated its ADR program and converted all ADRs into ordinary shares. As a reason, the company stated that the investor base is increasingly located in North America, with around 40.8% of the company being owned by North American investors, compared to 24.6% held by French investors, including employees.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95]

In March 2026, the US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) paid TotalEnergies \$928 million to stop the wind project and fossil fuel investment planned for the US east cost.[^cite_note-96] The money was reimbursed on the condition that it be used to reinvest in hydrocarbon projects.[^cite_note-97]

### Investments

In 1937, [Iraq Petroleum Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_Petroleum_Company) (IPC), 23.75 percent owned by Total,[^cite_note-RLA-112] signed an oil concession agreement with the Sultan of Muscat. IPC offered financial support to raise an armed force that would assist the Sultan in occupying the interior region of Oman, an area that geologists believed to be rich in oil. This led to the 1954 outbreak of [Jebel Akhdar War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebel_Akhdar_War) in Oman that lasted for more than 5 years.[^cite_note-OmansInsurgencies-113]

Total has been a significant investor in the Iranian energy sector since 1990.[^cite_note-114] In 2017, Total and the [National Iranian Oil Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Company) (NIOC) signed a contract for the development and production of [South Pars](9037100.md), the world's largest gas field. The project will have a production capacity of 2 billion cubic feet per day. The produced gas will supply the Iranian domestic market starting in 2021.[^cite_note-\:0-49]

During the [European Union](9317.md)'s sanctions against the military dictatorship [Myanmar](19457.md), Total is able to operate the [Yadana natural gas pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yadana_Project) from [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) to [Thailand](30128.md). Total is currently the subject of a lawsuit in French and Belgian courts for the condoning and use of the country's civilian slavery to construct the pipeline. The documentary 'Total Denial' shows the background of this project.[^cite_note-115] The NGO [Burma Campaign UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_Campaign_UK) is currently campaigning against this project.

### Energy Deal with ADNOC

In a move to cope with the [2021–2022 global energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%932022_global_energy_crisis), which started with the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic and aggravated with Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, France's TotalEnergies and UAE's [ADNOC](7992255.md) signed a strategic deal to partner on energy projects "for cooperation in the area of energy supplies".

The deal was secured on the second day of the UAE leader Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed Al-Nahyan's visit to Paris in 2022. The visit marked the UAE president's first overseas state visit since assuming the post earlier that year.

The deal was aimed at identifying and targeting potential joint investment projects in the UAE, France, and elsewhere in the sectors of renewables, hydrogen, and nuclear energy, as told by the French government in one of its statements. According to French President Emmanuel Macron's aides, France had been eager to secure diesel supply from the UAE.[^cite_note-130]

The deal also received criticism from human rights groups that pressured Macron not to give the then "crown prince a pass on the UAE's atrocious human rights record", per the statement published by Human Rights Watch on its website.[^cite_note-131]

## Controversies

### Environmental and safety records

In 1999, the Total SA company was fined €375,000 for the [MV Erika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Erika) oil spill that stretched 400 kilometers from [La Rochelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rochelle) to the western tip of [Brittany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittany). The company was only fined that amount because it was only partially liable because Total SA did not own the ship. The plaintiffs had sought more than \$1.5 billion in damages. More than 100 groups and local governments joined in the suit. The Total company was fined just over \$298,000. The majority of the money will go to the French government, several environmental groups, and various regional governments. The Total SA company was also fined \$550,000 for the amount of marine pollution that came from it. After the oil spill it tried to restore its image and have opened a sea turtle conservation project in [Masirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masirah) in recent years.

Prior to the verdict in which Total was found guilty one of the counterparts in the incident, Malta Maritime Authority (MMA), was not to be tried for having any hand in the incident. In 2005, Total submitted a report to the Paris courts which stated that Total had gathered a group of experts which stated the tanker was corroded and that Total was responsible for it. The courts sought a second expert reviewing this information, which was turned down.[^cite_note-132]

In 2001, the [AZF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AZF_\(factory\)) chemical plant exploded in [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse), France, while belonging to the Grande Paroisse branch of Total.

In 2008, Total was required to pay €192 million in compensation to victims of the pollution caused by the sinking of the ship MV Erika. This was in addition to the €200 million that Total spent to help clean up the spill. The company appealed twice against the verdict, losing both times.

In 2016, Total was ranked as the second-best of 92 oil, gas, and mining companies on indigenous rights in the Arctic.[^cite_note-133] According to the CDP Carbon Majors Report 2017, the company was one of the top 100 companies producing [carbon emissions](9528025.md) globally, responsible for 0.9% of global emissions from 1998 to 2015.[^cite_note-\:1-134] In 2021, Total was ranked as the 2nd most environmentally responsible company out of 120 oil, gas, and mining companies involved in resource extraction north of the Arctic Circle in the Arctic Environmental Responsibility Index (AERI).[^cite_note-135]

According to a 2021 study, Total personnel were aware about the role that its products played in global warming as early as 1971, as well as throughout the 1980s. Despite this awareness, the company promoted doubt regarding the science of global warming by the late 1980s, and ultimately settled on a position in the late 1990s of publicly accepting climate science, while still promoting doubt and trying to delay climate action.[^cite_note-136] In August 2024, South Africa's advertising regulator ruled that TotalEnergies' promotion of sustainability in an advertising campaign in the country was misleading. The campaign was run in collaboration with South Africa's nature conservation authority, Sanparks, to encourage people to visit the country's national parks.[^cite_note-137]

### Bribery

Total has been accused of bribery on multiple occasions.

Total is being implicated in a bribe commission scandal which is currently emerging in [Malta](19137.md). It has emerged that Total had told Maltese agents that it would not be interested in doing business with them unless their team included George Farrugia, who is under investigation in the procurement scandal. George Farrugia has recently been given a presidential pardon in exchange for information about this scandal. [Enemalta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enemalta), Malta's energy supplier, swiftly barred Total and its agents, Trafigura from bidding and tenders. An investigation is currently underway and three people have been arraigned.[^cite_note-138]

On 16 December 2008, the managing director of the Italian division of Total, Lionel Levha, and ten other executives were arrested by the public Prosecutor's office of [Potenza, Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potenza,_Italy), for a corruption charge of €15 million to undertake the oilfield in [Basilicata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilicata) on contract. Also arrested was the local deputy of [Partito Democratico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partito_Democratico) Salvatore Margiotta and an Italian entrepreneur.[^cite_note-ilgiornale-139][^cite_note-la7-140]

In 2010, Total was accused of bribing Iraqi officials during former president Saddam Hussein's regime to secure oil supplies. A United Nations report later revealed that Iraqi officials had received bribes from oil companies to secure contracts worth over \$10bn.[^cite_note-141] On 26 February 2016, the [Paris Court of Appeals](37196535.md) considered Total guilty and ordered the company to pay a fine of €750,000 for corrupting Iraqi civil servants. The court's ruling overturns an earlier acquittal in the case.

In 2013, a case was settled that concerned charges that Total bribed an Iranian official with \$60 million, which it documented as a "consulting charge," and which unfairly gave it access to Iran's Sirri A and Sirri E oil and gas fields. The bribery gave it a competitive advantage, earning it an estimated \$150 million in profits. The Securities Exchange Commission and the Department of Justice settled the charges, expecting Total to pay \$398 million.[^cite_note-142]

### 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine

Following the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) which began on 24 February, many international, particularly Western companies pulled out of Russia. On 1 March, TotalEnergies announced it "will no longer provide capital for new projects in Russia" but has retained ownership of its 19.4% stake in privately owned [Novatek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novatek), 20% stake in the [Yamal project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamal_project) and 10% stake in Arctic LNG 2.[^cite_note-143] This has led to criticism as insufficient, particularly given complete divestment of other major Western energy companies, and the European Union announcement of becoming more energy independent from Russia.[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146] Similarly in August 2022, an investigation by [Global Witness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Witness) showed that a Siberian gas field part-owned by TotalEnergies has been supplying a refinery, which is producing jet fuel for Russian warplanes. This contradicts Total's claims that this was unrelated to Russian military operations in Ukraine.[^cite_note-147] On 26 April 2024, while presenting the firm's interim financial report TotalEnergy CEO Patrick Pouyanne said that importing Russian LNG to EU is not a very profitable operation and if EU sanctions Russian LNG, the price of LNG will go up quickly and globally TotalEngergy's portfolio will benefit from it.[^cite_note-148] According to a 2024 analysis done by [IEEFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Energy_Economics_and_Financial_Analysis), despite EU sanctions, Russian LNG import to France almost doubled to 4.4 billion cubic meters in the first half of 2024 compared to the same period a year ago.[^cite_note-149] On 30 September 2025, Patrick Pouyanne suggested that Russian liquefied natural gas shipments could be redirected to other countries like India and Turkey if the European Union bans imports while sparing a key facility in Russia.[^cite_note-150]

### Africa

In December 2022, the NGOs Friends of the Earth, Survie and four Ugandan NGOs sent the oil group Total to court and accused it of violating the law on the duty of vigilance of large French companies in terms of human rights and environment.[^cite_note-151] The Tilenga Project, which TotalEnergies is undertaking in conjunction with [China National Offshore Oil Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Offshore_Oil_Corporation) consists of drilling for oil in the [Murchison Falls National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murchison_Falls_National_Park), a habitat for diverse species of birds and animals.[^cite_note-152] The project also involves building a pipeline from the site in land-locked Uganda to Tanga in Tanzania. Critics of the project are concerned that, since the proposed pipeline passes through Lake Victoria and close to a number of wildlife areas in Tanzania and Kenya, oil spills could threaten the lake and could have adverse effects on the wildlife, some of which is endangered, in various national parks.[^cite_note-153] In March 2025, a judicial investigation was opened in France for involuntary manslaughter against TotalEnergies in Mozambique.[^cite_note-154] In December 2025, the [United Kingdom](31717.md) pulled back US\$1.15 billion from TotalEnergies' US\$20 billion gas project in Mozambique citing risks of rising jihadism in the region.[^cite_note-155]

### Human rights abuses by hired task force

Over a three-year period between 2020 and 2023, Total helped fund a military task force of up to 1,000 regular troops to protect its gas infrastructure from insurgents during the [insurgency in Cabo Delgado](56119804.md). Internal reports revealed that between mid-2020 and late 2022 residents of nearby villages regularly reported human rights violations including torture, detentions and disappearances at the hands of the task force.[^cite_note-156] Despite being aware of these of allegations Total continued funding the task force, opting to selectively punish offenders by withholding a monetary "hardship bonus".
