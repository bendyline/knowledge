It broadcasts at 101.9 [MHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megahertz) with a radiated power of 48 [kW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt).

In [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics) and [telecommunications](33094374.md), a **radio transmitter** or just **transmitter** (often abbreviated as **XMTR** or **TX** in technical documents) is an [electronic device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_device) which produces [radio waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_wave) with an [antenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antenna_\(radio\)) with the purpose of [signal transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_transmission) to a [radio receiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_receiver). The transmitter itself generates a [radio frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency) [alternating current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_current), which is applied to the [antenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antenna_\(radio\)). When excited by this alternating current, the antenna [radiates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_radiation) radio waves.

Transmitters are necessary component parts of all electronic devices that communicate by [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_communication), such as [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_broadcasting) (audio) and [television broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_broadcasting) stations, [cell phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_phone), [walkie-talkies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkie-talkie), [wireless computer networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_LAN), [Bluetooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluetooth) enabled devices, [garage door openers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garage_door_opener), [two-way radios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-way_radio) in aircraft, ships, spacecraft, [radar](25676.md) sets and navigational beacons. The term *transmitter* is usually limited to equipment that generates radio waves for [communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication_engineering) purposes; or [radiolocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiolocation), such as [radar](25676.md) and navigational transmitters. Generators of radio waves for heating or industrial purposes, such as [microwave ovens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_oven) or [diathermy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diathermy) equipment, are not usually called transmitters, even though they often have similar circuits.

The term is popularly used more specifically to refer to a [broadcast transmitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_transmitter), a transmitter used in [broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcasting), as in *FM radio transmitter* or [television transmitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_transmitter). This usage typically includes both the transmitter proper, the antenna, and often the building it is housed in.

## Description



A radio transmitter is usually part of a [radio communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_communication) system which uses [electromagnetic waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_wave) ([radio waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_wave)) to transport information (in this case sound) over a distance.

A transmitter can be a **separate group** among all pieces of electronic equipments, or an [electrical circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_network) within another electronic device. A transmitter and a [receiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_receiver) combined in one unit is called a [transceiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transceiver). The purpose of most transmitters is [radio communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_communication) of information over a distance. The information is provided to the transmitter in the form of an electronic signal called the modulation signal, such as an [audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_signal) (sound) signal from a microphone, a [video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_signal) (TV) signal from a video camera, or in [wireless networking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_networking) devices, a [digital signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_\(electronics\)) from a computer. The transmitter generates a [radio frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency) signal which when applied to the antenna produces the radio waves, called the [carrier signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_signal). It combines the carrier with the modulation signal, a process called [modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulation). The information can be added to the carrier in several different ways, in different types of transmitters. In an [amplitude modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplitude_modulation) (AM) transmitter, the information is added to the radio signal by varying its [amplitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplitude). In a [frequency modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_modulation) (FM) transmitter, it is added by varying the radio signal's [frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency) slightly. Many other types of modulation are also used.

The radio signal from the transmitter is applied to the [antenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antenna_\(radio\)), which radiates the energy as radio waves. The antenna may be enclosed inside the case or attached to the outside of the transmitter, as in portable devices such as cell phones, walkie-talkies, and [garage door openers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garage_door_opener). In more powerful transmitters, the antenna may be located on top of a building or on a separate tower, and connected to the transmitter by a [feed line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feed_line), that is a [transmission line](41811.md).

## Operation

Main article: [Radio transmitter design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_transmitter_design)



Animation of a [half-wave dipole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-wave_dipole) antenna transmitting [radio waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_wave), showing the [electric field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_field) lines. The antenna in the center is two vertical metal rods, with an alternating current applied at its center from a radio transmitter (not shown). The voltage charges the two sides of the antenna alternately positive *(+)* and negative *(−)*. Loops of electric field (black lines) leave the antenna and travel away at the [speed of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_light); these are the radio waves. This animation shows the action slowed enormously

[Electromagnetic waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_wave) are radiated by [electric charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_charge) when they are [accelerated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acceleration).[^cite_note-Serway-1][^cite_note-Ellingson-2] [Radio waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_wave), electromagnetic waves of radio [frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency), are generated by time-varying [electric currents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_current), consisting of [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron) flowing through a metal conductor called an [antenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antenna_\(radio\)) which are changing their velocity and thus accelerating.[^cite_note-Balanis-3][^cite_note-Ellingson-2] An [alternating current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_current) flowing back and forth in an antenna will create an oscillating [magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_field) around the conductor. The alternating voltage will also charge the ends of the conductor alternately positive and negative, creating an oscillating [electric field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_field) around the conductor. If the [frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency) of the oscillations is high enough, in the [radio frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency) range above about 20 kHz, the oscillating coupled electric and magnetic fields will radiate away from the antenna into space as an electromagnetic wave, a radio wave.

A radio transmitter is an [electronic circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_circuit) which transforms [electric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power) from a power source, a battery or mains power, into a [radio frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency) alternating current to apply to the antenna, and the antenna radiates the energy from this current as radio waves.[^cite_note-HowRadioWorks-4] The transmitter also encodes information such as an [audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_signal) or [video signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_signal) into the radio frequency current to be carried by the radio waves. When they strike the antenna of a [radio receiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_receiver), the waves excite similar (but less powerful) radio frequency currents in it. The radio receiver extracts the information from the received waves.

### Components

A practical radio transmitter mainly consists of the following parts:

- In high power transmitters, a [power supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_supply) circuit to transform the input electrical power to the higher [voltages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage) needed to produce the required power output.

- An [electronic oscillator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_oscillator) circuit to generate the [radio frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency) signal. This usually generates a [sine wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine_wave) of constant [amplitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplitude), called the [carrier wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_wave) because it produces the radio waves which "carry" the information through space. In most modern transmitters, this is a [crystal oscillator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_oscillator) in which the frequency is precisely controlled by the vibrations of a [quartz crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz_crystal). The [frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency) of the carrier wave is considered the frequency of the transmitter.

- A [modulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulator) circuit to add the information to be transmitted to the carrier wave produced by the oscillator. This is done by varying some aspect of the carrier wave. The information is provided to the transmitter as an electronic signal called the [modulation signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulation). The modulation signal may be an [audio signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_signal), which represents [sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound), a [video signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_signal) which represents moving images, or for data in the form of a [binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_numeral_system) [digital signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal) which represents a sequence of [bits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_digit), a [bitstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitstream). Different types of transmitters use different [modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulation) methods to transmit information:&#x20;

  - In an AM ([amplitude modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplitude_modulation)) transmitter the [amplitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplitude) (strength) of the carrier wave is varied in proportion to the modulation signal.

  - In an FM ([frequency modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_modulation)) transmitter the [frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency) of the carrier is varied by the modulation signal.

  - In an FSK ([frequency-shift keying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency-shift_keying)) transmitter, which transmits digital data, the frequency of the carrier is shifted between two frequencies which represent the two [binary digits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_digit), 0 and 1.

  - OFDM ([orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_frequency-division_multiplexing)) is a family of complicated [digital modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_modulation) methods very widely used in high bandwidth systems such as [Wi-Fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi-Fi) networks, [cellphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellphone), [digital television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_television) broadcasting, and [digital audio broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_audio_broadcasting) (DAB) to transmit digital data using a minimum of [radio spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_spectrum) bandwidth. OFDM has higher [spectral efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_efficiency) and more resistance to [fading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fading) than AM or FM. In OFDM multiple radio carrier waves closely spaced in frequency are transmitted within the radio channel, with each carrier modulated with bits from the incoming [bitstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitstream) so multiple [bits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_digit) are being sent simultaneously, in parallel. At the receiver the carriers are demodulated and the bits are combined in the proper order into one bitstream.

Many other types of [modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulation) are also used. In large transmitters the oscillator and modulator together are often referred to as the *exciter*.

[^cite_note-nab-5]

- A radio frequency (RF) [amplifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplifier) to increase the power of the signal, to increase the range of the radio waves.

- An [impedance matching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impedance_matching) ([antenna tuner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antenna_tuner)) circuit to transform the output [impedance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_impedance) of the transmitter to match the impedance of the antenna (or the [transmission line](41811.md) to the antenna), to transfer power efficiently to the antenna. If these impedances are not equal, it causes a condition called [standing waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_wave), in which the power is reflected back from the antenna toward the transmitter, wasting power and sometimes overheating the transmitter.

In higher frequency transmitters, in the [UHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_high_frequency) and [microwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave) range, free running oscillators are unstable at the output frequency. Older designs used an oscillator at a lower frequency, which was multiplied by [frequency multipliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_multiplier) to get a signal at the desired frequency. Modern designs more commonly use an oscillator at the operating frequency which is stabilized by phase locking to a very stable lower frequency reference, usually a crystal oscillator.

## Regulation

Two radio transmitters in the same area that attempt to transmit on the same frequency will interfere with each other, causing garbled reception, so neither transmission may be received clearly. [Interference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency_interference) with radio transmissions can not only have a large economic cost, it can be life-threatening (for example, in the case of interference with emergency communications or [air traffic control](48563.md)).

For this reason, in most countries, use of transmitters is strictly controlled by law. Transmitters must be licensed by governments, under a variety of license classes depending on use such as [broadcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast), [marine radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_radio), [Airband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airband), [Amateur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_radio) and are restricted to certain frequencies and power levels. A body called the [International Telecommunication Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Telecommunication_Union) (ITU) allocates the [frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency) bands in the [radio spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_spectrum) to various classes of users. In some classes, each transmitter is given a unique [call sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_sign) consisting of a string of letters and numbers which must be used as an identifier in transmissions. The operator of the transmitter usually must hold a government license, such as a [general radiotelephone operator license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_radiotelephone_operator_license), which is obtained by passing a test demonstrating adequate technical and legal knowledge of safe radio operation.

Exceptions to the above regulations allow the unlicensed use of low-power short-range transmitters in consumer products such as [cell phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_phone), [cordless telephones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordless_telephone), [wireless microphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_microphone), [walkie-talkies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkie-talkie), [Wi-Fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi-Fi) and [Bluetooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluetooth) devices, [garage door openers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garage_door_opener), and [baby monitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_monitor). In the US, these fall under [Part 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_15) of the [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) (FCC) regulations. Although they can be operated without a license, these devices still generally must be [type-approved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_approval) before sale.

## History

Main article: [History of radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_radio)



Hertz discovering radio waves in 1887 with his first primitive radio transmitter (background).

The first primitive radio transmitters (called [spark gap transmitters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spark_gap_transmitter)) were built by German physicist [Heinrich Hertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Hertz) in 1887 during his pioneering investigations of radio waves. These generated radio waves by a high voltage [spark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_spark) between two conductors. Beginning in 1895, [Guglielmo Marconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guglielmo_Marconi) developed the first practical radio communication systems using these transmitters, and radio began to be used commercially around 1900. Spark transmitters could not transmit [audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_signal) (sound) and instead transmitted information by [radiotelegraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiotelegraphy): the operator tapped on a [telegraph key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegraph_key) which turned the transmitter on-and-off to produce radio wave pulses spelling out text messages in telegraphic code, usually [Morse code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_code). At the receiver, these pulses were sometimes directly recorded on paper tapes, but more common was audible reception. The pulses were audible as beeps in the receiver's earphones, which were translated back to text by an operator who knew Morse code. These spark-gap transmitters were used during the first three decades of radio (1887–1917), called the [wireless telegraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_telegraphy) or "spark" era. Because they generated [damped waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damped_wave), spark transmitters were electrically "noisy". Their energy was spread over a broad band of [frequencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency), creating [radio noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency_interference) which interfered with other transmitters. Damped wave emissions were banned by international law in 1934.

Two short-lived competing transmitter technologies came into use after the turn of the century, which were the first [continuous wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_wave) transmitters: the [arc converter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_converter) ([Poulsen arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poulsen_Arc)) in 1904 and the [Alexanderson alternator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexanderson_alternator) around 1910, which were used into the 1920s.

All these early technologies were replaced by [vacuum tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_tube) transmitters in the 1920s, which used the [feedback oscillator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_oscillator#Feedback_oscillator) invented by [Edwin Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Armstrong) and [Alexander Meissner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Meissner) around 1912, based on the [Audion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audion) ([triode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triode)) vacuum tube invented by [Lee De Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_De_Forest) in 1906. Vacuum tube transmitters were inexpensive and produced [continuous waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_wave), and could be easily [modulated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulation) to transmit audio (sound) using [amplitude modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplitude_modulation) (AM). This made AM [radio broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_broadcasting) possible, which began in about 1920. Practical [frequency modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_modulation) (FM) transmission was invented by [Edwin Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Armstrong) in 1933, who showed that it was less vulnerable to noise and static than AM. The first FM radio station was licensed in 1937. Experimental [television](29831.md) transmission had been conducted by radio stations since the late 1920s, but practical [television broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_broadcasting) didn't begin until the late 1930s. The development of [radar](25676.md) during [World War II](32927.md) motivated the evolution of high frequency transmitters in the [UHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrahigh_frequency) and [microwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave) ranges, using new active devices such as the [magnetron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetron), [klystron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klystron), and [traveling wave tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traveling_wave_tube).

The invention of the [transistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor) allowed the development in the 1960s of small portable transmitters such as [wireless microphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_microphone), [garage door openers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garage_door_opener) and [walkie-talkies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkie-talkie). The development of the [integrated circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit) (IC) in the 1970s made possible the current proliferation of [wireless devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_device), such as [cell phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_phone) and [Wi-Fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi-Fi) networks, in which integrated digital transmitters and receivers ([wireless modems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_modem)) in portable devices operate automatically, in the background, to exchange data with [wireless networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_network).

The need to conserve bandwidth in the increasingly congested [radio spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_spectrum) is driving the development of new types of transmitters such as [spread spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_spectrum), [trunked radio systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trunked_radio_system) and [cognitive radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_radio). A related trend has been an ongoing transition from [analog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_signal) to [digital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_\(signal_processing\)) radio transmission methods. [Digital modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_modulation) can have greater [spectral efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_efficiency) than [analog modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_modulation); that is it can often transmit more information ([data rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit_rate)) in a given [bandwidth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandwidth_\(signal_processing\)) than analog, using [data compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_compression) algorithms. Other advantages of digital transmission are increased [noise immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_immunity), and greater flexibility and processing power of [digital signal processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_processing) [integrated circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit).
