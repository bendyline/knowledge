**Cowlitz County** is a [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_\(United_States\)) located in the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [Washington](13015878.md). As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), its population was 110,730.[^cite_note-QF-1] The county seat is [Kelso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelso,_Washington),[^cite_note-2] and its largest city is [Longview](137888.md). The county was formed in April 1854.[^cite_note-historylink-3] Its name derives from the anglicized version of the [Cowlitz Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowlitz_people) term *Cow-e-liske*, meaning either 'river of shifting sands' or 'capturing the medicine spirit.' Cowlitz comprises the Longview, WA [Metropolitan statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area), which is also included in the [Portland](23503.md)-[Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver,_Washington)-[Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem,_Oregon), [OR](26811621.md)-WA [Combined statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_metropolitan_area).[^cite_note-4]

## History

Prior to the Europeans' arrival to the area, it was inhabited by numerous Native American tribes, with the Cowlitz tribe being the largest. They were drawn to the region by the abundance of [salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmon). The Cowlitz are considered to be the first regional inhabitants to engage in commerce as they traded extensively with other tribes in [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Washington) and [Eastern Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Washington). The Cowlitz Indian population declined significantly from the 1829-1830 [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox) outbreak.

European explorers discovered and began navigating the [Columbia River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River) in 1792 as [British](19097669.md) Lieutenant W. R. Broughton sailed up the river to and past present-day Cowlitz County. Then on November 5, 1805, [Lewis and Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition) camped at the mouth of the [Kalama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalama_River). Over the following days, they reached the present sites of Kelso and Longview.

By the 1820s, the [Hudson's Bay Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson's_Bay_Company) had established a lucrative [fur trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade) in the region. Furs were shipped down the [Cowlitz River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowlitz_River) to the Columbia, where they were loaded and shipped around the world. Trade declined significantly in the late 1830s, as overhunting reduced the annual yields, and wearing fur had become less fashionable.

During the next several decades, white settlement of the region was in full swing. Most of the settlers homesteaded near the tributaries that fed the Columbia River, forming settlements. The first was Monticello, near present-day Longview. In 1841, several families with the HBC directed [Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Sinclair_\(fur_trader\)) expedition from [Red River Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Colony) settled there.

On November 25, 1852, at Monticello, settlers from the Cowlitz and [Puget Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puget_Sound) regions drafted a petition (the [Monticello Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monticello_Convention)) to the federal government, calling for a separate territory north of the Columbia River to be carved out of the existing [Oregon Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Territory). The petition was successful; three months later, the [United States Congress](31756.md) formed the Columbia Territory, although it was soon renamed [Washington Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Territory).

The newly separated territory was governed by two existing counties. In August 1845, the Oregon territorial government had created Vancouver County. Its boundary covered the entire area of present-day Washington. In December of that same year, the Oregon territorial government sliced off the eastern portion to create [Lewis County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_County,_Washington). In 1849, the reduced Vancouver County was renamed [Clark County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_County,_Washington). When the new Washington territorial government began functioning, among its first actions was the creation of Cowlitz County from the southwestern portion of Clark County. This proclamation was finalized on April 24, 1854, signed into law by Governor [Isaac Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Stevens). Later in 1854, the western portion of the new county was partitioned off to form [Wahkiakum County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahkiakum_County,_Washington); otherwise, the county's boundary has remained unchanged until the present.

Nearly every town that sprang up in the late 19th century began around a [logging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logging) or [lumber-milling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawmill) operation. In the latter half of the 1920s, the [Weyerhaeuser Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyerhaeuser) and [Long-Bell Lumber Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-Bell_Lumber_Company) established processing facilities. At the time, these two facilities were the first- and second-largest in the world. The county is still heavily dependent on the timber industry.[^cite_note-ESD-5]

Four towns have functioned as the Cowlitz County seat:[^cite_note-6]

- [Monticello](137888.md) (1854–1865)

- [Freeport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeport,_Washington?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1865–1872)

- [Kalama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalama,_Washington) (1872–1922)

- [Kelso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelso,_Washington) (1922–Present)

## Geography

Cowlitz County is in the southwestern part of Washington . According to the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the county has a total area of 1,166 square miles (3,020 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 26 square miles (67 km<sup>2</sup>) (2.2%) are covered by water.[^cite_note-7]

Cowlitz County is part of the Puget Sound – Willamette Depression, a geologic formation extending southward from the Puget Sound to the [Willamette Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willamette_Valley) in [Oregon](26811621.md). Nestled against the [Cascade Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascade_Range), many of the county's major rivers originate in this range, including the Columbia, Cowlitz, [Coweeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coweeman_River), Kalama, [Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_River_\(Washington\)) and [Toutle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toutle_River).[^cite_note-ESD-5]

Cowlitz County is one of the state's smaller counties (28th of 39).[^cite_note-8]

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), 110,730 people, 42,787 households, and 28,644 families were residing in the county.[^cite_note-15] The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) was 97.0 inhabitants per square mile (37.5/km<sup>2</sup>). The 45,424 housing units had an average density of 39.8 inhabitants per square mile (15.4/km<sup>2</sup>).

About 22.7% of residents were under 18, 5.6% were under 5, and 19.8% were 65 or older; the median age was 41.0 years. For every 100 females, there were 98.1 males, and for every 100 females 18 and over, there were 96.6 males.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-16] 67.0% of residents lived in urban areas and 33.0% lived in rural areas.[^cite_note-Census2020DHC-17]

### 2010 census

As of the [2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census), 102,410 people, 40,244 households, and 27,241 families were living in the county. The population density was 89.8 people per square mile (34.7 people/km<sup>2</sup>). The 43,450 housing units had an average density of 38.1 units per square mile (14.7 units/km<sup>2</sup>). The racial makeup of the county was 88.9% White, 1.5% Asian, 1.5% American Indian, 0.6% black or African American, 0.2% Pacific islander, 3.5% from other races, and 3.7% from two or more races. Those of Hispanic or Latino origin made up 7.8% of the population. In terms of ancestry, 24.4% were [German](152735.md), 15.0% were [Irish](775859.md), 12.3% were [English](18803164.md), 6.2% were [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegians), and 5.4% were [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_ancestry).

Of the 40,244 households, 31.8% had children under 18 living with them, 50.2% were married couples living together, 11.8% had a female householder with no husband present, 32.3% were not families, and 25.8% of all households were made up of individuals. The average household size was 2.51 and the average family size was 2.99. The median age was 40.2 years.

The median income for a household in the county was \$45,877 and for a family was \$55,002. Males had a median income of \$48,329 versus \$32,438 for females. The per capita income for the county was \$22,948. About 11.8% of families and 16.9% of the population were below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line), including 23.3% of those under 18 and 7.1% of those 65 or over.

## Politics

The county had been reliably Democratic in Presidential elections for over three decades, and [Walter Mondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Mondale) won this county in [Ronald Reagan](25433.md)'s 49-state landslide in 1984. [Donald Trump](4848272.md) won a majority of the vote in 2016, 2020, and 2024, becoming the first Republican to win this county since Reagan in 1980.
