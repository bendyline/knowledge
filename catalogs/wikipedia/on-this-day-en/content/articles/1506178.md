The **Tooro Kingdom** is one of the 17 [recognised traditional kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_monarchies_in_Uganda) located within the borders of [Uganda](31816.md).

The people native to the kingdom are the [Batooro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tooro_people), and their language is likewise called [Rutooro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutooro).[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-\:1-2] The Batooro and those of the neighbouring kingdom of [Bunyoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyoro_people), speak the [Runyoro/Rutooro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyoro-Tooro_language) dialect of the Runyakitara language and share a similar background and cultural traits.[^cite_note-\:1-2] The Batooro live on Uganda's western border, south of [Lake Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Albert_\(Africa\)).[^cite_note-\:0-1]

## History

The Tooro Kingdom evolved out of a breakaway segment of [Bunyoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunyoro) sometime before the nineteenth century.[^cite_note-3] It was founded in 1830 when [Omukama](2022535.md) [Kaboyo Olimi I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olimi_I_of_Tooro), the eldest son of [Omukama of Bunyoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omukama_of_Bunyoro) [Nyamutukura Kyebambe III of Bunyoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyamutukura_Kyebambe_III_of_Bunyoro), seceded and established his own independent kingdom.[^cite_note-\:1-2][^cite_note-\:0-1] Absorbed into Bunyoro-Kitara in 1876, it reasserted its independence in 1891.

In 1967, with the ratification of the [Third constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Uganda#Third_constitution_\(1967%E2%80%931995\)) of Uganda, Tooro's monarchy was abolished, alongside the monarchies of [Bunyoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunyoro), [Buganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda), [Ankole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankole), and [Busoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busoga), but was reinstated in 1993.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

## Cultural influence

Main articles: [Friedensreich Hundertwasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedensreich_Hundertwasser) and [Empaako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empaako)

The Austrian painter [Friedensreich Hundertwasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedensreich_Hundertwasser) (1928–2000) spent some time there in the 1960s where he painted a number of works and named them after the kingdom.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

The Batooro people have a strong culture but similar in stratification to Banyoro.[^cite_note-\:3-6] They have a strong cultural naming system (PET NAME) known as Empaako.[^cite_note-\:5-7][^cite_note-\:4-8] With the Empaako naming system, children are given one of twelve names shared across the communities in addition to their given and family names.[^cite_note-\:4-8][^cite_note-\:7-9] Addressing someone by his or her Empaako is a positive affirmation of cultural ties. It can be used as a form of greeting or a declaration of affection, respect, honour or love.[^cite_note-\:4-8][^cite_note-\:5-7]

Use of Empaako can defuse tension or anger and sends a strong message about social identity and unity, peace and reconciliation.[^cite_note-\:4-8][^cite_note-\:6-10] The Empaako names are: Amooti, Abooki, Akiiki, Ateenyi, Adyeeri, Atwooki, Abwooli, Araali, Acaali, Bbala, and Okaali.[^cite_note-\:4-8][^cite_note-\:6-10][^cite_note-\:7-9]

3. [Nyaika Kyebambe I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyaika_of_Tooro): 1866–1871 and 1871–1872

8. [Kakende Nyamuyonjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakende_of_Tooro): 1875–1876 and 1879–1880

9. [Katera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katera_of_Tooro): 1876–1877&#x20;

   1. *[Interregnum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interregnum), reverted to [Bunyoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunyoro)*: 1880–1891
