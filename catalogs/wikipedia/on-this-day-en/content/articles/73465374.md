***Hamnet*** is a 2025 [historical drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_drama) film directed by [Chloé Zhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlo%C3%A9_Zhao), who co-wrote the screenplay with [Maggie O'Farrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggie_O'Farrell), based on the [2020 novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamnet_\(novel\)) by O'Farrell. The film dramatises the family life of [William Shakespeare](32897.md) and his wife [Agnes Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hathaway_\(wife_of_Shakespeare\))[^cite_note-6] as they cope with the death of their 11-year-old son [Hamnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamnet_Shakespeare).[^cite_note-7] It stars [Jessie Buckley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessie_Buckley) and [Paul Mescal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Mescal) as Agnes and William, alongside [Emily Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Watson), [Joe Alwyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Alwyn), and [Jacobi Jupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobi_Jupe) in supporting roles.

*Hamnet* had its world premiere at the [52nd Telluride Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/52nd_Telluride_Film_Festival) on 29 August 2025 and received a [limited theatrical release](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_release) by Focus Features in the United States and Canada on 26 November. It received a wide theatrical release on 5 December and was released by [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures_UK) in the United Kingdom on 9 January 2026. Critical reception was positive, with Buckley's performance receiving particular praise. The film received [numerous accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_accolades_received_by_Hamnet_\(film\)), including winning the [Best Motion Picture – Drama](186059.md) and [Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Drama) for Buckley at the [83rd Golden Globe Awards](81659305.md), and eight nominations at the [98th Academy Awards](79369474.md), including [Best Picture](61702.md), [Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director) for Zhao, with Buckley winning the [Academy Award for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress).

## Plot

A written prologue states that in [Stratford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratford-upon-Avon), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England), "Hamnet" and "Hamlet" were considered the same name.[^cite_note-8]

[William Shakespeare](32897.md) works as a tutor to pay off his family's debt. He leaves his students after seeing a mysterious [Agnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hathaway_\(wife_of_Shakespeare\)) summon a hawk with her [falconry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falconry) glove, and they share a moment. William's mother, [Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Shakespeare), warns him of rumours that Agnes is the daughter of a forest [witch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witchcraft_in_early_modern_Britain) who taught her [herbal lore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbal_medicine), which Agnes later uses to treat a cut on William's forehead. She asks him for a story, and he recounts the legend of [Orpheus and Eurydice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orpheus_and_Eurydice), delighting her. Agnes reads William's palm, foretelling a successful future for him, and two children at her deathbed. The pair consummate their relationship, impregnating Agnes, leading her family to disown her and forcing her to move in with the Shakespeares. The pair hurriedly marry, and Agnes gives birth to [Susanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susanna_Hall) in the woods.

William retaliates when his father, [John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Shakespeare), beats him for rejecting manual labour. Seeing William's [frustration with writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writer's_block), Agnes suggests to her brother Bartholomew to send him to London, leaving her and Susanna in Stratford. Later, a pregnant Agnes tries to go outside to give birth, but William's family tells her the river has flooded and keep her in the house, where she gives birth to twins [Hamnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamnet_Shakespeare) and [Judith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judith_Quiney), the latter seemingly [stillborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stillbirth). Remembering being kept from her mother's deathbed as a child, Agnes demands to hold the baby despite superstition. Judith awakes, and Agnes promises to protect her. Years later, a now-successful William returns home intermittently while the children grow up very close. As they bury Agnes's hawk, she tells the children to make a wish to the hawk's spirit, who she says will carry them in its heart. William leaves for London again and tells a distraught Hamnet to look out for the family; Agnes reassures him by foretelling that he will join his father's theatre company in a swordfighting role.&#x20;

William wanders the streets of London during an outbreak of [bubonic plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubonic_plague) and watches a puppet show depicting the plague carrying people off to death. In Stratford, Judith contracts plague. As the family treat her, Hamnet lies beside her and evokes the tale of the hawk to encourage her, proclaiming he wants to take her place to trick death. Judith recovers, but Hamnet falls gravely ill and dies; on his deathbed, he [envisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-death_experience) himself on a stage calling for his mother and makes a wish to the hawk. William rushes home and is distraught to find Hamnet dead, and his absence strains his marriage to Agnes as they cope with Hamnet's death. William buys the [largest house in Stratford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Place) and departs for London again. Agnes holds his hand and says she now sees nothing. William rehearses *[Hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet)* in London, but is frustrated with his cast's flat delivery. In despair, he leans over the edge of a jetty on the [River Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Thames) and recites his "[To be, or not to be](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_be,_or_not_to_be)" monologue from the play.

Agnes's stepmother Joan shows her a [playbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programme_\(booklet\)) for a production of *Hamlet* in London and upbraids her for marrying William, but Agnes rebukes her. Agnes and Bartholomew travel to London to see William. Finding him absent from home, they resolve to attend the first performance of *Hamlet* at the [Globe Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe_Theatre). Agnes is initially offended, thinking her son's name is being profaned. However, upon seeing William as the [ghost of Hamlet's father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_\(Hamlet\)), she realizes the play is a tribute to Hamnet, and is moved to tears by the scene between [Hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Hamlet) and his father. Backstage, William, having noticed Agnes, breaks down crying while listening to the play, and returns to see Agnes from the wings. The play progresses as Hamlet engages [Laertes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laertes_\(Hamlet\)) in final combat, fulfilling Hamnet's dream for the role. As Hamlet dies, Agnes reaches for the actor's hand as she had held William's when they first met, and the rest of the audience reaches toward him in turn. She envisions Hamnet on the stage, as seen earlier in his dying vision. He moves from sadness to a smile before disappearing backstage through a hole resembling the forest cave. For the first time since Hamnet's death, Agnes laughs and smiles.

## Production

See also: [ *Hamnet* (soundtrack)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamnet_\(soundtrack\))

A stage production of [Maggie O'Farrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggie_O'Farrell)'s novel was announced in November 2022,[^cite_note-10] with the film rights having been acquired prior to publication by London-based [Liza Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liza_Marshall) and her company Hera Pictures, who then partnered with [Neal Street Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neal_Street_Productions).[^cite_note-11] In April 2023, [Chloé Zhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlo%C3%A9_Zhao) was hired to direct the film, and would write the screenplay alongside O'Farrell.[^cite_note-12]

In May 2023, [Paul Mescal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Mescal) and [Jessie Buckley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessie_Buckley) entered negotiations to star in the film.[^cite_note-13] Mescal confirmed in a January 2024 interview that he and Buckley would star.[^cite_note-14]

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography) was originally scheduled to begin in [London](17867.md) on 3 June 2024.[^cite_note-15] Production instead began in [Wales](69894.md) on 29 July 2024, and wrapped on 30 September. While most of the film was shot in [Herefordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herefordshire), England, including the village of [Weobley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weobley), scenes were also filmed in London at [the Charterhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Charterhouse), which served as the largest London location for the production.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] The scenes set at the [Globe Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe_Theatre) were shot at a replica of the building constructed by production designer [Fiona Crombie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiona_Crombie) at [Elstree Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elstree_Studios_\(Shenley_Road\)) backlot, rather than at [Shakespeare's Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare's_Globe) (which Zhao and Crombie felt was too ornate for the film).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-Desowitz-20] [Joe Alwyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Alwyn) and [Emily Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Watson) were added to the cast in August, and [Steven Spielberg](26940.md) joined the film as a producer.[^cite_note-FocusFeatures-21] [Łukasz Żal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81ukasz_%C5%BBal) was the cinematographer[^cite_note-22] and [Max Richter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Richter) the film's composer,[^cite_note-23] whose 2004 track "[On the Nature of Daylight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Nature_of_Daylight)" is also used in the film.[^cite_note-24]

Music was integral to the production from start to finish with Richter having composed 30 minutes of original music to be used during filming. This music was used by Zhao on set to establish the tone of the film for the actors and crew before Richter worked on the official composition in post-production. [^cite_note-25] Jessie Buckley shared Richter's 2004 track "On the Nature of Daylight" with Zhao during filming, which inspired Zhao to reframe the original ending. "The director played it on set, on a loop over and over, throughout the three days they shot the final sequence of the film, building it into the foundation of Agnes’s reach through time and the veil of death".[^cite_note-26] By the time Richter shared his original composition for the ending with Zhao, she had already committed to "On the Nature of Daylight" for the ending, with the original composition now being used during the credits.

## Literary references

In addition to adapting O'Farrell's book, the film repeatedly quotes from the Old English [Nine Herbs Charm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Herbs_Charm), an alliterative spell (*[galdor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galdr)*) from [Anglo-Saxon England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_England). The film quotes from two translations of the text: one from [philologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philology) Joseph S. Hopkins and another from Stephen Pollington. Regarding the use of his translation in the film, Hopkins says "It is a great joy to play a role in presenting the Nine Plants Spell to such a large audience in the contemporary period, surely providing the most exposure the spell has received since Anglo-Saxon England".[^cite_note-nineherbs-27]

## Release

[Focus Features](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focus_Features) acquired worldwide rights to *Hamnet* in August 2024, with its parent company [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures) handling its international distribution; Indian distribution rights were acquired by [Reliance Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliance_Entertainment) in December 2025 under a pre-existing output deal with [Amblin Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amblin_Entertainment).[^cite_note-FocusFeatures-21][^cite_note-28] It had its world premiere at the [52nd Telluride Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/52nd_Telluride_Film_Festival) on 29 August 2025.[^cite_note-29] In July 2025, the film was announced as part of the Gala Presentations lineup of the 50th [2025 Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Toronto_International_Film_Festival),[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-TIFF-31] where it won the prestigious [People's Choice Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_International_Film_Festival_People's_Choice_Award).[^cite_note-32] It was screened in the non-competitive section 'Grand public' of the [20th Rome Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Rome_Film_Festival) in October 2025 before its theatrical release,[^cite_note-33] in the official selection of the [70th Valladolid International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/70th_Valladolid_International_Film_Festival) on 27 October 2025 (for its Spanish premiere),[^cite_note-34] and closed the [38th Tokyo International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/38th_Tokyo_International_Film_Festival) on 5 November 2025.[^cite_note-gen\:variety-35]

The film received a [limited theatrical release](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_theatrical_release) in the United States on 26 November 2025, ahead of a wide release one week later on 5 December 2025.[^cite_note-36] It would later be released in the United Kingdom on 9 January 2026, and in Australia on 15 January.[^cite_note-TheGuardian-37] As of January 24, 2026, screening expanded to a total of 1,276 theatres and ranked #10 at the box office with \$1.8 million for a domestic total approaching \$18 million.[^cite_note-38]

### Home media

*Hamnet* was released for [streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_media), and was then made available on [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD), [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray) and [Ultra HD Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_HD_Blu-ray) for home viewing on March 3, 2026.[^cite_note-39]

## Reception

### Critical response

On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator) website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes), 86% of 350 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 8.3/10. The website's consensus reads: "Breaking hearts and mending them in one fell swoop, *Hamnet* speculates on the [inspiration behind Shakespeare's masterpiece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sources_of_Hamlet) with palpable emotional force thanks to Jessie Buckley and Paul Mescal's astonishing performances."[^cite_note-Rotten_Tomatoes-40] [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic), which uses a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean), assigned the film a score of 84 out of 100, based on 54 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".[^cite_note-Metacritic-41]



Buckley's performance as Agnes received widespread acclaim from critics, which earned her the [Academy Award for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress).

Buckley's performance in particular was widely praised. David Fear of *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* opined that people "will be talking about Jessie Buckley's performance for years".[^cite_note-42] *[Screen Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_International)*'s Tim Grierson thought Mescal's role was similar to his previous work but "the regularly superb Buckley is revelatory as a wild creature who experiences the exhilaration of motherhood as well as the heartbreak of loss."[^cite_note-43] Johnny Oleksinski at the *[New York Post](102227.md)* wrote, "It's Buckley who's giving one of those rare turns that simply beggars belief. She swings back and forth from cast iron to porcelain. The actress is thunderous, playful, grounded and ethereal."[^cite_note-44] [Peter Debruge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Debruge?action=edit\&redlink=1) of *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))* declared the film to be "so emotionally raw as to be almost excruciating at times" "featuring a heroic performance from Jessie Buckley".[^cite_note-45]

[Bilge Ebiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilge_Ebiri) of *[Vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulture_\(magazine\))* described *Hamnet* as "devastating, maybe the most emotionally shattering movie I've seen in years".[^cite_note-46] Angie Han of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* summed the film in the bottom line as "a tremendously acted heartbreaker".[^cite_note-47] Pete Hammond of *[Deadline Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood)* wrote that *Hamnet*, "with its quiet determination to say much about how art is affected by life, is unlike anything else".[^cite_note-48] [David Ehrlich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ehrlich) of *[IndieWire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndieWire)* affirms that with *Hamnet,* "It would be hard to imagine a more fitting tribute to Shakespeare's most widely interpreted play." On the performances, Ehrlich notes that the character of Agnes is not built on tropes but is "anchored by the primordial rawness of Buckley's astonishing performance." He found Mescal's performance to be "cathartically transcendent, because it at last rewards that search... as Will starts looking for his son in the space between life and death."[^cite_note-49]

Richard Lawson in *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* gave it four stars, calling it a "poignant adaptation of Maggie O'Farrell's 2020 novel with a stirring tearjerker ending".[^cite_note-TheGuardian-37] [BBC](19344654.md) film critics Nicholas Barber and Caryn James deemed the movie to be the best of 2025 thanks to its rich and emotionally touching characters, its themes and its imagery.[^cite_note-50] On the contrary, *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)*'s [Kyle Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Smith_\(critic\)) called it a "quintessential [Oscar bait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_bait) (highbrow foundation; maximal crying and emoting) but is dogged by intellectual [anachronism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anachronism)."[^cite_note-51]
