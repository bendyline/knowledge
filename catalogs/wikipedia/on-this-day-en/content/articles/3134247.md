**Stutterheim** is a town with a population of 46,730 in [South Africa](17416221.md), situated in the Border region of the [Eastern Cape](313645.md) province. It is named after [Richard von Stutterheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_von_Stutterheim).

## History

The area's earliest human population were [Bushmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_people). [Khoikhoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoikhoi) groups roamed the area with their cattle before [Xhosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_people) groups moved in during the mid-17th century CE. Missionaries arrived in the area in the 1830s from the [Berlin Missionary Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Missionary_Society), followed by German settlers from the 1850s, and further waves of English colonists later on.

The town was originally named for Baron [Richard Carl Gustav Ludwig Wilhelm Julius von Stutterheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Carl_Gustav_Ludwig_Wilhelm_Julius_von_Stutterheim), who became a major-general in the British Army as the head of the [British German Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_German_Legion) and spent eight months in South Africa before returning to Germany. It was later renamed Dohne after the first missionary in the area, [Jacob Ludwig Döhne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Ludwig_D%C3%B6hne), but in 1857 it was reverted to its previous name, with the name Döhne referring only to a [small station nearby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%B6hne).[^cite_note-baron-2] The [Cape Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Colony) received a degree of independence in 1872 when [Responsible Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Responsible_Government) was declared and, in 1877, the government of Prime Minister [John Molteno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Molteno) began construction of the railway line connecting Stutterheim to [East London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_London,_Eastern_Cape) on the coast. This was officially opened in August 1878, and led to significant growth and economic development in and around the town.[^cite_note-3]

On 20 May 1879 Stutterheim was proclaimed a municipality. Today Stutterheim is part of a much larger municipality named [Amahlathi Local Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amahlathi_Local_Municipality).[^cite_note-4]

## Economy and tourism

Due to its fair annual rainfall, Stutterheim is a flourishing forestry centre which includes large pine, gum and wattle plantations that are established on the slopes of the Kologha Mountains. This scenic town is situated about 70 km from [East London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_London,_South_Africa) on the [N6 highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N6_road_\(South_Africa\)) to the north. It is the home of many picturesque hiking trails, as well as mountain streams. Nearby Gubu Dam is well-stocked with trout. A number of cattle and sheep stud farms are also to be found in Stutterheim. Wool, beef, dairy and poultry are other central products which the area yields.
