**Tamás Sulyok**[^cite_note-4] (born 24 March 1956) is a Hungarian politician and lawyer who served as the [president of Hungary](380393.md) from 2024 to 2026 and the [president of the Constitutional Court of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Hungary) from 2016 until 2024.&#x20;

Sulyok was [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Hungarian_presidential_election) in a snap vote after his predecessor [Katalin Novák](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katalin_Nov%C3%A1k) was implicated in a [pedophile pardon scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katalin_Nov%C3%A1k_presidential_pardon_scandal) and resigned from office. Although officially a [political independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_independent), Sulyok was closely associated with [Viktor Orbán](750582.md)'s [Fidesz](404647.md) party while in office, after being nominated and elected by the then-ruling [Fidesz–KDNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidesz%E2%80%93KDNP) parliamentary majority.[^cite_note-5] Consequently, in 2026, after the [Tisza Party](76606756.md) [won a parliamentary supermajority](79337461.md) on a platform pledging the dismissal of Sulyok and other [Orbán-era](81566732.md) appointees, the [National Assembly](585416.md) passed a [constitutional amendment](5602839.md) ending Sulyok's term and triggering [a new presidential election](83724643.md).[^cite_note-6]

## Family background

Tamás Sulyok was born in [Kiskunfélegyháza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiskunf%C3%A9legyh%C3%A1za) on 24 March 1956 as the fourth and youngest child of lawyer László Sulyok (1909–1988) and economics teacher Márta Boleszny. He has two elder brothers, who are also lawyers, and a sister, who became a food engineer. László Sulyok practiced as a lawyer in [Székesfehérvár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sz%C3%A9kesfeh%C3%A9rv%C3%A1r) since 1937. He married Márta Boleszny in 1941. Tamás' paternal uncle was [Cistercian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistercians) friar and librarian Ignác János Sulyok (1911–2006), who served as prior of [Zirc Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zirc_Abbey) from 1991 to 2004. His paternal grandparents were city police commissioner János Sulyok (1871–1963) and Anna Knittelhoffer (1886–1974), who married in 1904. His maternal grandparents were secondary school principal Antal Boleszny and school teacher Róza Kuntz.[^cite_note-father-7]

Tamás Sulyok stated that his great-uncle was [Dezső Sulyok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dezs%C5%91_Sulyok?action=edit\&redlink=1), a prominent post-war politician, however, this is not supported by publicly available documents.[^cite_note-8]

During a 2023 interview, Tamás Sulyok claimed that his father was sentenced to death [in absentia](582826.md) by the People's Tribunal after [World War II](32927.md), because, as a divorce lawyer, he took on the case of a woman whose ex-husband later became the [Communist party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Communist_Party) secretary of the city. His father hid in Eastern Hungary for a decade to avoid execution.[^cite_note-9] However, researches of historians [László Karsai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_Karsai?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Krisztián Ungváry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kriszti%C3%A1n_Ungv%C3%A1ry) and Anna Gergely, which published shortly after Sulyok's taking office, question the veracity of this statement. Accordingly, László Sulyok was a member of the pro-[Nazi](21212.md) and [anti-Semitic](1078.md) [Hungarian National Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_National_Socialist_Party) led by [Fidél Pálffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fid%C3%A9l_P%C3%A1lffy) and [László Baky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_Baky), who were convicted of war crimes and executed after WW2. Sulyok served as head of the party's [Fejér County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fej%C3%A9r_County_\(former\)) branch during the war years.[^cite_note-10] Anna Gergely presented four documents which prove that, in the summer of 1944, László Sulyok and his associates, in an official letter bearing their own signature, requested from Lajos Kerekes, the mayor of Székesfehévár the allocation of the apartments of the [deported Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust_in_Hungary) to their party organization, as well as the provision of "Jewish furniture" According to the documents, their request was partially granted.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] There is no trace either that László Sulyok was ever sentenced to death.[^cite_note-father-7]

Tamás Sulyok initially denied the charges stating that his late father "was a socially sensitive, patriotic, [philo-Semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosemitism) man".[^cite_note-13] He explained his previous statements with the so-called family legendarium.[^cite_note-14] Remembering the 80th anniversary of the Holocaust in April 2024, he has already said that he was shaken by what has been revealed to him in public in recent weeks about the past of his father, who died almost 40 years ago. "I now bow my head before all the victims of the Holocaust, taking on the past, my own, personal past," he said.[^cite_note-15]

## Legal career

Sulyok graduated in law from the Faculty of Political Science and Law of the [József Attila University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Szeged) of [Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szeged) in 1980. He began his career as a court draftsman at the Csongrád County Court. He passed the bar exam in 1982 and worked as a legal advisor until 1991. He worked as lawyer of the Sulyok–Japport law firm from 1991 to 1996, then Sulyok & Ádám law firm since 1997. In this capacity, he served as the legal representative of the Szeged local government office between 1998 and 2002, under mayor [László Bartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_Bartha_\(politician\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) (Fidesz). Simultaneously, he also functioned as the legal representative of the property management company that operated the city's assets. During the [2002 municipal elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Hungarian_local_elections?action=edit\&redlink=1), he was one of the local intellectuals, who supported the independent mayoral candidate [Elemér Balogh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elem%C3%A9r_Balogh?action=edit\&redlink=1), also backed by Fidesz. Although Sulyok was never a party member, he was among the local Fidesz intellectual supporters by that time. The Sulyok & Ádám law firm represented the party or its affiliated companies in several cases in Szeged.[^cite_note-16]

He served as a [honorary consul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_consul) of [Austria](26964606.md) from 2000 to 2014.[^cite_note-17] Sulyok he obtained a European law specialist qualification from the Law Continuing Education Institute of the Faculty of Law of the [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E%C3%B6tv%C3%B6s_Lor%C3%A1nd_University) (ELTE) in 2004. He had been teaching constitutional law as an invited lecturer, holding seminars and optional collegiums at the Faculty of Law of the University of Szeged since September 2005. He obtained a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) degree from the University of Szeged in 2013.[^cite_note-18] His research topics include the constitutional status of the lawyer profession in Hungary, the regulations on the internal market of the [European Union](9317.md) and the connections between legal services.[^cite_note-jogiforum.hu-19]

In May 2024, [Democratic Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Coalition_\(Hungary\)) politician [Klára Dobrev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kl%C3%A1ra_Dobrev) accused Sulyok, presenting authentic documents, of having participated in the transfer of Hungarian farmlands to Austrian entrepreneurs and companies in the early 2000s, circumventing the relevant legal prohibition through [legal loopholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_loophole).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] She handed over the documents supporting her accusations to the prosecutor's office, adding the comment that "\[...] whatever [Chief Prosecutor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Prosecutor_of_Hungary) [Péter Polt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A9ter_Polt) does with the documents, it will not change the fact that Tamás Sulyok committed crimes." Subsequently, in June 2024, the Szeged Regional Investigative Prosecutor's Office rejected Dobrev's complaint for abuse of office, fraud and other crimes.[^cite_note-22]

### Constitutional Court judge

Sulyok was appointed as a judge of the [Constitutional Court of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Hungary) in 2014 and became its head in 2016. During his tenure, he oversaw several controversial rulings such as those involving the rights of teachers to go on strike.[^cite_note-aljazeera-23]

## Business career

Beside his legal career, Sulyok also had commercial interests. Shortly after the ["regime change"](24259849.md) in 1989–1990, he founded his company Selco in Szeged, which later operated in [Budapest](36787.md), [Kecskemét](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kecskem%C3%A9t) and [Siófok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Si%C3%B3fok) too.[^cite_note-24] Selco initially sold cookware sets, combined with demonstration cooking, and then opened stores selling all kinds of products that were considered trendy at the time, from jewelry to videocassettes to quality spirits, while the company also profited from currency trading.[^cite_note-25] Selco was operated as an [MLM system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-level_marketing) and was eventually shut down in 1992 or 1993 after it was discovered that their agents had sold without a license in many cases.[^cite_note-26] Later, in response to criticism, the Constitutional Court commented on Sulyok's interest at the time by stating that "his company did not unlawfully harm anyone."[^cite_note-27]

## Presidency (2024–2026)

In February 2024, he became the nominee of the [Fidesz](404647.md) party for [President](380393.md) following the resignation of [Katalin Novák](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katalin_Nov%C3%A1k) due to uproar over her [pardoning of an associate in a sexual abuse case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katalin_Nov%C3%A1k_presidential_pardon_scandal). His nomination was approved in a two-thirds vote (134 in favor, 5 against and 60 leaving the chamber in protest)[^cite_note-ap-28] by the [National Assembly](585416.md) on 26 February, with support from Fidesz and its coalition partner, the [Christian Democratic People's Party](1811856.md), after which he took his oath of office, although he would not be formally assuming the position until 5 March. Opposition parties criticized his nomination, describing Sulyok as politically inexperienced, and held a rally in [Budapest](36787.md) on 25 February calling for direct presidential elections.[^cite_note-aljazeera-23]



Sulyok (right) with Argentine president [Javier Milei](56599840.md) in March 2026

In his inaugural address, Sulyok expressed his intent to be a follower of the letter of the law who would seek to refrain from engaging in Hungary’s political life. He also denounced the sanctions and procedures initiated by the [European Union](9317.md) against Hungary over concerns in the rule of law and democratic governance, saying that "the correctly defined concept of the rule of law is being lost, transformed from an ideal into an idol in today's Europe as part of a purely utilitarian political approach", and emphasizing that EU member states should retain their legal national sovereignty.[^cite_note-ap-28]

Upon assuming office, his first act as president was to sign a bill approving the [accession of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden%E2%80%93NATO_relations) to the [North Atlantic Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Treaty_Organization) on 5 March.[^cite_note-29]

In December 2025, the Orbán government changed the law to make it more difficult to remove the President of the Republic. Previously, a simple majority could declare the president unable to fulfill his duties, temporarily passing his powers to the [Speaker of Parliament](19626723.md). The new law required a two-thirds majority as well as the approval of the Constitutional Court to suspend the president's powers. This change, requested by Sulyok himself, was widely seen as an attempt to protect Sulyok's position in the event of a victory for the then-opposition [Tisza Party](76606756.md), which was leading independent polls.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

### Dispute with Péter Magyar

In the [2026 Hungarian parliamentary election](79337461.md), the Tisza Party under [Péter Magyar](76453946.md) won a two-thirds [supermajority](638313.md) in Parliament. During the election campaign, Magyar had repeatedly promised to remove Sulyok and other major Orbán-era officials if Tisza won a two-thirds majority, accusing them of aiding the "dismantling the rule of law ‌and democracy." Magyar claimed Sulyok was "unworthy of embodying the unity of the Hungarian nation." In the weeks after winning the election and in his inaugural address as Prime Minister on 9 May 2026, Magyar repeatedly demanded that Sulyok resign by 31 May. He threatened to use his party's parliamentary supermajority to remove the president otherwise.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

On 4 July 2026, Magyar introduced the seventeenth amendment to the [Fundamental Law of Hungary](5602839.md) in the National Assembly, one clause of which would remove Sulyok from office immediately upon the amendment entering into effect.[^cite_note-35] Sulyok and the opposition claimed this proposal violated the principles of democracy and of the [rule of law](25166191.md).[^cite_note-36] Sulyok argued: "removing public office holders in a manner that openly violates the rule of law ... inflicts a deep wound on the constitutional values of democracy".[^cite_note-37]

Magyar argued instead that it was necessary to remove "puppets" of the former government who had "done nothing to oppose the dismantling of the rule of law and democracy" in order to "free our country from the captivity of the political and economic mafia that has ruled for the past 16 years."[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

On 10 July 2026, Fidesz supporters held a rally in [Budapest](36787.md) protesting against efforts by the government to oust Sulyok.[^cite_note-40]

### Removal from office

On 13 July 2026, the National Assembly adopted the [seventeenth amendment](5602839.md#2026%E2%80%93present:_Tisza_government);[^cite_note-41] [Fidesz](404647.md) lawmakers boycotted the vote.[^cite_note-42] Under the Fundamental Law, Sulyok had five days to sign the amendment or refer it to the Constitutional Court, which would have only been able to conduct a procedural review. Magyar said that if Sulyok were to refuse to sign the amendment, this would constitute a violation of the Fundamental Law for which the National Assembly would impeach him.[^cite_note-43] Sulyok signed the amendment on 18 July, ending his term and triggering a [presidential election](83724643.md).[^cite_note-44] His removal from office went into effect on 20 July.[^cite_note-45]
