**Luhansk Oblast** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Луганська область, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Luhanska oblast*; [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Луганская область, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *Luganskaya oblast*), also referred to as **Luhanshchyna** (Луганщина), is the easternmost [oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Ukraine) (province) of [Ukraine](31750.md). Its administrative center is the city of [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk). The oblast was established in 1938 and bore the name *Voroshilovgrad Oblast* until 1958 and again from 1970 to 1991.[^cite_note-11] Before the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), its population was estimated at 2,102,921.[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-9]

Important cities in Luhansk Oblast include [Alchevsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchevsk), [Antratsyt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antratsyt), [Brianka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brianka), [Kadiivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadiivka), [Kirovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirovsk,_Luhansk_Oblast), [Krasnodon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnodon), [Khrustalnyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khrustalnyi), Luhansk, [Lysychansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysychansk), [Pervomaisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervomaisk,_Luhansk_Oblast), [Rovenky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rovenky), [Rubizhne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubizhne), [Sievierodonetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sievierodonetsk) and [Sverdlovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sverdlovsk,_Ukraine). All of the oblast is in the [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas) region.

In 2014, large parts of the oblast, including the capital Luhansk, came under the control of Russian-backed separatists who declared the [Luhansk People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk_People's_Republic), leading to a [war against Ukrainian government forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas_\(2014%E2%80%932022\)). Since the 2022 [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), the oblast has come almost entirely under Russian occupation and has been the scene of [heavy fighting](71944889.md), which continues in some places. In late September 2022, Russia declared the [annexation of the entire oblast, along with three others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_annexation_of_Donetsk,_Kherson,_Luhansk_and_Zaporizhzhia_oblasts), though the annexation remains internationally unrecognized. Russian-backed officials claimed in July 2025 that Russian forces had captured the last Ukrainian-held areas in Luhansk Oblast, bringing the entire region under their control.[^cite_note-12] They made the same claim in April 2026, though Ukraine denied it.[^cite_note-13]

## Geography



A topographic map of the oblast

Luhansk Oblast is in far [eastern Ukraine](13065940.md). Its north–south length is 250 km, and east–west width 190 km. It covers an area of 26,700 km<sup>2</sup>, 4.42% of the total area of Ukraine.

The oblast has the longest segment of Ukraine's international border with Russia among other regions (*see [State Border of Ukraine](31351346.md)*), consisting of 746 km (464 mi). The abutting Russian oblasts are [Belgorod Oblast](478857.md) to the north, [Voronezh Oblast](473401.md) to the northeast, [Rostov Oblast](478744.md) to the east. Abutting Ukrainian oblasts are [Kharkiv Oblast](494901.md) to the west, and [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md) to the south.

The region is located in the valley of the [Siversky Donets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets) river, which flows west to east through the oblast and splits it approximately in half. The southern portion of the region is elevated by the Donetsk Ridge, which is close to the southern border. The highest point is [Mohyla Mechetna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohyla_Mechetna?action=edit\&redlink=1) (367 m (1,204 ft)), the highest point of Donetsk Ridge. The left bank of the Siversky Donets is part of the Starobilsk Plain. To the north this transforms into the [Central Russian Upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Russian_Upland).

The territory was formerly part of the [Wild Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Fields), and former administrative units in the territory of the current oblast included [Sloboda Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloboda_Ukraine), [Slavo-Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavo-Serbia), [Yekaterinoslav Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinoslav_Governorate), [Donets Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets_Governorate) and [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md).

### Soviet Ukraine (1938–1991)

See also: [Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic)



A monument to [Don Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Cossacks) in Luhansk. "To the sons of glory and freedom", 2012

The oblast originated in 1938 as Voroshylovhrad (Russian: Voroshilovgrad) Oblast ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Ворошиловградська область, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Voroshylovhradska oblast*) after the Donetsk Oblast was split between Voroshylovhrad and Stalino (today [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md)) oblasts. Following the [Soviet invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Poland), [Starobilsk](534897.md) was the location of a prisoner of war camp for Poles, who were then massacred in the [Katyn massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyn_massacre) in 1940. After the invasion by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) in 1941, the region came under a German military administration, due to its proximity to frontlines. It was occupied at the end of 1942 as part of [Case Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Blue) German offensive directed towards [Stalingrad](32585.md).

Soon after the [battle of Stalingrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad), the Luhansk (at that time Voroshilovgrad in honor of [Kliment Voroshilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kliment_Voroshilov)) region again became the center of military operations during the Soviet counter-offensive [operation Little Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Little_Saturn) in the spring of 1943. In the summer of 1943, the region was liberated from the Nazi Germany Armed Forces. During the Soviet era, the Oblast bore its current name between 1958 and 1970.

In the December [1991 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Ukrainian_independence_referendum), 83.86% of votes in the oblast were in favor of the [Declaration of Independence of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_of_Ukraine).

### Independent Ukraine (1991–2014)

#### Pro-Russian insurgency (2014–2022)

See also: [Russian-occupied territories of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-occupied_territories_of_Ukraine)

On 8 April 2014, following the [annexation of Crimea by Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), pro-Russian separatists occupying the Luhansk Oblast administrative building planned to declare the independence of the region as the Luhansk Parliamentary Republic, after other pro-Russian separatists declared [Donetsk People's Republic](42425747.md) in the Donetsk Oblast (7 April 2014). When the Luhansk Parliamentary Republic ceased to exist, the separatists declared the [Luhansk People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk_People's_Republic) on 27 April 2014. They held a [disputed referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Donbas_status_referendums) on separating from Ukraine on 11 May 2014. The legitimacy of the referendums was not recognized by any government.[^cite_note-Referendum_on_joining_Russia-14] Ukraine does not recognize the referendum, while the EU and US said the polls were illegal and fraudulent.[^cite_note-15] Subsequently, the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas_\(2014%E2%80%932022\)) began.

As a result of the war in Donbas, Luhansk insurgents control the southern third of the oblast, which includes the city of [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk), the region's most populous city and the capital of the oblast. Due to this, most oblast government functions have moved to [Sievierodonetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sievierodonetsk), which forces of the Government of Ukraine recaptured in July 2014. Many universities located in the occupied areas have moved to government-controlled cities such as Sievierodonetsk, [Starobilsk](534897.md) or [Rubizhne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubizhne).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] A survey conducted in December 2014 by the Kyiv International Institute of Sociology found that 5.7% of the oblast's population supported their region joining Russia, 84.1% did not support the idea, and the rest were undecided or did not respond. Insurgent-controlled areas were not polled.[^cite_note-DT150103-18]

#### Russian occupation (2022–present)

During the 2022 [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), Russian ground forces entered the occupied territory of Luhansk Oblast by crossing the Russian border on 22 February 2022. They invaded government-controlled territory across the line of contact and the Russian border on 24 February. As of 26 May 2022 they had occupied all but 5% of the region.[^cite_note-19]

During the mid-2022 [battle of Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Donbas_\(2022\)), Russian troops attacked and eventually captured the cities of [Sieverodonetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sievierodonetsk) and [Lysychansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysychansk) during May and June 2022 in two of the most significant and most intense battles of the [Eastern Ukraine offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Ukraine_offensive). By 3 July 2022, Russian and allied troops controlled all cities in the oblast. On 4–5 July 2022, during the international [Ukraine Recovery Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_Recovery_Conference) (URC 2022) in [Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugano), Finland, [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden%E2%80%93Ukraine_relations), and the Czech Republic pledged to support the postwar rebuilding of the Luhansk region.[^cite_note-20] On 11 September 2022, there were unconfirmed reports that [Bilohorivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilohorivka,_Luhansk_Oblast) near Lysychansk, was recaptured. On 19 September 2022, [Ukrainian forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Armed_Forces) confirmed this.

In late September 2022, an [annexation referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_annexation_referendums_in_Russian-occupied_Ukraine) was held in Luhansk on joining the Russian Federation, although Ukraine along with the United Nations and most observers declared the referendum to be illegitimate and fraudulent.[^cite_note-21] Following the staged victory in the voting, the region and the so-called Luhansk People's Republic [were absorbed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Southern_and_Eastern_Ukraine) into Russia.[^cite_note-22] The [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) subsequently passed a [resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_ES-11/4) calling on countries not to recognise what it described as an "attempted illegal annexation" and demanded that Russia "immediately, completely and unconditionally withdraw".[^cite_note-23]

As of 5 October 2022, nearly all of the oblast is occupied by Russia, which claims the oblast as the [Luhansk People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk_People's_Republic) (LPR), a self-declared state turned Russian federal subject. The [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas_\(2014%E2%80%932022\)) and the subsequent 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine saw heavy fighting in the oblast, with [Sievierodonetsk captured in June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sievierodonetsk_\(2022\)) by Russian and LPR forces[^cite_note-24] after an assault lasting several weeks,[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] and the oblast's last major settlement under Ukrainian control, Lysychansk, captured by Russian and Russia-backed forces on 2 July.[^cite_note-27] The next day, [Russia's Minister of Defence](7875884.md) announced that the entire territory of the oblast had been "liberated",[^cite_note-28] but three weeks later the governor of the oblast reported heavy fighting was still ongoing.[^cite_note-29] On 4 September, Ukrainian forces launched a [counteroffensive in eastern Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Ukrainian_Kharkiv_counteroffensive) and recaptured small parts of [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md) and, on 1 October, [Lyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Lyman_\(2022\)). Ukrainian forces also pushed through the stalemate at the Luhansk Oblast border and, most notably, recaptured Bilohirivka while engaging LPR forces in Lysychansk. Since then, there has been continued fighting in the western parts of the region in a renewed [Luhansk Oblast campaign](71944889.md).

Like the other provinces of Ukraine, Luhansk Oblast has a double jurisdiction. The oblast is predominantly administrated by the Luhansk Oblast State Administration, headed by the [governor of the oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_local_state_administration), who is appointed by the [President of Ukraine](20688479.md). The province has a representative body, the provincial council, which is headed by its chairman and elected by popular vote.

The province is primarily divided into 18 *[raions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raion)* (districts), and 37 cities, including 14 [cities of regional significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_regional_significance_\(Ukraine\)). The administrative center is [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk). These raions are listed below with their areas and populations.[^cite_note-30]

The province's secondary division consists of various municipalities. Those municipalities may consist of one or more populated places. The municipalities are administratively subordinate to the raion in which they are located, with the exception of 14 cities subordinated directly to the oblast. The city of Luhansk is subdivided into its own four city-districts (boroughs).

All subdivisions are governed by their respective councils (*radas*).

The population is largely Russian-speaking, although ethnic [Ukrainians](145144.md) constitute a majority (58.0%). Among the minorities are native Russians (39.1%), Belarusians (0.8%), and others (1.4%). Ukrainians constitute the majority in all raions except for [Stanytsia-Luhanska Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanytsia-Luhanska_Raion) and [Krasnodon Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnodon_Raion), both of which are east of Luhansk. Ethnic [Russians](59510.md) also constitute the majority in regionally significant cities, such as [Krasnodon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnodon), [Sverdlovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sverdlovsk,_Luhansk_Oblast), [Khrustalnyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khrustalnyi) and [Kadiivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadiivka).

In the 2001 Ukrainian Census, more than 68.8% of the population considered themselves Russian speakers, while 30.0% considered themselves Ukrainian speakers. The Russophone population predominates in the southern portion of the region and around the city of Luhansk, while the northern region is less populated, mostly agricultural and Ukrainophone.

Its population (as of 2004) of 2,461,506 constitutes 5.13% of the overall Ukrainian population. The Luhansk Oblast rates fifth in Ukraine by the number of its inhabitants, having an average population density of 90.28/km<sup>2</sup>. About 87% of the population lives in urban areas, while the remaining 13% reside in agricultural areas.

## Transport

Through the region pass two major European routes  [E50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E50) and  [E40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E40). There are 24 Russo-Ukrainian international [border checkpoints](36430484.md#Luhansk_.E2.80.93_Belgorod) of various entry.

-  [E50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E50) within the Luhansk Oblast uses highway  [M 03](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_M03_\(Ukraine\)) that starts from [Debaltseve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debaltseve) ([Donetsk Oblast](494889.md)), passes through the city of [Khrustalnyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khrustalnyi), and enters the [Russian Federation](25391.md) at the [border checkpoint "Dovzhansky"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dovzhansky_\(border_checkpoint\)) (settlement Dovzhanske, town of Biryukove).

-  [E40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E40) within the Luhansk Oblast uses highway  [M 04](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_M04_\(Ukraine\)) that starts from [Debaltseve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debaltseve) ([Donetsk Oblast](494889.md)), passes through the city of [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk), and enters the [Russian Federation](25391.md) at the [border checkpoint "Izvaryne"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izvaryne-Donetsk) (town of Izvaryne).

- There is also another highway  [H 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_H21_\(Ukraine\)) that runs from north to south and connects [Starobilsk](534897.md), [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk), and [Khrustalnyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khrustalnyi) with [Donetsk](562951.md).

Rail transportation was administered by the Donetsk Railway until the Russian takeover.

There is also its regional airport [Luhansk International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk_International_Airport) with its own carrier.
