The **government of Argentina** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Gobierno de la República Argentina*), within the framework of a [federal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_system), is a [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system) [representative democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic). The [president of Argentina](229473.md) is both [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and [head of government](37570.md). [Executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power) is exercised by the president. [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power) is vested in the [National Congress](1589508.md). The [Judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) is independent from the Executive and from the Legislature, and is vested in the [Supreme Court](2800711.md) and the lower national tribunals. From the own government is also called **Presidency of the Nation** (Spanish: *Presidencia de la Nación*).

## Executive branch

See also: [Ministries of the Argentine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministries_of_the_Argentine_Republic)

The current composition of the executive branch includes solely the [president](229473.md), who is the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and is formally given the power over the administration to follow through with the interests of the nation. The president is also the chief of the [Argentine Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Armed_Forces).

The president and the [vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_vice_presidents_of_Argentina) are elected through [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage) by the nation. The [constitutional reform of 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_amendment_of_the_Argentine_Constitution) introduced a *two-round system*, more popularly known in the country as [ballotage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballotage), by which the winning presidential ticket has to receive either more than 45% of the overall valid votes, or at least 40% of it and a 10% lead over the runner-up. In any other case, the two leading tickets get to face a second round whose victor will be decided by a simple majority. This mechanism was not necessary in the [1995 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Argentine_general_election), when it could have first come into use, nor in the ones in [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Argentine_general_election), [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Argentine_general_election), [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Argentine_general_election), and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Argentine_general_election). However, it was instrumental in the selection of [Néstor Kirchner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A9stor_Kirchner) in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Argentine_general_election), of [Mauricio Macri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Macri) in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Argentine_general_election), and, most recently, of [Javier Milei](56599840.md) in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Argentine_general_election).

The cabinet of ministers is appointed by the president, but is not technically part of the executive power.[^cite_note-1] The vice president belongs to the legislative branch, since they also hold the presidency of the senate.

## Legislative branch



Palace of the Argentine National Congress, seat of the Legislature

The [National Congress](1589508.md) ([Spanish](26825.md): *Congreso Nacional*) constitutes the legislative branch of government. The Congress consists of the [Senate](751152.md) (72 seats), presided by the Vice-President of the Nation, and the [Chamber of Deputies](751124.md) (257 seats), currently presided by [Julián Domínguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juli%C3%A1n_Dom%C3%ADnguez), deputy for [Buenos Aires province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires_province). Senators stay in office for six years, and deputies, for four.

Each of the [Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Argentina) and the [Autonomous City of Buenos Aires](255919.md) elect deputies and senators directly. Deputies are representatives of the whole people of the Nation, while Senators represent their districts. Each district elects a number of deputies roughly proportional to their overall population by [proportional representation](23298.md), and three senators: two for the majority, and one for the first minority. Members of both chambers are allowed indefinite re-elections.

Every two years, each of the 24 electoral districts (the twenty-three Provinces and the Autonomous City of Buenos Aires) elects one half of their lower chamber representatives. Districts with an odd number of Deputies elect one more or one fewer of them on each election. As for the Senators, the twenty-four districts are divided into three groups consisting of eight districts. Every two years all eight districts of one of those groups elect all their three senators, assigning two of them from the party that obtains the majority, and one from the first minority party. Six years later, the same group of districts will hold its next senatorial elections.

## Judiciary branch



Main building of the Argentine Supreme Court

The Judiciary Branch is composed of federal judges and others with different jurisdictions and of a [Supreme Court](2800711.md) with nine members (one President, one Vice President, and seven Ministers), appointed by the President with approval of the Senate, who may be deposed by Congress. As of December 2015 there are six vacancies, which then President Kirchner stated she did not intend to fill.

- President of the Supreme Court: Dr. [Horacio Rosatti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horacio_Rosatti)

- Vice-President of the Supreme Court: Dr. [Carlos Rosenkrantz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Rosenkrantz)

- Minister of the Court: Dr. [Juan Carlos Maqueda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Maqueda)

- Minister of the Court: Dr. [Ricardo Lorenzetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Lorenzetti)

## Provincial and municipal governments

Further information: [Administrative divisions of Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Argentina)

Argentina is divided into 23 districts called [Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Argentina) and one [autonomous district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_city), which hosts the national capital, the Autonomous City of [Buenos Aires](255919.md) (which is [conurbated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Buenos_Aires) into the [province of Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires_Province)). Each of the provinces has its own constitution, laws, authorities, form of government, etc., though these must first and foremost comply with the [national constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Argentina) and laws.

The government of each province has three branches. The Executive, Legislative and Judiciary. The Executive branch is led by a [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Governors_in_Argentina). The Legislative Branch may be organized as a [unicameral](204304.md) or a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism) system, i.e:, with either one or two chambers or houses; currently, eight of the 24 provinces has a bicameral legislature.[^cite_note-2]

Each province, except for [Buenos Aires Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires_Province), is divided into districts called [departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Argentina) (*departamentos*). Departments are merely administrative divisions; they do not have governing structures or authorities of their own. They are in turn divided into [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality) (cities, towns and villages). Each province has its own naming conventions and government systems for different kinds of municipalities. For example, [Córdoba Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba_Province,_Argentina) has *municipios* (cities) and *comunas* (towns); [Santa Fe Province](520560.md) further distinguishes between first- and second- tier *municipios*; [Chaco Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaco_Province) refers to every populated center as *municipios*, in three categories.

The Province of Buenos Aires has a different system. Its territory is divided into 134 districts called *[partidos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partidos_of_Buenos_Aires)*, each of which usually contains several cities and towns.

Regardless of the province, each department/partido has a head town (*cabecera*), often though not necessarily the largest urban center, and in some provinces often named the same as their parent district.

Municipalities are ruled by mayors, usually called [Intendant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intendant#Argentina) (*intendente*) in the case of cities and towns (the larger categories). A city has a legislative body called the Deliberative Council (*Concejo Deliberante*). The smaller towns have simpler systems, often ruled by commissions presided by a [communal president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communal_president?action=edit\&redlink=1) (*presidente communal*) or a similarly named authority.

[Buenos Aires](255919.md) city, seat of the National Government, was declared an [autonomous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_city) by the [1994 constitutional reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_reform_of_the_Argentine_Constitution). Its mayor, formerly chosen by the [President of the Republic](229473.md), is now elected by the people, and receives the title of [Chief of Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_and_chiefs_of_government_of_Buenos_Aires) (*Jefe de Gobierno*). Other than that, Buenos Aires, as the provinces, has its own Legislative Branch (a unicameral Legislature) and elect [deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_of_Argentina) and [senators](751152.md) as representatives to the [National Congress](1589508.md).
