The **Supreme Federal Court** ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Supremo Tribunal Federal*, [\[suˈpɾẽmu tɾibuˈnaw fedeˈɾaw\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese), **STF**) is the [supreme court](4913027.md) (court of last resort) of [Brazil](3383.md), serving primarily as the country's [constitutional court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_court). It is the highest [court of law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_law) in Brazil for constitutional issues and its rulings cannot be appealed. On cases involving exclusively non-constitutional issues, regarding federal laws, the highest court is, by rule, the [Superior Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_Court_of_Justice_\(Brazil\)).

## History

The current court was preceded by the House of Appeals of Brazil (Casa de Suplicação do Brasil), which was inaugurated [during the colonial era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Brazil) on 10 May 1808, the year that the Portuguese royal family (the [House of Braganza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Braganza)) arrived in Rio de Janeiro after [fleeing to Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_the_Portuguese_court_to_Brazil).[^cite_note-\:0-1]<sup>: 256 </sup>

The Brazilian [proclamation of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Brazil) and the adoption of the [Imperial Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Constitution_of_1824) in 1824 preceded the establishment of the Supreme Court of Justice (*Supremo Tribunal de Justiça*) in 1829, which served as the [Brazilian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Brazil)'s supreme court.[^cite_note-\:0-1]<sup>: 256 </sup> With the [fall of the monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_Republic_\(Brazil\)) and Brazil's first [Republican Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Constitution_of_1891), the current court was established.[^cite_note-2]

Although the constitutional norms that regulated the creation of the court allowed [Deodoro da Fonseca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deodoro_da_Fonseca), Brazil's first president, to nominate an entirely new court, the president chose to nominate as the first members of the Supreme Federal Court the ministers who were then serving as members of the imperial court that preceded it.

Two hundred members have served on the court. The Constitution of 1891 provided that the court would have 15 members. When [Getúlio Vargas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get%C3%BAlio_Vargas) came into power, the number of members was reduced to 11. The number was changed to 16 in 1965, but returned to 11 in 1969 and has not changed since. Of all Presidents of Brazil, only [Café Filho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caf%C3%A9_Filho) and [Carlos Luz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Luz) (acting) never nominated a minister.

All judicial and administrative meetings of the STF have been broadcast live on television since 2002. The court is open for the public to watch the meetings.

On 8 January 2023, the building was [attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_invasion_of_the_Brazilian_Congress) by supporters of the former president, [Jair Bolsonaro](46603496.md).[^cite_note-3] On November 13, 2024, [a bomb was set off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Bras%C3%ADlia_attack) outside the court. The perpetrator was the only fatality.[^cite_note-4]

## Functions

Alongside its appeal competence, mostly by the Extraordinary Appeal (*Recurso Extraordinário*), the Court has a small range of cases of [original jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_jurisdiction), including the power of [judicial review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_review), judging the [constitutionality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionality) of laws passed by the [National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_of_Brazil), through a Direct Action of Unconstitutionality (*Ação Direta de Inconstitucionalidade*, or ADI). There are also other mechanisms for reaching the Court directly, such as the Declaratory Action of Constitutionality (*Ação Declaratória de Constitucionalidade*, or ADC) and the Direct Action of Unconstitutionality by Omission (*Ação Direta de Inconstitucionalidade por Omissão* or ADO).

## Case law

In May 2009 *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* called the Supreme Federal Court "the most overburdened court in the world, thanks to a plethora of rights and privileges entrenched in the country's 1988 constitution (...) till recently the tribunal's decisions did not bind lower courts. The result was a court that is overstretched to the point of mutiny. The Supreme Court received 100,781 cases last year."[^cite_note-5]

[Overruling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precedent#Horizontality) seems to be frequent in STF jurisprudence: "three years ago when the STF adopted the understanding that defendants who have a conviction upheld by a single appellate court may be sent to jail to begin serving their sentences. (...) The 2016 decision happened largely due to a change in opinion from Minister [Gilmar Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilmar_Mendes) (...). He had voted against sending defendants to jail after a single failed appeal in 2009, but changed his mind in 2016. Jump to 2019, and the circumstances – both political and judicial – have changed".[^cite_note-6]

## President and Vice President

Main article: [President of the Supreme Federal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Supreme_Federal_Court)

The President of the STF and its Vice President are elected by their peers for a two-year term by [secret ballot](479210.md). The incumbent president is Minister Luís Roberto Barroso.[^cite_note-7]

Reelection for a consecutive term is not allowed. By tradition, the most senior minister who has not yet served in the presidential role is elected as the president by the court members, to avoid politicisation of the court.

If all currently sitting members have already served in the presidential role, the rotation starts all over again. However, due to vacancies caused by the [compulsory retirement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_retirement) age and subsequent appointment of new ministers, it is very rare for the cycle to be ever completed. Some ministers are forced to retire before their turn for the presidency arrives, as was expected to happen with [Teori Zavascki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teori_Zavascki).

According to the same convention, the minister who is next in the line of succession for the presidency will serve as the vice-president for the time being. Also by tradition, the elections of the president and vice-president are never unanimous, there being always one isolated minority vote in each election, as the ministers who are to be elected never cast their votes for themselves; such votes are cast either for the dean of the court— its most senior member — or for some other elder minister that the one to be elected admires and wants to pay homage to.

The chief justice is also the 4th in the [presidential line of succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_presidential_line_of_succession), when the [President of the Republic](273638.md) becomes prevented to be in charge, being preceded by the [Vice President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_Brazil), the [President of the Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Chamber_of_Deputies_\(Brazil\)), and the [President of the Federal Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Federal_Senate_\(Brazil\)), as provided in Article 80 of the [Brazilian Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Constitution).[^cite_note-CF-8]

## Current members

Main article: [List of ministers of the Supreme Federal Court (Brazil)](52333856.md)

The eleven judges of the court are called Ministers (*Ministros*), although have no similarity with the government body of ministers. They are appointed by the [President](273638.md) and approved by the [Federal Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Senate_of_Brazil), with approval requiring an absolute majority of 41 votes. While there is no term length, there is a [mandatory retirement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_retirement) age of 75.[^cite_note-9]
