The **Military Forces of Colombia** (Spanish: *Fuerzas Militares de Colombia*) are the unified [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_forces) of the [Republic of Colombia](5222.md). They consist of the [Colombian Army](2697243.md), the [Colombian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Navy) and the [Colombian Aerospace Force](6719810.md). The [National Police of Colombia](7278792.md), although technically not part of the military, is controlled and administered by the [Ministry of National Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_National_Defense_\(Colombia\)), and national [conscription](5735.md) also includes service in the National Police, thus making it a *[de facto](43594.md)* [gendarmerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie) and a branch of the military. The [president of Colombia](251302.md) is the military's [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief), and helps formulate defence policy through the Ministry of National Defence, which is in charge of day-to-day operations.

The Military Forces of Colombia have their roots in the Army of the Commoners (*Ejército de los Comuneros*), which was formed on 7 August 1819 – before the establishment of the present-day Colombia – to meet the demands of the [Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bol%C3%ADvar's_campaign_to_liberate_New_Granada) against the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire). After their triumph in the war, the Army of the Commoners disbanded, and the [Congress of Angostura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Angostura) created the [Gran Colombian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Colombia) Army to replace it, thus establishing the first military service branch of the country.

The Colombian military was operationally involved in [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia_during_World_War_II) and was the only Latin American country to send troops to the [Korean War](16772.md). Ever since the advent of the [Colombian Conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Conflict), the Colombian military has been involved in combat, pacification, [counter-insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-insurgency), and [drug interdiction](1181646.md) operations all over the country's national territory. Recently it has participated in counter-piracy efforts in the [Horn of Africa](62142.md) under [Operation Ocean Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Ocean_Shield) and [Operation Atalanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Atalanta).

The military of Colombia is the third largest in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md) in terms of active personnel and has the fourth largest expenditure in the [Americas](29833.md), behind the [United States Armed Forces](32212.md), the [Canadian Armed Forces](182792.md) and the [Brazilian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Armed_Forces) respectively.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

## Services

The [Colombian Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Constitution_of_1991) includes two overlapping definitions of what could be defined as 'armed forces' in English:

- The Public Force (*La Fuerza Pública*): Includes the Military Forces proper **and** the National Police (Title VII, chapter VII, Art. 216)

- The Military Forces (*Las Fuerzas Militares*): Includes only the 3 major military service branches: Army, Navy and Aerospace Force (Title VII, chapter VII, Art. 217)

This is a subtle yet important distinction, both in terms of emphasizing the civil nature of the National Police, but also adapting the national police to function as a paramilitary force which can perform military duties as a result of the [Colombian Conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Conflict). This has led to some of the most important police units adopting military training and conducting special operations alongside the Colombian Army, Aerospace Force, and Navy. Therefore, the functions of the Colombian Police in practical terms are similar to those of a gendarmerie, like the [Spanish Civil Guard](325008.md) and the [Carabineros de Chile](5446963.md), which maintain military ranks for all police personnel.

## Funding

In 2000, Colombia assigned 3.9% of its GDP to defense.[^cite_note-6] By 2008 this figure had risen to 4.8%, ranking it 14th in the world.[^cite_note-7] The armed forces number about 250,000 uniformed personnel: 145,000 military and 105,000 police. These figures do not include assistance personnel such as cooks, medics, mechanics, and so on. This makes the Colombian military one of the largest and most well-equipped in Latin America. Many Colombian military personnel have received military training assistance directly in Colombia and also in the United States. The United States has provided equipment and financing to the Colombian military and police through the military assistance program, foreign military sales, and the international narcotics control program, all currently united under the auspices of [Plan Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_Colombia).
