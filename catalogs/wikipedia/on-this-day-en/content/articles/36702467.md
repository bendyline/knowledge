# Laos–South Korea relations

**Laos–South Korea relations** refer to the bilateral relations between [Laos](17752.md) and [South Korea](27019.md). Formal diplomatic relations between the two countries began in 1974. After the [Pathet Lao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathet_Lao) overthrew the [Kingdom of Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Laos) and established the Lao People's Democratic Republic, relations were severed for a time, and ties with South Korea were highly unfriendly. Diplomatic relations were restored in 1995, after which relations have developed positively.[^cite_note-Korea-1] In some cases, countries that have established diplomatic relations with Laos but do not have an embassy there appoint their ambassador in South Korea as a non-resident ambassador to Laos.[^cite_note-2]

## Political exchanges

The [Kingdom of Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Laos) and South Korea established ambassador-level diplomatic relations on 22 June 1974.[^cite_note-Korea-1] In August of the same year, South Korea opened its embassy in Laos, which had been established in 1973.[^cite_note-3] However, on 24 July 1975, South Korea, following an anti-communist policy, severed diplomatic relations with Laos after the [Pathet Lao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathet_Lao) took control. On 2 December 1975, [Souphanouvong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souphanouvong) and others overthrew the Kingdom of Laos during the [Laotian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laotian_Civil_War) and established the communist Lao People's Democratic Republic[^cite_note-4], subsequently strengthening diplomatic ties with [North Korea](21255.md), which shared a similar ideology.[^cite_note-5] During the following decade, relations between South Korea and Laos remained tense and hostile.

The situation began to improve as Laos implemented its comprehensive reform policies, and South Korean President [Roh Tae-woo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roh_Tae-woo) pursued the [Nordpolitik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordpolitik) in 1988 to develop diplomatic relations with socialist countries. Subsequently, both sides initiated unofficial academic and sports exchanges. Laos also sent a delegation to participate in the [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Summer_Olympics) held in [Seoul](19159283.md) that year.[^cite_note-6] Diplomatic relations were formally restored on 25 October 1995, and the South Korean government reopened its embassy in [Vientiane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vientiane) the following year.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

Since the restoration of diplomatic relations, bilateral ties have steadily strengthened. Laotian President [Choummaly Sayasone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choummaly_Sayasone), Prime Ministers [Thongsing Thammavong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thongsing_Thammavong) and [Thongloun Sisoulith](5489678.md), and National Assembly President [Pany Yathotou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pany_Yathotou) have all visited South Korea.[^cite_note-9] South Korean Presidents [Park Geun-hye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Geun-hye) and [Moon Jae-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Jae-in) have also visited Laos during their respective terms.[^cite_note-Korea-1]
