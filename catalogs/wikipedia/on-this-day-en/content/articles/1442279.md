Parliamentary elections to the Assembly of Kosovo ([Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_language): *Kuvendi i Kosovës*, [Serbian Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Cyrillic_alphabet): Скупштина Косова, [transliterated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transliteration) *Skupstina Kosova*) have been held eight times since 2001, with the latest in February 2021.[^cite_note-1] The Assembly was an institution within the [Provisional Institutions of Self-Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Institutions_of_Self-Government) (PISG) established by the [United Nations Interim Administration Mission in Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Interim_Administration_Mission_in_Kosovo) (UNMIK) to provide 'provisional, democratic self-government' in advance of a decision on the [final status of Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_status_process). [Kosovo](17391.md), formerly a province of [Serbia](29265.md), came under [UN](31769.md) administration in 1999 and unilaterally declared its independence in February 2008. The Assembly elected in 2007 continued in office after the declaration of independence.

Under Kosovo's constitutional framework, which established the PISG, elections were to be held every three years for the Assembly. The Assembly then in turn elects a president and prime minister. The Assembly had 120 members elected for a three-year term: 100 members elected by [proportional representation](23298.md), and 20 members representing national minorities (10 Serbian, four Roma, Ashkali and Egyptian, three Bosniak, two Turkish and one Gorani). Under the new constitution of 2008, the guaranteed seats for Serbs and other minorities remains the same, but in addition they may gain extra seats according to their share of the vote. Kosovo has a [multi-party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party) system, with numerous [parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_parties) and the system of proportional representation and guaranteed minority representation means that no one party is likely to have a parliamentary majority. In addition, a minimum number of ministers were and remain reserved for Serbs and other minorities. The [voting age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_age) in Kosovo is 18.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

## Parliamentary elections (latest)

### 2025

See also: [December 2025 Kosovan parliamentary election](81646979.md)

[Albin Kurti](4774301.md)'s [Vetëvendosje](3225595.md) party received 51% of the vote, translating to 57 seats. While short of the 61 seats needed to form a government, Vetëvendosje gathered support from non-Serb minority representatives as it did after the 2021 elections. The [Democratic Party of Kosovo](1260606.md) came second with 20%, maintaining a similar level of support from the last election. The [Democratic League of Kosovo](1256321.md) came in third with 13%, dropping in support from 18% earlier the same year.[^cite_note-Zeqiri-4][^cite_note-5] Turnout was estimated at 47%.[^cite_note-6] With the fourth successive victory for Vetëvendosje in parliamentary elections, Kurti was sworn in for a third term as Prime Minister on 11 February 2026.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## Community Assembly of Kosovo

Main article: [Community Assembly of Kosovo and Metohija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Assembly_of_Kosovo_and_Metohija)

According to UNMIK practice, Serbian national referendums and elections for Parliament and President were allowed in Kosovo, but local elections were organized separately by UNMIK and the PISG. In spite of this, Serbia carried out local elections in Kosovo in 2008; these were not recognized by UNMIK.

The Community Assembly of Kosovo and Metohija is a local government created by the Serbian minority in the Kosovo city of [Mitrovica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovska_Mitrovica) in response to the [2008 Kosovo declaration of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Kosovo_declaration_of_independence).[^cite_note-formation-10] The first elections for the Assembly took place on May 11, 2008[^cite_note-launch-11] to coincide with the [2008 Serbian local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Serbian_local_elections).

In the [Brussels agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels_Agreement_\(2013\)), the government of Serbia agreed to integrate Kosovo Serb political structures into the government of Kosovo.
