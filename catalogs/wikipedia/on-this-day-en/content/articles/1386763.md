East façade of the [Caserne de la Cité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caserne_de_la_Cit%C3%A9), seat of the Police Prefecture viewed from [Notre-Dame de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre-Dame_de_Paris), with the [Hôtel-Dieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B4tel-Dieu,_Paris) hospital on the right

The **Paris Police Prefecture** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *préfecture de police de Paris* [\[pʁefɛktyʁ də pɔlis də paʁi\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)), officially the **Police Prefecture** (*préfecture de police*), is the unit of the French [Ministry of the Interior](871703.md) that provides [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police), [emergency services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_services), and various administrative services to the population of the city of [Paris](22989.md) and the surrounding three suburban *[départements](38523.md)* of [Hauts-de-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauts-de-Seine), [Seine-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Saint-Denis), and [Val-de-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val-de-Marne). It is headed by the **Paris Prefect of Police** (*le Préfet de police de Paris*), officially called the **Prefect of Police** (*le Préfet de police*), and since 1871 has been headquartered in the [Caserne de la Cité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caserne_de_la_Cit%C3%A9), a prominent building erected in 1863-1867.&#x20;

The Paris Police Prefecture supervises the Paris Police force, the [Paris Fire Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Fire_Brigade), and various administrative departments in charge of issuing ID cards and driver licenses or monitoring alien residents. The Prefecture of Police also has security duties in the wider [Île-de-France](1716427.md) *[région](58846.md)* as the *Préfet de Police* is also *Préfet de Zone de Défense* (Prefect for the Defense zone).[^cite_note-1] Since 2017, it has acquired direct responsibility for the three main airports of the Paris area ([Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle_Airport), [Orly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orly_Airport) and [Le Bourget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris%E2%80%93Le_Bourget_Airport)).

In addition to the *Préfecture de Police*, the French government created the [Paris Municipal Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Municipal_Police?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *[Police municipale de Paris](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police%20municipale%20de%20Paris)*) in 2021. In contrast with the Préfecture, the [municipal police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_Police_\(France\)) report to the city government, rather than to the national government. Municipal police officers began patrolling city streets on foot, bicycle, and by car starting on October 18, 2021. The goal of the municipal police is to "make neighbourhoods safer and more peaceful and ensure that public space is shared," for example by enforcing laws on parking, littering, breaking up quarrels, and assisting homeless or elderly residents.[^cite_note-2]

As it is the capital of [France](5843419.md), with government assemblies and offices and foreign embassies, Paris poses special issues of security and public order. Consequently, the national government has been responsible for providing law enforcement and emergency services since the creation of the Lieutenancy General of Police (*lieutenance générale de police*) by [Louis XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV) on March 15, 1667. Disbanded at the start of the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) in 1789, it was replaced by the current Prefecture of Police created by [Napoléon I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napol%C3%A9on_I) on February 17, 1800. This means that, up until 2021, Paris did not have its own *police municipale* and that the [Police Nationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_Nationale) provided all of these services directly as a subdivision of France's [Ministry of the Interior](871703.md).

Policemen assigned to "*la PP*" are part of the [Police nationale](614023.md) but the Police Prefect reports directly to the [Interior Minister](871703.md), not to the director of the Police nationale (*Directeur général de la Police nationale* or DGPN). In Parisian slang, the police were sometimes known as "the archers", a very old slang term in reference to the archers of the long-defunct [Royal Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Watch).

Paris also has the "[Direction de la Prévention, de la Sécurité et de la Protection](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direction%20de%20la%20pr%C3%A9vention,%20de%20la%20s%C3%A9curit%C3%A9%20et%20de%20la%20protection)" (DPSP) (Prevention, Security and Protection Directorate) which is composed of Agents with [municipal police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_Police_\(France\)) powers[^cite_note-3] titled inspecteurs de sécurité (Security Inspectors).[^cite_note-4] The DPSP reports to the [Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_\(France\)) of Paris.

## Jurisdiction

The jurisdiction of the Prefecture of Police was initially the [Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine_\(department\)) *[département](38523.md)*. Its jurisdiction also included the *[communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commune_in_France)* (municipalities) of [Saint-Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Cloud), [Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A8vres), [Meudon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meudon), and [Enghien-les-Bains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enghien-les-Bains), which were located in the [Seine-et-Oise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-et-Oise) *[département](38523.md)*. These four communes were added in the 19th century to the jurisdiction of the Prefecture of Police in order to ensure special protection of the imperial/royal residences located there.

The Seine *département* was disbanded in 1968 and the jurisdiction of the Prefecture of Police is now the city of Paris (which is both a commune and a *département*) and the three surrounding *départements* of [Hauts-de-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauts-de-Seine), [Seine-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Saint-Denis), [Val-de-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val-de-Marne). This territory made up of four *départements* is larger than the pre-1968 Seine *département*.

The Prefecture of Police also has limited jurisdiction over the whole [Île-de-France](1716427.md) *[région](58846.md)* for the coordination of law enforcement, including combatting [cybercrime](341598.md). The Prefect of Police, acting as Prefect of the Defense Zone of Paris (*Préfet de la Zone de Défense de Paris*), is in charge of planning non-military defense measures to keep public order, guarantee the security of public services, and organize rescue operations (in case of natural disaster) for the whole Île-de-France *région* (which is made up of eight *départements*, the four inner ones being the regular jurisdiction of the Prefecture of Police, and the four outer ones being outside of its regular jurisdiction). As such, he coordinates the work of the departmental *[préfets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pr%C3%A9fet)* of Île-de-France.

## Nomination and missions

Headed by a [prefect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefect) titled The "Prefect of Police", who (as are all prefects) is named by the [President](24899.md) in the Council of Ministers, and operates under the Minister of the Interior, commands the [Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefecture) which is responsible for the following:

- security of Paris, if necessary in collaboration with the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_France);

- issuing identification cards, driver's licenses, passports, residential and work permits for foreigners;

- motor vehicle registration and traffic control;

- registration of [associations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voluntary_association), and their creation, status modification and dissolution;

- protection of the environment, general salubrity;

- determining the dates of discount sales in large stores which can be held only twice a year;

- issuing permits to bakeries/boulangeries for their summer vacation to assure that all the bakeries in a given neighborhood are not closed at the same time;

- management of police and firefighters.

The Prefect of Police can issue *arrêtés* (local [writs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writs)) defining rules pertaining to his field of competency. For instance, the rules of operation and security of Paris public parks are issued as joint arrêtés from the [Mayor of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Paris) and the Prefect of Police.

Until 1977, Paris had indeed no elected mayor and the police was essentially in the hands of the *préfet de police*. However, the powers of the mayor of Paris were increased at the expense of those of the *Préfet de Police* in 2002, notably for traffic and parking decisions (the *préfet* retains the responsibility on main thoroughfares such as the [Avenue des Champs-Élysées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champs-%C3%89lys%C3%A9es), and on any street during the organization of [demonstrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonstration_\(people\))).

There is also a prefect of [Paris](22989.md), prefect for the [Île-de-France](1716427.md) region, whose services handle some tasks not devoted to the Police Prefect, such as certain classes of building permits.

## Organization



[Peugeot 307](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot_307) of the Paris Police Prefecture.



Police boat

The PP is headed by a politically appointed prefect who is assisted by the *prevote*, who is the senior police officer of the force. The Prefecture of Police is divided into three sub-prefectures headed by prefects due to their importance.

Because the Police Prefecture provides some services that are normally provided by city governments, its funding partially comes from the City of Paris and other city governments within its jurisdiction.

In addition to forces from the National Police, the Police Prefecture has [traffic wardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_warden) or [crossing guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossing_guard) who enforce parking rules; it has recently added some wardens that direct traffic at crossroads and other similar duties, known as *circulation*, with specific [uniforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform).

## Prefect and Director of the Cabinet

Consists of the Cabinet (staff) itself, the [Gendarmerie Nationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Gendarmerie) Liaison Office, and 6 Local Directorates:

- Public Security – uniformed police officers&#x20;

  - Lost and Found Property

  - Central Accident Service

- Public Order and Traffic Control – uniformed police who protect public buildings, provide crowd and traffic control services

- Judicial Police (*Police judiciaire*) – detectives and investigators (the *[36 quai des Orfèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direction_R%C3%A9gionale_de_Police_Judiciaire_de_Paris)*)

- General Information – records

- Inspectorate – internal affairs

- [Paris Fire Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Fire_Brigade) – the military unit which provides all fire and emergency ambulance services (other emergency medical services are provided by [SAMU](9581361.md)/[SMUR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMUR))

and other agencies:

- Classified Facility Inspectorate

- Psychiatric Infirmary

- Toxicology Laboratory

- Central Laboratory-explosives, pollution, chemical analysis, electrical and fire safety, etc.

## List of lieutenant generals and prefects of police



Plaque honoring [Gabriel Nicolas de la Reynie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Nicolas_de_la_Reynie) flanking the monumental entrance of the Police Prefecture building on rue de la Cité

Before the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), the head of the Paris Police was the *lieutenant général de police*, whose office was created in March 1667 when the first modern police force in the world was set up by the government of King [Louis XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV) to police the city of Paris. The office vanished at the start of the French Revolution and police was vested in the hands of the [Paris Commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Commune_\(French_Revolution\)). Reorganized by [Napoléon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napol%C3%A9on_Bonaparte) in 1800, the Paris Police has been headed by the *préfet de police* since that time.
