The **2026 United States Senate election in Maine** will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect a member of the [United States Senate](24909346.md) to represent the state of [Maine](19977.md). [Republican](32070.md) incumbent [Susan Collins](375195.md) is seeking a sixth term. She is being challenged by [Democratic](5043544.md) former state Senate president [Troy Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_Jackson). The election will be conducted with [ranked-choice voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant-runoff_voting).

In the June 9 primaries, Collins was the only candidate to qualify for the Republican nomination. Sullivan harbor master [Graham Platner](80792208.md) won the Democratic primary over Governor [Janet Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Mills) with the support of progressive leaders, but withdrew from the race on July 10 following sexual assault accusations and other controversies. Jackson was chosen to replace Platner via a [nominating convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_conference#United_States) held on July 25.

As Collins is the only Republican senator representing a state that President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) did not win in any of his three presidential campaigns, the race is considered to be among the most competitive in 2026. Democrats have not won a Senate election in Maine since [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_United_States_Senate_election_in_Maine).[^cite_note-1]

## Background

The election will be held on November 3, 2026,[^cite_note-2] coinciding with [U.S. House](19468510.md) [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Maine), a [gubernatorial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Maine_gubernatorial_election), and other local elections.[^cite_note-3] Both the primary and general elections will be conducted with [ranked-choice voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant-runoff_voting).[^cite_note-4] Incumbent [Republican](32070.md) U.S. senator [Susan Collins](375195.md) is running for re-election to a sixth term.[^cite_note-5] Both primary elections took place on June 9, 2026.[^cite_note-6]

The northernmost state in [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England), [Maine](19977.md) is considered to be a moderately [blue state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_states_and_blue_states). Maine has voted for every Democratic presidential nominee since [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_United_States_presidential_election_in_Maine), and Democratic nominee [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris) defeated Republican nominee [Donald Trump](4848272.md) by approximately seven percentage points in the [2024 United States presidential election in Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_Maine). Democrats also control the [governorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine), the [state legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_Legislature), and both seats in Maine's [U.S. House](19468510.md) [congressional delegation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine's_congressional_delegations).[^cite_note-7] Since 2018, Collins has been the only Republican representing any New England state in either chamber of Congress.[^cite_note-8]

Collins was first elected in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_United_States_Senate_election_in_Maine). She has been re-elected in four subsequent elections, significantly outperforming other Republicans in the state. In [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_Senate_election_in_Maine), despite almost all polls and analysts predicting that she would lose her re-election bid, Collins defeated Democratic nominee [Sara Gideon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Gideon) by a nine-point margin. Democratic presidential nominee [Joe Biden](145422.md) carried Maine by about nine percentage points on the same ballot.[^cite_note-9]

This election is expected to be among the most competitive Senate races in 2026.[^cite_note-10] Collins is the only Republican senator who represents one of the 19 states that [Donald Trump](4848272.md) did not win in any of his three presidential campaigns.[^cite_note-CP1-11] With the decline of [ticket splitting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split-ticket_voting), Collins is widely viewed as the most vulnerable incumbent Republican senator in 2026.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-Newsweek-13] Collins is also the only Republican senator facing re-election in a state that [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris) won in the [2024 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_Maine).[^cite_note-14] Since the [2024 United States Senate elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_Senate_elections), Collins has been the last senator to represent a non-swing state (in other words, a reliably [Democratic or Republican state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_states_and_blue_states)) that typically supports the opposing party.[^cite_note-15]

## Republican primary

Incumbent U.S. senator [Susan Collins](375195.md) officially announced her re-election bid in February 2026.[^cite_note-CollinsInterest-16] Despite Collins receiving backlash from some Republicans for not supporting all of Trump's agenda during his [second term as president](78298108.md), the Republican Party nonetheless supports her re-election campaign due to the fact that she is the only Republican who has been electorally successful in Maine in recent years.[^cite_note-17] However, she faced an unsuccessful primary challenge from former police officer Dan Smeriglio, who argued that she votes with [Senate Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Democratic_Caucus) too often.[^cite_note-Calabrese-18][^cite_note-Smeriglio-19] Ultimately, Collins was the only Republican to qualify for the primary ballot.[^cite_note-20]

## Democratic primary

In April 2025, former congressional staffer Jordan Wood became the first Democrat to enter the race, running on a platform opposed to the [Trump administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Donald_Trump) and [Elon Musk](909036.md).[^cite_note-43]

On August 19, Sullivan harbor master [Graham Platner](80792208.md) launched a [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States) campaign backed by [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders) and organized labor, positioning himself against the "[Democratic establishment](5043544.md)" and drawing support from both liberal voters and some [2024 Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_2024_presidential_campaign) voters.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] In February 2026, *Politico* described Platner as "unlike any other recent popular candidate the state has seen". Noting Platner's large social media following, *Politico* added: "He is brash. He is progressive. He has drawn crowds of hundreds of people, national attention and millions in campaign dollars".[^cite_note-46]

After weeks of speculation and leaked campaign plans,[^cite_note-47] Gov. [Janet Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Mills) entered the race in October 2025.[^cite_note-48] The DSCC's close involvement in her campaign prompted backlash and allegations of favoritism in the primary.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-pt-50] Mills was widely viewed as a top Democratic recruit; she was encouraged to run by [Senate Minority Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_leaders_of_the_United_States_Senate) [Chuck Schumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Schumer), Senator [Kirsten Gillibrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsten_Gillibrand), and the [Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Senatorial_Campaign_Committee) (DSCC),[^cite_note-51] although Sanders publicly discouraged her candidacy.[^cite_note-52]

Following Mills's entry, several candidates, including Dan Kleban and Daira Smith-Rodriguez, withdrew and endorsed Mills.[^cite_note-KlebanWithdrawal-53][^cite_note-RodriguezWithdrawal-54] Smith-Rodriguez cited concerns over past comments by Platner regarding sexual assault in the military.[^cite_note-auto3-55] Wood later withdrew to run for the [U.S. House](19468510.md).[^cite_note-wgme-56]

In January 2026, Platner briefly paused his campaign due to his wife's medical treatment abroad[^cite_note-57] before resuming later that month. He returned to the campaign trail with protests against [Immigrations and Customs Enforcement](216537.md) (ICE) enforcement actions.[^cite_note-58]

In February 2026, Mills committed to multiple debates ahead of the June 9 primary.[^cite_note-59]

Mills, Platner, and [2024 nominee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_Senate_election_in_Maine) David Costello qualified for the primary ballot.[^cite_note-60] In addition, [University of Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maine) adjunct professor Andrea LaFlamme started a write-in bid for the Democratic nomination.[^cite_note-\:1-61]

The Platner/Mills contest was seen as a key contest between the Democratic Party's [centrist](39619035.md) establishment wing and the [populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populism_in_the_United_States) [left-wing](18499.md) and [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States) faction, with Mills representing the former and Platner representing the latter.[^cite_note-62]

In March 2026, Mills began running [attack ads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_ad) highlighting Platner's controversies. By April 2026, multiple polls had been released which showed Mills as much as 38 points behind Platner in the primary.[^cite_note-63] Shortly thereafter, the Mills campaign stopped running the attack ads and had made no additional ad purchases across any platform, leading to rumors that she would withdraw from the race.[^cite_note-\:2-64]

On April 30, Mills announced that she was suspending her campaign, but she did not endorse him. Her withdrawal led to Schumer and Gillibrand announcing they would now help Platner to defeat Susan Collins.[^cite_note-65]

Following the end of Mills' campaign, Platner was the only prominent and competitive Democrat left in the race, making him the most likely Democratic nominee.[^cite_note-66] Mills' name remained on the primary ballot,[^cite_note-67] and Costello also continued his campaign.[^cite_note-68] On June 9, Platner won the primary;[^cite_note-69] he received over 150,000 votes, more than any other Democratic candidate in a primary in Maine's history.[^cite_note-70]

### Platner misconduct controversies

Shortly after Mills entered the race, Platner came under fire for resurfaced controversial [Reddit](3829005.md) posts criticizing police officers, calling rural Americans "stupid" and referring to himself as a "communist", resulting in his campaign's political director, former state representative [Genevieve McDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genevieve_McDonald), quitting the campaign and calling Platner "unelectable"; however, [Democratic National Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_National_Committee) Chair [Ken Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Martin) stated that the comments were not disqualifying,[^cite_note-71] and Platner apologized in a five-minute video posted to social media. On October 21, Platner released a video of himself dancing and singing shirtless at his brother's wedding, revealing he had a chest tattoo that resembled the *[Totenkopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totenkopf)* used by [Nazi Germany](21212.md)'s *[Schutzstaffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzstaffel)*. Platner stated he got the tattoo while inebriated with other [U.S. Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Marines) while stationed in [Croatia](5573.md), mistaking the symbol for a generic [skull and crossbones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skull_and_crossbones). He had the [tattoo covered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover-up_tattoo),[^cite_note-72] and professed his shame and disgust for getting a tattoo that resembled the fascist insignia.[^cite_note-73] Platner also called himself an anti-fascist "[supersoldier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersoldier)" in an old Reddit comment.[^cite_note-74] After the controversies, Democrats such as Senator [Chris Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Murphy) defended Platner and said that he "sounds like a human being" who is honest about his mistakes.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-Popat-77] Senator [Ruben Gallego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruben_Gallego) called his campaign "authentic" and said that he has "the right to grow out of his stupidity" and is not going to be a "crypto-[Fetterman](5303368.md)".[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-Popat-77]

On February 26, 2026, Platner quote-tweeted a clip from Donald Trump's [2026 State of the Union Address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_State_of_the_Union_Address), criticizing Trump's speech. The clip had been posted by anti-Semitic white supremacist [Stew Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stew_Peters).[^cite_note-MediaiteTweet-79] The tweet was deleted hours later, with a spokesperson for Platner's campaign telling *[The Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hill_\(newspaper\))* that they were unaware of Peters's views.[^cite_note-hilltweet-80][^cite_note-portlandtweet-81]

In May 2026, reports surfaced that Platner's wife had alerted his campaign team to explicit messages he was sending to women on the messaging app [Kik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kik_\(app\)), expressing concern that the behavior could hurt him politically. Additional reports during the same period drew attention to Platner's Nazi-linked tattoos, as well as past social media posts widely described as bigoted.[^cite_note-82]

In June 2026, *The New York Times* published an article including allegations of physical abuse and "unsettling" behavior from multiple women.[^cite_note-83] In response, Platner told media that his poor behavior was attributed to PTSD from his service in the Marine Corps. While he specifically denied the accusations made by one woman, he did not dispute the characterization of his behavior toward a second one.[^cite_note-84]

## Platner's withdrawal

On July 6, 2026, a woman Platner had previously dated accused him of [raping](7980471.md) her while he was drunk nearly five years prior.[^cite_note-Politico_SA-239][^cite_note-Newsweek_SA-240][^cite_note-241] Platner denied the accusation, accusing it of being a political attack to derail his campaign.[^cite_note-Politico_SA-239][^cite_note-Newsweek_SA-240] The next day, another former girlfriend alleged that he would [take condoms off during sex without her consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stealthing).[^cite_note-242][^cite_note-243]

Shortly after the rape allegation, US Senator [Ruben Gallego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruben_Gallego) and US Representative [Ro Khanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ro_Khanna) rescinded their endorsements of Platner,[^cite_note-244] and the [Maine Democratic Party](9427377.md) released a statement calling on Platner to suspend his campaign.[^cite_note-245] Platner later stated on the same day that the campaign would "tak\[e] the time to reflect on the best path forward," fueling speculation that he would withdraw from the race.[^cite_note-246] Soon, numerous organizations and US senators, including those who had endorsed Platner, called on him to exit the race.[^cite_note-PoliticoCallsToDropOut-247][^cite_note-248] Senate Democratic Leader [Chuck Schumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Schumer) and [Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Senatorial_Campaign_Committee) chair [Kirsten Gillibrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsten_Gillibrand), who also rescinded their endorsements, stated that the DSCC would not invest in the race as long as Platner remained on the ballot.[^cite_note-PoliticoCallsToDropOut-247][^cite_note-249][^cite_note-KruesiRiccardiAP-250] On July 8, Platner announced that he would drop out of the race,[^cite_note-251] filing his withdrawal two days later.[^cite_note-WithdrawalReceived-87]

## Democratic nominating convention

### Process

No political party in Maine had replaced their nominee in any prior election. While the exact process to be used was not immediately certain, Maine Democratic Party officials and others like [Maine Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_Senate) president [Mattie Daughtry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattie_Daughtry) called for an open and inclusive process.[^cite_note-252] On July 8, the Maine Democratic Party voted to hold a state [party convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_conference#United_States) to nominate a candidate to replace Platner as the Democratic nominee.[^cite_note-ConDetailsBangor-253] The convention was held on July 25 in [Bangor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangor,_Maine).[^cite_note-254] In order to qualify for the convention, replacement candidates had until July 15 to declare their intent to run; they also had until July 21 to collect at least 500 signatures, with at least 50 signatures from eight different counties.[^cite_note-255] The convention consisted of 601 delegates overall, including the party's state committee.[^cite_note-ConDetailsBangor-253][^cite_note-256] 500 of the delegates were elected via county-level [caucuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucus#Caucuses_to_select_election_candidates) held on July 18–19.[^cite_note-slates-257][^cite_note-258] All delegates at the convention were officially unpledged,[^cite_note-259] though candidates published slates of committed supporters before the county meetings.[^cite_note-slates-257]

### Campaign and convention

Leading up to and following Platner's withdrawal, former gubernatorial candidates [Nirav Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirav_D._Shah), [Troy Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_Jackson), and [Shenna Bellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenna_Bellows) were viewed as likely frontrunners for the replacement nomination.[^cite_note-KruesiRiccardiAP-250][^cite_note-260][^cite_note-261] Jackson was the first to declare his candidacy for the Senate nomination,[^cite_note-262] while Shah was the first candidate to receive sufficient signatures to qualify.[^cite_note-263] Following the county meetings, at least 456 of the selected delegates were pledged supporters of Jackson,[^cite_note-267] placing him in the lead for the nomination.[^cite_note-BangorDelegateResults-264][^cite_note-268][^cite_note-Pellish_&_Howard_2026-269] Bellows and Shah, along with candidates Dan Kleban and Jordan Wood, all withdrew from the race on July 19.[^cite_note-270]

Jackson and healthcare executive Saundra Pelletier were the only candidates to qualify for the convention ballot and continue their campaigns after the county caucuses.[^cite_note-2CandidatesWBUR-271] Jackson won the convention vote over Pelletier, 566–5.[^cite_note-ConvResultUsaToday-272]
