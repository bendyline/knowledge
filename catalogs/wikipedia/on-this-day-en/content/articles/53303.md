**Nuuk** (Greenlandic pronunciation: [\[nuːk\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Greenlandic) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/31/Nuuk.ogg/Nuuk.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/31/Nuuk.ogg/Nuuk.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; formerly [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *Godthåb*)[^cite_note-2] is the [capital](181337.md) and [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Greenland) of [Greenland](12118.md), an autonomous territory of [Denmark](11746442.md). It is the seat of the [Sermersooq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sermersooq) municipality and the [government of Greenland](23389805.md) and is the territory's largest cultural and economic center. In January 2025, it had a population of 20,113[^cite_note-bank.stat.gl-3]—more than a third of the territory's population—making it one of the smallest [capital cities in the world by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_capitals_by_population).

&#x20;The city was founded in 1728 by the Danish-Norwegian missionary [Hans Egede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Egede) when he relocated from the earlier Hope Colony (*Haabets Koloni*), where he had arrived in 1721; the governor [Claus Paarss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claus_Paarss) was part of the relocation. The new colony was placed at the [Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit) settlement of Nûk and named *Godthaab* ('good hope'). *Nuuk* is the [Greenlandic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlandic_language) word for '[cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headland)' and is commonly found in Greenlandic place names. It is so named because of its position at the end of the [Nuup Kangerlua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuup_Kangerlua) [fjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjord) on the [Labrador Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador_Sea)'s eastern shore. Its [latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude), at 64°11' N, makes it the world's [northernmost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_northernmost_items) [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_capitals_by_latitude), a few kilometres farther north than [Reykjavík](25798.md). When [home rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_rule) was established in 1979, the authorization of place names was transferred to Greenlandic authorities, who preferred Greenlandic names to Danish ones. The name *Godthåb* mostly went out of use over the next two decades.

## History

### Early settlement

The site has a long history of habitation. The area around Nuuk was first occupied by the ancient, pre-Inuit, [Paleo-Eskimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Eskimo) people of the [Saqqaq culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saqqaq_culture) as far back as 2200 BCE, when they lived in the area around the now abandoned settlement of [Qoornoq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qoornoq).[^cite_note-NT-4] For a long time, it was occupied by the [Dorset culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorset_culture) around the former settlement of [Kangeq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangeq), but they disappeared from the Nuuk district before 1000 AD. The Nuuk area was then inhabited by [Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsemen) settlers from around 1000 until the disappearance of the settlement [for uncertain reasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Greenland#Norse_settlement) during the 15th century.[^cite_note-EB-5]



The statue of [Hans Egede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Egede) in Nuuk

### Founding and early colonial period

The city proper was founded as the fort of **Godt-Haab** in 1728 by the [royal governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Greenland) [Claus Paarss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claus_Paarss), when he relocated the missionary and merchant [Hans Egede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Egede)'s earlier Hope Colony (*Haabets Koloni*) from [Kangeq Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangeq_Island) to the mainland.&#x20;

At that time, Greenland was formally still a [Norwegian colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_possessions_of_Norway#Former_dependencies_and_homelands) under the united [Danish-Norwegian Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_monarchs_of_Norway), but the colony had not had any contact for over three centuries. Paarss's colonists were mutinous soldiers, convicts, and prostitutes; within the first year, most died of scurvy and other ailments.&#x20;

In 1733 and 1734, a [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox) epidemic killed most of the native population as well as Egede's wife.[^cite_note-Wurm-6] Hans Egede returned to Denmark in 1736 after 15 years in Greenland, leaving his son [Poul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Egede) to continue his work.[^cite_note-7]

Godthaab became the seat of government for the Danish colony of [South Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Greenland),[^cite_note-8] while [Godhavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godhavn) (modern Qeqertarsuaq) was the capital of [North Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Greenland) until 1940, when the administration was unified in Godthaab.[^cite_note-Lemkin2008-9]

### Missionary settlements

In 1733, [Moravian missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_mission_in_Greenland) received permission to begin a mission on the island; in 1747, there were enough converts to prompt the construction of the [Moravian Brethren Mission House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_Brethren_Mission_House) and the formal establishment of the mission as **New Herrnhut** ([Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *Nye-Hernhut*).&#x20;

This became the nucleus of present-day Nuuk as many Greenlanders from the southeastern coast left their territory to live at the mission station. From this base, further missions were established at [Lichtenfels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichtenfels,_Greenland) (1748), [Lichtenau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichtenau,_Greenland) (1774), [Friedrichsthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrichsthal,_Greenland) (1824), [Umanak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umanak_\(mission\)) (1861), and [Idlorpait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idlorpait) (1864),[^cite_note-10] before they were discontinued in 1900 and folded into the Lutheran [Church of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Denmark).[^cite_note-11]



Nuuk ([Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *Godthåb*), c. 1878

### Cultural and political developments

In 1853, [Hinrich Johannes Rink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinrich_Johannes_Rink) came to Greenland and was surprised at how local Greenlandic culture and identity had been suppressed under Danish influence.&#x20;

In response, in 1861, he started the *[Atuagagdliutit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atuagagdliutit)*, Greenland's first newspaper, with a native Greenlander as editor. This newspaper based in Nuuk later became an important token of Greenlandic identity.

### World War II and postwar changes

During World War II, there was a reawakening of Greenlandic national identity. The use of written Greenlandic grew, and a council was assembled under [Eske Brun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eske_Brun)'s leadership in Nuuk. In 1940, an American and a Canadian Consulate were established in Nuuk.

Under new regulations in 1950, two councils amalgamated into one. This Countryside Council was abolished on 1 May 1979, when the Greenland Home Rule government renamed the city of Godthåb to Nuuk.&#x20;

The city boomed during the 1950s when Denmark began to modernize Greenland. Nuuk is populated today by both Inuit and Danes, as is Greenland as a whole. Over a third of Greenland's population lives in the Nuuk Greater Metropolitan area.[^cite_note-12]

According to a 2016 article in *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* examining indigenous influences on cities worldwide:[^cite_note-daley16-13]

> One city ... stands out. Nuuk ... has probably the highest percentage of aboriginal people of any city: almost 90% of Greenland's population of 58,000 is Inuit, and at least eight in 10 live in urban settlements. Nuuk also celebrates Inuit culture and history to an extent that is unprecedented in many cities with higher total aboriginal populations. By proportion and by cultural authority and impact, it may well be tiny Nuuk that is the most indigenous city in the world.[^cite_note-daley16-13]

## Geography

Nuuk is located around [64°10′N 51°44′W  /  64.167°N 51.733°W  / 64.167; -51.733](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Nuuk\&params=64_10_N_51_44_W_),[^cite_note-kommune_information-14] at the mouth of the [Nuup Kangerlua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuup_Kangerlua) fjord (formerly Baal's River[^cite_note-15]), 10 km (6.2 mi) from the shores of the [Labrador Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador_Sea) on Greenland's southwestern coast and about 240 km (150 mi) south of the [Arctic Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Circle). The fjord flows to the northwest, then turns southwest at [64°43′N 50°37′W  /  64.717°N 50.617°W  / 64.717; -50.617](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Nuuk\&params=64_43_N_50_37_W_), splitting into three arms in its lower run, with three big islands between the arms: [Sermitsiaq Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sermitsiaq_Island), [Qeqertarsuaq Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qeqertarsuaq_Island_\(Nuuk\)), and [Qoornuup Qeqertarsua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qoornuup_Qeqertarsua).[^cite_note-lp-16] The fjord widens into a bay dotted with [skerries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skerry) near its mouth, opening into Labrador Sea at approximately [64°03′N 51°58′W  /  64.050°N 51.967°W  / 64.050; -51.967](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Nuuk\&params=64_03_N_51_58_W_). [Sermitsiaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sermitsiaq_\(mountain\)) mountain, reaching a height of 1,210 m (3,970 ft), 20 km (12 mi) to the northeast, can be seen from almost everywhere in Nuuk. The nationwide newspaper *[Sermitsiaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sermitsiaq_\(newspaper\))* takes its name from the mountain. Closer to the town are the peaks of [Store Malene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Store_Malene), 790 m (2,590 ft), and Lille Malene, 420 m (1,380 ft).[^cite_note-dsd-17] The [magnetic declination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_declination) at Nuuk is extreme.[^cite_note-magnetic_variation-18][^cite_note-19]

### Climate



Climate chart of Nuuk

Nuuk has a maritime-influenced [tundra climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) [ET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra_climate)) with long, cold, snowy winters and short, cold summers. Although the winters are very cold, they are still milder than those in other tundra climates at similar latitudes, such as [Alaska](624.md) or parts of [Eastern Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Siberia). Instead, peak winter is similar to identical latitudes in the [Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries). On 21 December, the shortest day and longest night of the year, the sun rises at 11\:22 am and sets at 3\:28 pm. On 21 June, the longest day and shortest night of the year, the sun sets at 1\:03 am and rises at 3\:53 am, producing constant [civil twilight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_twilight). Nuuk occasionally has mild temperatures year-round, with each month having recorded 13 °C (55 °F) or warmer, although only June, July, August, and September have recorded what could be considered hot weather (defined as 22.5 °C (72.5 °F) or higher). The monthly averages range from −9 °C (16 °F) to 7 °C (45 °F), whereas all-time extremes range from −32.5 °C (−26.5 °F) on 14 January 1984 to 26.3 °C (79.3 °F) on 6 July 2008. The record wind in Nuuk is 68 km/h.

The average monthly temperature (7.4 °C (45.3 °F) in July) is colder than what is considered the [limit for trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_line) (10 °C (50 °F) during the warmest month). There are a few planted trees,[^cite_note-20] which do not sustain well.

With 19,872 inhabitants as of January 2024,[^cite_note-bank.stat.gl-3] Nuuk is by far Greenland's largest town. Its population has doubled since 1977, increased by over a third since 1990, and risen by almost 21% since 2000. In addition to those born in Greenland, data from 2015 showed 3,826 were born outside the country.[^cite_note-auto-26] Attracted by good employment opportunities with high wages, Danes have continued to settle in the town. Today, Nuuk has the highest proportion of Danes of any town in Greenland.[^cite_note-auto-26] Half of Greenland's immigrants live in Nuuk, which also has a quarter of the country's native population.[^cite_note-dsd-17]

## Government

As the capital of Greenland, Nuuk is its administrative center, containing all important government buildings and institutions. The public sector bodies are also the town's largest employer.[^cite_note-dsd-17]

As of January 2026, Nuuk's mayor is Avaaraq Olsen.[^cite_note-27]

Greenland's self-government parliament, the [Inatsisartut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inatsisartut), sits in Nuuk. It has 31 seats and its members are elected by popular vote on the basis of [proportional representation](23298.md) to serve four-year terms.[^cite_note-28] All of Greenland's major political parties have their headquarters in Nuuk, including the Inuit Ataqatigiit, [Siumut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siumut), [Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democrats_\(Greenland\)), [Atassut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atassut), [Association of Candidates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Candidates), and the [Women's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Party_\(Greenland\)).[^cite_note-29]

### KANUKOKA

Main article: [KANUKOKA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KANUKOKA)

KANUKOKA ([Greenlandic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlandic_language): *Kalaallit Nunaanni Kommunit Kattuffiat*) was based in Nuuk. It was an association of Greenland's municipalities, led by Enok Sandgreen.[^cite_note-reform-30] Its aim was to facilitate cooperation among all five municipalities of Greenland: [Avannaata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avannaata), [Kujalleq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kujalleq), [Qeqertalik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qeqertalik), [Qeqqata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qeqqata), and Sermersooq. But Sermersooq and Qeqertalik both withdrew and KANUKOKA dissolved on 31 July 2018.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The organisation ran the municipal elections every four years, with the last election taking place in 2016. All municipal authorities in Greenland were members of the organisation until its dissolution.[^cite_note-about-33] The association was overseen by [Maliina Abelsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maliina_Abelsen), the Minister for Social Affairs in the Government of Greenland.[^cite_note-reform-30][^cite_note-34]

## Economy



The [Port of Nuuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuuk_Port_and_Harbour)



[Air Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Greenland) headquarters in Nuuk

Although only a small town, Nuuk has developed trade, business, shipping and other industries. It began as a small fishing settlement with a harbor, but as the economy developed rapidly during the 1970s and 1980s, Nuuk's fishing industry declined.[^cite_note-35] Nuuk is nevertheless still home to almost half of Greenland's fishing fleet. The local [Royal Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Greenland) processing plant absorbs landed seafood amounting to over DKK 50 million (US\$7 million) per annum, mainly (80%) [shrimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrimp), but also [cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cod), [lumpfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumpfish) and [halibut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halibut).[^cite_note-dsd-17] Seafood, including seal, is also sold in abundance in Nuuk's fish markets, the largest being [Kalaaliaraq Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalaaliaraq_Market). Minerals including [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc) and [gold](12240.md) have contributed to Nuuk's economic development.[^cite_note-Barkham_2016-36]

Like much of Greenland, Nuuk heavily depends on Danish investment and relies on Denmark for block funding.[^cite_note-37]

### Energy

All of Greenland's electricity is supplied by the government-owned company [Nukissiorfiit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nukissiorfiit), which has a [monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly) on it.[^cite_note-38] Since 1993, Nuuk has received its electric power mainly from [Buksefjord hydroelectric power plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buksefjord_hydroelectric_power_plant) by way of a 132 kV powerline crossing [Ameralik fjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ameralik_Span) over a distance of 5,376 m (17,638 ft), the world's longest free span.[^cite_note-North_of_56-39][^cite_note-Arctic_Business_Network-40]

### Education



[University of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greenland)

Nuuk has several educational institutions of higher learning. The [University of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greenland) (Ilisimatusarfik), Greenland's only university, is in Nuuk. It was founded in 1987 and expanded in 2007 with the new building, *Ilimmarfik*, housing departments of journalism, management and economics, language, literature and media, cultural and social history, theology and religion, and social work. Nuuk is also home to the Department of Learning (Ilinniarfissuaq), Greenland's oldest educational facility, in the old colonial part of Nuuk (*Nuutoqaq*: Old Nuuk). Other notable educational institutions include the Department of Nursing and Health Science, Nuuk Technical College, and the Iron & Metal School.

### Healthcare



Queen Ingrid's Health Center

Nuuk is served by [Queen Ingrid's Health Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Ingrid's_Hospital#Queen_Ingrid's_Health_Center), the regional health center for Region Sermersooq. It has an emergency room and a central clinic with several [general practitioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_practitioner). Nuuk also has [Queen Ingrid's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Ingrid's_Hospital), Greenland's central hospital.

### Tourism

The [Nuuk Tourist Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuuk_Tourist_Office) was built in 1992 to house the headquarters of the new National Tourist Board of Greenland.[^cite_note-41]

### Shopping

Nuuk's shops offer local art and craftwork. In 2012, Greenland's first shopping center, [Nuuk Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuuk_Center) *(NC)*, opened. The center has Greenland's first underground parking. Several supermarkets exist, such as Nuuk Center, [Pisiffik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisiffik), [Brugseni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brugseni), and [Spar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spar_\(retailer\)).

## Transportation

A [Royal Arctic Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Arctic_Line) container ship in Nuuk

### Airport

Main article: [Nuuk Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuuk_Airport)

Nuuk has an international airport 4 km (2.5 mi) northeast of the town center. Built in 1979, it was extensively rebuilt and expanded in 2024 and is now [Air Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Greenland)'s international and domestic hub and base of operations.[^cite_note-42] Most traffic to, from, and within Greenland passes through the airport. It is served year-round with direct flights to [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Airport) and [Reykjavík-Keflavík](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keflav%C3%ADk_International_Airport) as well as almost all airports in Greenland. Seasonal routes are also operated to [New York-Newark](248492.md), [Billund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billund_Airport), [Aalborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalborg_Airport), and [Iqaluit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iqaluit).[^cite_note-43]

### Sea

Nuuk is connected to Denmark by the [Royal Arctic Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Arctic_Line) (in cooperation with [Eimskip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eimskip)), which sails container ships from [Aalborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalborg) via [Iceland](14531.md). They generally bring clothing, flour, medicine, timber, machinery, and non-perishable goods, and return with deep-frozen shrimp and fish. Most sea freight to other destinations in Greenland is trans-shipped in Nuuk via Royal Arctic Line.[^cite_note-dsf-44]

Nuuk is served twice a week by the coastal passenger ferry of the [Arctic Umiaq Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Umiaq_Line), which links the communities of the western coast, for most of the year.[^cite_note-45]

### Roads



A [bus](4146.md) in Nuuk

As of 2017, there are 80 km of local roads in Nuuk.[^cite_note-46] No roads connect Nuuk with other parts of Greenland.[^cite_note-47] Most buses and cars owned in Greenland operate in Nuuk.[^cite_note-48] There are three [traffic lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_light), 12 [roundabouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundabout), and one [road tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunnel) in Nuuk, the only examples of such in Greenland.[^cite_note-49] The city's [high street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Street) is [Aqqusinersuaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqqusinersuaq).[^cite_note-50]

Since 1980, the bus service [Nuup Bussii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuup_Bussii) provides [public transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) services in Nuuk.[^cite_note-51] Buses link the town center with the airport, the outlying districts and neighborhoods[^cite_note-52] of [Nuussuaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuussuaq_\(district\)), [Qinngorput](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinngorput), and [Qernertunnguit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qernertunnguit) in [Quassussuup Tungaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quassussuup_Tungaa).[^cite_note-53] In 2012, the buses transported more than 2 million passengers around the city of Nuuk.[^cite_note-54]

### Historical buildings

**Hans Egede's House**\
[Hans Egede's House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Egede_House), built in 1721 by the Norwegian missionary Hans Egede, is Greenland's oldest building. Standing close to the harbor among other old houses, it is now used for government receptions.[^cite_note-dsf-44][^cite_note-55]

**Nuuk Cathedral**\
The Church of Our Saviour of the [Lutheran](23371382.md) [diocese of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Greenland) was built in 1849, and the tower was added in 1884. The red building with a clock tower and steeple is a prominent site on the landscape.[^cite_note-56] The church received the status of [Nuuk Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuuk_Cathedral) in 1994. The first bishop was Kristian Mørk, followed in 1995 by Sofie Petersen, a Greenland native and the second woman in Denmark to become a bishop.[^cite_note-57]

Outside the Cathedral stand the [Statue of Hans Egede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Hans_Egede) and the [Statue of the Mother of the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_the_Mother_of_the_Sea), representing two different strands of Greenland's religious history.

The [Herrnhut House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herrnhut_House) was the center of the [Moravian mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_missions_in_Greenland) of [New Herrnhut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neu-Herrnhut).

**National Museum**\
[Greenland National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland_National_Museum) is in Nuuk and was one of the first museums established in Greenland, inaugurated in the mid-1960s.[^cite_note-58] It has many artifacts and exhibits related to Greenland's archaeology, history, art, and handicrafts, and contains the [Qilakitsoq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qilakitsoq) mummies.

**Modern architecture**

Examples of modern architecture include the [Katuaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katuaq) cultural center by [Schmidt Hammer Lassen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schmidt_Hammer_Lassen) (1997), the campus of the [University of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greenland) by Tegnestuen Nuuk and [KHR Arkitekter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KHR_Arkitekter) (2008), the [Nuuk Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuuk_Center) by KHR Arkitekter (2012), and the Anstalten Correctional Facility by [Friis & Moltke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friis_&_Moltke) and [Schmidt Hammer Lassen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schmidt_Hammer_Lassen) (2019).

### Cultural

Main articles: [Katuaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katuaq) and [Nuuk Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuuk_Art_Museum)



Nuuk Art Museum

Katuaq is a [cultural center](6065329.md) used for concerts, films, art exhibitions, and conferences. It was designed by [Schmidt Hammer Lassen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schmidt_Hammer_Lassen) and inaugurated on 15 February 1997. Katuaq contains two [auditoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auditorium), one seating 1,008 people and the other 508. The complex also contains an art school, library, meeting facilities, administrative offices, and a café.

The [Nuuk Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuuk_Art_Museum) is Greenland's only private art and crafts museum.[^cite_note-Kunst-59] It has a notable collection of local paintings, watercolors, drawings, and graphics, some by [Andy Warhol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Warhol); and figures in soapstone, ivory, and wood, with many items collected by archaeologists.

### Educational

Main article: [University of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greenland)

Ilisimatusarfik, also known as the University of Greenland, is in Nuuk and is the national university of Greenland. Most courses are taught in [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language), although a few are in Kalaallisut. As of 2007, the university had about 150 students (almost all Greenlanders), 14 academic staff, and five administrators.[^cite_note-statistic-60] Its library has about 30,000 volumes. The campus of the [University of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greenland), hosting [Statistics Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Greenland) and the main holdings of the [Public and National Library of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_and_National_Library_of_Greenland),[^cite_note-61] is at the district's northern end, near the road to [Nuuk Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuuk_Airport).[^cite_note-62]

The [National Library of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_and_National_Library_of_Greenland) in Nuuk is the country's largest reference library. It is devoted to preserving Greenland's cultural heritage and history.[^cite_note-ica-63] The library holdings are split between the public library in the town center and Ilimmarfik, the campus of the University of Greenland. As of 1 January 2008, there are 83,324 items in the library database at Ilimmarfik.[^cite_note-64]

## Sports



[Godthåbhallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godth%C3%A5bhallen) exterior



Teletårnet, Nuuk

Nuuk's sports clubs include [Nuuk IL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuuk_Idraetslag) (established in 1934), [B-67](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-67), and [GSS Nuuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%B8nlands_Seminarius_Sportklub). [Nuuk Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuuk_Stadium) is a multi-purpose stadium, used mostly for [football](10568.md) games. The stadium has a capacity of 2,000.[^cite_note-worldstadiums.com-65] The stadium can also be used as an entertainment venue: the Scottish rock band [Nazareth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazareth_\(band\)) performed at the venue.&#x20;

Nuuk also has the [Godthåbhallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godth%C3%A5bhallen), a handball stadium. It is the home of the [Greenland men's national handball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland_men's_national_handball_team) and has a capacity of 1,000.[^cite_note-worldstadiums.com-65] There is a hill for alpine skiing with an altitude difference around 300 meters on the mountain Lille Malene,[^cite_note-skilift.gl-66] with the valley station close to the airport terminal.[^cite_note-xpda-67] There is also the Nuuk golf course, the only arctic golf course in the world.[^cite_note-68]

An indoor [sports centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leisure_centre), [Inussivik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inussivik), was opened in 2002.[^cite_note-69]
