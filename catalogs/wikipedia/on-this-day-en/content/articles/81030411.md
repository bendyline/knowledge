**Operation Midway Blitz** is an operation by the [United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement](216537.md) (ICE) conducted in and around [Chicago](6886.md), Illinois, starting on September 8, 2025.[^cite_note-Aleaziz-2025-2] The [United States Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) (DHS) stated that the operation will arrest [illegal immigrants](5044573.md) with criminal records[^cite_note-Aleaziz-2025-2] and is intended to crack down on [sanctuary policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctuary_city).[^cite_note-HS_2025-09-08-3] Hundreds of DHS agents used a nearby [naval base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Station_Great_Lakes) as a staging area.[^cite_note-Aleaziz-2025-2]&#x20;

Some legal experts questioned the legality of the operation,[^cite_note-Aleaziz-2025-2] and Illinois Governor [JB Pritzker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JB_Pritzker) is planning legal action.[^cite_note-Kapos-2025-4] Pritzker's office has stated that the [Trump administration](78298108.md) did not communicate with his office about the operation.[^cite_note-Aleaziz-2025-2] The operation has caused anxiety among certain Chicago residents such as [Latinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans).[^cite_note-Aleaziz-2025-2] Religious organizations in Chicago have coordinated to schedule gatherings and make resources available for [immigrants](15051.md).[^cite_note-Aleaziz-2025-2][^cite_note-5] A hotline set up to report ICE sightings was very busy on the first day of the operation, confirming that enforcement actions increased.[^cite_note-Kapos-2025-4]&#x20;

On December 31, 2025, the National Guard ceased to be a part of the Chicago deployments.[^cite_note-6]&#x20;

## Background

Further information: [Domestic military deployments by the second Trump administration](80838047.md)

During [his 2024 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_2024_presidential_campaign), Trump stated he would use the military to end protests without consent from state governors, actions which his aides had previously talked him out of during his first term.[^cite_note-Savage_07082025-7] Also during Trump's [second campaign and current tenure](78298108.md), his administration has pursued a [deportation policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportation_and_removal_from_the_United_States) characterized as "maximalist",[^cite_note-8] and a mass-deportation campaign affecting hundreds of thousands of immigrants through detentions, confinements, and expulsions. The Trump administration has also launched successive purges of top military and intelligence leadership whose views were seen as being at odds with Trump.[^cite_note-9] During a September 30 meeting with over 800 generals and admirals, Trump stated that the deployments should be used as "training grounds for our military" and described America as waging "a war from within", adding that "America is under invasion from within" and that it was "\[n]o different than a foreign enemy, but more difficult in many ways because they don't wear uniforms".[^cite_note-Samuels_2025-10]&#x20;

In 2025, the Trump administration deployed forces of the federal government such as the National Guard to select U.S. cities. Deployments began in [Los Angeles in June 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2025_Los_Angeles_protests_against_mass_deportation), followed by [deployment to Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaring_a_Crime_Emergency_in_the_District_of_Columbia), in August 2025, and planned deployments to Memphis, Chicago and Portland, among others.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] On September 8, 2025, DHS announced that ICE would lead Operation Midway Blitz, a multi-agency surge aimed at "criminal illegal aliens" in Illinois. According to U.S. Representative [Lauren Underwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauren_Underwood), the operation began on September 6, 2025.[^cite_note-CBS_LU1-15] According to DHS, the mission honors Katie Abraham, a Chicago-area woman killed earlier in the year by an undocumented drunk driver in [Urbana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbana,_Illinois).[^cite_note-HS_2025-09-08-3][^cite_note-16]&#x20;

## Launch and scope

While announced as a Chicago crackdown, the operation's jurisdiction covers the entire state of Illinois and neighboring [Lake County, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_County,_Indiana).[^cite_note-17] In addition to ICE's Enforcement and Removal Operations (ERO), personnel from the [U.S. Marshals Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marshals_Service), [Drug Enforcement Administration](146720.md) and [Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Alcohol,_Tobacco,_Firearms_and_Explosives) (ATF) were included.[^cite_note-18] The [ICE processing facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadview_ICE_Facility) in the Chicago suburb of [Broadview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadview,_Illinois) was the site of clashes between protesters and federal agents.[^cite_note-TG_2025-10-04-19] [ABC 7 Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WLS-TV) reported that more than 250 people had been taken into custody during the first nine days, with detainees transferred to immigration detention centers in Indiana and Wisconsin.[^cite_note-20] Thirteen days after the operation began, DHS Assistant Secretary for Public Affairs Tricia McLaughlin stated that officials had arrested almost 550 people.[^cite_note-21] The operation includes detaining [truck drivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truck_driver) without immigration documentation. By November 11, 140 migrant drivers had been taken into custody.[^cite_note-22]&#x20;

Two people were detained and a third person, who was the driver, showed the agents her green card and was let go. Community members lined the street along the supermarket on Sunday afternoon to protest. Family members said the two individuals, now in federal custody, had no criminal history or record.[^cite_note-107]&#x20;

On October 31, ICE detained at least three U.S. citizens in the [Albany Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany_Park,_Chicago) neighborhood, and caused a vehicle crash in [Evanston, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evanston,_Illinois).[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110]&#x20;

### November

On November 3, community members of the [Village of Wheeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheeling,_Illinois) came together to advocate for a resolution that would prohibit civil immigration enforcement agencies from using Village-owned property. The resolution was introduced after immigration officers attempted to use a fire station's parking lot. The resolution ultimately passed in an almost unanimous vote.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112]&#x20;

On November 5, hundreds of people protested in the [North Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Center,_Chicago) neighborhood after ICE took a teacher out of a daycare and detained her while children and parents were present that morning.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]&#x20;

On November 8, the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) again deployed [tear gas](1202646.md) in the [Little Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Village,_Chicago) neighborhood, claiming that an unidentified person had shot at them from a moving vehicle. No injuries were reported.[^cite_note-115] That same day in [Cicero, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero,_Illinois), a U.S. citizen stated that he, his wife and their 1-year-old child were pepper sprayed in a [Sam's Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam%27s_Club) parking lot by an ICE agent and he required hospitalization due to an asthmatic reaction.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]&#x20;

On November 10, ICE agents posed for a photo op at [The Bean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bean) downtown. The photo op came after agents tear-gassed a [Little Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Village,_Chicago) street and were spotted in the area numerous times. At the Bean, one agent shouted, "Everyone say, 'Little Village!'"[^cite_note-118]&#x20;

On November 13, [Greg Bovino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Bovino) left [Chicago](6886.md) to go to [Charlotte, North Carolina](57447.md), and the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) left the [Great Lakes Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_Naval_Base) while stating that operations in Chicago would continue throughout the winter. Meanwhile, the Department of Homeland Security released a list of 608 names of people that ICE had detained in the Chicago area and that were ordered released from custody by Judge Jeffrey Cummings on November 12, due to their arrests violating the Castañon Nava consent decree and the [Fourth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution). Of the 608 people on the list, only 16 had criminal histories.[^cite_note-119]&#x20;

On November 15, a [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense) official stated that hundreds of National Guard troops that were dispatched to [Chicago](6886.md) and [Portland, Oregon](23503.md) but not deployed due to legal challenges would be returning to Texas and California.[^cite_note-120]&#x20;

### December

On December 31, Trump announced that the National Guard deployment to Chicago was now over.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122]&#x20;

### Illinois v. Trump

| Illinois v. Trump |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Court | [Northern District of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_District_of_Illinois) |
| Started | October 6, 2025 |
| Docket nos. | [1\:25-cv-12174](http://www.courtlistener.com/docket/71559895/state-of-illinois-v-trump/) (N.D. Ill.)  [25-2798](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/71608106/state-of-illinois-v-donald-j-trump/) (7th Cir.)  [25A443](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25a443.html) (Supreme Court) |
| Case history |  |
| Appealed to | [7th Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Circuit) |
| Court membership |  |
| Judge sitting | [April Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_Perry) |

Local and state leaders filed a lawsuit objecting and challenging the deployment of the national guard to Chicago in early October 2025. U.S. district judge April Perry, stated at an emergency hearing that the deployment could continue forward but any troop action before the October 9 hearing could be used as evidence against the Trump administration.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-Ramirez-2025-132] On October 9, 2025, Judge Perry temporarily blocked Trump's deployment of the National Guard in Chicago for 14 days, stating that the federal government did not meet the burden needed to send the National Guard into Illinois and that the deployment was unconstitutional, violating the [Posse Comitatus Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posse_Comitatus_Act) and the [Tenth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenth_Amendment).[^cite_note-133] That same day, U.S. district judge Sara Ellis issued a temporary 14-day restraining order against ICE and Border Patrol agents, ruling that federal agents can't use tear gas, pepper spray or other weapons against journalists or peaceful protesters who don't pose a serious threat to law enforcement officers or others. Ellis also prohibited federal agents from using physical force against journalists or arresting them unless they are suspected of committing a crime. The order also required federal agents to wear badges or other "visible identification" so the public can know who they are, with exceptions for those officers who work undercover.[^cite_note-134]&#x20;

The [Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Seventh_Circuit) allowed the federalization of the national guard but kept Perry's ban on deployment.[^cite_note-SCOTUS_Dec_2025-135] "Within two hours of the panel issuing the order, a [Ninth Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_Circuit) judge called for a vote on whether the court should rehear" [Oregon v. Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%932026_Portland,_Oregon_protests#Legal) [en banc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/En_banc).[^cite_note-136] On December 23, 2025, the Supreme Court denied an application [for stay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_docket) by the administration of Perry's ruling in an unsigned order.[^cite_note-137] The Justice [Brett Kavanaugh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Kavanaugh) concurred and Justices [Alito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Alito), [Gorsuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorsuch) and [Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_Thomas) dissented.[^cite_note-SCOTUS_Dec_2025-135]&#x20;

[10 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_10_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 12406](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/12406) allows federalization of the national guard whenever "the President is unable with the regular force to execute the laws of the United States". The administration argued "the term refers to civilian law enforcement officers, such as those employed by Immigration and Customs Enforcement or the [Federal Protective Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Federal_Protective_Service)". Others argued "the term refers to the regular forces of the United States military". The Supreme Court said:[^cite_note-SCOTUS_Dec_2025-135]&#x20;

> We conclude that the term "regular forces" in §12406(3) likely refers to the regular forces of the United States military. This interpretation means that to call the Guard into active federal service under §12406(3), the President must be "unable" with the regular military "to execute the laws of the United States." Because the statute requires an assessment of the military’s ability to execute the laws, it likely applies only where the military could legally execute the laws. Such circumstances are exceptional: Under the [Posse Comitatus Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posse_Comitatus_Act), the military is prohibited from "execut\[ing] the laws" "except in cases and under circumstances expressly authorized by the Constitution or Act of Congress." [18 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_18_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 1385](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1385). So before the President can federalize the Guard under §12406(3), he likely must have statutory or constitutional authority to execute the laws with the regular military and must be "unable" with those forces to perform that function. \
> \
> &#x20;At this preliminary stage, the Government has failed to identify a source of authority that would allow the military to execute the laws in Illinois. The President has not invoked a statute that provides an exception to the Posse Comitatus Act.

— Unsigned

[Ian Millhiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Millhiser) wrote that the "order does not even engage" with the administrations argument that there was a "[rebellion](513246.md)" to allow federalization.[^cite_note-138] [Adam Liptak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Liptak) wrote that Kavanaugh's concurrence "pointed to the possibility of" Trump invoking the [Insurrection Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurrection_Act) "to send more conventional military troops to American cities".[^cite_note-139]&#x20;

## Reactions

Mayor [Brandon Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Johnson) said Chicago received "no notice of any enhanced immigration action," calling the operation "militarized immigration enforcement".[^cite_note-CBS_LU1-15][^cite_note-154] Governor [JB Pritzker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JB_Pritzker) accused federal officials of undermining the authority of local officials and suggested the possibility of taking legal action to challenge the surge.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156] Representative [Lauren Underwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauren_Underwood) said she learned about the arrest counts at a meeting with ICE leadership.[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158]&#x20;

Local advocacy groups organized protests outside the suburban ICE facilities while distributing "Know Your Rights" flyers in English and Spanish.[^cite_note-CBS_LU1-15] Community hotlines reported a surge in calls from community members looking for advice and significantly more reported sightings of federal agents.[^cite_note-159]&#x20;

In a post on [Truth Social](69077351.md), President Trump called for the arrests of Mayor Brandon Johnson, and Governor JB Pritzker, and raised claims that the two were failing to protect ICE agents in Chicago. Pritzker responded on [Twitter](9988187.md), calling Trump's demand part of the path to "full-blown authoritarianism" and that he and Johnson were elected officials checking Trump on his actions.[^cite_note-Ramirez-2025-132]&#x20;

On October 5, while speaking with [Fox News](11121.md), [Homeland Security Secretary](212926.md) [Kristi Noem](27663933.md) alleged that Chicago was a "war zone" and spoke out against comments made by Johnson against Trump's policies. Noem argued that the administration's actions have resulted in a safer Chicago, while Pritzker argued on [CNN's State of the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_Union_\(American_TV_program\)) that Noem's and Trump's actions were causing Chicago to become a war zone.[^cite_note-160]&#x20;

A soccer match between [Argentina](454699.md) and [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_national_football_team) set to be played at [Soldier Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldier_Field) on October 14 was moved to [Fort Lauderdale, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lauderdale,_Florida), due to unrest from the operation.[^cite_note-161] Another soccer match on the same day between [Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela_national_football_team) and [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize_national_football_team), which was to be played at [SeatGeek Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeatGeek_Stadium) in [Bridgeview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgeview,_Illinois), was canceled.[^cite_note-162]&#x20;

The *[South Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park)* episode ["Turkey Trot"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park_season_28) parodied the repeated event of federal agents in Chicago deploying [tear gas](1202646.md) unprovoked on unarmed civilians.[^cite_note-163]&#x20;

The Lake Street Church in Evanston displayed a protest [Nativity scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativity_scene) with baby Jesus in a manger in the snow, wrapped in a silver emergency blanket with his wrists zip-tied, while Mother Mary is standing nearby, separated by a fence.[^cite_note-164]&#x20;

The *[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune)* won one of two [Pulitzer Prizes for Local Reporting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize_for_Local_Reporting) awarded in 2026 for "...its powerful coverage of the Trump administration's militarized immigration sweep of the city that described in vivid, muscular prose how the siege-like incursion of ICE agents unified Chicagoans in resistance."[^cite_note-NYT_Pulitzer-165][^cite_note-Pulitzer_Staff_of_Trib-166]&#x20;
