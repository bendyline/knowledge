**Arkansas** ([/ˈɑːrkənsɔː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b5/En-us-Arkansas3.ogg/En-us-Arkansas3.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b5/En-us-Arkansas3.ogg/En-us-Arkansas3.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, [*AR-kən-saw*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)[^cite_note-Arkansas-11]) is a landlocked [state](18618239.md) in the [West South Central region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_South_Central_region) of the [Southern United States](179553.md).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-wsc-13] It borders [Missouri](19571.md) to the north, [Tennessee](30395.md) and [Mississippi](16949861.md) to the east, [Louisiana](18130.md) to the south, [Texas](29810.md) to the southwest, and [Oklahoma](22489.md) to the west. Its name derives from the [Osage language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osage_language), and refers to their relatives, the [Quapaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quapaw) people.[^cite_note-14] The state's diverse geography ranges from the mountainous regions of the [Ozark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ozarks) and [Ouachita Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouachita_Mountains), which make up the [U.S. Interior Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Interior_Highlands), to the densely forested land in the south known as the [Arkansas Timberlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Timberlands), to the eastern lowlands along the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) and the [Arkansas Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Delta).

Previously part of [French Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Louisiana) and the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase), the [Territory of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Arkansas) was [admitted to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admitted_to_the_Union) as the 25th state on June 15, 1836.[^cite_note-15] Much of the Delta had been developed for cotton plantations, and landowners there largely depended on [enslaved African Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States)' labor. In 1861, Arkansas seceded from the United States and joined the [Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War). On returning to the Union in 1868, Arkansas continued to suffer economically, due to its overreliance on the large-scale [plantation economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation_economy). Cotton remained the leading commodity crop, and the cotton market declined. Because farmers and businessmen did not diversify and there was little industrial investment, the state fell behind in economic opportunity. In the late 19th century, the state instituted various [Jim Crow laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow_laws) to disenfranchise and segregate the African-American population. During the [civil rights movement](49001.md) of the 1950s and 1960s, Arkansas, particularly [Little Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock), was a major battleground in efforts to integrate schools. Following [World War II](32927.md) in the 1940s, Arkansas began to diversify its economy and see prosperity. During the 1960s, the state became the base of the [Walmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walmart) corporation, the [world's largest company by revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_companies_by_revenue), headquartered in [Bentonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bentonville,_Arkansas).

Arkansas is the [29th largest by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area) and the [33rd most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population) state, with a population of just over three million at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census).[^cite_note-Bureau_2021-16] The [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States) and [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_Arkansas) is [Little Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock,_Arkansas), in the central part of the state, a hub for transportation, business, culture, and government. The northwestern corner of the state, namely the [Fayetteville–Springdale–Rogers Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayetteville%E2%80%93Springdale%E2%80%93Rogers_Metropolitan_Area), is a population, education, cultural, and economic center. The [Fort Smith Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Smith_metropolitan_area) is also an economic center and is known for its historic sites related to western expansion and the persecution of Native Americans.[^cite_note-17]

In the 21st century, Arkansas's economy is based on service industries, aircraft, poultry, steel, and tourism, along with important commodity crops of cotton, [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybeans) and [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice). The state supports a network of public [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University) and colleges, including two major university systems: [Arkansas State University System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_State_University_System) and [University of Arkansas System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas_System). [Arkansas's culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Arkansas) is observable in museums, theaters, novels, television shows, restaurants, and athletic venues across the state.

## Etymology

The name *Arkansas* initially applied to the [Arkansas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_River). It derives from a [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) term, *Arcansas*, their plural term for their transliteration of *akansa*, an [Algonquian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_languages) term for the [Quapaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quapaw) people,[^cite_note-bright-18] which is believed to translate to "south wind people".[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] These were a [Dhegiha Siouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhegiha_Siouan)-speaking people who settled in Arkansas around the 13th century. *Kansa* is likely also the root term for [Kansas](16716.md), which was named after the related [Kaw people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaw_people).[^cite_note-bright-18]

The name has been pronounced and spelled in various ways.[^cite_note-Arkansas-11] In 1881, the state legislature defined the official pronunciation of Arkansas as having the final "s" be silent (as it would be in French). A dispute had arisen between the state's two senators over the pronunciation issue. One favored [/ˈɑːrkənsɔː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) ([*AR-kən-saw*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)), the other [/ɑːrˈkænzəs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) ([*ar-KAN-zəs*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)).[^cite_note-Arkansas-11]

However, even the legislative resolution itself is at odds with the common [General American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_American) pronunciation of the state's name, [/ˈɑːrkənsɔː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), since the second "a" is reduced and unstressed as a [schwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwa), while the first and last "a" are either pronounced as \[ɑ] ([open back unrounded vowel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_back_unrounded_vowel)) and \[ɔ] ([open-mid back rounded vowel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-mid_back_rounded_vowel)) or both the same as \[ɑ], with the [cot-caught](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cot-caught) merger; on the other hand, in the [Italian language](14708.md), the "Italian sound" of the vowel "a" is transcribed as an [open central unrounded vowel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_central_unrounded_vowel) (\[ä]).

In 2007, the state legislature passed a non-binding resolution declaring that the possessive form of the state's name is *Arkansas's*, which the state government has increasingly followed.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

### Early history

Main articles: [New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France), [Louisiana (New France)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_France\)), [French and Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_Indian_War), [Treaty of Paris (1763)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1763\)), [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain), [Louisiana (New Spain)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_Spain\)), and [Treaty of Aranjuez (1801)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Aranjuez_\(1801\))



[Platform mounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platform_mound) were constructed frequently during the [Woodland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland_Period) and [Mississippian periods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture).

Before European settlement of North America, Arkansas was inhabited by indigenous peoples for thousands of years. The [Caddo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caddo), [Osage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osage_Nation), and [Quapaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quapaw) peoples encountered European explorers. The first of these Europeans was Spanish explorer [Hernando de Soto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hernando_de_Soto_\(explorer\)) in 1541, who crossed the Mississippi and marched across central Arkansas and the Ozark Mountains. After finding nothing he considered of value and encountering native resistance the entire way, he and his men returned to the Mississippi River, where de Soto fell ill. From his deathbed, he ordered his men to massacre all the men of the nearby village of Anilco, whom he feared had been plotting with a powerful polity down the Mississippi River, *[Quigualtam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quigualtam)*. His men obeyed and did not stop with the men, but were said to have massacred women and children as well. He died the following day in what is believed to be the vicinity of modern-day [McArthur, Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McArthur,_Arkansas), in May 1542. His body was weighted down with sand, and he was consigned to a watery grave in the Mississippi River under cover of darkness by his men. De Soto had attempted to deceive the native population into thinking he was an immortal deity, sun of the sun, in order to forestall attack by outraged Native Americans on his by then weakened and bedraggled army. In order to keep the ruse up, his men informed the locals that de Soto had ascended into the sky. His will at the time of his death listed "four Indian slaves, three horses and 700 hogs," which were auctioned off. The starving men, who had been living off maize stolen from natives, immediately began butchering the hogs and later, under the command of former [aide-de-camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aide-de-camp) Moscoso, attempted an overland return to Mexico. They made it as far as Texas before running into territory too dry for maize farming and too thinly populated to sustain themselves by stealing food from the locals. The expedition promptly backtracked to Arkansas. After building a small fleet of boats, they then headed down the Mississippi River and eventually on to Mexico by water.[^cite_note-HUDSONKNIGHTS-24][^cite_note-25]

Later explorers included the French [Jacques Marquette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Marquette) and [Louis Jolliet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Jolliet) in 1673, and Frenchmen [Robert La Salle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_La_Salle) and [Henri de Tonti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_de_Tonti) in 1681.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Tonti established [Arkansas Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Post) at a Quapaw village in 1686, making it the first European settlement in the territory.[^cite_note-ce-28] The early Spanish or French explorers of the state gave it its name, which is probably a phonetic spelling of the [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_language) tribe's name for the [Quapaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quapaw) people, who lived downriver from them.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-Arkansas-11] The name Arkansas has been pronounced and spelled in a variety of fashions. The region was organized as the [Territory of Arkansaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Arkansaw) on July 4, 1819, and was admitted to the United States as the state of Arkansas on June 15, 1836. The name was historically [/ˈɑːrkənsɔː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), [/ɑːrˈkænzəs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), and several other variants. Historically and modernly, the people of Arkansas call themselves either "Arkansans" or "Arkansawyers". In 1881, the [Arkansas General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_General_Assembly) passed Arkansas Code 1-4-105 (official text):

> Whereas, confusion of practice has arisen in the pronunciation of the name of our state and it is deemed important that the true pronunciation should be determined for use in oral official proceedings.

> And, whereas, the matter has been thoroughly investigated by the State Historical Society and the Eclectic Society of Little Rock, which have agreed upon the correct pronunciation as derived from history, and the early usage of the American immigrants.

> Be it therefore resolved by both houses of the General Assembly, that the only true pronunciation of the name of the state, in the opinion of this body, is that received by the French from the native Indians and committed to writing in the French word representing the sound. It should be pronounced in three (3) syllables, with the final "s" silent, the "a" in each syllable with the Italian sound, and the accent on the first and last syllables. The pronunciation with the accent on the second syllable with the sound of "a" in "man" and the sounding of the terminal "s" is an innovation to be discouraged.

Citizens of the [state of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Kansas) often pronounce the [Arkansas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_River) as [/ɑːrˈkænzəs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), in a manner similar to the common pronunciation of the name of their state.

Settlers, such as fur trappers, moved to Arkansas in the early 18th century. These people used Arkansas Post as a home base and [entrepôt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrep%C3%B4t).[^cite_note-ce-28] During the colonial period, Arkansas changed hands between [France](5843419.md) and [Spain](26667.md) following the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War), although neither showed interest in the remote settlement of Arkansas Post.[^cite_note-30] In April 1783, Arkansas saw its only battle of the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), a brief [siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Arkansas_Post_\(American_Revolutionary_War\)) of the post by British Captain James Colbert with the assistance of the [Choctaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choctaw) and [Chickasaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chickasaw).[^cite_note-31]

### Purchase and statehood

Main articles: [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase), [District of Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Louisiana), [Louisiana Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Territory), [Missouri Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Territory), [Organic act § List of organic acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_act#List_of_organic_acts), and [Arkansas Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Territory)



Map of the [Arkansas Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Territory)

[Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte) sold [French Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_France\)) to the United States in 1803, including all of Arkansas, in a transaction known today as the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase). French soldiers remained as a garrison at [Arkansas Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Post). Following the purchase, the balanced give-and-take relationship between settlers and Native Americans began to change all along the frontier, including in Arkansas.[^cite_note-32] Following [a controversy over allowing slavery in the territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_compromise), the [Territory of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Arkansas) was organized on July 4, 1819.[^cite_note-Arkansas-11] Gradual emancipation in Arkansas was struck down by one vote, the [Speaker of the House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives) [Henry Clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Clay), allowing Arkansas to organize as a slave territory.[^cite_note-33]

[Slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States) became a wedge issue in Arkansas, forming a geographic divide that remained for decades. Owners and operators of the cotton [plantation economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation_economy) in southeast Arkansas firmly supported slavery, as they perceived [slave labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery) as the best or "only" economically viable method of harvesting their commodity crops.[^cite_note-34] The "hill country" of northwest Arkansas was unable to grow cotton and relied on a cash-scarce, [subsistence farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence_farming) economy.[^cite_note-35]



Arkansas statehood, 100th anniversary issue; released on June 15, 1936, on the 100th anniversary of Arkansas statehood. The old state house is depicted at center, the fort surrounding the [Arkansas Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Post) at left with the present day state capitol building at right.

[^cite_note-36]

As European Americans settled throughout the East Coast and into the Midwest, in the 1830s the United States government forced the [removal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Removal) of many [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) tribes to Arkansas and [Indian Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Territory) west of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River).

Additional Native American removals began in earnest during the territorial period, with final Quapaw removal complete by 1833 as they were pushed into Indian Territory.[^cite_note-37] The capital was relocated from Arkansas Post to [Little Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock,_Arkansas) in 1821, during the territorial period.[^cite_note-38]

See also: [Admission to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union) and [List of U.S. states by date of admission to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_date_of_admission_to_the_Union)

When Arkansas applied for statehood, the slavery issue was again raised in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) Congress eventually approved the [Arkansas Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Constitution) after a 25-hour session, admitting Arkansas on June 15, 1836, as the 25th state and the 13th [slave state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_state), having a population of about 60,000.[^cite_note-39] Arkansas struggled with taxation to support its new state government, a problem made worse by [a state banking scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holford_Bonds) and worse yet by the [Panic of 1837](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panic_of_1837).

### Civil War and Reconstruction

Main articles: [Ordinance of Secession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinance_of_Secession), [Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America), and [Arkansas in the American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_in_the_American_Civil_War)



&#x20;[Lakeport Plantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakeport_Plantation), built c. 1859

In early antebellum Arkansas, the southeast Arkansas slave-based economy developed rapidly. On the eve of the American Civil War in 1860, enslaved African Americans numbered 111,115 people, just over 25% of the state's population.[^cite_note-40] A plantation system based largely on cotton agriculture developed that, after the war, kept the state and region behind the nation for decades.[^cite_note-41] The wealth developed among planters of southeast Arkansas caused a political rift between the northwest and southeast.[^cite_note-bol-42]

Many politicians were elected to office from [the Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Family_\(Arkansas_politics\)), the dominant [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_party_\(United_States\)) political force in antebellum Arkansas. Residents generally wanted to avoid a civil war. When the Gulf states seceded in early 1861, delegates to a convention called to determine whether Arkansas should secede referred the question back to the voters for a referendum to be held in August.[^cite_note-bol-42] Arkansas did not secede until [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) demanded Arkansas troops be sent to [Fort Sumter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Sumter) to quell [the rebellion there](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fort_Sumter) and Governor [Henry Rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Rector) refused. On May 6, the members of the state convention, having been recalled by the convention president, voted to terminate Arkansas's membership in the Union and join the [Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America).[^cite_note-bol-42]



Cannons at [Battle of Pea Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pea_Ridge) site

Arkansas held a very important position for the Rebels, as it controlled the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) and the surrounding Southern states. The bloody [Battle of Wilson's Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Wilson's_Creek) just across the border in Missouri shocked many Arkansans who thought the war would be a quick and decisive Southern victory. Battles early in the war took place in northwest Arkansas, including the [Battle of Cane Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cane_Hill), [Battle of Pea Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pea_Ridge), and [Battle of Prairie Grove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Prairie_Grove). Union general [Samuel Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Ryan_Curtis) swept across the state to [Helena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena-West_Helena,_Arkansas) in the Delta in 1862. Little Rock was captured the following year. The government shifted the state Confederate capital from [Hot Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas) to [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_Arkansas) from 1863 to 1865, for the remainder of the war. Throughout the state, [guerrilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla) warfare ravaged the countryside and destroyed cities.[^cite_note-43] Passion for the Confederate cause waned after implementation of programs such as the draft, high taxes, and martial law.

Under the [Military Reconstruction Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_era#Military_Reconstruction), Congress declared Arkansas restored to the Union in June 1868, after the Legislature accepted the 14th Amendment. Republican-dominated Reconstruction legislatures expanded civil rights by establishing the South's first comprehensive state-supported public education systems and by establishing universal male suffrage, regardless of race. These conventions and governments, which temporarily disenfranchised former Confederate officials, enacted these reforms. The State soon came under the control of the [Radical Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Republicans) and Unionists, and, led by Governor [Powell Clayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powell_Clayton), they presided over a time of great upheaval as Confederate sympathizers and the [Ku Klux Klan](16779.md) fought the new developments, particularly voting rights for African Americans.

### End of Reconstruction and late 19th century

In 1874, the [Brooks-Baxter War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooks-Baxter_War), a political struggle between factions of the [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Republican_Party) shook Little Rock and the state governorship. It was settled only when President [Ulysses S. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant) ordered [Joseph Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Brooks_\(politician\)) to disperse his militant supporters.[^cite_note-44]

Following the Brooks-Baxter War, a new state constitution was ratified, re-enfranchising former Confederates and effectively ending Reconstruction.

In 1881, the Arkansas state legislature enacted a bill adopting an official pronunciation of the state's name to combat a simmering controversy.

After Reconstruction, the state began to receive more [immigrants](4599312.md) and [migrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migrant_worker). Chinese, [Italian](385155.md), and [Syrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian) men were recruited for farm labor in the developing Delta region. None of these nationalities stayed long at farm labor; the Chinese especially, as they quickly became small merchants in towns around the Delta. Many Chinese became such successful merchants in small towns that they were able to educate their children at college.[^cite_note-45]

Construction of railroads enabled more farmers to get their products to market. It also brought new development into different parts of the state, including the Ozarks, where some areas were developed as resorts. In a few years at the end of the 19th century, for instance, [Eureka Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eureka_Springs) in [Carroll County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carroll_County,_Arkansas) grew to 10,000 people, rapidly becoming a tourist destination and the state's fourth-largest city. It featured newly constructed, elegant resort hotels and spas planned around its natural springs, considered to have healthful properties. The town's attractions included horse racing and other entertainment. It appealed to a wide variety of classes, becoming almost as popular as [Hot Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).

### Rise of the Jim Crow laws and early 20th century

See also: [Elaine massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaine_massacre)



A group of [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American) boys in [Little Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock,_Arkansas) in 1938

In the late 1880s, the worsening agricultural depression catalyzed Populist and third party movements, leading to interracial coalitions. Struggling to stay in power, in the 1890s the Democrats in Arkansas followed other Southern states in passing legislation and constitutional amendments that [disfranchised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disfranchisement_after_Reconstruction_era) blacks and poor whites. In 1891 state legislators passed a requirement for a [literacy test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_test), knowing it would exclude many blacks and whites. At the time, more than 25% of the population could neither read nor write. In 1892, they amended the state constitution to require a [poll tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poll_tax_\(United_States\)) and more complex residency requirements, both of which adversely affected poor people and sharecroppers, forcing most blacks and many poor whites from voter rolls.

By 1900 the Democratic Party expanded use of the [white primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_primary) in county and state elections, further denying blacks a part in the political process. Only in the primary was there any competition among candidates, as Democrats held all the power. The state was a Democratic one-party state for decades, until after passage of the federal [Civil Rights Act of 1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Act_of_1964) and [Voting Rights Act of 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_Rights_Act_of_1965) to enforce constitutional rights.[^cite_note-46]

Between 1905 and 1911, Arkansas began to receive a small immigration of [German](152735.md), [Slovak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovaks), and Scots-Irish from Europe. The German and Slovak peoples settled in the eastern part of the state known as the [Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie), and the Irish founded small communities in the southeast part of the state. The Germans were mostly Lutheran and the Slovaks were primarily Catholic. The Irish were mostly Protestant from [Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster), of Scots and Northern Borders descent. Some early 20th-century immigration included people from eastern Europe. Together, these immigrants made the Delta more diverse than the rest of the state. In the same years, some black migrants moved into the area because of opportunities to develop the bottomlands and own their own property.

Black sharecroppers began to try to organize a farmers' union after World War I. They were seeking better conditions of payment and accounting from white landowners of the area cotton plantations. Whites resisted any change and often tried to break up their meetings. On September 30, 1919, two white men, including a local deputy, tried to break up a meeting of black sharecroppers who were trying to organize a farmers' union. After a white deputy was killed in a confrontation with guards at the meeting, word spread to town and around the area. Hundreds of whites from Phillips and neighboring areas rushed to suppress the blacks, and started attacking blacks at large. [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Arkansas) [Charles Hillman Brough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Hillman_Brough) requested federal troops to stop what was called the [Elaine massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaine_massacre). White mobs spread throughout the county, killing an estimated 237 blacks before most of the violence was suppressed after October 1.[^cite_note-encyclopediaofarkansas.net-47] Five whites also died in the incident. The governor accompanied the troops to the scene; President [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson) had approved their use.



Map of the flood of 1927 in Arkansas

The [Great Mississippi Flood of 1927](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mississippi_Flood_of_1927) flooded the areas along the Ouachita Rivers along with many other rivers.

Based on the order of President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) given shortly after the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan)'s [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor), nearly 16,000 [Japanese Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Americans) were forcibly removed from the [West Coast of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States) and incarcerated in two internment camps in the [Arkansas Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Delta).[^cite_note-encyclopedia-48] The [Rohwer Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohwer_War_Relocation_Center) in [Desha County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desha_County) operated from September 1942 to November 1945 and at its peak interned 8,475 prisoners.[^cite_note-encyclopedia-48] The [Jerome War Relocation Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_War_Relocation_Center) in [Drew County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_County) operated from October 1942 to June 1944 and held about 8,000.[^cite_note-encyclopedia-48]

### Fall of segregation

After the [Supreme Court](31737.md) ruled segregation in public schools unconstitutional in [*Brown&#x20;*&#x76;.*&#x20;Board of Education of Topeka, Kansas*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_v._Board_of_Education) (1954), some students worked to integrate schools in the state. The [Little Rock Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock_Nine) brought Arkansas to national attention in 1957 when the federal government had to intervene to protect African-American students trying to integrate a high school in the capital. Governor [Orval Faubus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orval_Faubus) had ordered the [Arkansas National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_National_Guard) to help segregationists prevent nine African-American students from enrolling at Little Rock's Central High School. After attempting three times to contact Faubus, President [Dwight D. Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower) sent 1,000 troops from the active-duty 101st Airborne Division to escort and protect the African-American students as they entered school on September 25, 1957. In defiance of federal court orders to integrate, the governor and city of Little Rock decided to close the high schools for the remainder of the school year. By the fall of 1959, the Little Rock high schools were completely integrated.[^cite_note-49]

### Boundaries

Arkansas borders [Louisiana](18130.md) to the south, [Texas](29810.md) to the southwest, [Oklahoma](22489.md) to the west, [Missouri](19571.md) to the north, and [Tennessee](30395.md) and [Mississippi](16949861.md) to the east. The [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) classifies Arkansas as a [southern](179553.md) state, sub-categorized among the [West South Central States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_South_Central_States).[^cite_note-wsc-13] The [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) forms most of its eastern border, except in [Clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_County,_Arkansas) and [Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greene_County,_Arkansas) counties, where the [St. Francis River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Francis_River) forms the western boundary of the [Missouri Bootheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Bootheel), and in many places where the channel of the Mississippi has meandered (or been straightened by man) from its original 1836 course.

### Terrain



The [Ozarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozarks) rise behind a bend in the [Buffalo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_National_River) from an overlook on the Buffalo River Trail.

Arkansas can generally be split into two halves, the highlands in the northwest and the lowlands of the southeast.[^cite_note-50] The highlands are part of the Southern Interior Highlands, including [The Ozarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ozarks) and the [Ouachita Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouachita_Mountains). The southern lowlands include the [Gulf Coastal Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Coastal_Plain) and the [Arkansas Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Delta).[^cite_note-51] This split can yield to a regional division into northwest, southwest, northeast, southeast, and central Arkansas. These regions are broad and not defined along county lines. Arkansas has seven distinct natural regions: the Ozark Mountains, Ouachita Mountains, [Arkansas River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_River_Valley), Gulf Coastal Plain, [Crowley's Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowley's_Ridge), and the Arkansas Delta, with [Central Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Arkansas) sometimes included as a blend of multiple regions.[^cite_note-52]



The flat terrain and rich soils of the Arkansas Delta near [Arkansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_City,_Arkansas) are in stark contrast to the northwestern part of the state.

The southeastern part of Arkansas along the [Mississippi Alluvial Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_Alluvial_Plain) is sometimes called the Arkansas Delta. This region is a flat landscape of [rich alluvial soils formed by repeated flooding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta) of the adjacent Mississippi. Farther from the river, in the southeastern part of the state, the Grand Prairie has a more undulating landscape. Both are fertile agricultural areas. The Delta region is bisected by a geological formation known as [Crowley's Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowley's_Ridge). A narrow band of rolling hills, Crowley's Ridge rises 250 to 500 feet (76 to 152 m) above the surrounding alluvial plain and underlies many of eastern Arkansas's major towns.[^cite_note-53]

Northwest Arkansas is part of the [Ozark Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozark_Plateau) including the [Ozark Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozark_Mountains), to the south are the [Ouachita Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouachita_Mountains), and these regions are divided by the [Arkansas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_River); the southern and eastern parts of Arkansas are called the Lowlands.[^cite_note-54] These mountain ranges are part of the [U.S. Interior Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Interior_Highlands) region, the only major mountainous region between the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains) and the [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains).[^cite_note-55] The state's highest point is [Mount Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Magazine) in the [Ouachita Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouachita_Mountains),[^cite_note-56] which is 2,753 feet (839 m) above sea level.[^cite_note-sh-5]



Cedar Falls in [Petit Jean State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petit_Jean_State_Park)

Arkansas is home to many [caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_caves_in_Arkansas), such as [Blanchard Springs Caverns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blanchard_Springs_Caverns). The State Archeologist has catalogued more than 43,000 Native American living, hunting and tool-making sites, many of them Pre-Columbian burial mounds and rock shelters. [Crater of Diamonds State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crater_of_Diamonds_State_Park) near [Murfreesboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murfreesboro,_Arkansas) is the world's only diamond-bearing site accessible to the public for digging.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] Arkansas is home to a dozen Wilderness Areas totaling 158,444 acres (641.20 km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-59] These areas are set aside for outdoor recreation and are open to hunting, fishing, hiking, and primitive camping. No mechanized vehicles nor developed campgrounds are allowed in these areas.[^cite_note-60]

### Hydrology



The [Buffalo National River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_National_River) is one of many attractions that give the state its nickname, *The Natural State*.

Arkansas has many rivers, lakes, and reservoirs within or along its borders. Major tributaries to the Mississippi River include the [Arkansas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_River), the [White River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_River_\(Arkansas\)), and the [St. Francis River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Francis_River).[^cite_note-Federal_Writers_1987,_p._8-61] The Arkansas is fed by the [Mulberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulberry_River_\(Arkansas\)) and [Fourche La Fave Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourche_La_Fave_River) in the Arkansas River Valley, which is also home to [Lake Dardanelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Dardanelle). The [Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_National_River), [Little Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Red_River_\(Arkansas\)), [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_River_\(Arkansas\)) and [Cache Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cache_River_\(Arkansas\)) are all tributaries to the [White River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_River_\(Arkansas\)), which also empties into the Mississippi. [Bayou Bartholomew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayou_Bartholomew) and the [Saline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saline_River_\(Ouachita_River\)), [Little Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Missouri_River_\(Arkansas\)), and [Caddo Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caddo_River) are all tributaries to the [Ouachita River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouachita_River) in south Arkansas, [which empties into the Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_River_Control_Structure) in Louisiana. The [Red River](194242.md) briefly forms the state's boundary with Texas.[^cite_note-62] Arkansas has few natural lakes and many reservoirs, such as [Bull Shoals Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull_Shoals_Lake), [Lake Ouachita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ouachita), [Greers Ferry Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greers_Ferry_Lake), [Millwood Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millwood_Lake), [Beaver Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaver_Lake_\(Arkansas\)), [Norfork Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfork_Lake), [DeGray Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeGray_Lake), and [Lake Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Conway).[^cite_note-63]

### Flora and fauna



The [White River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_River_\(Arkansas\)) in eastern Arkansas

Arkansas's mix of warm temperate moist forest and subtropical bottomland is divided into three broad ecoregions: the Ozark, Ouachita-Appalachian Forests; the Mississippi Alluvial and Southeast USA Coastal Plains; and the Southeastern USA Plains.[^cite_note-64] The state is further divided into seven subregions: the Arkansas Valley, [Boston Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Mountains), [Mississippi Alluvial Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_Alluvial_Plain), [Mississippi Valley Loess Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowley's_Ridge), Ozark Highlands, Ouachita Mountains, and the South Central Plains.[^cite_note-65] A 2010 [United States Forest Service](42652.md) survey determined that 18,720,000 acres (7,580,000 ha) of Arkansas's land is forestland, or 56% of the state's total area.[^cite_note-66] Dominant species in Arkansas's forests include *[Quercus](39696.md)* (oak), *[Carya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hickory)* (hickory), *[Pinus echinata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_echinata)* (shortleaf pine) and *[Pinus taeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_taeda)* (loblolly pine).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

Arkansas's plant life varies with its climate and elevation. The [pine belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Timberlands) stretching from the Arkansas delta to Texas consists of dense oak-hickory-pine growth. Lumbering and paper milling activity is active throughout the region.[^cite_note-69] In eastern Arkansas, one can find *[Taxodium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxodium)&#x20;*(cypress), *[Quercus nigra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_nigra)* (water oaks), and hickories with their roots submerged in the Mississippi Valley bayous indicative of the Deep South. [Saw palmetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saw_palmetto) and [needle palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Needle_palm) both range into Arkansas.[^cite_note-70] Nearby Crowley's Ridge is the only home of the [tulip tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liriodendron) in the state, and generally hosts more northeastern plant life such as the [beech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beech) tree.[^cite_note-71] The northwestern highlands are covered in an oak-hickory mixture, with [Ozark white cedars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juniperus_ashei), *[cornus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornus_\(genus\))* (dogwoods), and *[Cercis canadensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercis_canadensis)* (redbuds) also present. The higher peaks in the Arkansas River Valley play host to scores of ferns, including the *[Physematium scopulinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physematium_scopulinum)* and *[Adiantum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adiantum)* (maidenhair fern) on Mount Magazine.[^cite_note-72] The white-tailed deer is the official state mammal.[^cite_note-73]

### Climate

Further information: [Climate change in Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Arkansas)



All of Arkansas falls within the humid subtropical Köppen climate classification.



Rare snowy winter at [Historic Washington State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Washington_State_Park) in [Hempstead County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hempstead_County,_Arkansas)

Arkansas generally has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate). While not bordering the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md), Arkansas, is still close enough to the warm, large body of water for it to influence the weather in the state. Generally, Arkansas, has hot, humid summers and slightly drier, mild to cool winters. In [Little Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock,_Arkansas), the daily high temperatures average around 93 °F (34 °C) with lows around 73 °F (23 °C) in July. In January highs average around 51 °F (11 °C) and lows around 32 °F (0 °C). In [Siloam Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siloam_Springs,_Arkansas) in the northwest part of the state, the average high and low temperatures in July are 89 and 67 °F (32 and 19 °C) and in January the average high and low are 44 and 23 °F (7 and −5 °C). Annual precipitation throughout the state averages between about 40 and 60 inches (1,000 and 1,500 mm); it is somewhat wetter in the south and drier in the northern part of the state.[^cite_note-74] Snowfall is infrequent but most common in the northern half of the state.[^cite_note-Federal_Writers_1987,_p._8-61] The half of the state south of Little Rock is more apt to see ice storms. Arkansas's record high is 120 °F (49 °C) at [Ozark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozark,_Arkansas) on August 10, 1936; the record low is −29 °F (−34 °C) at [Gravette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravette,_Arkansas), on February 13, 1905.[^cite_note-75]

Arkansas is known for extreme weather and frequent storms. A typical year brings thunderstorms, tornadoes, and hail. Occasional cold snaps stand to bring varying amounts of snow, as well as ice storms. Between both the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains) and the [Gulf States](11969.md), Arkansas, receives around 60 days of thunderstorms. Arkansas is located in [Dixie alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixie_alley), and very near [tornado alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_alley). As a result, a few of the most destructive tornadoes in U.S. history have struck the state.[^cite_note-76] While sufficiently far from the coast to avoid a direct hit from a hurricane, Arkansas can often get the remnants of a [tropical system](8282374.md), which dumps significant amounts of rain in a short time and spawns smaller tornadoes.[^cite_note-77]

## Demographics

Main article: [Demographics of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Arkansas)

### Population

Left: Arkansas's population distribution in 2020. Red indicates high density in urban areas, white indicates low density in rural areas, blue indicates water.\

The [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) estimated that the population of Arkansas was 3,017,804 on July 1, 2019, a 3.49% increase since the [2010 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census).[^cite_note-AnnualEstUS-88] At the [2020 U.S. census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), Arkansas had a resident population of 3,011,524.

From fewer than 15,000 in 1820, Arkansas's population grew to 52,240 during a special census in 1835, far exceeding the 40,000 required to apply for statehood.[^cite_note-89] Following statehood in 1836, the population doubled each decade until the [1870 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1870_United_States_census) conducted following the American Civil War. The state recorded growth in each successive decade, although it gradually slowed in the 20th century.

It recorded population losses in the [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_United_States_census) and [1960 censuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_United_States_census). This outmigration was a result of multiple factors, including farm mechanization, decreasing labor demand, and young educated people leaving the state due to a lack of non-farming industry in the state.[^cite_note-90] Arkansas again began to grow, recording positive growth rates ever since and exceeding two million by the [1980 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_United_States_census).[^cite_note-91] Arkansas's rate of change, age distributions, and gender distributions mirror national averages. [Minority group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_group) data also approximates national averages. There are fewer people in Arkansas of Hispanic or Latino origin than the national average.[^cite_note-census-92] The [center of population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_population) of Arkansas for 2000 was located in [Perry County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perry_County,_Arkansas), near Nogal.[^cite_note-93]

According to [HUD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Housing_and_Urban_Development)'s 2022 [Annual Homeless Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Homeless_Assessment_Report_to_Congress), there were an estimated 2,459 [homeless](19394651.md) people in Arkansas.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95]

European Americans have a strong presence in the northwestern [Ozarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozarks) and the central part of the state. African Americans live mainly in the southern and eastern parts of the state. Arkansans of Irish, English and German ancestry are mostly found in the far northwestern Ozarks near the Missouri border. Ancestors of the Irish in the Ozarks were chiefly [Scots-Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Scots_people), Protestants from [Northern Ireland](21265.md), the [Scottish](26994.md) lowlands and northern England part of the largest group of immigrants from [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) and [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ireland) before the American Revolution. [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_American) and [Scots-Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots-Irish_American) immigrants settled throughout the back country of the South and in the more mountainous areas. Americans of [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_American) stock are found throughout the state.[^cite_note-106]

A 2010 survey of the principal ancestries of Arkansas's residents revealed the following:[^cite_note-107] 15.5% [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American), 12.3% [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_American), 11.5% [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans), 11.0% [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_ethnicity), 10.1% [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_American), 4.7% [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_American), 2.1% [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_American), 1.7% [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_American), 1.7% [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_American), 1.6% [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_American), and 1.4% [Scots-Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots-Irish_American).

Most people identifying as "American" are of English descent or Scots-Irish descent. Their families have been in the state so long, in many cases since before statehood, that they choose to identify simply as having American ancestry or do not in fact know their ancestry. Their ancestry primarily goes back to the original 13 colonies, and for this reason many of them today simply claim American ancestry. Many people who identify as of Irish descent are in fact of Scots-Irish descent.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111]

According to the American Immigration Council, in 2015, the top countries of origin for Arkansas's immigrants were Mexico, El Salvador, India, Vietnam, and Guatemala.[^cite_note-112]

According to the 2006–2008 American Community Survey, 93.8% of Arkansas's population (over the age of five) spoke only English at home. About 4.5% of the state's population spoke Spanish at home. About 0.7% of the state's population spoke another [Indo-European language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_language). About 0.8% of the state's population spoke an [Asian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Asia), and 0.2% spoke other languages.

Like most other Southern states, Arkansas is part of the [Bible Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_Belt) and predominantly [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism). The largest denominations by number of adherents in 2010 were the [Southern Baptist Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Baptist_Convention) with 661,382; the [United Methodist Church](32304.md) with 158,574; non-denominational Evangelical Protestants with 129,638; the [Catholic Church](606848.md) with 122,662; and [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) with 31,254. Other religions include Islam, [Judaism](15624.md), [Wicca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicca)/[Paganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paganism), Hinduism, Buddhism, and some residents have no religious affiliation.[^cite_note-114]

In 2014, the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) determined that 79% of the population was Christian, dominated by evangelicals in the Southern Baptist and independent Baptist churches. In contrast with many other states, the Catholic Church as of 2014 was not the largest Christian denomination in Arkansas. Of the unaffiliated population, 2% were [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism) in 2014.[^cite_note-pew2014-113] By 2020, the [Public Religion Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Religion_Research_Institute) determined 71% of the population was Christian.[^cite_note-115] Arkansas continued to be dominated by evangelicals, followed by [mainline Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainline_Protestant) and [historically black or African American churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_church).

## Economy

See also: [Economy of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Arkansas), [List of Arkansas companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Arkansas_companies), and [Arkansas locations by per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_locations_by_per_capita_income)



The [Simmons Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simmons_Tower) in [Little Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock,_Arkansas) is the state's tallest building.

In 2025, Arkansas's gross domestic product (GDP) was \$198.4 billion and the state's per capita personal income was \$61,752.[^cite_note-116] The [median household income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_household_income) was \$55,432 in 2023, which ranked 47th.[^cite_note-u858-117]

Once a state with a cashless society in the uplands and [plantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation_economy) agriculture in the lowlands, Arkansas's economy has evolved and diversified. Six [Fortune 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) companies are based in Arkansas, including the #1 company atop the list, the mega-retailer [Walmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walmart), founded by [Sam Walton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Walton) in 1962, and headquartered in [Bentonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bentonville,_Arkansas).[^cite_note-f500-118] In 2025, 99.3% of businesses in Arkansas were [small businesses](276495.md), and employed 46.1% of the state's workforce.[^cite_note-119]

The state's agriculture outputs are poultry and eggs, soybeans, sorghum, cattle, cotton, rice, hogs, and milk. Its industrial outputs are food processing, electric equipment, fabricated metal products, machinery, and paper products. Arkansas's mines produce natural gas, oil, crushed stone, bromine, and vanadium.[^cite_note-120] According to [CNBC](223441.md), Arkansas was the 20th-best state for business in 2025, with the 11th-lowest cost of doing business, 12th-lowest cost of living, 36th-best workforce, 30th-best economic climate, 28th in education,41st-best infrastructure and the 31st-friendliest business environment.[^cite_note-121]

As of May 2025, the state's unemployment rate was 3.7%.[^cite_note-122]

### Industry and commerce

Arkansas's earliest industries were [fur trading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trading) and agriculture, with development of cotton [plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantations_in_the_American_South) in the areas near the Mississippi River. They were dependent on slave labor through the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War).[^cite_note-123]

Today only about three percent of the population are employed in the agricultural sector,[^cite_note-124] it remains a major part of the state's economy, ranking 13th in the nation in the value of products sold.[^cite_note-agcensus-125] Arkansas is the nation's largest producer of rice, [broilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broiler), and turkeys,[^cite_note-126] and ranks in the top three for [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), [pullets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pullets), and aquaculture (catfish).[^cite_note-agcensus-125] Forestry remains strong in the [Arkansas Timberlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Timberlands), and the state ranks fourth nationally and first in the South in softwood lumber production.[^cite_note-127] Automobile parts manufacturers have opened factories in eastern Arkansas to support auto plants in other states. [Bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite) was formerly a large part of the state's economy, mined mostly around [Saline County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saline_County,_Arkansas).[^cite_note-128]

Tourism is also very important to the Arkansas economy; the official state nickname "The Natural State" was created for state tourism advertising in the 1970s, and is still used to this day. The state maintains [52 state parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Arkansas_state_parks) and the [National Park Service](161535.md) maintains seven properties in Arkansas. The completion of the [William Jefferson Clinton Presidential Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Jefferson_Clinton_Presidential_Library) in Little Rock has drawn many visitors to the city and revitalized the nearby [River Market District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Market_District_\(Little_Rock,_Arkansas\)). Many cities also hold festivals, which draw tourists to Arkansas culture, such as The Bradley County Pink Tomato Festival in Warren, [King Biscuit Blues Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Biscuit_Blues_Festival), [Ozark Folk Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozark_Folk_Center), [Toad Suck Daze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toad_Suck_Daze), and [Tontitown Grape Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tontitown,_Arkansas).[^cite_note-129]

The [Hernando de Soto Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hernando_de_Soto_Bridge) crosses over the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) into [West Memphis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Memphis,_Arkansas).

Transportation in Arkansas is overseen by the [Arkansas Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Department_of_Transportation) (ArDOT), headquartered in [Little Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock,_Arkansas). Several main corridors pass through Little Rock, including [Interstate 30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_30_in_Arkansas) (I-30) and [I-40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_40_in_Arkansas) (the nation's 3rd-busiest trucking corridor).[^cite_note-130] Arkansas first designated a state highway system in 1924, and first numbered its roads in 1926. Arkansas had one of the first paved roads, the [Dollarway Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollarway_Road), and one of the first members of the [Interstate Highway System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System). The state maintains a large system of [state highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Arkansas_state_highways) today, in addition to eight [Interstates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Interstate_Highways_in_Arkansas) and 20 [U.S. Routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._Routes_in_Arkansas).

In northeast Arkansas, [I-55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_55_in_Arkansas) travels north from [Memphis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee) to [Missouri](19571.md), with a new spur to [Jonesboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonesboro,_Arkansas) ([I-555](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_555)). Northwest Arkansas is served by the segment of [I-49](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_49_in_Arkansas) from Fort Smith to the beginning of the [Bella Vista Bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Highway_549). This segment of I-49 currently follows mostly the same route as the [former section of I-540](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_540_\(Arkansas\)#History) that extended north of I-40.[^cite_note-131] The state also has the 13th largest [state highway system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Arkansas_state_highways) in the nation.[^cite_note-132]



The [Missouri and Northern Arkansas Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_and_Northern_Arkansas_Railroad)

Arkansas is served by 2,750 miles (4,430 km) of railroad track divided among twenty-six railroad companies including three [Class I railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_I_railroads).[^cite_note-133] Freight railroads are concentrated in southeast Arkansas to serve the industries in the region. The [Texas Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Eagle), an Amtrak passenger train, serves five stations entirely in the state [Walnut Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walnut_Ridge_\(Amtrak_station\)), [Little Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock_\(Amtrak_station\)), [Malvern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malvern_\(Amtrak_station\)), [Arkadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkadelphia_\(Amtrak_station\)), and [Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_station_\(Arkansas\)), along with [Texarkana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texarkana_\(Amtrak_station\)) on the border with Texas.

Arkansas also benefits from the use of its rivers for commerce. The [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) and [Arkansas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_River) are both major rivers. The [United States Army Corps of Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Corps_of_Engineers) maintains the [McClellan-Kerr Arkansas River Navigation System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McClellan-Kerr_Arkansas_River_Navigation_System), allowing barge traffic up the Arkansas River to the [Port of Catoosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Catoosa) in [Tulsa, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa,_Oklahoma).

There are four airports with commercial service: [Clinton National Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_National_Airport) (formerly Little Rock National Airport or Adams Field), [Northwest Arkansas Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Arkansas_Regional_Airport), [Fort Smith Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Smith_Regional_Airport), and [Texarkana Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texarkana_Regional_Airport), with [dozens of smaller airports in the state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Arkansas).

Intercity bus services across the state are provided by [Flixbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flixbus), [Greyhound Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines), and [Jefferson Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Lines).[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135]

Public transit and community transport services for the elderly or those with developmental disabilities are provided by agencies such as the [Central Arkansas Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Arkansas_Transit_Authority) and the [Ozark Regional Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozark_Regional_Transit), organizations that are part of the [Arkansas Transit Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Transit_Association).

Main article: [Politics and government of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_and_government_of_Arkansas)

As with the federal government of the United States, political power in Arkansas is divided into three branches: executive, legislative, and judicial. Each officer's term is four years long. Office holders are [term-limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term-limited) to two full terms plus any partial terms before the first full term.[^cite_note-NCSL2013-136]

### Executive

Main article: [Governor of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Arkansas)

See also: [List of governors of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Arkansas) and [Arkansas Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Cabinet)

The governor of Arkansas is [Sarah Huckabee Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Huckabee_Sanders). Sanders is a [Republican](32070.md) and was inaugurated on January 10, 2023.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] The six other elected executive positions in Arkansas are [lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Arkansas), [secretary of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_of_Arkansas), [attorney general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Attorney_General), [treasurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_State_Treasurer), [auditor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_State_Auditor), and [land commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Land_Commissioner).[^cite_note-139] The governor also appoints the leaders of various state boards, committees, and departments. Arkansas governors served two-year terms until a referendum lengthened the term to four years, effective with the 1986 election. Individuals elected to these offices are limited to a lifetime total of two four-year terms per office.

In Arkansas, the lieutenant governor is elected separately from the governor and thus can be from a different political party.[^cite_note-140]

### Legislative

Main article: [Arkansas General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_General_Assembly)

The [Arkansas General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_General_Assembly) is the state's [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) bodies of legislators, composed of the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Senate) and [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_House_of_Representatives). The Senate contains 35 members from districts of approximately equal population. These districts are redrawn decennially with each US census, and in election years ending in "2", the entire body is put up for reelection. Following the election, half of the seats are designated as two-year seats and are up for reelection again in two years, these "half-terms" do not count against a legislator's term limits. The remaining half serve a full four-year term. This staggers elections such that half the body is up for reelection every two years and allows for complete body turnover following redistricting.[^cite_note-141] Arkansas House members can serve a maximum of three two-year terms. House districts are redistricted by the Arkansas Board of Apportionment.

In the 2012 elections, Arkansas voters elected a 21–14 Republican majority in the Senate and the Republicans gained a 51–49 majority in the House of Representatives.[^cite_note-142] The 2012 elections established a Republican dominance in both bodies of legislature.

The Republican Party's [majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority) status in the Arkansas State House of Representatives after the 2012 elections, is the party's first since 1874. Arkansas was the last state of the [old Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) to not have Republican control of either chamber of its house since the American Civil War.[^cite_note-sen-143]

Following the term limits changes, studies have shown that lobbyists have become less influential in state politics. Legislative staff, not subject to term limits, have acquired additional power and influence due to the high rate of elected official turnover.[^cite_note-NCSL2013-136]

### Judicial

Main article: [Courts of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courts_of_Arkansas)

Arkansas's judicial branch has five court systems: [Arkansas Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Supreme_Court), [Arkansas Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Court_of_Appeals), Circuit Courts, District Courts and City Courts.

Most cases begin in district court, which is subdivided into state district court and local district court. State district courts exercise district-wide jurisdiction over the districts created by the General Assembly, and local district courts are presided over by part-time judges who may privately practice law. 25 state district court judges preside over 15 districts, with more districts created in 2013 and 2017. There are 28 judicial circuits of Circuit Court, with each contains five subdivisions: criminal, civil, probate, domestic relations, and juvenile court. The jurisdiction of the Arkansas Court of Appeals is determined by the Arkansas Supreme Court, and there is no [right of appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeal) from the Court of Appeals to the high court. The Arkansas Supreme Court can review Court of Appeals cases upon application by either a party to the litigation, upon request by the Court of Appeals, or if the Arkansas Supreme Court feels the case should have been initially assigned to it. The twelve judges of the Arkansas Court of Appeals are elected from judicial districts to renewable six-year terms.

The Arkansas Supreme Court is the court of last resort in the state, composed of seven justices elected to eight-year terms. Established by the Arkansas Constitution in 1836, the court's decisions can be appealed to only the [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md).

### Federal

Both Arkansas's U.S. senators, [John Boozman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Boozman) and [Tom Cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cotton), are Republicans. The state has four seats in [U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md). All four seats are held by Republicans: [Rick Crawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Crawford_\(politician\)) ([1st district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas's_1st_congressional_district)), [French Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Hill_\(politician\)) ([2nd district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas's_2nd_congressional_district)), [Steve Womack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Womack) ([3rd district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas's_3rd_congressional_district)), and [Bruce Westerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Westerman) ([4th district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas's_4th_congressional_district)).[^cite_note-sw-144]

The [Clinton Presidential Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Presidential_Center) in Little Rock

Arkansas governor [Bill Clinton](3356.md) brought national attention to the state with a long speech at the [1988 Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Democratic_National_Convention) endorsing [Michael Dukakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Dukakis). Some journalists suggested the speech was a threat to his ambitions; Clinton defined it "a comedy of error, just one of those fluky things".[^cite_note-147]

He won the Democratic nomination for president in 1992. Clinton presented himself as a "[New Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Democrats_\(United_States\))" and used incumbent [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush)'s [broken promise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Read_my_lips:_no_new_taxes) against him to win the [1992 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._presidential_election,_1992) with 43.0% of the vote to Bush's 37.5% and independent billionaire [Ross Perot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Perot)'s 18.9%.

Most Republican strength traditionally lay mainly in the northwestern part of the state, particularly [Fort Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Smith,_Arkansas) and [Bentonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bentonville,_Arkansas), as well as North Central Arkansas around the [Mountain Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Home,_Arkansas) area. In the latter area, Republicans have been known to get 90% or more of the vote, while the rest of the state was more Democratic. After 2010, Republican strength expanded further to the Northeast and Southwest and into the Little Rock suburbs. The Democrats are mostly concentrated to central Little Rock, the Mississippi Delta, the Pine Bluff area, and the areas around the southern border with Louisiana.

Arkansas has elected only three Republicans to the U.S. Senate since [Reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_era_of_the_United_States): [Tim Hutchinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Hutchinson), who was defeated after one term by [Mark Pryor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Pryor); [John Boozman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Boozman), who defeated incumbent [Blanche Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blanche_Lincoln); and [Tom Cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cotton), who defeated Pryor in 2014. Before 2013, the General Assembly had not been controlled by the Republican Party since Reconstruction, with the GOP holding a 51-seat majority in the state House and a 21-seat (of 35) in the state Senate following victories in 2012. Arkansas was one of the only states of the former [Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) other than Florida and Virginia that sent two Democrats to the U.S. Senate for any period during the first decade of the 21st century.

In 2010, Republicans captured three of the state's four seats in the [U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md). In 2012, they won election to all four House seats. Arkansas held the distinction of having a U.S. House delegation composed entirely of military veterans ([Rick Crawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Crawford_\(politician\)), [Army](32087.md); [Tim Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Griffin), [Army Reserve](1187028.md); [Steve Womack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Womack), [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_of_the_United_States); [Tom Cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cotton), Army). When Pryor was defeated in 2014, the entire congressional delegation was in GOP hands for the first time since [Reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_era_of_the_United_States).

Reflecting the state's large [evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism_in_the_United_States) population, Arkansas has a strong [social conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conservatism_in_the_United_States) bent. In the aftermath of the landmark Supreme Court decision *[Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobbs_v._Jackson_Women's_Health_Organization)*, Arkansas became one of nine states where [abortion](765.md) is banned.[^cite_note-148] Under the [Arkansas Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Constitution), Arkansas is a [right to work state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-to-work_law). Its voters passed a ban on [same-sex marriage](92656.md) in 2004, with 75% voting yes,[^cite_note-149] although that ban has been inactive since the Supreme Court protected same-sex marriage in *[Obergefell v. Hodges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obergefell_v._Hodges)*.

Arkansas retains the [death penalty](5902.md). Authorized methods of execution include the [electric chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_chair).[^cite_note-150]

### Military

The [Strategic Air Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Air_Command) facility of [Little Rock Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock_Air_Force_Base) was one of eighteen silos in the command of the [308th Strategic Missile Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/308th_Armament_Systems_Wing#Intercontinental_Ballistic_Missiles) (308th SMW), specifically one of the nine silos within its [374th Strategic Missile Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/374th_Strategic_Missile_Squadron) (374th SMS). The squadron was responsible for Launch Complex 374–7, site of the [1980 explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Damascus_Titan_missile_explosion) of a [Titan II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_\(missile\)) Intercontinental Ballistic Missile (ICBM) in [Damascus, Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus,_Arkansas).[^cite_note-151]

### Taxation

Taxes are collected by the [Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Department_of_Finance_and_Administration).[^cite_note-152]

## Health

See also: [List of hospitals in Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_in_Arkansas)



[UAMS Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAMS_Medical_Center), Little Rock

As of 2012, Arkansas, as with many Southern states, has a high incidence of premature death, infant mortality, cardiovascular deaths, and occupational fatalities compared to the rest of the United States.[^cite_note-health-153] The state is tied for 43rd with [New York](8210131.md) in percentage of adults who regularly exercise.[^cite_note-154] Arkansas is usually ranked as one of the least healthy states due to high obesity, smoking, and sedentary lifestyle rates,[^cite_note-health-153] but according to a Gallup poll, Arkansas made the most immediate progress in reducing its number of uninsured residents after the [Affordable Care Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient_Protection_and_Affordable_Care_Act) passed. The percentage of uninsured in Arkansas dropped from 22.5 in 2013 to 12.4 in August 2014.[^cite_note-155]

The [Arkansas Clean Indoor Air Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_smoking_bans_in_the_United_States#Arkansas), a statewide smoking ban excluding bars and some restaurants, went into effect in 2006.[^cite_note-156]

Healthcare in Arkansas is provided by a network of hospitals as members of the Arkansas Hospital Association. Major institutions with multiple branches include Baptist Health, [Community Health Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Health_Systems), and [HealthSouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HealthSouth). The [University of Arkansas for Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas_for_Medical_Sciences) (UAMS) in Little Rock operates the [UAMS Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAMS_Medical_Center), a [teaching hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teaching_hospital) ranked as high performing nationally in [cancer](105219.md) and [nephrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nephrology).[^cite_note-157] The pediatric division of UAMS Medical Center is known as [Arkansas Children's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Children's_Hospital), nationally ranked in pediatric [cardiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiology) and heart surgery.[^cite_note-158] Together, these two institutions are the state's only [Level I trauma centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Level_I_trauma_center).[^cite_note-159]

## Education

Main article: [Education in Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Arkansas)

See also: [List of colleges and universities in Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_Arkansas), [List of high schools in Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_high_schools_in_Arkansas), and [List of school districts in Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_school_districts_in_Arkansas)

Arkansas has 1,064 state-funded kindergartens, elementary, junior and senior high schools.[^cite_note-160]

The state supports a network of public [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University) and colleges, including two major university systems: [Arkansas State University System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_State_University_System) and [University of Arkansas System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas_System). The [University of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas), flagship campus of the University of Arkansas System in Fayetteville was ranked #63 among public schools in the nation by *[U.S. News & World Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._News_&_World_Report)*.[^cite_note-161] Other public institutions include [Arkansas State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_State_University), [University of Arkansas at Pine Bluff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas_at_Pine_Bluff), [Arkansas Tech University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Tech_University), [Henderson State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson_State_University), [Southern Arkansas University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Arkansas_University), and [University of Central Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Central_Arkansas) across the state. It is also home to 11 private colleges and universities including [Hendrix College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrix_College), one of the nation's top 100 [liberal arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_arts) colleges, according to *U.S. News & World Report*.[^cite_note-162]

In the 1920s the state required all children to attend public schools. The school year was set at 131 days, although some areas were unable to meet that requirement.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164]

Generally prohibited in the [Western world](21208200.md) at large, [school corporal punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_corporal_punishment) is not unusual in Arkansas, with 20,083 public school students[^cite_note-165] [paddled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddle_\(spanking\)) at least one time, according to government data for the 2011–12 school year.[^cite_note-CORPUN_US_SCHOOL-166] The rate of corporal punishment in public schools is higher only in [Mississippi](16949861.md).[^cite_note-CORPUN_US_SCHOOL-166]

Main article: [Culture of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Arkansas)



[Van Buren Confederate Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Buren_Confederate_Monument) at the [Crawford County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crawford_County,_Arkansas) Courthouse in [Van Buren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Buren,_Arkansas)

The culture of Arkansas includes distinct cuisine, dialect, and traditional festivals. Sports are also very important to the culture, including football, baseball, basketball, hunting, and fishing. Perhaps the best-known aspect of Arkansas's culture is the stereotype that its citizens are shiftless hillbillies.[^cite_note-168] The reputation began when early explorers characterized the state as a savage wilderness full of outlaws and thieves.[^cite_note-169] The most enduring icon of Arkansas's hillbilly reputation is *[The Arkansas Traveller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Washburn)*, a painted depiction of a folk tale from the 1840s.[^cite_note-170] Though intended to represent the divide between rich southeastern plantation Arkansas planters and the poor northwestern hill country, the meaning was twisted to represent a Northerner lost in the Ozarks on a white horse asking a backwoods Arkansan for directions.[^cite_note-171] The state also suffers from the racial stigma common to former Confederate states, with historical events such as the [Little Rock Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock_Nine) adding to Arkansas's enduring image.[^cite_note-172]

Art and history museums display pieces of cultural value for Arkansans and tourists to enjoy. [Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Bridges_Museum_of_American_Art) in [Bentonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bentonville,_Arkansas) was visited by 604,000 people in 2012, its first year.[^cite_note-173] The museum includes walking trails and educational opportunities in addition to displaying over 450 works covering five centuries of American art.[^cite_note-174] Several historic town sites have been restored as Arkansas [state parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Arkansas_state_parks), including [Historic Washington State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Washington_State_Park), [Powhatan Historic State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powhatan_Historic_State_Park), and [Davidsonville Historic State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidsonville_Historic_State_Park).

Arkansas features a variety of native music across the state, ranging from the [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues) heritage of [West Memphis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Memphis,_Arkansas), [Pine Bluff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Bluff,_Arkansas), [Helena–West Helena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena%E2%80%93West_Helena,_Arkansas) to [rockabilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockabilly), [bluegrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluegrass_music), and folk music from the Ozarks. Festivals such as the [King Biscuit Blues Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Biscuit_Blues_Festival) and [Bikes, Blues, and BBQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bikes,_Blues,_and_BBQ) pay homage to the history of blues in the state. The Ozark Folk Festival in [Mountain View](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_View,_Arkansas) is a celebration of Ozark culture and often features folk and bluegrass musicians. Literature set in Arkansas such as *[I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Know_Why_the_Caged_Bird_Sings)* by [Maya Angelou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_Angelou) and *[A Painted House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Painted_House)* by [John Grisham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Grisham) describe the culture at various time periods.

### Sports and recreation



The flooded, forested [bottomlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upland_and_lowland_\(freshwater_ecology\)#Lowland) of east Arkansas attract wintering waterfowl.

Team sports and especially collegiate football are important to Arkansans. College football in Arkansas began from humble beginnings, when the [University of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas) first fielded a team in [1894](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1894_Arkansas_Cardinals_football_team). Over the years, many Arkansans have looked to [Arkansas Razorbacks football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Razorbacks_football) as the public image of the state.[^cite_note-175] Although the University of Arkansas is based in [Fayetteville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayetteville,_Arkansas), the Razorbacks have always played at least one game per season at [War Memorial Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Memorial_Stadium_\(Arkansas\)) in [Little Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock,_Arkansas) in an effort to keep fan support in central and south Arkansas.

[Arkansas State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_State_University) became the second [NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Football_Bowl_Subdivision) (FBS) (then known as Division I-A) team in the state in 1992 after playing in lower divisions for nearly two decades. The two schools have never played each other, due to the University of Arkansas's policy of not playing intrastate games.[^cite_note-176] Two other campuses of the [University of Arkansas System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas_System) are Division I members. The [University of Arkansas at Pine Bluff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas_at_Pine_Bluff) is a member of the [Southwestern Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Athletic_Conference), a league whose members all play football in the second-level [Football Championship Subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Championship_Subdivision) (FCS). The [University of Arkansas at Little Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas_at_Little_Rock), known for sports purposes as Little Rock, joined the [Ohio Valley Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Valley_Conference) in 2022 after playing in the [Sun Belt Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Belt_Conference); unlike many other OVC members, it does not field [a football program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NCAA_Division_I_non-football_programs). In July 2026, Little Rock will move to the [United Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Athletic_Conference), which is transitioning from a [football-only league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Athletic_Conference_\(football\)) to an all-sports conference via a rebranding of the Western Athletic Conference. It will be one of two UAC members without a football program. The state's other Division I member is the [University of Central Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Central_Arkansas) (UCA), which joined the [Atlantic Sun Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Sun_Conference) (then officially the ASUN Conference) in 2021 after leaving the FCS [Southland Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southland_Conference). UCA, which played in the football-only version of the UAC from 2023 to 2025, will move from the ASUN to the all-sports UAC in July 2026. Seven of Arkansas's smaller colleges play in [NCAA Division II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_II), with six in the [Great American Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_American_Conference) and one in the [MIAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-America_Intercollegiate_Athletic_Association). Two other small Arkansas colleges compete in [NCAA Division III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_III), in which [athletic scholarships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_scholarship) are prohibited. High school football also began to grow in Arkansas in the early 20th century.

Baseball runs deep in Arkansas and was popular before the state hosted [Major League Baseball](38776.md) (MLB) [spring training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_training) in [Hot Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas) from 1886 to the 1920s. Two [minor league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_League_Baseball) teams are based in the state. The [Arkansas Travelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Travelers) play at [Dickey–Stephens Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickey%E2%80%93Stephens_Park) in [North Little Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Little_Rock,_Arkansas), and the [Northwest Arkansas Naturals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Arkansas_Naturals) play in [Arvest Ballpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvest_Ballpark) in [Springdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springdale,_Arkansas). Both teams compete in the [Texas League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_League).

Hunting continues in the state. The state created the [Arkansas Game and Fish Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Game_and_Fish_Commission) in 1915 to regulate hunting.[^cite_note-177] Today a significant portion of Arkansas's population participates in hunting [duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duck) in the [Mississippi flyway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_flyway) and [deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer) across the state.[^cite_note-Sutherlin_1996,_p._164-178] [Ducks Unlimited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ducks_Unlimited) has called [Stuttgart, Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart,_Arkansas), "the epicenter of the duck universe".[^cite_note-179] Millions of acres of public land are available for both bow and modern gun hunters.[^cite_note-Sutherlin_1996,_p._164-178]

Fishing has always been popular in Arkansas,[^cite_note-180] and the sport and the state have benefited from the creation of reservoirs across the state. Following the completion of [Norfork Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfork_Dam), the [Norfork Tailwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfork_Tailwater) and the [White River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_River_\(Arkansas\)) have become a destination for [trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trout) fishers. Several smaller retirement communities such as [Bull Shoals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull_Shoals,_Arkansas), [Hot Springs Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_Village,_Arkansas), and [Fairfield Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfield_Bay,_Arkansas) have flourished due to their position on a fishing lake. The [National Park Service](161535.md) has preserved the [Buffalo National River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_National_River) in its natural state and fly fishers visit it annually.

Arkansas has a fleeting association with [ice hockey in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_in_the_United_States). The [Arkansas GlacierCats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_GlacierCats) debuted in 1998 at the [Barton Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barton_Coliseum) in [Little Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock,_Arkansas).[^cite_note-181] Attendance dropped after a solid first season. Dave Berryman sold a 51% interest in the team to [Equity Media Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equity_Media_Holdings) in 2001 but the team's situation continued to worsen. By 2002 the team saw fewer than 3,000 fans per game and, despite seeing a slight increase in their fourth season, the team folded in 2003. The following year, junior hockey came to Arkansas with the [Texarkana Bandits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Bandits). The team began well, posting a winning season in its first year and then increasing its win total over the following two campaigns. Unfortunately, when a more promising spot opened in the [St. Louis](27687.md) market, the team moved despite increased interest from the local community.[^cite_note-182] The University of Arkansas fields an American Collegiate Hockey Association (ACHA) team known as the Arkansas Razorbacks Hockey Club. The team competes in Division II & III of the ACHA and has contributed to the growth of ice hockey in Arkansas since its establishment in 2007.[^cite_note-183] The program has achieved significant milestones, including multiple regional tournament appearances and fostering the development of college hockey in the region.[^cite_note-184] As of 2023, no players from Arkansas have achieved notability in ice hockey. The state, has one of the lowest engagement rates in the nation with just 415 people registered with [USA Hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Hockey) in 2022: 48th in the nation.[^cite_note-185]

[^cite_note-Arkansas-11]: &#x20; The region was organized as the [Territory of Arkansaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Arkansaw) on July 4, 1819, but the territory was admitted to the United States as the state of Arkansas on June 15, 1836. The name was historically pronounced [/ˈɑːrkənsɔː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), [/ɑːrˈkænzəs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), and several other variants. The residents of Arkansas have called themselves either "Arkansans" or "Arkansawyers". In 1881, the [Arkansas General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_General_Assembly) passed the following concurrent resolution, now Arkansas Code April 1 105:<sup>[\[18\]](#cite_note-21)</sup>  Whereas, confusion of practice has arisen in the pronunciation of the name of our state and it is deemed important that the true pronunciation should be determined for use in oral official proceedings. And, whereas, the matter has been thoroughly investigated by the State Historical Society and the Eclectic Society of Little Rock, which have agreed upon the correct pronunciation as derived from history, and the early usage of the American immigrants. Be it therefore resolved by both houses of the General Assembly, that the only true pronunciation of the name of the state, in the opinion of this body, is that received by the French from the native Indians and committed to writing in the French word representing the sound. It should be pronounced in three (3) syllables, with the final "s" silent, the "a" in each syllable with the Italian sound, and the accent on the first and last syllables. The pronunciation with the accent on the second syllable with the sound of "a" in "man" and the sounding of the terminal "s" is discouraged by Arkansans.  Despite this, the state's name is still frequently mispronounced, especially by non-Americans; in fact, it is spelled in [Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrillic_alphabet) with the [*ar-KAN-zəs*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key) pronunciation. Citizens of the [state of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Kansas) often pronounce the [Arkansas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_River) as [/ɑːrˈkænzəs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), in a manner similar to the common pronunciation of the name of their state.
