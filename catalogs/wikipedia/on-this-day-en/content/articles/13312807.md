**Lichterfelde** (German pronunciation: [\[ˈlɪçtɐˌfɛldə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c2/De-Lichterfelde.ogg/De-Lichterfelde.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c2/De-Lichterfelde.ogg/De-Lichterfelde.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a locality in the [borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroughs_of_Berlin) of [Steglitz-Zehlendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steglitz-Zehlendorf) in [Berlin](3354.md), Germany. Until 2001 it was part of the former borough of Steglitz, along with [Steglitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steglitz) and [Lankwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lankwitz). Lichterfelde is home to institutions like the [Berlin Botanical Garden and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Botanical_Garden_and_Botanical_Museum), the [German Federal Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Intelligence_Service) (BND), the [German Federal Archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Federal_Archives) and the [Charité](2559577.md) university hospital's *Benjamin Franklin Campus*. Many embassies and landmark-protected buildings are located in the affluent mansion settlement in [Lichterfelde West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichterfelde_West).

## History

The [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) village *_Lichtervelde_* was founded in the 13th century by Flemish settlers. It witnessed considerable growth in the 19th century when the two "villa colonies" of *[Lichterfelde-West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichterfelde-West)* and *Lichterfelde-Ost* were founded: elegant settlements for wealthy Berliners consisting completely of [villas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa) or [mansions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansion). These settlements and the historical villages of |Lichterfelde and Giesensdorf were united in 1880 under the name *_Groß-Lichterfelde_* (Greater Lichterfelde).



*Preußische Hauptkadettenanstalt*

Lichterfelde was chosen as the seat of the Prussian Main [Military academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_academy), the *[Preußische Hauptkadettenanstalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preu%C3%9Fische_Hauptkadettenanstalt)*, in 1882, and the district became the home to many famous families from the [German nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_nobility) due to their connections with the [Prussian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Army). The world's first commercially successful electrified [tram](30733.md) line, the [Gross-Lichterfelde tramway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross-Lichterfelde_tramway), opened between the *Lichterfelde-Ost* railway station and the *Hauptkadettenanstalt* in 1881.

In 1920 Lichterfelde became part of [Greater Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Berlin_Act). Between 1920 and 1933, the former military academy in Lichterfelde was used by the [Berlin Police](34339160.md). From 1933 to 1945, the grounds of the military academy were the home of the *[Leibstandarte SS Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_SS_Panzer_Division_Leibstandarte_SS_Adolf_Hitler)*. During this same period, the anti-Nazi [Kreisau Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kreisau_Circle) resistance group surrounding Count [Peter Yorck von Wartenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Yorck_von_Wartenburg) and [Helmuth James Graf von Moltke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmuth_James_Graf_von_Moltke) held their secret meetings inside Wartenburg's apartment on *_Hortensienstraße 50_*, Lichterfelde-West, during the [Third Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Reich). [Generaloberst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generaloberst) [Ludwig Beck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Beck) had his house on *_Goethestraße 24_* in Lichterfelde-Ost. In 1942–1945, Lichterfelde was the location of a subcamp of the [Sachsenhausen concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsenhausen_concentration_camp).[^cite_note-ushm-2] There were seven known escape attempts, five successful.[^cite_note-ushm-2]

From 1945 to 1994 the *Hauptkadettenanstalt* was in use as "Andrews Barracks" by the [United States Army](32087.md)'s [Berlin Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Berlin). Today it belongs to the [German Federal Archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Federal_Archives), headquartered in [Koblenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koblenz). Two other [kasernes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaserne) "Roosevelt Barracks" in *Gardeschützen-Kaserne* (former seat of the [Prussian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Army)'s [Guards Rifles Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guards_Rifles_Battalion)) and "[McNair Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McNair_Barracks)", a former *[Telefunken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telefunken)* manufacturing plant on *Goerzallee* were nearby.

While Lichterfelde-Ost was in parts badly damaged during [World War II](32927.md), [Lichterfelde West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichterfelde_West) is still largely intact and today one of the prime residential areas of Berlin. The Lichterfelde locality also houses the [Berlin-Dahlem Botanical Garden and Botanical Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin-Dahlem_Botanical_Garden_and_Botanical_Museum) and the *Campus [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin)*, built in 1968 and today part of the *[Charité](2559577.md)* [university hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_hospital).

## Quarters of Lichterfelde

### Lichterfelde West

Main article: [Lichterfelde West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichterfelde_West)

Lichterfelde West was developed as a settlement of mansions and is one of the wealthiest residential areas of Berlin. It is home to the [Berlin Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Botanical_Garden_and_Botanical_Museum) and embassies. The [Teltow Canal](27634460.md) geographically separates it from the eastern parts of Lichterfelde. Its 19th-century commercial area is centered around the [Lichterfelde West railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin-Lichterfelde_West_station), which also serves nearby [Free University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Berlin) in neighboring [Dahlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahlem_\(Berlin\)). Most of the commercial and residential buildings in Lichterfelde West are protected landmarks.

### Lichterfelde Ost

Lichterfelde Ost, like Lichterfelde West, was also developed as a settlement of mansions, yet many of the estates were damaged in World War II. The Lichterfelde Ost railway station serves as a hub for regional rail and commuter rail and is surrounded by a large commercial area.

### Lichterfelde Süd

Lichterfelde Süd was developed in the 1960s and 1970s and is large made up of suburban housing estates, being a significant architectural deviation from the older mansion settlements. The [Otto Lilienthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Lilienthal) Memorial Park with the artificial conical hill, from which he started many of his flight attempts, is located in Lichterfelde Süd.

- [Otto Lilienthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Lilienthal) monument - the memorial plaque which is located in Schütte-Lanz-Straße 25, Berlin-Lichterfelde, was built in memory of Otto Lilienthal - a German pioneer of human aviation who became known as the Glider King. He was the first person to make successful gliding flights.
