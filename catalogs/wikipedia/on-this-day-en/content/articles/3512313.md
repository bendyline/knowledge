Iceland has been represented at the [Eurovision Song Contest](9954.md) 37 times since its debut in [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1986). It has missed only three (soon to be four) contests since then, in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1998) and [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2002), when prevented from competing due to finishing outside qualification places the preceding years, and the [2026](79992910.md) and [2027](80066921.md) contests due to the inclusion of [Israel](3190362.md) in the context of the [Gaza war](74998519.md). The country's best result is second place, which it achieved with "[All Out of Luck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Out_of_Luck)" by [Selma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selma_Bj%C3%B6rnsd%C3%B3ttir) in [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1999) and "[Is It True?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Is_It_True%3F_\(Yohanna_song\))" by [Yohanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yohanna) in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2009). The Icelandic participating broadcaster in the contest is [Ríkisútvarpið](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%9AV) (RÚV), which select its entrant with the national competition [Söngvakeppnin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B6ngvakeppnin).

Iceland has achieved a total of seven top ten placements, with the others being "[Eitt lag enn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eitt_lag_enn)" by [Stjórnin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stj%C3%B3rnin) finishing fourth ([1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1990)), "[Nei eða já](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nei_e%C3%B0a_j%C3%A1)" by [Heart 2 Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_2_Heart_\(band\)) seventh ([1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1992)), "[Open Your Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Your_Heart_\(Birgitta_song\))" by [Birgitta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birgitta_Haukdal) eighth ([2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2003)), "[Hatrið mun sigra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatri%C3%B0_mun_sigra)" by [Hatari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatari_\(band\)) tenth ([2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2019)), and "[10 Years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_Years_\(song\))" by [Daði og Gagnamagnið](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da%C3%B0i_og_Gagnamagni%C3%B0) fourth ([2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2021)). Since the introduction of the semi-final round in 2004, Iceland has failed to qualify for the final nine times, including four years consecutively (2015–18). To date, Iceland is the only Nordic country that has yet to win the contest.

## Participation

[Ríkisútvarpið](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%9AV) (RÚV) is a full member of the [European Broadcasting Union](10380.md) (EBU), thus eligible to participate in the [Eurovision Song Contest](9954.md). It has participated in the contest representing Iceland since its [31st edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1986) in 1986, 20 years after RÚV was founded.

## History

Iceland's best result in the contest is second place, which it has achieved twice: in [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1999) with the song "[All Out of Luck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Out_of_Luck)" performed by [Selma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selma_Bj%C3%B6rnsd%C3%B3ttir), beaten by [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1999)'s "[Take Me to Your Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_Me_to_Your_Heaven_\(song\))" by [Charlotte Nilsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Perrelli), and in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2009) with "[Is It True?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Is_It_True%3F_\(Yohanna_song\))" performed by [Yohanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3hanna_Gu%C3%B0r%C3%BAn_J%C3%B3nsd%C3%B3ttir), beaten by [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2009)'s "[Fairytale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairytale_\(Alexander_Rybak_song\))" by [Alexander Rybak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Rybak).

However, Iceland's worst result in the contest is last place, which has been achieved four times to date: in [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1989) when "[Það sem enginn sér](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Ea%C3%B0_sem_enginn_s%C3%A9r)" by [Daníel Ágúst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan%C3%ADel_%C3%81g%C3%BAst_Haraldsson) received 0 points, in [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2001) when "[Angel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_\(Two_Tricky_song\))" by [Two Tricky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Tricky) received 3 points, in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2018) when "[Our Choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Choice)" by [Ari Ólafsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ari_%C3%93lafsson) received 15 points in the first semi final, and in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2024) when "[Scared of Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scared_of_Heights)" by [Hera Björk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hera_Bj%C3%B6rk) received 3 points in the first semi final.

With the introduction of semi-finals in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2004), Iceland automatically qualified for the final that year due to [Birgitta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birgitta_Haukdal)'s eighth place the previous year. In [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2008), Iceland reached the final for the first time since then, with "This Is My Life" by [Euroband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurobandi%C3%B0). Iceland qualified for the final in seven consecutive contests between [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2008) and [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2014) before failing to qualify for the final from [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2015) to [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2018). In [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2019), [Hatari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatari_\(band\)) brought the country back to the final for the first time since 2014, finishing tenth, which was followed by a fourth-place finish for [Daði og Gagnamagnið](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da%C3%B0i_og_Gagnamagni%C3%B0) in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2021), Iceland's joint-second best result to date, and a 23rd-place finish for [Systur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systur) in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2022). Further non-qualifications came in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2023) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2024). [VÆB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A6b) brought the country back to the final in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2025) breaking the countries two year non qualification streak. They finished 25th in the final, receiving zero points by the juries. [RÚV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%9AV) withdrew from [2026](79992910.md) due to Israel's inclusion.

Despite these mixed fortunes, Iceland is the second most successful country never to have won the contest (behind only [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest)).

[Sigríður Beinteinsdóttir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigr%C3%AD%C3%B0ur_Beinteinsd%C3%B3ttir) has participated five times (as a member of a group in [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1990) and [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1992), as a solo artist in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1994), and as a background vocalist in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1991) and [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006)). [Hera Björk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hera_Bj%C3%B6rk) has also participated five times (as a backing vocalist in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2008), [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2009) and [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2015), and as a solo artist in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2010) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2024)). Stefán Hilmarsson has participated twice (as a member of a group in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1988) and in a duo with Eyfi in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1991)), as have [Selma Björnsdóttir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selma_Bj%C3%B6rnsd%C3%B3ttir) ([1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1999) and [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2005)), [Eiríkur Hauksson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eir%C3%ADkur_Hauksson) (as a member of a group in [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1986) and as a solo artist in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2007); Eiríkur has additionally participated for Norway in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1991) as a member of [Just 4 Fun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_4_Fun_\(band\))). [Jón Jósep Snæbjörnsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3n_J%C3%B3sep_Sn%C3%A6bj%C3%B6rnsson) entered as a solo artist in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2004) before participating in a duo with [Greta Salóme Stefánsdóttir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greta_Sal%C3%B3me_Stef%C3%A1nsd%C3%B3ttir) in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2012); Greta Salóme later entered as a solo artist in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2016).

## In popular culture

The 2020 [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) comedy film *[Eurovision Song Contest: The Story of Fire Saga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest:_The_Story_of_Fire_Saga)* stars [Will Ferrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Ferrell) and [Rachel McAdams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_McAdams), who portray a fictional duo from Iceland competing in Eurovision. Hannes Óli Ágústsson, who plays Olaf Yohansson in the film, reprised his role for the voting segment of the [2021 contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2021) final, in which he presented the points on behalf of the Icelandic jury.[^cite_note-dingdong-123]
