[Brazil](3383.md) has a diversified [mixed economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_economy). It is the largest in [Latin America](18524.md), the [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere) and the second in [Americas](29833.md), in nominal terms.[^cite_note-33] As of 2026, it is an [upper-middle income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GNI_\(nominal\)_per_capita) and [developing](78449.md) economy with the second largest [GDP](12594.md) in the [Americas](29833.md) in nominal and [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) terms. It is ranked as the tenth largest economy in the world in nominal terms, and the [8th largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(PPP\)) in PPP terms.[^cite_note-IMFWEODE-34][^cite_note-IMFWEODE2-35] In 2024, according to *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)*, Brazil was the 7th largest country in the world by number of billionaires.[^cite_note-36] Brazil is one of the ten chief [industrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_\(economics\)) states in the world according to [International Labour Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Labour_Organization).[^cite_note-37] According to the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) (IMF), Brazil's nominal GDP was US\$2.642 trillion; the country has a long history of being among the largest economies in the world and the GDP per capita was US\$12,313 per capita.[^cite_note-IMFWEODE-34][^cite_note-IMFWEODE2-35]

The country is rich in natural resources. From 2000 to 2012, Brazil was one of the [fastest-growing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth) major economies in the world, with an average annual GDP growth rate of over 5%. Its GDP surpassed that of the United Kingdom in 2012, temporarily making Brazil the world's sixth-largest economy. However, Brazil's economic growth decelerated in 2013[^cite_note-38] and the country [entered a recession in 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%932016_Brazilian_economic_recession). The economy started to recover in 2017, with a 1% growth in the first quarter, followed by a 0.3% growth in the second quarter compared to the same period of the previous year. It officially exited the recession. According to data released on 3 March 2026 by the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE), Brazil's economy grew by 3.4% in 2024 and 2.3% in 2025, indicating robust growth in the last two years. In the fourth quarter of 2025, gross domestic product (GDP) increased by 0.1% compared with the third quarter, when it had also recorded growth of 0.1%, reflecting relative stability in quarterly performance.[^cite_note-39]

According to the [World Economic Forum](172185.md), Brazil was the top country in upward evolution of [competitiveness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_\(companies\)) in 2009, gaining eight positions among other countries, overcoming Russia for the first time, and partially closing the competitiveness gap with [India](14533.md) and [China](5405.md) among the [BRICS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRICS) economies. Important steps taken since the 1990s toward fiscal sustainability, as well as measures taken to liberalize and open the economy, have significantly boosted the country's competitiveness fundamentals, providing a better environment for private-sector development.[^cite_note-weforum.org-40] Brazil is a member of diverse economic organizations, such as [Mercosur](25869317.md), [Prosur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_for_the_Progress_and_Development_of_South_America), [G8+5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G8+5), [G20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-20_major_economies), [WTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization), [Paris Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Club), [Cairns Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairns_Group), and is advanced to be a permanent member of the [OECD](33853117.md).[^cite_note-41]

From a colony focused on primary sector goods (sugar, gold and cotton), Brazil managed to create a diversified industrial base during the 20th century, including the adoption of [artificial intelligence in the industrial sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_in_the_Brazilian_industry), enhancing productivity and innovation. The steel industry is a prime example of that, with Brazil being the 9th largest steel producer in 2018, and the 5th largest steel net exporter in 2018.[^cite_note-42] [Gerdau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerdau) is the largest producer of [long steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_steel) in the Americas, and [Vale](1873300.md) is the largest producer of [iron ore](145352.md) in the world.[^cite_note-43] [Petrobras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrobras), the Brazilian oil and gas company, is the most valuable company in Latin America.[^cite_note-44]

## History

Main articles: [Economic history of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history_of_Brazil) and [Economy of the Empire of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Empire_of_Brazil)

When the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal) explorers arrived in the 16th century, the [native tribes of current-day Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_Brazil) totaled about 2.5 million people and had lived virtually unchanged since the [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age). From Portugal's [colonization of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Brazil) (1500–1822) until the late 1930s, the Brazilian economy relied on the production of [primary products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_products) for [exports](307592.md).

In the [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire), Brazil was a colony subjected to an imperial [mercantile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercantile) policy, which had three main large-scale economic production cycles – sugar, gold and from the early 19th century on, coffee. The economy of Brazil was heavily dependent on African [slave labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_labor) until the late 19th century (about 3 million imported African slaves in total). In that period Brazil was also the colony with the largest number of European settlers, most of them [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) (including Azoreans and Madeirans) but also some Dutch (see [Dutch Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Brazil)), Spaniards, English, French, Germans, Flemish, Danish, Scottish and Sephardic [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Jews).

Subsequently, Brazil experienced a period of strong economic and demographic growth accompanied by mass immigration from Europe, mainly from [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Brazilian) (including the [Azores](3226.md) and [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira)), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Brazilian), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Brazilian), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Brazilian), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Brazilian), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Brazilian), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Brazilian), [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Brazilian) and [Russia](25391.md). Smaller numbers of immigrants also came from the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Brazilian), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Brazilian), [Finland](10577.md), [Iceland](14531.md) and the [Scandinavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_Brazilian) countries, [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanians_in_Brazil), [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Brazilian), [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_Brazilian), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Brazilian), [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Brazilian), [Latvia](17514.md), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Brazilian), [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Brazilian), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Brazilian), [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croat_Brazilian), [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Brazilian), [Malta](19137.md), [North Macedonia](23564616.md) and [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg_Brazilian), the [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Brazilian) (mainly from [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Brazilian), [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Brazilian) and [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Brazilian)), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Brazilian), the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederados) and [South Africa](18947965.md), until the 1930s. In fact, international mass immigration to Brazil during the 19th century had positive effects on the country's [human capital development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_capital). Immigrants usually exhibited better formal and informal training than native Brazilians and tended to have more entrepreneurial spirit. Their arrival was beneficial for the region, not only because of the skills and knowledge they brought to the country themselves, but also because of spillover effects of their human capital to the native Brazilian population. Human capital [spillover effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spillover_\(economics\)) were strongest in regions with the highest numbers of immigrants, and the positive effects are still observable today, in some regions.[^cite_note-45]

In 2007, with a population of over 190 million and abundant natural resources, Brazil is one of the ten largest markets in the world, producing tens of millions of tons of steel, 26 million tons of cement, 3.5 million television sets, and 3 million refrigerators. In addition, about 70 million cubic meters of petroleum were being processed annually into fuels, lubricants, propane gas, and a wide range of hundreds of petrochemicals.[^cite_note-\:0b-46][^cite_note-47]

Brazil has at least 161,500 kilometers of paved roads, more than 150 gigawatts of installed electric power capacity[^cite_note-48] and its real per capita GDP surpassed US\$9,800 in 2017.[^cite_note-\:0-49][^cite_note-50] Its industrial sector accounts for three-fifths of the South American economy's industrial production.[^cite_note-BG-51] The country's [scientific and technological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_science_and_technology) development is argued to be attractive to [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment), in 2019, Brazil occupied the 4th largest destination for foreign investments, behind only the United States, China and Singapore.[^cite_note-52]

The agricultural sector, locally called the *agronegócio* (agro-business), has also been dynamic: for two decades this sector has kept Brazil among the most highly productive countries in areas related to the rural sector.[^cite_note-BG-51] The agricultural sector and the mining sector also supported trade surpluses which allowed for massive currency gains (rebound) and external debt paydown. Due to a downturn in Western economies, Brazil found itself in 2010 trying to halt the appreciation of the real.[^cite_note-53]

One of the most important [corruption cases in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Brazil) concerns the company [Odebrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odebrecht). Since the 1980s, Odebrecht has spent several billion dollars in the form of bribes to bribe parliamentarians to vote in favour of the group. At the municipal level, Odebrecht's corruption was aimed at "stimulating privatisations", particularly in water and sewer management.[^cite_note-54]

In the production of animal proteins, Brazil is today one of the largest countries in the world. In 2019, the country was the world's largest exporter of [chicken meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_meat).[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] It was also the second largest producer of [beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef),[^cite_note-62] the world's third largest producer of [milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk),[^cite_note-63] the world's fourth largest producer of [pork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pork)[^cite_note-64] and the seventh largest producer of [eggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_as_food) in the world.[^cite_note-65]

Agribusiness contributes to Brazil's [trade balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_balance), in spite of [trade barriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_barrier) and subsidizing policies adopted by the developed countries.[^cite_note-BGA-66]

In the space of fifty five years (1950 to 2005), the population of Brazil grew from 51 million to approximately 187 million inhabitants,[^cite_note-67] an increase of over 2 percent per year. Brazil created and expanded a complex [agribusiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agribusiness) sector.[^cite_note-BGA-66] However, some of this is at the expense of the environment, including the [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Basin).

The importance given to the rural producer takes place in the shape of the agricultural and cattle-raising plan and through another specific subsidy program geared towards family agriculture Programa de Fortalecimento da Agricultura Familiar (Pronaf), which guarantees financing for equipment and cultivation and encourages the use of new technology. With regards to family agriculture, over 800 thousand rural inhabitants are assisted by credit, research and extension programs. A special line of credit is available for women and young farmers.[^cite_note-BGA-66]



Brazil is the world's largest exporter of [chicken meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_meat). Farm in [Southern Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Brazil).

With The Land Reform Program, on the other hand, the country's objective is to provide suitable living and working conditions for families who live in areas allotted by the State, an initiative capable of generating [jobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employment). Through partnerships, public policies and international partnerships, the government is working towards guaranteeing [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure) for the settlements, following the examples of schools and health outlets. The idea is that access to land represents just the first step towards the implementation of a quality land reform program.[^cite_note-BGA-66]

Over 600,000 km<sup>2</sup> of land are divided into approximately five thousand areas of rural [property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property); an agricultural area currently with three borders: the [Central-western region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central-West_Region,_Brazil) ([savannah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savannah)), the [northern region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Region,_Brazil) (area of transition) and parts of the [northeastern region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Region,_Brazil) (semi-arid). At the forefront of grain crops, which produce over 110 million tonnes/year, is the [soybean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean), yielding 50 million tonnes.[^cite_note-BGA-66]

In the cattle-raising sector, the "green ox," which is raised in pastures, on a diet of hay and mineral salts, conquered markets in Asia, Europe and the Americas, particularly after the "[mad cow disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bovine_spongiform_encephalopathy)" scare period. Brazil has the largest cattle herd in the world, with 198 million heads,[^cite_note-68] responsible for exports of more than US\$1 billion/year.[^cite_note-BGA-66]

A pioneer and leader in the manufacture of short-fiber timber [cellulose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellulose), Brazil has also achieved positive results within the packaging sector, in which it is the fifth largest world producer. In the foreign markets, it answers for 25 percent of global exports of raw cane and refined sugar; it is the world leader in [soybean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean) exports and is responsible for 80 percent of the planet's [orange juice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_juice), and since 2003, has had the highest sales figures for beef and chicken.[^cite_note-BGA-66]

Brazil has the second-largest manufacturing sector in the Americas. Accounting for 28.5 percent of GDP, Brazil's industries range from automobiles, steel and petrochemicals to computers, aircraft, and [consumer durables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_durable). With increased economic stability provided by the [Plano Real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plano_Real), Brazilian and multinational businesses have invested heavily in new equipment and technology, a large proportion of which has been purchased from U.S. firms.

The [World Bank](36752.md) lists the main producing countries each year, based on the total production value. According to the 2019 list, Brazil has the 13th most valuable industry in the world (US\$173.6 billion). In the [Americas](29833.md), it is second only to the United States (2nd place) and Mexico (12th place).[^cite_note-94] In the [food industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_industry), in 2019, Brazil was the second largest exporter of processed foods in the world.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] In 2016, the country was the 2nd largest producer of [pulp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulp_\(paper\)) in the world and the 8th producer of [paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper).[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] In the [footwear industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footwear_industry), in 2019, Brazil ranked 4th among world producers.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] In 2019, the country was the 8th producer of [vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicles) and the 9th producer of [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel) in the world.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] In 2018, the [chemical industry](58721.md) of Brazil was the 8th in the world.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] In [textile industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_industry), Brazil, although it was among the 5 largest world producers in 2013, is very little integrated in world trade.[^cite_note-111] In the aviation sector, Brazil has [Embraer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer), the third largest aircraft manufacturer in the world, behind [Boeing](18933266.md) and [Airbus](26220236.md).

Brazil holds the position of South America's top oil and gas producer with record output exceeding 4.89 million boe/d in early 2026, primarily driven by massive pre-salt, deep-water offshore projects. State-controlled company [Petrobras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrobras) dominates the sector.

Brazil is a global powerhouse in iron and steel production, ranking as the ninth-largest steel producer and second-largest iron ore exporter worldwide. With 33.3 billion tons of iron ore reserves, the industry is a key economic driver, centered in Minas Gerais and Rio de Janeiro, with major producers including Gerdau and CSN.

The Brazilian pharmaceutical market is a rapidly growing sector, valued at approximately US\$38 billion in 2024 and projected to reach US\$73.79 billion by 2032.

In the last years, the [defence industry](608296.md) in Brazil achieved prominence with exports of more than US\$1 billion per year and sales abroad of high-technology products like the transport jet [Embraer C-390 Millennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_C-390_Millennium), the [Embraer EMB 314 Super Tucano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_EMB_314_Super_Tucano), a light [attack aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_aircraft), the 6×6 armoured personnel carrier [VBTP-MR Guarani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VBTP-MR_Guarani) and others armaments like pistols and rifles by the company [Taurus Armas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurus_Armas).[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-DF21-113] [Embraer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer) is one of the world's top 100 [defense contractors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_contractors).[^cite_note-114]

During the early 1990s, the banking sector accounted for as much as 16 percent of the GDP. ### Energy

The Brazilian government has undertaken an ambitious program to reduce dependence on imported petroleum. Imports previously accounted for more than 70% of the country's oil needs but Brazil became self-sufficient in oil in 2006–2007. Brazil was the 10th largest oil producer in the world in 2019, with 2.8 million barrels / day. Production manages to supply the country's demand.[^cite_note-121] In the beginning of 2020, in the production of [oil](23195.md) and [natural gas](22131.md), the country exceeded 4 million barrels of oil equivalent per day, for the first time. &#x20;Brazil is one of the main world producers of [hydroelectric power](381399.md). In 2019, Brazil had 217 hydroelectric plants in operation, with an installed capacity of 98,581 MW, 60.16% of the country's energy generation.[^cite_note-124] In the total generation of electricity, in 2019 Brazil reached 170,000 megawatts of installed capacity, more than 75% from renewable sources (the majority, hydroelectric).[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126]

In 2013, the [Southeast Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Region,_Brazil) used about 50% of the load of the National Integrated System (SIN), being the main energy consuming region in the country. The region's installed electricity generation capacity totaled almost 42,500 MW, which represented about a third of Brazil's generation capacity. The [hydroelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric) generation represented 58% of the region's installed capacity, with the remaining 42% corresponding basically to the [thermoelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoelectric) generation. São Paulo accounted for 40% of this capacity; Minas Gerais by about 25%; Rio de Janeiro by 13.3%; and Espírito Santo accounted for the rest. The [South Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Region,_Brazil) owns the [Itaipu Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itaipu_Dam), which was the largest hydroelectric plant in the world for several years, until the inauguration of [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam) in China. It remains the second largest operating [hydroelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric) in the world. Brazil is the co-owner of the Itaipu Plant with [Paraguay](23420.md): the dam is located on the [Paraná River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paran%C3%A1_River), located on the border between countries. It has an installed generation capacity of 14 [GW](21347693.md) for 20 generating units of 700 [MW](21347693.md) each. [North Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Region,_Brazil) has large hydroelectric plants, such as [Belo Monte Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belo_Monte_Dam) and [Tucuruí Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucuru%C3%AD_Dam), which produce much of the national energy. Brazil's hydroelectric potential has not yet been fully exploited, so the country still has the capacity to build several renewable energy plants in its territory.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128]

Nuclear energy accounts for about 4% of Brazil's electricity.[^cite_note-uic-139] The nuclear power generation monopoly is owned by [Eletronuclear (Eletrobrás Eletronuclear S/A)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eletronuclear), a wholly owned subsidiary of [Eletrobrás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eletrobr%C3%A1s). It consists of two [pressurized water reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressurized_water_reactor), Angra I, with capacity of 657 MW, connected to the power grid in 1982, and Angra II, with capacity of 1,350 MW, connected in 2000. ### Transport

Main article: [Transport in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Brazil)

See also: [List of highways in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highways_in_Brazil), [Rail transport in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Brazil), [List of airports in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Brazil), and [Ports of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ports_of_Brazil)



Road system in Brazil, with [divided highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_carriageway) highlighted in red.

[Transport in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Brazil) is basically carried out using the [road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road) mode, the most developed in the region. There is also a considerable infrastructure of [ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ports) and [airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airports). The [railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway) and [fluvial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluvial) sector, although it has potential, is usually treated in a secondary way.

Brazil has more than 1.7 million km of [roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roads), of which 215,000 km are paved, and about 17,000 km are [divided highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_carriageway). The two most important highways in the country are [BR-101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-101) and [BR-116](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-116).[^cite_note-150] Due to the [Andes Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes_Mountains), [Amazon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River) and [Amazon Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Forest), there have always been difficulties in implementing transcontinental or bioceanic highways. Practically the only route that existed was the one that connected Brazil to Buenos Aires, in Argentina and later to Santiago, in Chile. However, in recent years, with the combined effort of South American countries, new routes have started to emerge, such as Brazil-Peru ([Interoceanic Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interoceanic_Highway)), and a new highway between Brazil, Paraguay, northern Argentina and northern Chile ([Bioceanic Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bi-Oceanic_railway)).

There are more than 2,000 airports in Brazil. The country has the second largest number of airports in the world, behind only the United States. [São Paulo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo/Guarulhos_International_Airport), located in the Metropolitan Region of São Paulo, is the largest and busiest in the country – the airport connects São Paulo to practically all major cities around the world. Brazil has 44 international airports, such as those in [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro/Gale%C3%A3o_International_Airport), [Brasília](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bras%C3%ADlia_International_Airport), [Belo Horizonte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belo_Horizonte_International_Airport), [Porto Alegre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salgado_Filho_International_Airport), [Florianópolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herc%C3%ADlio_Luz_International_Airport), [Cuiabá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marechal_Rondon_International_Airport), [Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_International_Airport), [Recife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife/Guararapes%E2%80%93Gilberto_Freyre_International_Airport), [Fortaleza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortaleza_Airport), [Belém](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bel%C3%A9m/Val-de-Cans_International_Airport) and [Manaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Gomes_International_Airport), among others. The 10 busiest airports in South America in 2017 were: São Paulo-Guarulhos (Brazil), Bogotá (Colombia), São Paulo-Congonhas (Brazil), Santiago (Chile), Lima (Peru), Brasília (Brazil), Rio de Janeiro (Brazil), Buenos Aires-Aeroparque (Argentina), Buenos Aires-Ezeiza (Argentina), and Minas Gerais (Brazil).[^cite_note-151]

About [ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ports), Brazil has some of the busiest ports in South America, such as [Port of Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Santos), [Port of Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Rio_de_Janeiro), [Port of Paranaguá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Paranagu%C3%A1), [Port of Itajaí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Itaja%C3%AD), [Port of Rio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Rio_Grande), [Port of São Francisco do Sul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_S%C3%A3o_Francisco_do_Sul) and [Suape Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suape_Port). The 15 busiest ports in South America are: Port of Santos (Brazil), Port of Bahia de Cartagena (Colombia), Callao (Peru), Guayaquil (Ecuador), Buenos Aires (Argentina), San Antonio (Chile), Buenaventura (Colombia), Itajaí (Brazil), Valparaíso (Chile), Montevideo (Uruguay), Paranaguá (Brazil), Rio Grande (Brazil), São Francisco do Sul (Brazil), Manaus (Brazil) and Coronel (Chile).[^cite_note-152]

The Brazilian [railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway) network has an extension of about 30,000 kilometers. It is basically used for transporting ores.[^cite_note-153]

Among the main Brazilian [waterways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterways), two stand out: Hidrovia Tietê-Paraná (which has a length of 2,400 km, 1,600 on the Paraná River and 800 km on the Tietê River, draining agricultural production from the states of Mato Grosso, Mato Grosso do Sul, Goiás and part of Rondônia, Tocantins and Minas Gerais) and Hidrovia do Solimões-Amazonas (it has two sections: Solimões, which extends from Tabatinga to Manaus, with approximately 1600 km, and Amazonas, which extends from Manaus to Belém, with 1650 km.

Almost entirely passenger transport from the [Amazon basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_basin) is done by this waterway, in addition to practically all cargo transportation that is directed to the major regional centers of Belém and Manaus). In Brazil, this transport is still underutilized: the most important waterway stretches, from an economic point of view, are found in the Southeast and South of the country. Its full use still depends on the construction of locks, major dredging works and, mainly, of ports that allow intermodal integration.

[Science and technology in Brazil](1525228.md) has entered the international arena in recent decades. The central [agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_agency) for science and technology in [Brazil](3383.md) is the [Ministry of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Science_and_Technology_\(Brazil\)), which includes the [CNPq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNPq) and [Finep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finep). This ministry also has a direct supervision over the [National Institute for Space Research](30875637.md) ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Instituto Nacional de Pesquisas Espaciais* — INPE), the [National Institute of Amazonian Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Amazonian_Research) (Portuguese: *Instituto Nacional de Pesquisas da Amazônia* — INPA), and the [National Institute of Technology (Brazil)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Technology_\(Brazil\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) (Portuguese: *Instituto Nacional de Tecnologia* — INT). The ministry is also responsible for the Secretariat for Computer and Automation Policy (Portuguese: *Secretaria de Política de Informática e Automação* — SPIA), which is the successor of the SEI. The Ministry of Science and Technology, which the [Sarney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Sarney) government created in March 1985, was headed initially by a person associated with the nationalist ideologies of the past. Although the new minister was able to raise the budget for the science and technology sector, he remained isolated within the government and had no influence on policy making for the economy.

With the new ministry, the science and technology agencies increased in size but lost some of their former [independence](62450.md) and flexibility, and they became more susceptible to patronage politics. Most of the resources of the CNPq were channeled to fellowship programs procedures for quality control and no mechanisms to make the fellows active in the country's science and technology institutions. New groups competed for resources and control of the country's agencies of science, technology, and [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education). These groups included political parties, unionized university professors and employees, scientific societies, and special interest groups within the scientific and technological community. The SBPC (Brazilian Society for Scientific Development) shed its image as a semi-autonomous association of scientists to become an active lobbyist for more public resources and the protection of national [technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology) from international competition.[^cite_note-155] Brazil was ranked 52nd in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2025, up from 66th in 2019.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158]

The country's export model, until today, is excessively based on exports of basic or semi-manufactured products, generating criticism, since such model generates little monetary value, which prevents further growth in the country in the long run. There are several factors that cause this problem, the main ones being: the excessive collection of taxes on production (due to the country's economic and legislative model being based on [state capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_capitalism) and not on Free-Market Capitalism), the lack or deficiency of infrastructure (means of transport such as roads, railways and ports that are insufficient or weak for the country's needs, bad logistics and excessive bureaucracy) for export, high production costs (expensive energy, expensive fuel, expensive maintenance of trucks, expensive loan rates and bank financing for production, expensive export rates), the lack of an industrial policy, the lack of focus on adding value, the lack of aggressiveness in international negotiations, in addition to abusive tariff barriers imposed by other countries on the country's exports. Because of this, Brazil has never been very prominent in international trade.

### Sustainable growth

Portuguese explorers arrived in 1500, but it was only in 1808 that Brazil obtained a permit from the [Portuguese colonial government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) to set up its first factories and manufacturers. In the 21st century, Brazil became the eighth largest economy in the world. Originally, its exports were basic raw and primary goods, such as sugar, rubber and gold. Today, 84% of exports are of manufactured and semi-manufactured products.

The period of great economic transformation and growth occurred between 1875 and 1975.[^cite_note-176]

In the last decade, domestic production increased by 32.3%. [Agribusiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agribusiness) (agriculture and cattle-raising), which grew by 47% or 3.6% per year, was the most dynamic sector – even after having weathered international crises that demanded constant adjustments to the Brazilian economy.[^cite_note-177] The Brazilian government also launched a program for economic development acceleration called [Programa de Aceleração do Crescimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programa_de_Acelera%C3%A7%C3%A3o_do_Crescimento), aiming to spur growth.[^cite_note-178]

Brazil's transparency rank in the international world is 75th according to [Transparency International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International).[^cite_note-179]

### Control and reform policies

See also: [Taxation in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_Brazil), [Welfare in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_in_Brazil), [Universal basic income in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_basic_income_in_Brazil), [Poverty in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_Brazil), [Income inequality in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_inequality_in_Brazil), [Unemployment in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_in_Brazil), [Social security in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_security_in_Brazil), and [Pension reform in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pension_reform_in_Brazil)

Among measures recently adopted to [balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_\(accounting\)) the economy, Brazil carried out reforms to its social security (state and retirement pensions) and tax systems. These changes brought with them a noteworthy addition: a Law of Fiscal Responsibility which controls public expenditure by the executive branches at federal, state and municipal levels. At the same time, investments were made towards administration efficiency and policies were created to encourage exports, industry and trade, thus creating "windows of opportunity" for local and international investors and producers.

With these alterations in place, Brazil has reduced its vulnerability: it does not import the oil it consumes; it has halved its domestic debt through exchange rate-linked certificates and has seen exports grow, on average, by 20% a year. The exchange rate does not put pressure on the industrial sector or inflation (at 4% a year), and does away with the possibility of a [liquidity crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidity_crisis). As a result, the country, after 12 years, has achieved a positive balance in the accounts which measure exports/imports, plus interest payments, services and overseas payment. Thus, respected economists say that the country will not be deeply affected by the current world economic crisis.[^cite_note-180]

In 2017, President [Michel Temer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Temer) refused to make public the list of companies accused of "[modern slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_slavery)". The list, made public yearly since the presidency of [Luís Inácio Lula da Silva](99328.md) in 2003, was intended to persuade companies to settle their fines and conform to labor regulations, in a country where corruption of the political class risked compromising respect for the law. The relations of the president-in-office with the "landowner lobby" were denounced by dismissed President [Dilma Rousseff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilma_Rousseff) on this occasion.[^cite_note-181]

### Consistent policies

See also: [Brazil Cost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_Cost) and [Brazilian disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_disease)

Support for the productive sector has been simplified at all levels; active and independent, [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_of_Brazil) and the Judiciary Branch carry out the evaluation of rules and regulations. Among the main measures taken to stimulate the economy are the reduction of up to 30 percent on manufactured products tax (IPI), and the investment of \$8 billion on road cargo transportation fleets, thus improving distribution logistics. Further resources guarantee the propagation of business and information telecenters.

The policy for industry, technology and foreign trade, at the forefront of this sector, for its part, invests \$19.5 billion in specific sectors, following the example of the software and semiconductor, pharmaceutical and medicine product, and capital goods sectors.[^cite_note-182]

### Entrepreneurship



[Embraer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer), a Brazilian company, is the third-largest [commercial jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_jet) maker in the world, just behind [Airbus](26220236.md) and [Boeing](18933266.md).

According to a search of [Global Entrepreneurship Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Entrepreneurship_Monitor) in 2011 Brazil had 27 million adults aged between 18 and 64 either starting or owning a business, meaning that more than one in four Brazilian adults were entrepreneurs. In comparison to the other 54 countries studied, Brazil was the third-highest in total number of entrepreneurs. The [Institute of Applied Economic Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Applied_Economic_Research) (Ipea), a government agency, found that 37 million jobs in Brazil were associated with businesses with less than 10 employees.[^cite_note-184]

Even though Brazil ranks internationally as one of the hardest countries in the region to do business due to its complicated bureaucracy, there is a healthy number of entrepreneurs, thanks to the huge internal consumer market and various government programs.

The most recent research of Global Entrepreneurship Monitor revealed in 2013 that 50.4% of Brazilian new entrepreneurs are men, 33.8% are in the 35–44 age group, 36.9% completed high school and 47.9% earn 3–6 times the Brazilian minimum wage. In contrast, 49.6% of entrepreneurs are female, only 7% are in the 55–64 age group, 1% have postgraduate education and 1.7% earn more than 9 times the minimum wage.[^cite_note-185]

### Credit rating

Brazil's [credit rating](1194146.md) was downgraded by Standard & Poor's (S\&P) to BBB in March 2014, just one notch above junk.[^cite_note-186] It was further downgraded in January 2018 by S\&P to BB−, which is 2 notches below investment grade.[^cite_note-187]
