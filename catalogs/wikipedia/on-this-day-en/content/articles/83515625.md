# Rastafari in Africa



A Rasta street vendor in South Africa's [Eastern Cape](313645.md)

[Rastafari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastafari), a religion that emerged in Jamaica during the 1930s, promoted an [Afrocentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrocentrism) ideology. In particular, it draws on [Ethiopianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopianism) in according Ethiopia a particularly important place in its worldview.&#x20;

Since at least the 1960s, Rastafari has established a presence in Africa itself. This partly arose from Jamaican Rastas moving to Africa, but alternatively involved Africans converting to the religion.&#x20;

## West Africa

Some Rastas in the African diaspora have followed through with their beliefs about resettlement in Africa, with Ghana and Nigeria being particularly favoured.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEdmonds201278-1] In West Africa, Rastafari has spread largely through the popularity of reggae,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESavishinsky1994b26–27-2] gaining a larger presence in Anglophone areas than their Francophone counterparts.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESavishinsky1994b33-3]

Caribbean Rastas arrived in Ghana during the 1960s, encouraged by its first post-independence president, [Kwame Nkrumah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah), while some native Ghanaians also converted to the religion.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWhite2010304,_306–307-4] The largest congregation of Rastas has been in southern parts of Ghana, around [Accra](56824.md), [Tema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tema), and the [Cape Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Coast),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWhite2010314-5] although Rasta communities also exist in the Muslim-majority area of northern Ghana.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiddleton2006152-6] Middleton suggests that Rasta migrants' dreadlocks resembled the hairstyles of the native [fetish priests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetish_priest), which may have assisted the presentation of these Rastas as having authentic African roots in Ghanaian society.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiddleton2006154–155-7] Ghanaian Rastas have also complained of social ostracism and prosecution for cannabis possession, while non-Rastas in Ghana often consider them to be "drop-outs", "too Western", and "not African enough".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiddleton2006161–162-8]

A smaller number of Rastas are found in Muslim-majority countries of West Africa, such as Gambia and Senegal.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESavishinsky1994b31–32-9] One West African group that wear dreadlocks are the [Baye Faal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baye_Faal?action=edit\&redlink=1), a [Mouride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouride) sect in [Senegambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegambia), some of whose practitioners have started calling themselves "Rastas" in reference to their visual similarity to Rastafari.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESavishinsky1994211-10] The popularity of dreadlocks and marijuana among the Baye Faal may have been spread in large part through access to Rasta-influenced reggae in the 1970s.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESavishinsky1994214-11] A small community of Rastas also appeared in Burkina Faso.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWittmann2011158–159-12]

## East Africa

In the 1960s, a Rasta settlement was established in Shashamane, Ethiopia, on land made available by Haile Selassie's Ethiopian World Federation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEdmonds201279-13] The community faced many problems; 500 acres were confiscated by the Marxist government of [Mengistu Haile Mariam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengistu_Haile_Mariam).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEdmonds201279-13] There were also conflicts with local Ethiopians, who largely regarded the incoming Rastas, and their Ethiopian-born children, as foreigners.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEdmonds201279-13] The Shashamane community peaked at a population of 2,000, although subsequently declined to around 200.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEdmonds201279-13]

By the early 1990s, a Rasta community existed in [Nairobi](21482.md), Kenya, whose approach to the religion was informed both by reggae and by traditional [Kikuyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikuyu_people) religion.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETurner199182-14] Rastafari groups have also appeared in Zimbabwe,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESibanda2016182-15] Malawi[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENtombanaMaganga20201-16] and in South Africa;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChawane2014220-17] in 2008, there were at least 12,000 Rastas in the country.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMhango2008234-18] At an [African Union](10634933.md)/Caribbean Diaspora conference in South Africa in 2005, a statement was released characterising Rastafari as a force for integration of Africa and the African diaspora.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENewland2013225-19]
