The **governor of West Virginia** is the [head of government](37570.md) of [West Virginia](32905.md)[^cite_note-wvc7-5-2] and the commander-in-chief of the [state](18618239.md)'s [military forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_National_Guard).[^cite_note-wvc7-12-3] The governor has a duty to enforce state laws,[^cite_note-wvc7-5-2] and the power to either approve or veto bills passed by the [West Virginia Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_Legislature),[^cite_note-4] to convene the legislature at any time,[^cite_note-5] and, except when prosecution has been carried out by the [House of Delegates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_House_of_Delegates), to grant [pardons](288593.md) and reprieves.[^cite_note-6]

Since West Virginia was [admitted to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union) on June 20, 1863, during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), 37 men have served as governor.[^cite_note-7] Two, [Arch A. Moore Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch_A._Moore_Jr.) (West Virginia's 28th and 30th governor) and [Cecil H. Underwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_H._Underwood) (West Virginia's 25th and 32nd governor), served two nonconsecutive terms in office. The longest-serving governor was Moore, who served for three terms over twelve years. The state's first governor after admission into the Union, [Arthur I. Boreman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_I._Boreman), served the most consecutive terms, resigning a week before the end of his third term. Before the state's admission, [Francis Harrison Pierpont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Harrison_Pierpont), the "Father of West Virginia,"[^cite_note-theintelligencer.net-8] was elected governor during the [Wheeling Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheeling_Convention) of 1861. [Daniel D.T. Farnsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_D._T._Farnsworth) was senate president at the time; he filled the last seven days of Boreman's term and remains the shortest-serving governor. Underwood has the unusual distinction of being both the youngest person to be elected as governor (age 34 upon his first term in 1957) and the oldest to both be elected and serve (age 74 upon his second term in 1997; age 78 at the end of his second term in 2001). The current governor is [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_Republican_Party) [Patrick Morrisey](37898732.md), who assumed office on January 13, 2025.

To serve as governor, a person must be at least 30 years old, and must have been a citizen of West Virginia for at least five years at the time of inauguration.[^cite_note-wvc7-1-9] Under the current [Constitution of West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_West_Virginia), ratified in 1872, the governor serves a four-year term commencing on the Monday after the second Wednesday in January, following an election.[^cite_note-wvc7-1-9] The original constitution of 1863 only called for a two-year term.[^cite_note-10] Since 1968, a governor may be reelected any number of times, but not more than twice in a row.[^cite_note-wvc7-4-11] Any partial term served counts toward the limit of two consecutive terms.[^cite_note-wvc7-4-11]

The constitution makes no mention of a [lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_governor_\(United_States\)); if the governorship becomes vacant, the [senate president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_Senate#Senate_President) stands first in the line of succession. If more than one year remains in the governor's term at the time of vacancy, a new election is held for the balance of the term. If less than a year remains, the senate president acts as governor for the remainder of the term.[^cite_note-const_7-16-12] A bill passed in 2000 grants the senate president the honorary title of lieutenant governor,[^cite_note-legis.state.wv.us-13] but this title is rarely used in practice and the terms of the senate president do not correspond with governorships. The same bill states that the line of succession after the senate president will be the speaker of the [House of Delegates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_House_of_Delegates), followed by the state attorney general, the state auditor and former governors, in inverse order of term, that are in residence in the state at the time of the vacancy.[^cite_note-legis.state.wv.us-13]

## Qualifications

Anyone who seeks to be elected Governor of West Virginia must meet the following qualifications:[^cite_note-14]

- Be a citizen of the United States

- Be a resident of West Virginia for at least five years preceding the election

- Be a duly qualified elector of West Virginia

- Be at least 30 years old
