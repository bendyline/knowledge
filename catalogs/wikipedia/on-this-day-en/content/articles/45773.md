**Kinshasa** ([/kɪnˈʃɑːsə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); French: [\[kinʃasa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); [Lingala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingala_language): *Kinsásá*), formerly named **Léopoldville** ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Leopoldstad*) from 1881 to 1966, is the [capital](181337.md) and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) of the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md). Kinshasa is one of the world's fastest-growing [megacities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megacity), with an estimated population of 18.5 million in 2026.[^cite_note-7] It is the [most densely populated city in the DRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo), the [third-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Africa_by_population) and [third-largest metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_Africa_by_population) in Africa, the world's [seventh-most populous city proper](14649921.md) (the most populous outside of [China](5405.md)), and the [fourth-most populous capital city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_capitals_by_population) in the world. It is the leading [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy), [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics), and [cultural center](6065329.md) of the DRC,[^cite_note-Cécile_B._Vigouroux_&_Salikoko_S._Mufwene-2008-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] housing several industries including [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing),[^cite_note-12] [telecommunications](33094374.md),[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] [banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo), and [entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The city also hosts some of the DRC's significant institutional buildings, such as the [People's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Palace_\(Kinshasa\)), [Palace of the Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_the_Nation_\(Kinshasa\)), [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo), [Court of Cassation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Cassation_\(Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo\)), [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_State_\(Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo\)), [African Union City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_City), [Marble Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble_Palace,_Kinshasa), [Government House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_House,_Kinshasa), [Kinshasa Financial Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_Financial_Center), and other national departments and agencies.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

The Kinshasa site has been inhabited by [Teke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teke_people) and Humbu people for centuries and was known as *Nshasa* before transforming into a [commercial hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_area) during the 19th and 20th centuries.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015a-21][^cite_note-Moorsel-1968-22] The city was named Léopoldville by [Henry Morton Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Morton_Stanley) in honor of [Leopold II of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_II_of_Belgium).[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015a-21][^cite_note-Ness-2017-23][^cite_note-Boya-2010-24] The name was changed to Kinshasa in 1966 during [Mobutu Sese Seko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobutu_Sese_Seko)'s *[Zairianisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authenticit%C3%A9_\(Zaire\))* campaign as a tribute to *Nshasa* village.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015a-21] Covering 9,965 square kilometers, Kinshasa stretches along the southern shores of the [Pool Malebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pool_Malebo) on the [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River). It forms an expansive crescent across flat, low-lying terrain at an average altitude of about 300 meters.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015a-21] Kinshasa borders the [Mai-Ndombe](8502103.md), [Kwilu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwilu_Province), and [Kwango Provinces](33835978.md) to the east; the [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River) delineates its western and northern perimeters, constituting a [natural border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_border) with the [Republic of the Congo](19599929.md); to the south lies the [Kongo Central Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_Central). Across the river sits [Brazzaville](52710.md), the smaller capital of the neighboring Republic of the Congo, forming the world's closest pair of capital cities despite being separated by a four-kilometer-wide unbridged span of the Congo River.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015a-21][^cite_note-27]

Kinshasa also functions as one of the [26 provinces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo](3984115.md); it is administratively divided into [24 communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_Kinshasa), which are further subdivided into 365 [quarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo).[^cite_note-28] With an expansive administrative region, over 90 percent of the province's land remains rural, while urban growth predominantly occurs on its western side.[^cite_note-area-29] Kinshasa is the largest nominally [Francophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_distribution_of_French_speakers) urban area globally, with [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) being the language of government, education, media, public services and high-end commerce, while [Lingala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingala) is used as a [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca) in the street. The city's inhabitants are popularly known as *[Kinois](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Kinois)*, with the term "[Kinshasans](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Kinois)" used in English terminology.[^cite_note-Cécile_B._Vigouroux_&_Salikoko_S._Mufwene-2008-8][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

The [National Museum of the Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) is the DRC's most prominent and central museum. The [College of Advanced Studies in Strategy and Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coll%C3%A8ge_des_Hautes_%C3%89tudes_de_Strat%C3%A9gie_et_de_D%C3%A9fense) is the highest military institution in the DRC. The [National Pedagogical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A9_P%C3%A9dagogique_Nationale) is the DRC's first pedagogical university. [N'Djili International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'djili_Airport) is the largest airport in the nation.[^cite_note-33] In 2015, Kinshasa was designated as a [City of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Music_\(UNESCO\)) by [UNESCO](21786641.md) and has been a member of the [Creative Cities Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Cities_Network) since then.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] [Nsele Valley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsele_Valley_Park) is the largest [urban park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_park) in Kinshasa. According to the [2016 annual ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_cities_for_expatriate_employees), Kinshasa is Africa's most expensive city for [expatriate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate) employees, ahead of close to 200 global locations.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

## Toponymy

There are several theories about the origin of the name *Kinshasa*. Paul Raymaekers, an [anthropologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropologist) and [ethnologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnology), suggests that the name derives from the combination of the [Kikongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_language) and Kihumbu languages.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015a-21] The prefix "*Ki(n)*" signifies a hill or inhabited area and "*Nsasa*" or "*Nshasa*" refers to a bag of salt. According to Raymackers, *Kinshasa* was a significant trading site where people from the Lower Congo (now [Kongo Central Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_Central)) and [South Atlantic Ocean](698.md) exchanged salt for goods such as [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron), [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery) and [ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory) brought by those from the Upper Congo (now [Tshopo Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tshopo_Province)).[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015a-21] However, Hendrik van Moorsel, an anthropologist, historian and researcher, proposes that [Bateke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teke_people) [fishermen](242592.md) traded fish for cassava with locals along the riverbank, and the place of this exchange was called "*Ulio*".[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015a-21][^cite_note-Moorsel-1968-22] In [Teke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teke_languages), "exchange" is "*Utsaya*", and "place of exchange" is "*Intsaya*". Thus, the name evolved from *Ulio* to *Intsaya*, and later, under the influence of Kikongo, transformed into *Kintsaya*, eventually becoming *Kinshasa*.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015a-21] *Kinshasa*, also known as *N'shasa*, is regarded as the primary "place of exchange" on the southern bank of the [Pool Malebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pool_Malebo), where [bartering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barter) occurred even before the commercial boom of [Kintambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kintambo).[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015a-21]

The name *Nshasa* is believed to originate from the Teke verb "*tsaya*" (*tsaa*), meaning "to exchange", and the noun "*intsaya*" (*insaa*), referring to any market or place of exchange. It was at this location that Teke [brokers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broker) traded ivory and slaves from the Banunu slave traders, often mistaken for the [Yanzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanzi_people), for European trade items brought by the Zombo and [Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_people) people.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015a-21] Despite the various theories, the historical name of *Kinshasa* is known to have been *Nshasa*, as documented by [Henry Morton Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Morton_Stanley) during his crossing of [Africa](5334607.md) from [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar) to [Boma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boma,_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) in 1874–1877 when he mentioned visiting "the king of Nshasa" on 14 March 1877.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015a-21][^cite_note-Boya-2010-24][^cite_note-Ness-2017-23]

## History

Main articles: [History of Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Kinshasa), [Timeline of Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Kinshasa), and [Kinshasa (commune) § History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_\(commune\)#History)



View of Léopoldville station and port (1884)



Kinshasa village (1912)

In pre-colonial times, the area was inhabited by two trading centres, [Ntamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ntamo) and [Ntsaasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ntsaasa?action=edit\&redlink=1), which were part of the [Tio Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tio_Kingdom).[^cite_note-\:7-39]<sup>: 247–55 </sup>

The city was established as a trading post by [Henry Morton Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Morton_Stanley) in 1881.[^cite_note-40] It was named Léopoldville in honor of Stanley's employer [King Leopold II of the Belgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_II_of_the_Belgians). He would then proceed to take control of most of the [Congo Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Basin) as the [Congo Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Free_State), not as a colony but as his private property. The post flourished as the first navigable port on the Congo River above [Livingstone Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livingstone_Falls), a series of rapids over 300 kilometres (190 miles) below Leopoldville. At first, all goods arriving by sea or being sent by sea had to be carried by porters between Léopoldville and [Matadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matadi), the port below the rapids and 150 km (93 mi) from the coast. The completion of the [Matadi–Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matadi%E2%80%93Kinshasa_Railway) [portage railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portage_railway), in 1898, provided an alternative route around the rapids and sparked the rapid development of Léopoldville. In 1914, a pipeline was installed so that crude oil could be transported from Matadi to the upriver steamers in Leopoldville.[^cite_note-britannica.com-41] By 1923, the city was elevated to capital of the [Belgian Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Congo), replacing the town of [Boma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boma,_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) in the Congo estuary, pursuant to the Royal Decree of 1 July 1923, countersigned by the [Minister of the Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_the_Colonies_\(Belgium\)), Louis Franc.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015a-21][^cite_note-britannica.com-41] This transition, finalized on 31 October 1929, led to the development of a new administrative *[quartier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarter_\(urban_subdivision\))* located between [Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_\(commune\)), then emerging as a major commercial center, and Léopoldville-West, a preexisting settlement. The selected site was named [Kalina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombe,_Kinshasa) (now [Gombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombe,_Kinshasa)) and developed as the colonial administrative center.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Before this, Léopoldville was designated an "urban district", encompassing exclusively the [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_Kinshasa) of [Kintambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kintambo) and the current Gombe, which burgeoned around [Ngaliema Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngaliema_Bay).[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015a-21][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-Luaka-2005-45] Then the communes of [Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_\(commune\)), [Barumbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barumbu), and [Lingwala](32807292.md) emerged. In the 1930s, these communes predominantly housed employees of Chanic, Filtisaf, and [Utex Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TEXAF).[^cite_note-Luaka-2005-45]

In 1941, legislative ordinance n°293/AIMO of 25 June 1941, conferred Kinshasa the status of a city and established an Urban Committee (*Comité Urbain*), with an allocated area of 5,000 hectares and a population of 53,000.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-Luaka-2005-45] Concurrently, it became the colony's capital, the [Congo-Kasaï Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo-Kasa%C3%AF)'s capital, and the [Moyen Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongala) district. The city was demarcated into two zones: the urban zone, comprising Léo II, Léo-Ouest, Kalina, Léo-I, or Léo-Est, and Ndolo; and the indigenous zone to the south. The urban populace swelled in 1945 with the cessation of [forced labor](541048.md), facilitating the influx of native Africans from rural regions. Léopoldville then became predominantly inhabited by the [Bakongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_people) ethnic group.[^cite_note-Luaka-2005-45]

In the 1950s, [planned urban centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_community) such as [Lemba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemba,_Kinshasa), [Matete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matete), and a segment of Ndjili were established to accommodate workers from the [Limete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limete) industrial zone.[^cite_note-Luaka-2005-45] [Lovanium University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lovanium_University), the colony's inaugural university, was founded in 1954.[^cite_note-Luaka-2005-45] By 1957, Léopoldville comprised eleven communes and six adjunct regions: [Kalamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalamu), [Dendale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasa-Vubu,_Kinshasa) (present-day [Kasa-Vubu commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasa-Vubu,_Kinshasa)), [Saint Jean](32807292.md) (now [Lingwala](32807292.md)), [Ngiri-Ngiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngiri-Ngiri), [Kintambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kintambo), [Limete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limete), [Bandalungwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandalungwa), [Léopoldville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombe,_Kinshasa) (current [Gombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombe,_Kinshasa)), [Barumbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barumbu), [Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_\(commune\)), and [Ngaliema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngaliema); along with the adjunct regions of Lemba, [Binza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binza_group), [Makala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makala), [Kimwenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimwenza), [Kimbanseke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimbanseke), and Kingasani. Subsequently, the adjunct regions of Ndjili and Matete were incorporated.[^cite_note-Luaka-2005-45]

After gaining its independence on 30 June 1960, following [riots in 1959](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9opoldville_riots), the [Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_the_Congo_\(L%C3%A9opoldville\)) [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Belgian_Congo_general_election) its first prime minister, [Patrice Lumumba](49240.md) whose perceived pro-Soviet leanings were viewed as a threat by Western interests. This being the height of the Cold War, the U.S. and Belgium did not want to lose control of the strategic wealth of the Congo, in particular its uranium. Less than a year after Lumumba's election, the Belgians and the U.S. bought the support of his Congolese rivals and set in motion the events that culminated in Lumumba's assassination.[^cite_note-47] In 1964, [Moïse Tshombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo%C3%AFse_Tshombe) decreed the expulsion of all nationals of [Republic of the Congo](19599929.md), [Burundi](21490998.md) and [Mali](19127.md), as well as all political refugees from [Rwanda](25645.md).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-NYT-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] In 1965, with the help of the U.S. and Belgium, [Joseph-Désiré Mobutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobutu_Sese_Seko) seized power in the Congo. He initiated a policy of "[Authenticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authenticit%C3%A9_\(Zaire\))", attempting to renativize the names of people and places in the country. On 2 May 1966, the government announced that the nation's major cities would be restored to their pre-colonial names, effective on 30 June, the sixth anniversary of independence.[^cite_note-52] Léopoldville was renamed *Kinshasa*, for [a village named Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_\(commune\)) that once stood near the site. Kinshasa grew rapidly under Mobutu, drawing people from across the country who came in search of their fortunes or to escape ethnic strife elsewhere, thus adding to the many ethnicities and languages already found there.[^cite_note-britannica.com-41]

### Urban history

#### Colonial urban planning



The arrival of the first [locomotive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locomotive) in Léopoldville, Congo Free State, c. 1898



Photograph of [Kinshasa Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gare_de_l'Est,_Kinshasa), c. 1905

European [urban development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning) began in 1881 and was guided by [rigid planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_plan) principles to preserved an orderly and aesthetically pleasing environment.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015f-53] A major urban expansion was the construction of the [Matadi–Léopoldville Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matadi%E2%80%93Kinshasa_Railway), which was initiated in 1890 and completed in 1911. The project was emblematic of colonial progress and served as a critical link between the port of Léopoldville and the coastal city of [Matadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matadi).[^cite_note-Piette-2011a-54] After its completion, the station began to transform, with the erection of [prefabricated residences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefabricated_home) known as "[Danish houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Denmark)", which were imported from Belgium. These permanent structures gradually replaced the temporary tents that had accommodated early explorers, soldiers, and mercenaries.[^cite_note-Piette-2011a-54] As Léopoldville developed into an administrative and military center, agents of the Congo Free State, and, after 1908, those of the Belgian Congo, settled in the area. Europeans often worked as engineers, architects, carpenters, and builders,[^cite_note-Piette-2011a-54] and were supported by mercenaries and trained African laborers, who were instrumental in the physical construction. By 1902, the [Kitambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kintambo) (now [Kintambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kintambo)) area was entirely occupied by European constructions.[^cite_note-Piette-2011a-54] The urban center expanded as surrounding villages grew, drawn by the prospects of employment and commerce. These settlements would eventually constitute the earliest *cités indigènes*, which were designated residential areas for the African population.[^cite_note-Piette-2011a-54]



A 1942 view of Léopoldville from the balcony of the A.B.C. Hotel. Established in 1914, the A.B.C. Hotel was the city's first hotel complex, which was constructed by the *Compagnie Commerciale et Agricole d'Alimentation du Bas-Congo* (ABC), a subsidiary of the *[Compagnie du Congo pour le Commerce et l'Industrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compagnie_du_Congo_pour_le_Commerce_et_l'Industrie)* (CCCI), which also oversaw the construction of the [Matadi–Léopoldville Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matadi%E2%80%93Kinshasa_Railway). The hotel formed part of a broader colonial initiative to expand infrastructure and hospitality services in the Belgian Congo.

[^cite_note-55]

[^cite_note-56]

At this early stage, the connection between Léopoldville (commonly referred to as "*Léo*") and [Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_\(commune\)) consisted of an eight-kilometer track that was often impassable during the rainy season. Transportation was rudimentary, with a few bicycles, limited private cars, and even dromedaries in use, as public transit infrastructure was virtually nonexistent.[^cite_note-Piette-2011a-54] In 1911, George Moulaert, an influential colonial administrator, drafted a comprehensive urban plan for Léopoldville and the greater Kinshasa area that would guide spatial development for decades, while [World War I](4764461.md) accelerated local economic activity.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-Piette-2011a-54] Largely cut off from Europe, the Belgian Congo experienced a period of relative prosperity in which motorboats and trucks increasingly replaced traditional transport such as canoes and human porters, and by the end of the war in 1918 Léopoldville rivaled other Congolese cities and seized the attention of Belgian architects who saw it as a potential model for colonial urban experimentation. Colonial urbanization remained tightly controlled and reflected the aspirations and limitations of Belgian policy,[^cite_note-Piette-2011a-54] and in 1922 a decree mandated that large companies provide housing for their African workers, a requirement that major firms such as [Huileries du Congo Belge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huileries_du_Congo_Belge) (HCB) met by building workers' camps, while smaller enterprises often failed to comply.[^cite_note-Piette-2011b-58] The existing framework, governed by the decree on "the labor contract between natives and civilized masters", proved insufficient to meet growing housing demands, which then prompted [religious missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_missionary) to extend loans to African residents for [self-construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-build) before becoming overwhelmed, after which a special fund was established to provide interest-free loans for house construction using locally available materials.[^cite_note-Piette-2011b-58]

#### Segregation



The [Kinshasa Central Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_Central_Market) was launched in January 1944 to serve the commercial needs of the African population as urban development and colonial segregation measures grew more pronounced.

&#x20;Colonial authorities enforced a [racially segregated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation) urban model in which African residents were confined to separate indigenous [quarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarter_\(urban_subdivision\)), known as *cités indigènes*, such as Léo I and Léo II.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015f-53] These zones were delineated by colonial authorities which allocated land in rudimentary grids and, lacking modern infrastructure such as sewers, were equipped only with basic sanitation facilities, typically simple [pit latrines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pit_latrine) consisting of holes dug or drilled into the ground. During the 1920s, the urban growth brought the indigenous quarters into proximity with European residential areas, particularly near the Matadi–Kinshasa Railway and the [Boulevard Albert 1er](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevard_du_30_Juin) (now [Boulevard du 30 Juin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevard_du_30_Juin)).[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015f-53] This heightened European settlers' concerns about public health and urban hygiene, which prompted the Léopoldville Urban Committee in 1933 to establish a buffer known as the "*neutral zone*", which, although recommended to be 800 meters wide, was implemented at only 250 to 300 meters because of logistical constraints and exemptions granted to some Europeans living in the area.[^cite_note-Mboka-2011-59][^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015f-53] Known as [Parc De Bock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_Botanical_Garden), now the [Kinshasa Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_Botanical_Garden), this buffer was established by District Commissioner Fernand De Bock with agronomist Roeck and included the [Kinshasa Zoological Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_Zoological_Garden), and served as a sanitary barrier and a leisure space for Europeans.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015f-53] The park also contained [vegetable gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitchen_garden) tended by unemployed volunteers,[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015f-53] while immediately south of the neutral zone lay the indigenous area, which encompassed key facilities such as [Ndolo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'Dolo_Airport), [Kinshasa Central Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_Central_Market), [Kinshasa General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_General_Hospital), a TSF wireless station, a [golf course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf_course), a cemetery, and [Camp Léopold II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokolo_Military_Camp) (now [Camp Kokolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokolo_Military_Camp)).[^cite_note-Mboka-2011-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]



A busy [city square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_square) in Léopoldville, 1943

Belgian colonial authorities, motivated by a [paternalistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paternalism) ideology associated with the so-called "[civilizing mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilizing_mission)", initiated efforts to provide formal housing for the African population, particularly for those employed in urban areas. In the aftermath of the [Second World War](32927.md), a large-scale residential project called the *Nouvelle Cité* was launched on 407 hectares in [Dendale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasa-Vubu,_Kinshasa), present-day [Kasa-Vubu commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasa-Vubu,_Kinshasa), which was overseen by the Service de la Population Noire under Dendale's direction, and by 1947, just two years after its inception, it housed over 8,000 residents, including future President [Joseph Kasa-Vubu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kasa-Vubu).[^cite_note-Mboka-2011-59][^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015f-53] To address additional housing needs for the African population, the Office des Cités Africaines (OCA) was tasked with urban development and, beginning in 1949, oversaw a ten-year plan encompassing [urban design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_design), infrastructure, community facilities, and housing construction.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015f-53] By 1959, over 40,000 homes had been built following a controlled, segregationist approach, with planning initially led by urban planner Georges Ricquier and later taken over by Maurice Heymans in 1953, while the new city plan preserved European dominance over the historical center ([La Gombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombe,_Kinshasa)), limited expansion to adjacent zones, and promoted industrial development in [Limete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limete).[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015f-53]

Post-1950s [urban expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl) arose due to the "sprawl of *quartiers*, the high demand for housing among the population, and the increasing distance between riverside employment zones and *cités indigènes*". Communes like Kasa-Vubu and [Ngiri-Ngiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngiri-Ngiri) featured structured housing with proper streets, sanitation systems, [twin houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Houses), and [single-level row buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terraced_house). Some housing areas were self-built but still regulated, with residents adhering to official layouts and construction guidelines based on their resources.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015f-53] Between 1954 and 1960, OCA developed [Matete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matete), [Bandalungwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandalungwa), and [Lemba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemba,_Kinshasa), alongside the [satellite city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_city) of [Ndjili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndjili,_Kinshasa). These included prearranged roads, [drainage systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_system_\(agriculture\)), sewer networks, and paved primary access routes.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015f-53]

For the first time, two-[story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storey) houses and collective apartment buildings appeared, particularly in Bandalungwa, Matete, and Lemba. [Kalamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalamu) followed with the expansion of Camp Kaouka and the 20 May neighborhood beyond the Funa River.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015f-53] Further east, across the Yolo River, residential development continued in Limete and its accompanying industrial zone.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015f-53]

### Unrest in 1991 and the First Congo War

Further information: [First Congo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Congo_War)

In 1991 the city had to [fend off rioting soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Zaire_unrest), who were protesting the government's failure to pay them. Subsequently a rebel uprising began, which in 1997 finally brought down the regime of Mobutu.[^cite_note-britannica.com-41]

The eruption of the [First Congo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Congo_War) (1996–1997), closely tied to the aftermath of the 1994 [Rwandan genocide](676659.md), significantly intensified instability in Kinshasa and across the broader region. Following the genocide, the [Rwandan Patriotic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Patriotic_Front) (RPF), under [Paul Kagame](393593.md), launched military operations into eastern Zaire, not only targeting [Hutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutu) insurgents but also committing large-scale violence against Hutu civilians.[^cite_note-Prunier-2008-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] [UNHCR](31964.md) consultant [Robert Gersony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Gersony) estimated that between 5,000 and 10,000 people were killed monthly in mid-1994 alone. The influx of over two million Rwandan Hutu refugees into eastern Zaire further exacerbated security and humanitarian tensions, particularly in provinces such as [South Kivu](446959.md).[^cite_note-Prunier-2008-62][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] President Mobutu's regime proved incapable of managing the crisis, thus facilitating the conditions for war.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009e-67] By 1996, foreign-backed militias, including the [Rwandan Patriotic Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Patriotic_Army) (RPA), the [Ugandan People's Defence Force](56928.md) (UPDF), and Burundi's *[Forces Armées Burundaises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forces_Arm%C3%A9es_Burundaises)* (FAB), began supporting Congolese [Tutsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutsi) groups such as the [Banyamulenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banyamulenge).[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009e-67] Belgian legal and political scholar [Filip Reyntjens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filip_Reyntjens) describes the First Congo War as the convergence of two overlapping goals: the genuine resistance of Congolese Tutsi, who feared retaliation, and the strategic use of this resistance by the Rwandan government to justify the RPA's military involvement in Zaire.[^cite_note-68]

The fall of Kinshasa in May 1997 to the Rwandan- and Ugandan-backed *[Alliance des Forces Démocratiques pour la Libération du Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_des_Forces_D%C3%A9mocratiques_pour_la_Lib%C3%A9ration_du_Congo)* (AFDL), led by [Laurent-Désiré Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent-D%C3%A9sir%C3%A9_Kabila), was followed by widespread human rights abuses.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009a-69] AFDL and RPA forces carried out [extrajudicial killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrajudicial_killing), acts of [torture](47702.md), [rape](7980471.md), and targeted former regime officials as well as members of the elite [Special Presidential Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Presidential_Division) (DSP).[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009a-69] Between 18 and 22 May 1997, volunteer teams from the [Congolese Red Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Cross_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) collected between 228 and 318 bodies in Kinshasa and its outskirts and evacuated wounded civilians to local medical facilities. Reports from the [United Nations Special Rapporteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_special_rapporteur) and the [United Nations Mapping Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DRC_Mapping_Exercise_Report) indicate that the security situation deteriorated further between May and June 1997.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009a-69] During this period, AFDL and RPA units, often with the participation of civilians, carried out [public executions](4733727.md), frequently incinerating the bodies in neighborhoods such as [Masina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masina,_Kinshasa), [Matete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matete), and Kingabwa ([Limete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limete) commune). At the GLM (*Groupe Litho Moboti*) building, detainees, many of them former members of the [Zairean Armed Forces](8029.md#Zaire_\(1971%E2%80%931997\)) (*Forces Armées Zaïroises*; FAZ) or political opponents, were routinely executed and their bodies discarded in the [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River), a practice halted only after intervention by human rights advocates alerted by local fishermen.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009a-69] In September 1997, the security crisis in Kinshasa was compounded by cross-border shelling from [Brazzaville](52710.md). Armed factions involved in a separate conflict in Brazzaville launched artillery fire into Kinshasa between 29 September and 1 October, killing at least 21 civilians. In retaliation, FAC and RPA forces shelled Brazzaville for two consecutive days.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009a-69]

#### Systematic torture, detention conditions, and crackdowns on political opposition

In June and July 1997, detainees in [military prisons](1505096.md) at [Kokolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokolo_Military_Camp) and [Tshatshi camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_Tshatshi_Military_Camp) suffered ill-treatment that led to numerous deaths from torture, disease, and medical neglect.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009a-69] Reports from late 1997 indicate that at least 24 wounded former members of the [Rwandan Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Armed_Forces) (*Forces Armées Rwandaises*, ex-FAR) disappeared after being transferred from hospitals to military camps, where they were subjected to threats and degrading treatment, while the militarization of Kinshasa's public life continued under the new regime, as units of the [Congolese Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) (*Force Aérienne Congolaise*, FAC) and the RPA, particularly the young [child soldiers](42426471.md) known as *Kadogo*, imposed harsh disciplinary measures on civilians, including public floggings and whippings with the *chicotte*, a "leather-thonged whipping device". These punishments often resulted in severe internal injuries and deaths due to internal bleeding.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009a-69]

Beginning in June 1997, the new authorities ordered ex-FAZ to undergo political re-education at [Kitona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitona) military base in [Bas-Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_Central) (now [Kongo Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_Central)), and in their absence, soldiers from the newly formed FAC and RPA occupied military camps in Kinshasa, including CETA and Tshatshi, where they raped large numbers of women and girls, many of whom were family members of ex-FAZ soldiers. Victims were often subjected to [sexual slavery](173634.md) and [forced domestic labor](541048.md).[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009a-69] At [Kokolo Military Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokolo_Military_Camp), similar atrocities happened, including [gang rapes](1371871.md) and the random abduction and [assault of women](218407.md) in nearby *quartiers*, while at a location known as "Camp Américain", ferocious crimes were reported, such as the case of a girl who was gang-raped, tortured, and had hot [wax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wax) poured over her [genitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genitals).[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009a-69] [Violence against women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_violence_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) extended outside military compounds, as numerous reports from the period reveal that FAC and RPA soldiers carried out systematic sexual violence across Kinshasa, including against [sex workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_worker) and women [detained arbitrarily](13435705.md).[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009a-69]

Opposition activists and their families were frequently subjected to harassment, arbitrary arrest, and torture. Women related to political figures were especially vulnerable to sexual violence during state crackdowns.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009a-69] Activists from major opposition parties such as the *[Parti Lumumbiste Unifié](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parti_Lumumbiste_Unifi%C3%A9)* (PALU), *[Union pour la Démocratie et le Progrès Social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_pour_la_D%C3%A9mocratie_et_le_Progr%C3%A8s_Social)* (UDPS), and the *Front pour la Survie de la Démocratie au Congo* (FSDC) were frequent targets. In July 1997, FAC and RPA soldiers killed between one and four PALU members during a protest crackdown and injured several others.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009a-69] The residence of PALU leader [Antoine Gizenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_Gizenga) was ransacked, resulting in the death of an activist and the severe beating of six more. Documented cases of torture and rape continued into 1998. Notably, on 10 December 1997, two sisters of the FSDC president were gang-raped by FAC and RPA personnel.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009a-69] The FSDC leader himself, a former Mobutu loyalist, was arrested in February 1998 and subjected to torture while detained in the central prison and the Mikonga military training facility.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009a-69]

### Second Congo War

Further information: [Second Congo War](877492.md)



Visit of President [Laurent-Désiré Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent-D%C3%A9sir%C3%A9_Kabila) to [João de Deus Pinheiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_de_Deus_Pinheiro), the European Commissioner for Relations with Parliament, Culture, and Audiovisual, to discuss the armed conflict raging in the country, November 1998

The [Second Congo War](877492.md) also plunged Kinshasa into a period of insecurity and military conflict after a dramatic breakdown in relations between President Laurent-Désiré Kabila and his former allies in Rwanda and Uganda, whom Kabila accused of undermining Congolese sovereignty and holding ambitions to carry out a *[coup d'état](25914154.md)*.[^cite_note-Muiu-2009-70][^cite_note-Katulondi-2019-71] Allegations also surfaced that the [United States](3434750.md) had provided indirect support to Rwanda during this period, including claims of [military training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_education_and_training) by the Rwanda Interagency Assessment Team (RIAT), ostensibly to secure access to the DRC's vast mineral wealth.[^cite_note-Snow-2023-72][^cite_note-73] American individuals and institutions, such as Roger Winter of the [U.S. Committee for Refugees and Immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Committee_for_Refugees_and_Immigrants), were further implicated in allegedly abetting insurgent activities.[^cite_note-Snow-2023-72] In response to these tensions, President Kabila dismissed Rwandan General [James Kabarebe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Kabarebe) from his position as [Chief of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_Armed_Forces_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) and demanded the withdrawal of all Rwandan military personnel from Congolese territory.[^cite_note-Muiu-2009-70][^cite_note-Katulondi-2019-71] This move provoked a swift counteroffensive, as Rwanda and Uganda backed the formation of a new rebel group, the *[Rassemblement Congolais pour la Démocratie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rassemblement_Congolais_pour_la_D%C3%A9mocratie)* (RCD), which launched its rebellion from the city of Goma on 2 August 1998.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009c-74] The insurrection was spearheaded by mutinous units within the Congolese armed forces in coordination with Rwandan, Ugandan, and Burundian troops. Within weeks, RCD forces had captured large swathes of territory in the eastern and northern parts of the country, including regions of [North](446958.md) and [South Kivu](446959.md), [Orientale Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientale_Province), North [Katanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katanga_Province), and [Équateur Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89quateur_\(former_province\)).[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009c-74] Their advance toward Kinshasa and the western province of Bas-Congo was halted by military intervention from [Angola](701.md) and [Zimbabwe](34399.md), both of which deployed troops in support of Kabila's government.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009c-74]

This escalation led to the effective partition of the DRC, as Kabila's administration, reinforced by military contingents from Angola, Zimbabwe, [Namibia](21292.md), [Chad](5488.md), and [Sudan](27421.md), retained authority over the western and central regions. Conversely, the eastern part of the country fell under the control of the RCD's military wing, the *Armée Nationale Congolaise* (ANC), which was supported by Rwandan, Ugandan, and Burundian forces.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009c-74] In response, Kabila aligned with a range of non-state actors and militias, notably the [Mayi-Mayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mai-Mai) as well as Rwandan and Burundian Hutu rebel groups including the *[Forces pour la Défense de la Démocratie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forces_pour_la_D%C3%A9fense_de_la_D%C3%A9mocratie)* (FDD) and the *[Armée de Libération du Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arm%C3%A9e_de_Lib%C3%A9ration_du_Rwanda)* (ALiR), the latter composed in part of former members of the Rwandan Armed Forces and the [Interahamwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interahamwe) militia. Uganda, while occupying substantial portions of Orientale Province, simultaneously sponsored the establishment of the *[Mouvement pour la Libération du Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouvement_pour_la_Lib%C3%A9ration_du_Congo?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (MLC), under the leadership of [Jean-Pierre Bemba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Bemba), to administer the Ugandan-controlled regions of Équateur.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009c-74] Diverging strategic interests between Uganda and Rwanda eventually led to a split within the RCD itself, giving rise to two rival factions: the Rwanda-aligned RCD–Goma and the Uganda-supported RCD–ML.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009c-74] Despite their military superiority, these rebel coalitions faced persistent challenges in exerting full administrative and security control over rural areas, where they were met with sustained resistance from local militias, interethnic hostility, and community mistrust.[^cite_note-75]

By late August 1998, forces of the ANC, RPA, and UPDF clashed with troops from FAC and the [Zimbabwe Defence Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_Defence_Forces) (ZDF) for control of the capital. The ZDF employed heavy artillery to bombard densely populated areas, including [Kimbanseke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimbanseke), [Masina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masina,_Kinshasa), [Ndjili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndjili,_Kinshasa), and the Kingatoko village, which located near the Bas-Congo border.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009b-76] These attacks resulted in the deaths of approximately 50 civilians and left 282 wounded during the night of 27 to 28 August, leading to mass displacement as residents fled to safer parts of the city, while the ZDF's indiscriminate use of heavy weapons struck hospitals, religious buildings, and other non-combatant infrastructure without differentiating between military and civilian targets.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009b-76] In some instances, Congolese military authorities exacerbated civilian casualties by ordering residents to remain in their homes.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009b-76] On 28 August, in [Mont-Ngafula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_Ngafula), FAC soldiers brutally murdered two Red Cross volunteers, one by crushing his skull, as they tried to rescue victims in Mitendi and Mbenseke, which left several others injured.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009b-76] Rebel forces also targeted critical infrastructure, including the [Inga hydroelectric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Inga_Dam) station in [Bas-Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_Central), which was seized by ANC, RPA, and UPDF troops on 13 August 1998 and had its turbines stopped for three weeks, cutting electricity and water to Kinshasa and parts of Bas-Congo and severely disrupting hospitals.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009b-76]

#### Human rights abuses, crackdowns on Cabindan separatists, and Joseph Kabila's rise to power

Political opponents and civilians were subject to extrajudicial executions, torture, rape, and arbitrary detentions.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009b-76] Between August 1998 and January 2001, approximately 50 incident reports were submitted to the [United Nations Commission on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Commission_on_Human_Rights) and its mechanisms, including the [Working Groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_group) on arbitrary detention and enforced disappearances, and the Special Rapporteurs on torture and extrajudicial killings. Members of opposition parties such as the UDPS and PALU were particularly targeted, often detained in notorious facilities including the *Police d'intervention rapide* (PIR), the *Direction des renseignements généraux et services spéciaux* (DRGS, also called Kin Mazière), IPKIN (ex-Circo), and [Kokolo Military Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokolo_Military_Camp).[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009b-76] A major crackdown happened on 28 October 2000, when President Kabila's security forces arrested at least 93 people, including 60 soldiers and 33 civilians from North Kivu, South Kivu, and [Maniema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maniema), accused of plotting a *coup d'état* involving Anselme Masasu Nindaga, a founding AFDL member; some detainees were [summarily executed](1227309.md) or tortured to death, while others were imprisoned for over three years and only released following a government-issued general amnesty. In line with its military alliance with Angola, the Kinshasa government also targeted members of the [Front for the Liberation of the Enclave of Cabinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_for_the_Liberation_of_the_Enclave_of_Cabinda) (FLEC), a separatist movement seeking independence for the Angolan province of Cabinda, shutting down FLEC offices in Kinshasa between 1998 and 1999, arresting numerous Cabindan militants, many of whom were tortured, forcibly transferred to Angola, or remain missing.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009b-76]



[George W. Bush](3414021.md) meets with [Joseph Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kabila) (left), [Thabo Mbeki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thabo_Mbeki) of South Africa (center), and [Paul Kagame](393593.md) of Rwanda (right) at the [Waldorf Astoria New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldorf_Astoria_New_York).

After the [assassination of President Laurent-Désiré Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Laurent-D%C3%A9sir%C3%A9_Kabila) on 16 January 2001, his son and successor, [Joseph Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kabila), was quickly installed as president and prioritized the de-escalation of armed conflict and the promotion of national reconciliation, particularly through the organization of the Inter-Congolese Dialogue (ICD), which brought together government representatives, rebel movements, opposition parties, and civil society in a comprehensive peace process.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009d-77] In March 2001, the [United Nations Mission in the Democratic Republic of Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MONUSCO) (MONUC) deployed personnel to key conflict zones to monitor compliance with the [Lusaka Ceasefire Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusaka_Ceasefire_Agreement), yet violence continued, particularly in North and South Kivu, where clashes involved groups such as the Mayi-Mayi militias, FDD, ALiR, and ANC.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009d-77] The ICD officially opened on 25 February 2002 in [Sun City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_City_\(South_Africa\)), South Africa, and a preliminary [power-sharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_sharing) agreement was signed on 19 April 2002 between Joseph Kabila and [Jean-Pierre Bemba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Bemba), though it faced opposition from RCD-Goma and parties including the UDPS.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009d-77] Progress continued with a major diplomatic breakthrough on 30 July 2002, when Rwanda and the DRC signed a peace accord in [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria), agreeing that Rwanda would withdraw its troops in exchange for the disarmament and repatriation of Hutu militias such as the [FDLR](1676875.md), followed by a similar agreement with Uganda in [Luanda](18402.md) on 6 September 2002 to withdraw Ugandan forces and stabilize [Ituri Province](1545860.md).[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009d-77]

By year's end, foreign troops from Rwanda, Uganda, Zimbabwe, Angola, and Namibia had begun withdrawing, a move that culminated in the Global and All-Inclusive Agreement in Pretoria on 17 December 2002, which established a transitional power-sharing government and integrated former belligerents into a [unified national army](8029.md).[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009d-77] Despite ongoing challenges, the process advanced with the ICD ratifying the agreement on 1 April 2003 in Sun City, along with a memorandum on transitional governance and armed group integration, and transitional institutions were formally inaugurated on 30 June 2003.[^cite_note-Mapping-report.org-2009d-77] Nevertheless, President Kabila, who remained in power until 2019, faced persistent opposition in Kinshasa, with his controversial victory in the [2006 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_general_election) triggering widespread unrest that prompted the deployment of [European Union forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Military_Operation_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_\(2006\)) to support [MONUSCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MONUSCO) in maintaining order,[^cite_note-Trapido2016-78][^cite_note-DR_Congo_election-79] and another unrest emerged in 2016 when the [Independent National Electoral Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_National_Electoral_Commission_\(Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo\)) announced a two-year delay in holding new presidential elections, a decision that ignited mass protests in September and December marked by street barricades, violent clashes, and a high civilian death toll.[^cite_note-DR_Congo_election-79][^cite_note-80]

## Geography



Dawn at the banks of the [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River) in [Ngaliema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngaliema) commune

### Location

Kinshasa is strategically situated on the southern bank of the expansive [Malebo Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pool_Malebo), which covers 9,965 square kilometers in a broad [crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crescent) shape over flat, low-lying terrain with an average elevation of about 300 meters.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] It is bordered to the east by the provinces of [Mai-Ndombe](8502103.md), [Kwilu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwilu_Province), and [Kwango](33835978.md), to the south by [Kongo Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_Central), and to the north and west by the [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River), which forms the [natural boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_border) with the [Republic of the Congo](19599929.md).[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015b-83]

The Congo River, Africa's second-longest river after the [Nile](21244.md), has the continent's highest [discharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discharge_\(hydrology\)) and serves as a critical transportation route across much of the Congo Basin, with river barges navigable between Kinshasa and [Kisangani](588207.md) and along several tributaries. It is also a significant source of [hydroelectric power](381399.md), with the potential downstream of Kinshasa to generate electricity sufficient for roughly half of Africa's population.[^cite_note-84]

### Relief



A view of [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River) from Kinshasa



[Sunset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset) by the Congo River in Kinshasa

Topographically, Kinshasa has a [marshy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh), [alluvial plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvial_plain), with altitudes ranging from 275 to 300 meters, along with hilly terrain that elevates from 310 to 370 meters.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015b-83][^cite_note-86] It has four principal features: the [Malebo Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pool_Malebo), a large [water body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_of_water) with islands and [islets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islet); the Kinshasa Plain, a highly urbanizable area prone to drainage problems; the [terrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrace_\(earthworks\)), a series of low [ridges](714908.md) overlooking the plain; and the hills area, marked by deep valleys and [cirque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirque)-shaped formations.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015b-83]

The Malebo Pool extends roughly 35 kilometers long and 25 kilometers wide, bordered by [Ngaliema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngaliema) in the west and [Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku,_Kinshasa) in the east, and passes through other [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_Kinshasa) including [Gombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombe,_Kinshasa), [Barumbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barumbu), [Limete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limete), [Masina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masina,_Kinshasa), and [Nsele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsele).[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015b-83] The Kinshasa Plain has a banana-like shape and is surrounded by eastward-oriented hills. Comprising mainly [sandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand) alluvial deposits, this plain covers an area of about 20,000 hectares and hosts some of the city's most densely populated and urbanized communes,[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015b-83][^cite_note-Sukisa-2008-87] such as Limete, [Kalamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalamu), [Bandalungwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandalungwa), [Ngiri-Ngiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngiri-Ngiri), [Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_\(commune\)), Barumbu, and [Lingwala](32807292.md). Despite its urban potential, the plain is highly vulnerable to seasonal [flooding](50482.md) and [waterlogging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterlogging_\(agriculture\)) due to inadequate [drainage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_drain) infrastructure, with recurrent inundation occurring during the [rainy season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season).[^cite_note-Sukisa-2008-87]



Sunset along the banks of the Congo River in Kinshasa

The terrace is mainly situated in the city's western expanse, between [N'djili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndjili,_Kinshasa) and [Mont-Ngafula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_Ngafula). It comprises stony blocks of soft [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) and [silica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_dioxide)-covered yellow [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay), topped with brown [silt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silt), and ranges from 10 to 25 meters in height. It retains vestiges of an ancient surface.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015b-83] The hills area commences several kilometers from the Malebo Pool and is characterized by deep valleys and cirque-shaped formations. These hills reach heights surpassing 700 meters and exhibit gentle, rounded contours sculpted by local rivers.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015b-83] While their eastern counterparts may reflect remnants of the [Batéké Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat%C3%A9k%C3%A9_Plateau), their origins in the west and south remain enigmatic.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015b-83] This region forms a natural amphitheater shaped by river [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) processes, which have been exacerbated by anthropogenic activities, often resulting in significant [environmental degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_degradation).[^cite_note-Sukisa-2008-87] Communes located within or adjacent to these hilly areas include Mont-Ngafula, [Bumbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumbu), [Selembao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selembao), [Kisenso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisenso), and Ngaliema. In addition, [Makala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makala) and [Ngaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngaba), although partially located within the plains, gradually ascend into the adjacent highlands.[^cite_note-Sukisa-2008-87]

### Hydrography



Nsele River

Kinshasa's hydrographic landscape is defined by an intricate and predominantly [dendritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_system_\(geomorphology\)#Dendritic_drainage_pattern) network of rivers and valleys that converge toward Pool Malebo. This fluvial system, which predominantly flows from the southern and southwestern highlands toward the north and northeast, is shaped by the region's topographical gradient and its underlying lithological structure.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] The city's current hydrological dynamics are the cumulative result of long-term [tectonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonics) shifts, [sedimentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sediment) processes, and [climatic variability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_variability_and_change) that have defined the development of [river systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_system_\(geomorphology\)) over [geological time scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geologic_time_scale).[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88]





[Ndjili River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndjili_River)

The hydrographic system is composed of two main categories of rivers: [allochthonous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allochthonous) (originating outside the city) and autochthonous (local or internal to the city). The allochthonous rivers, which include the [N'djili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndjili_River), N'sele, and the Pool Malebo, form the city's eastern and northern boundaries.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] These rivers have their sources far beyond the city's southern hills and generally flow in a south-to-north direction. The N'djili River, in particular, is prone to recurrent [seasonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Season) [flooding](50482.md) in its lower reaches, frequently disrupting critical infrastructure.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] In contrast, the autochthonous rivers, such as the Funa, [Lukunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukunga_River), Bombo, Mai-Ndombe, and Mbale, are generated from localized watersheds scattered throughout the urban area.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88][^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015b-83][^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] These rivers often carve deeply incised valleys, especially in the elevated southern regions. The Funa River is commonly but inaccurately referred to as the [Kalamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalamu), a misnomer derived from the [Teke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teke_languages)-Humbu term for "[watercourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream)".[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88]

#### Geomorphology

The current hydrographic network overlays an ancient system, likely dating back to the [Tertiary period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_period), which originally drained from south to north but was subsequently modified by tectonic deformation following the late [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) [peneplanation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peneplanation). These geodynamic changes reoriented some rivers east–west in the eastern parts of the [Congo Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Basin) and west–east in the west.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] Traces of this paleonetwork persist in the form of broad, flat-bottomed valleys known as *dambos* or *dembos*, which emerged during the [Holocene epoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene). Characterized by diffuse channels and lacking pronounced [riverbeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream_bed), these depressions often remain [waterlogged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterlogging_\(agriculture\)) throughout the year due to the persistent [upwelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upwelling) of the [groundwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundwater) [table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_table).[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] In recent centuries, however, increased hydrological stress, marked by heightened peak [runoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runoff_\(hydrology\)), has induced the incision of more distinct channels within these formerly [marshy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh) landscapes.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] This hydrological transformation is evidenced by a marked rise in [surface runoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_runoff) and a reduction in [infiltration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infiltration_\(hydrology\)) retention, resulting in flashier hydrographs, more frequent flood events, and accelerated [erosional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) processes.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] Recent studies estimate Kinshasa's runoff coefficient has increased to approximately 13%, a stark contrast to the near-zero levels before urban expansion and [land-use change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-use_change).[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88]

#### Aquifers and water table



A view of Congo River from Kinshasa

Closely connected to the surface drainage network are the city's groundwater systems, particularly the perched [aquifers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquifer) found in its permeable [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) substratum.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] These aquifers, often extending beyond the limits of their corresponding surface [catchments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catchment_hydrology), are sustained by deep [percolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percolation) facilitated by the sandy and porous characteristics of the regional soils.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] During the [dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season), rivers are primarily fed by these underground aquifers, which are recharged during the [rainy season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season). Early seasonal rains are absorbed into the soil and do not contribute to surface runoff until the retention threshold is surpassed, at which point excess water seeps downward to replenish groundwater reserves.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] The region's high [soil permeability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permeability_of_soils) also facilitates the formation of erosion cirques, particularly on steep slopes where groundwater undercutting and concentrated surface runoff progressively destabilize hillsides, often culminating in [landslides](37208.md).[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] These geomorphic hazards have become increasingly pronounced in the city's rapidly urbanizing fringe zones, where informal settlement expansion, [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation), and inadequate [land-use planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-use_planning) intensify environmental vulnerability.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88]

### Soil and geology



[Mount Mangengenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Mangengenge) is located southeast of Kinshasa

The city's soils predominantly belong to the Arenoferrasol classification,[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] characterized by fine-textured [sands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand) with [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay) content typically below 20%, low [organic matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_matter), and saturated absorbent complexes.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015b-83] These soils has a strong [acidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid) and are chemically deficient.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] Despite their limited nutrient availability, they experience regular rainfall for approximately eight to nine months annually, resulting in significant leaching and intense [hydrolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrolysis) of soil [minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral). This extended exposure to precipitation adversely affects [soil fertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_fertility) and agricultural potential.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88]

Geologically, Kinshasa is situated on a [Precambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precambrian) basement complex, mainly composed of finely stratified red [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) that often contains [feldspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feldspar), visible at the base of the rapids near Mount Ngaliema and south of the N'djili River, where the rock shows strong resistance to erosion.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015b-83]

The soil is shaped by these geological variations: [schisto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schist)-[limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) formations, along with older [lithologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithology) associated with the West-Congolian Orogeny, generate yellow, clay-rich soils that are largely sterile and exhibit low permeability proportional to their sand content, while schist-sandstone and clay-limestone substrata produce yellow to light brown clayey sands with slightly better permeability but minimal fertility.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] Fertile soils are primarily located within [alluvial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvium) and [colluvial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colluvium) deposits, where sediment accumulation enhances nutrient content and moisture retention.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88]

In elevated terrains and along the slopes of deeply incised valleys, hydric erosion actively reshapes the schist-sandstone relief and ancient [massifs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massif). In these areas, soils are predominantly grayish sandy or clay-sandy in texture.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] In lower-lying regions, a distinctive local material known as *Lemba* sand is prevalent, although certain pockets of clay-sandy soils yield improved crop outcomes.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88]

### Vegetation





[Kinshasa Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_Botanical_Garden)

Vegetation consists of gallery [forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest), [savannas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna), and semi-[aquatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquatic_plant) to [aquatic plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquatic_plant) communities, particularly within the valleys surrounding the Pool Malebo.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015b-83] The hilly zones of the region were originally dominated by grassland species, notably *[Loudetia demeusei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loudetia#species)* and *[Schizachyrium semiberbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schizachyrium)*, which are characteristic tufted hemicryptophytes.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] However, the natural vegetation has undergone substantial transformation due to [anthropogenic influences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_impact_on_the_environment), most notably [urban sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl). As the city expanded, inhabitants introduced a range of [fruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit)-bearing trees and [ornamental plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornamental_plant), often cultivated as living hedges.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88]

The gallery forests, typically aligned along principal watercourses and occupying humid valleys associated with the Guineo-Congolese ombrophile-type, have experienced progressive degradation.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015b-83] These once-continuous forest belts have been reduced to fragmented pre-forest fallows, consisting of [secondary growths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_growth) of varying maturity and structure.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015b-83] Historical vegetation mapping reveals that Kinshasa's [forest cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_cover) declined significantly over the latter half of the 20th century: from 46% in 1960 to 36% in 1982, and plummeting further to just 15% by 1987.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] In parallel, the coverage of forest–savanna mosaics and terrestrial [grassland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassland) formations expanded, rising from 48% in 1960 to 56% in 1982, and reaching 64% by 1987.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88] In contrast, areas dominated by aquatic and [swamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamp) vegetation remained relatively stable throughout this period.[^cite_note-Imwangana-2010-88]

### Residential and commercial areas

Kinshasa is a city of sharp contrasts, with affluent residential and commercial areas and three universities alongside sprawling slums.[^cite_note-Flouriot2013-93] The older and wealthier part of the city (*ville basse*) is located on a flat area of [alluvial sand and clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvium) near the river, while many newer areas are found on the [eroding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) red soil of surrounding hills.[^cite_note-Kayembe2009-2][^cite_note-Trapido2016-78] Older parts of the city were laid out on a geometric pattern, with *de facto* racial segregation becoming *de jure* in 1929 as the European and African neighborhoods grew closer together. [City plans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning) of the 1920s–1950s featured a *[cordon sanitaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordon_sanitaire_\(politics\))* or buffer between the white and black neighborhoods, which included the central market as well as parks and gardens for Europeans.[^cite_note-BeeckmansBigon2016-94]

Urban planning in post-independence Kinshasa has been limited. The *Mission Française d'Urbanisme* drew up some plans in the 1960s which envisioned a greater role for [automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile) transportation but did not predict the city's significant population growth. Thus much of the [urban structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_structure) has developed without guidance from a master plan. According to [UN-Habitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN-Habitat), the city is expanding by eight square kilometers per year. It describes many of the new neighborhoods as [slums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slum), built in unsafe conditions with inadequate infrastructure.[^cite_note-ChirisaEtAl2017-95] Nevertheless, spontaneously developed areas have in many cases extended the [grid street plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_plan) of the original city.[^cite_note-Flouriot2013-93]

### Climate

Under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), Kinshasa has a [tropical wet and dry climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_wet_and_dry_climate) (*Aw*). Its lengthy rainy season spans from October through May, with a relatively short dry season, between June and September. Kinshasa lies south of the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator), so its dry season begins around its winter solstice, which is in June. This is in contrast to African cities further north featuring this climate where the dry season typically begins around December. Kinshasa's dry season is slightly cooler than its wet season, though temperatures remain relatively constant throughout the year.

## Demographics

### Population



Night view of Kinshasa along [Boulevard du 30 Juin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevard_du_30_Juin)

Kinshasa is the [most populous city in the Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo#Major_cities_by_population), with its metropolitan area estimated at around 18,552,800 people as of January 2026,[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] making it the nation's most densely populated city, [Africa's third-largest metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_Africa_by_population), and the [fourth-most populous capital in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_capitals_by_population).

The city has experienced rapid demographic growth since the early 1900s, driven by [migration from rural areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_flight), [natural population increase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate_of_natural_increase), and [political instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_decay). Its population grew from 5,000 in 1889 to 10,000 in 1910 and 39,530 in 1930, with an annual rise of roughly 4,700 people.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015c-105] Between 1935 and 1945, the growth rate rose from 1.1% to 1.5% annually due to wartime economic mobilization,[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015c-105] and the 1940s saw the population expand from 50,000 to more than 200,000.[^cite_note-106] By the time [Congo gained independence in 1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congolese_Independence_Speech), Kinshasa covered 5,500 hectares and had 400,000 residents. Subsequent decades saw increased urban migration, conflict-related displacements, and a 3.8% annual growth rate, bringing the population to 2.6 million in 1984,[^cite_note-Bukasa-2015-107][^cite_note-Bédécarrats2016-108] 5.3–7.3 million in 2005,[^cite_note-Flouriot2013-93] and nearly 12 million by 2015.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015c-105]

According to [United Nations Human Settlements Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Human_Settlements_Programme), the city receives an average of 390,000 new migrants annually, with many fleeing conflict or economic hardship.[^cite_note-Iazzolino2016-110] A 2017 projection anticipated that Kinshasa's metropolitan population will reach 35 million by 2050, 58 million by 2075, and 83 million by 2100, making it one of the largest projected urban areas on the planet.[^cite_note-111]

#### Ethnic groups and migration

The original inhabitants of Kinshasa included the Humbu, [Teke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teke_people), and [Bamfununga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mfinu_language), alongside the [Yaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaka_people) and Banunu Bobangi from [Kongo Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_Central) and the river-trading [Bayanzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanzi_people). When [Henry Morton Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Morton_Stanley) arrived at the [Pool Malebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pool_Malebo), these groups formed the majority of the population, but as the area's influence grew, additional Congolese and African groups, including the [Lari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lari_people_\(Congo\)) from the Republic of the Congo and the Zombo from Angola, were drawn in, often by commercial opportunities.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015d-112] These newcomers settled on Humbu lands, where they established small villages such as Mikwa, Ngabwa, Ndolo, and Mfumo, some of which later developed into larger settlements. The Teke, who were "neither very mercantile nor warlike", were welcomed by the Humbu for protection against neighboring populations.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015d-112] Disputes arose, however, when chief Ngaliema ceded Humbu land to Stanley without consulting its traditional owners, which, according to scholars Sylvain Shomba Kinyamba, François Mukoka Nsenda, Donatien Olela Nonga, T.M. Kaminar, and W. Mbalanda, demonstrates that the Teke were not landowners in the same way as the Humbu and the Bamfunuga.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015d-112] The establishment of Pool Malebo and the subsequent growth of Kinshasa drew Congolese from various regions, as well as [West Africans](67393.md), Europeans, and Asians, who all sought employment as Kinshasa developed.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015d-112]

The city's [internal migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_migration) began during the colonial era and intensified after independence in 1960. Waves of Congolese from various provinces moved to Kinshasa in search of economic opportunity, political stability, and access to education and services.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015d-112] During the colonial era, significant migrations happened along two main routes: the river route for populations from the north and center of the Congo River, and the road route through [Bandundu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandundu_Province) and Kongo Central. This movement intensified after independence and peaked during the political transition of the 1990s and the First and Second Congo Wars in the east, which transformed Kinshasa into a refuge for displaced populations from across the country.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015d-112]



A woman dressed in a traditional African dress with a bright pink headscarf beside her young daughter wearing a maroon [hijab](68301.md) and a patterned skirt in Kinshasa

Beyond domestic migrations, Kinshasa has historically attracted transnational populations, including the Lari, who settled on fertile, vacant land and were the first to introduce various vegetables, such as cabbage, carrots, tomatoes, and [cucumbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucumber). The *Coastmen*, or *Ndingari*, a term that refers to West African migrants from present-day [Nigeria](21383.md), [Ghana](12067.md), [Mali](19127.md), [Senegal](27278.md), [Benin](3459.md), [Guinea](12176.md), and [Togo](30138.md).[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015d-112] Predominantly Muslim, they were primarily employed in public works, education, commerce, and other trades. They also influenced local culture by introducing [polygamy](24475.md) and [wax-printed fabrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_wax_prints) (*[pagnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrapper_\(clothing\))* wax).[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015d-112] Among the most demographically and culturally prominent expatriate communities are the Zombo, originally from [Maquela do Zombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maquela_do_Zombo) in Angola's [Uíge Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U%C3%ADge_Province). As the most populous foreign African demographic in Kinshasa, they initially migrated as traders and laborers and, from the 1950s onward, were extensively conscripted to work at the port of [CITAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compagnie_Industrielle_et_de_Transports_au_Stanley_Pool), where they primarily handled and transported colonial commodities such as palm derivatives, copal resin, cotton, and ivory.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015d-112]

The Zombo became known for their physical endurance and work ethic, making them ideal laborers in the eyes of colonial authorities. Outside the workplace, they introduced door-to-door trade in indigenous *quartiers*, selling rice, doughnuts, and beans, a practice that later evolved into mobile [street vending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_\(trade\)) with handmade carts (*pousse-pousse*).[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015d-112] Their numbers escalated markedly during World War II, spurred by economic incentives in the Belgian Congo and by repressive governance in Angola under [António de Oliveira Salazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_de_Oliveira_Salazar). The authoritarian policies of the [*Estado Novo*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estado_Novo_\(Portugal\)) regime, particularly the brutal enforcement tactics of the [PIDE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PIDE), pushed many Angolans to seek refuge in Kinshasa.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015d-112] Belgian colonial authorities, constrained by porous borders and entrenched kinship networks among the [Bakongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_people) across both nations, were largely ineffectual in stemming this border-crossing migration.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015d-112] In contrast to other diasporic groups, the Zombo showed a strong tendency toward integration and avoided repatriation while raising successive generations in Kinshasa, where they became culturally and linguistically assimilated. Zombo youths were often enlisted in the *[Force Publique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_Publique)*, while their elders became known for their frugality, temperance, and entrepreneurial self-sufficiency.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015d-112] Over time, many transitioned into artisanal vocations and small-scale manufacturing, and introduced handcrafted women's footwear fashioned from repurposed automobile tires.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015d-112]

Long-established communities such as the [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_people), [Greeks](42056.md), [Indians](7564733.md), and [Pakistanis](28171281.md) have remained active.[^cite_note-\:5-113]

### Language

The official language is French (See: [Kinshasa French vocabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_French#Kinshasa_French)). Kinshasa is the largest officially [Francophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francophonie) city in the world, though many residents struggle to speak it.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-Nadeau-115][^cite_note-Trefon-116] The city was the host of the [14th Francophonie Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_internationale_de_la_Francophonie) in October 2012.[^cite_note-117] Four [national languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo), such as [Lingala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingala), [Tshiluba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luba-Kasai_language), [Kikongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_language), and [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Swahili), are also spoken, along with several vernacular languages.[^cite_note-\:5-113][^cite_note-Manning-118]

## Government and politics

### Administrative history

Founded on 1 August 1881, as Léopold II's Station, Kinshasa has maintained a distinct administrative status over time, eventually becoming the administrative center for the [Stanley Pool District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Pool_District), Haute-N'sele, and Panzi-Kasaï.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015h-119] A [Royal Decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_order) promulgated on 11 April 1914 instituted a territorial reform in the [Belgian Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Congo), reaffirming Kinshasa's dual role as the colonial capital and the central administrative seat for the districts of [Bas-Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas-Congo_District), [Kwango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwango_District), [Kasaï](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasai_District), [Sankuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankuru_District), and Léopoldville.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015h-119] In 1941, Legislative Ordinance No. 293/AIMO of 25 June granted Kinshasa official city status and established an Urban Committee. Following Congolese independence, the colonial charter was replaced by the Fundamental Law of 19 May 1960, which designated Kinshasa as a neutral city and the political seat of national institutions.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015h-119] This legal evolution culminated in Ordinance No. 68/024 of 20 January 1968, which granted Kinshasa the same politico-administrative status comparable to that of the provinces, thus expanding its [communal subdivisions from 11 to 24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_Kinshasa). The advent of the Third Republic, as codified in the 2006 Constitution, officially institutionalized Kinshasa as a fully-fledged province.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015h-119]

### Government

Pursuant to Article 2 of Law No. 08/012 of 31 July 2008, a province is defined as a political and administrative component of the national territory endowed with legal personality and managerial autonomy over its human, economic, financial, and technical resources.[^cite_note-Leganet.cd-2008a-120][^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015h-119] Kinshasa exercises its constitutional mandates through two principal organs: the [Provincial Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Assembly_of_Kinshasa) and the Provincial Government.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015h-119] The Provincial Assembly serves as the city's [legislative body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_body).[^cite_note-Leganet.cd-2008a-120][^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015h-119] Comprising 48 deputies elected by [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage), the Assembly is administratively structured with a Bureau composed of a [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo), [Vice-President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-presidents_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo), [Questeur](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Questeur%20\(parlement\)), [Rapporteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapporteur), and Deputy Rapporteur.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015h-119]

The provincial executive branch is constituted by the [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Kinshasa), the Vice-Governor, and a cabinet of provincial ministers.[^cite_note-Leganet.cd-2008a-120][^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015h-119] The Provincial Director, serving as the Governor's principal administrative advisor, oversees the coordination of fifty departmental directorates and inspectorates.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015h-119] These entities function as provincial extensions of national ministries, entrusted with the territorial execution of state policies and regulatory directives.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015h-119]

#### Communes and neighborhood

The 24 communes are recognized as administrative subdivisions of the city, each further divided into smaller entities known as *[quartiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo)* (neighborhoods). As decentralized territorial entities (*entités territoriales décentralisées*, ETD), the communes have legal personality and administrative autonomy, and operates through two main governing bodies: the *Conseil Communal* (Communal Council) and the *Collège Exécutif Communal* (Communal Executive College).[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015i-121]

- The *Conseil Communal* serves as the commune's [deliberative body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deliberative_assembly). Its members, known as *Conseillers Communaux*, are elected by direct [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage). The council deliberates on all matters of communal interest, including those of an economic, social, cultural, or technical nature. It also elects the *[Bourgmestre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgomaster)* ([Mayor](101146.md)) and [Deputy Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_mayor) through [indirect suffrage](652583.md) and oversees the implementation of the executive's program of action.

  [^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015i-121]

- The *Collège Exécutif Communal* is responsible for the management of the commune and the implementation of decisions made by the Communal Council. It comprises the *Bourgmestre*, Deputy *Bourgmestre*, and two communal [aldermen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alderman) (*échevins communaux*), all appointed based on merit, credibility, and community representation. This executive body coordinates all tasks of communal interest and is led by the Bourgmestre, assisted by the Deputy.

  [^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015i-121]



Two women carrying a transparent ballot box marked with the logo of the [Independent National Electoral Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_National_Electoral_Commission_\(Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo\)) (CENI) at the [Palais du Peuple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_du_Peuple_\(Kinshasa\))

Every commune is managed by a *Bourgmestre* and their Deputy, and each is provisioned with public services such as [civil registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_registration) (*État civil*), sanitation, and general population services. These are supported by technical services representing central ministries, made available to municipal authorities to enable the exercise of devolved functions.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015i-121] These technical services include [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning) and development, rural infrastructure, [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), [fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishery) and [livestock](25160767.md), [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health), education, [environmental protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_protection), [alternative energy](25784.md), finance, budget, [demographic statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_statistics), and more.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015i-121] Additionally, specialized branches such as the [Agence Nationale de Renseignements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agence_nationale_de_renseignements_\(Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo\)) (ANR), the [Direction Générale de Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direction_G%C3%A9n%C3%A9rale_de_Migration) (DGM), and the Commissariat Communal of the [Congolese National Police](51178598.md) maintain a presence at the communal level.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015i-121]

Neighborhoods (*quartiers*) function as subunits of the communes. As of 2015, the 24 communes of Kinshasa are divided into approximately 310 neighborhoods. These *quartiers* vary in structure depending on whether they fall within formally planned urban zones or more informally developed areas.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015i-121] Popular [semi-urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peri-urbanisation) neighborhoods often exceed the size of planned residential districts and represent the core of the city's urban makeup. Each neighborhood is administered through a simplified structure that includes the *chef de quartier* (neighborhood chief), the *chef de quartier adjoint* (deputy), the *secrétaire du quartier* (secretary), the *chargé de la population* (population officer), and two to three agents *recenseurs* (enumerators).[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015i-121] These officials are appointed by the Governor of Kinshasa and assigned to their respective territories.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015i-121] In highly populated semi-urban neighborhoods, administrative overload is common due to insufficient infrastructure for public health, urban roads, potable water, and electricity, resources that remain concentrated in lower-density, affluent residential areas.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015i-121]

### Politics

As of 2015, the [Mouvement de Libération du Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_the_Congo) (MLC) held legislative predominance; however, the [provincial elections of 20 December 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_general_election#Provincial_assemblies) resulted in a power shift in favor of the presidential coalition, the [Union Sacrée de la Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Union_of_the_Nation) (USN), led by President [Félix Tshisekedi](57511508.md)'s [Union pour la Démocratie et le Progrès Social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_Democracy_and_Social_Progress) (UDPS), which secured 14 seats.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015h-119] Daniel Bumba assumed the governorship on 21 June 2024.[^cite_note-122] Kinshasa wields sovereign powers, encompassing the authority to issue [passports](15303414.md) and to act on behalf of the country in international forums.[^cite_note-Trapido2016-78] The city also hosts the headquarters of the [United Nations Organization Stabilization Mission in the Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MONUSCO) (MONUSCO), formerly known as MONUC.[^cite_note-123] In 2016, the United Nations bolstered its peacekeeping presence in Kinshasa in response to civil unrest related to President [Joseph Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kabila)'s controversial extension of power.[^cite_note-124]

In addition to state institutions, international and [non-governmental organizations](46539.md) exert significant influence on local development and governance.[^cite_note-125] The Belgian development agency, [Enabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enabel), has been a key actor since 2016 through its sponsorship of the Programme d'Appui aux Initiatives de Développement Communautaire (PAIDECO), a €6 million initiative aimed at stimulating economic development in the region. The initial implementation took place in [Kimbanseke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimbanseke), a densely populated hill commune with an estimated population of nearly one million.[^cite_note-126] In a strategic effort to address urban overcrowding and stimulate regional development, the provincial government established the *Comité Stratégique pour la Supervision du Projet d'Extension de la Ville de Kinshasa* (CSSPEVK) in October 2023. The committee, under the leadership of a provincial coordinator, was tasked with overseeing the "Kinshasa Kia Mona" urban expansion project in the Maluku district.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

## Economy

See also: [Economy of the Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo)





Street scenes in Kinshasa, Zaire, captured in 1973, by Dutch photographer Rob Mieremet

Historically, Kinshasa experienced a period of robust economic growth driven predominantly by a flourishing industrial sector. During its economic peak, often nostalgically referred to as *la **belle époque***, the city's industrial activities spanned diverse domains including [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing), [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile), [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy), and [assembly-line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_line) production.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015j-130] These industries produced goods for [domestic consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumption_\(economics\)) and international export. It was during this era that Kinshasa earned the affectionate monikers ***Kin la belle***, ***Kin-Kiese***, and ***Kin la joie***.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015j-130] By the 1970s, Kinshasa had become a critical economic hub in Zaire, employing approximately 25% of the country's salaried workforce and accounting for nearly half (50%) of the national wage bill. Surveys conducted between 1974 and 1977 indicated that 33.7% of Kinshasa's working population held positions as executives or skilled laborers, with respective shares of 6.5% and 27.2%.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015j-130] In terms of industrial concentration, Kinshasa in 1977 accounted for 49.9% of the country's [manufactured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing) industries.[^cite_note-Sukisa-2008-87] It ranked second in [secondary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_sector) activity with 18%, following [Katanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katanga_Province), which held 67.3%. In the [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector), Kinshasa ranked first with 27.3%, followed by Katanga at 22%. The city also hosted 22.8% of all [registered businesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_register) nationwide, compared to 18% in Katanga.[^cite_note-Sukisa-2008-87] By 1980, an estimated 412,000 people were in stable employment, supporting an average household of six. However, by the 1990s, inflation and economic deterioration significantly diminished [purchasing power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power).[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015j-130] Studies revealed that by 1988, only 20% of workers could meet basic [living costs](522934.md) on their wages, and by 1990, Kinshasa had become more expensive to live in than other interior cities of the country.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015j-130] In 1977, wages could cover 62% of basic needs and 51% of family needs; these figures declined steeply in the subsequent decades.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015j-130]



Modern buildings on the outskirts of Kinshasa, c. 1980–1993

&#x20;The 1990s marked a particularly devastating decade for Kinshasa's economy, beginning with waves of looting in 1991 and 1992. These events inflicted severe damage on the city's economic infrastructure. In the aftermath of these upheavals, an estimated 300,000 executives and skilled workers lost their jobs, with no prospect of compensation.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015j-130] According to the Agence Nationale des Entrepreneurs du Zaïre (ANEZA), Kinshasa lost approximately 100,000 jobs during this period. Subsequent years of [political instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_decay) and warfare further paralyzed the city's already weakened economic sectors.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015j-130] Since then, industrial activity in Kinshasa has largely stagnated or been dismantled altogether. The city's rapidly expanding population, combined with a shortage of economic reintegration mechanisms for [unemployed graduates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_unemployment) from technical and tertiary institutions, has worsened the unemployment crisis.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015j-130] The dismantling of the industrial sector has been significant, with much of the wage-earning population absorbed into the [informal sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informal_economy). This informal economy has now become the city's primary employment source, engaging nearly 70% of Kinshasa's total labor force.[^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015j-130]

### Companies, foreign exchange reserves, international support

Big manufacturing companies such as Marsavco S.A., All Pack Industries and Angel Cosmetics are located in the center of town (Gombe) in Kinshasa.

There are many other industries, such as [Trust Merchant Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_Merchant_Bank), located in the heart of the city. Food processing is a major industry, and construction and other service industries also play a significant role in the economy.[^cite_note-131]

Despite housing only about 13% of the DRC's population, Kinshasa generates approximately 85% of the country's [gross domestic product](12594.md).[^cite_note-ChirisaEtAl2017-95] A 2004 investigation found 70% of inhabitants employed informally, 17% in the public sector, 9% in the formal private sector, and 3% other, of a total 976,000 workers. Most new jobs are classified as informal.[^cite_note-Flouriot2013-93] By late 2022, the city's [foreign exchange reserves](1015869.md) had improved significantly, surpassing \$4.5 billion. The DRC maintains support and partnerships with major international organizations and financial institutions, including the [IMF](15251.md), [World Bank](36752.md), [African Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Development_Bank), the [European Union](9317.md), [China](5405.md), and France.[^cite_note-Direction_générale_du_Trésor-2023-132]

The [People's Republic of China](5405.md) has been heavily [involved in the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_relations) since the 1970s, when they financed the construction of the [Palais du Peuple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_du_Peuple_\(Kinshasa\)) and backed the government against rebels in the [Shaba war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaba_I). In 2007–2008 China and Congo signed an agreement for an \$8.5 billion loan for infrastructure development.[^cite_note-133] In recent years, Chinese entrepreneurs have increasingly dominated local markets in Kinshasa, and gradually displacing in the process formerly successful Congolese, West African, Indian, and Lebanese merchants.[^cite_note-134]

Mean household spending in 2005 was the equivalent of US\$2,150, amounting to \$1 per day per person. The median household spending was \$1,555, 66 cents per person per day. Among the poor, more than half of this spending goes to food, especially bread and cereal.[^cite_note-Flouriot2013-93]

### Tourism



In front of [Government Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B4tel_du_Gouvernement) located on [Boulevard Du 30 Juin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevard_du_30_Juin)



[Gombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombe,_Kinshasa) is Kinshasa's fastest-growing commune and is a [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district).



Employees and families from the US Embassy in Kinshasa

Several of Kinshasa's most visited areas are located in communes such as [Ngaliema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngaliema), [Kintambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kintambo), [Gombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombe,_Kinshasa), [Mont-Ngafula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_Ngafula), [Nsele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsele), [Lemba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemba,_Kinshasa), [Limete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limete), [Kalamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalamu), [Kasa-Vubu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasa-Vubu,_Kinshasa), and the [commune of Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_\(commune\)). Ngaliema is the city's earliest and most historically significant area, as it marks the [birthplace of Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pool_Malebo#European_penetration,_the_founding_of_L%C3%A9opoldville,_and_Kinshasa's_emergence_as_the_capital). Because of this, it has a high concentration of historical sites and monuments, ranging from the camp established by explorer [Henry Morton Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Morton_Stanley) and the [Stanley Pool Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pool_Malebo) to later phases of urban growth.[^cite_note-\:5-113] The commune is characterized by [panoramic views](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panorama) of the Congo River and [Ngaliema Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngaliema_Bay), centuries-old trees, and its numerous heritage sites. Sims Chapel (1891), built by Reverend Aaron Sims of the [American Baptist Foreign Mission Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Ministries_\(organization\)) and regarded as Kinshasa's first Christian building; the Saint-Léopold Catholic parish founded in 1899; and the [Institute of National Museums of Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_National_Museums_of_Congo), which preserves and exhibits national artifacts and historical collections.[^cite_note-\:5-113]

Also located there is the [Palais de Marbre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_de_Marbre), once a guest residence during Mobutu's rule and later the presidential residence under [Laurent-Désiré Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent-D%C3%A9sir%C3%A9_Kabila).[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137] Nearby stands the [Colonel Tshatshi Military Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_Tshatshi_Military_Camp), which is situated on [Mount Ngaliema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ngaliema) (formerly Mount Stanley), initially the residence of Léopoldville's colonial governor and later home to President [Joseph Kasa-Vubu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kasa-Vubu) after independence.[^cite_note-\:5-113] In 1966, President Mobutu renamed the site Mont Ngaliema and transformed it into a Presidential Park, landscaped by architect [Olivier-Clément Cacoub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier-Cl%C3%A9ment_Cacoub) and decorated with statues of figures such as [Leopold II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_II_of_Belgium) and Stanley, as well as the [Théâtre de la Verdure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%A9%C3%A2tre_de_Verdure_de_Mont_Ngaliema) amphitheater completed in 1970.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139] Ngaliema also includes the [African Union City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_City), created in 1967 for the [OAU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_African_Unity) summit, the Pioneers' Cemetery, remnants of colonial shipyards and port facilities, the Kintambo-Magasins commercial center, an old caravan route terminus, early communal buildings that once served as the region's first European hospital, artisanal furniture workshops using *kekele* [liana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liana), and natural sites known as the Symphonies Naturelles.[^cite_note-\:5-113]

Kintambo is also historically known as the site of the first contact with colonial powers. Situated at the base of Mount Ngaliema, it rapidly gained strategic and urban significance, becoming the first designated *cité indigène*. Owing to its location and past, the commune preserves important memories of the city, particularly of Léo-Ouest. Numerous landmarks and historic buildings reflect Kinshasa's origins and colonial history, with the Kintambo Vélodrome Stadium, built in 1936, and Saint Francis Parish, established in 1939, standing out as particularly emblematic of the commune's heritage.[^cite_note-\:5-113] In Gombe, there are well-known primary and secondary schools founded during the colonial era, such as [Boboto College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boboto_College), Bosangani High School (formerly Sacré-Cœur), the Gombe Institute (formerly the Royal Atheneum of Kalina), the Gombe Technical Institute, and Notre-Dame Institute. Apart from the [Académie des Beaux-Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acad%C3%A9mie_des_Beaux-Arts_\(Kinshasa\)), universities and other higher education institutions were created after independence. Beyond its scenic sunset views over the Congo River, Gombe hosts the nation's largest hotels and shopping complexes, including the Grand Hôtel de Kinshasa, [Hotel Memling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Memling), Hotel Venus, Fleuve Congo Hotel, Empire Complex, and the Presidential Galleries. The *[Palais de la Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_de_la_Nation_\(Kinshasa\))* and the [prime minister](8699891.md)'s office are also located here, along with the Nautic Club and the Kinshasa Yacht Club, which offer [river excursions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_cruise).[^cite_note-\:5-113]

[Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_\(commune\)) is the only one of the city's 24 communes to share its historical name with the capital itself, having been created at the start of the colonial period as a *cité indigène* that functioned mainly as a residential area for African workers and later became home to some of the city's earliest public, sports, and leisure infrastructures, including [Stade Cardinal Malula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Cardinal_Malula). It also has the oldest [Catholic mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_missions), Saint Peter's Church, which was founded in 1933. The historic [Kimwenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimwenza) Catholic Mission, dating back to the [Congo Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Free_State) era, is regarded as one of the city's major cultural landmarks, while the [Sainte Marie Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sainte_Marie_de_Kimwenza), established in July 1893, laid the foundations for the colonial and present-day [education systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo).[^cite_note-\:5-113] Mont-Ngafula tourist attractions include Lac Ma Vallée, Joli Camp Site, Auberge, the [Petites Chutes de la Lukaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petites_Chutes_de_la_Lukaya), Tilapia, and [Kasangulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasangulu). Along the Matadi Road, plaques, signposts, and scenic paths guide visitors to colonial-era villas. The area also hosts [Lola ya Bonobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lola_ya_Bonobo), a sanctuary dedicated to protecting these endangered primates. Nsele encompasses two major tourist destinations, Kinkole and Nsele. Kinkole is primarily known for its famous dish, Maboké, a fish steamed in banana leaves, which has contributed significantly to its reputation.[^cite_note-\:5-113] Lemba gained prominence with the establishment of [Lovanium University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lovanium_University) (now the University of Kinshasa), the country's first university, which was founded in 1954. It was also home to Kinshasa's earliest planned residential *quartiers*, including Righini, where the [Tabernacle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabernacle) of the Disciples of [William Marrion Branham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_M._Branham) is located. Mount Amba provides a panoramic view of Kinshasa.[^cite_note-\:5-113]

Limete was initially designed as a residential area for Europeans before evolving into the city's third-largest industrial hub after Gombe and Ngaliema. It hosts the [Limete Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limete_Tower) and a 6.5-meter statue of national hero [Patrice Lumumba](49240.md). Kasa-Vubu follows a grid-based urban layout with numerous access roads and broad avenues. Many streets and squares bear names recalling the victories of the *[Force Publique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_Publique)* during the 1940–45 [military campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_campaign), such as Victoire, Gambela, and Ethiopia. The commune also features Kimpwanza Square, a potent symbol of national independence.[^cite_note-\:5-113] Historically, it played a central role in political life, linked to leaders such as Joseph Kasa-Vubu and Gaston Diomi Ndongala of the [ABAKO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABAKO) party. Before [Matonge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matonge_\(Kinshasa\))'s rise, Kasa-Vubu was Kinshasa's main nightlife hub. The Kasa-Vubu monument stands at the junction of Victoire and Assossa avenues. Kalamu, home to the busiest Matonge *quartier*, is home to the Stade Tata Raphaël, famous for hosting the legendary boxing match between Muhammad Ali and George Foreman. Matonge is also a major hub of Kinshasa's music culture, building on the legacy of earlier pioneers such as Wendo Kolosoy and Le Grand Kallé. It gave rise to major musical bands including [Quartier Latin International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartier_Latin_International), [Viva La Musica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viva_La_Musica), and Molokaï Village, and has landmarks such as Place des Artistes, shopping centers, and other entertainment venues.[^cite_note-\:5-113]

The education system in DRC is plagued by low coverage, low quality and poor educational infrastructure, especially in rural areas. According to USAID (2018), 3.5 million children of primary school age are out of school, and 44% of those who do attend school started only after age six. Various statistical estimates by UNESCO, (2013) regarding secondary and tertiary education also reveal the difficulties facing the country. In DRC it is difficult to get a reliable estimate on the actual proportion of the population who can read and write, however, according to data from UIS (2016), the literacy rate of the population of 15 years and older in the country, is estimated to 77.04%. This rate is 88.5% for men and 66.5% for women. There is also a shortage of reading material, and no culture of reading for pleasure.[^cite_note-143]

## Health and medicine



Monkole Hospital, Kinshasa

There are twenty hospitals in Kinshasa, plus various medical centers and polyclinics.[^cite_note-144]

During the early 20th century, Kinshasa played a pivotal and unintended role in the emergence of the global [HIV-1](14170.md) pandemic. Historical analysis and genetic research indicate that the virus, which originated through a cross-species transmission event in Central Africa in the 1920s, found an ideal environment for dissemination within the city. The rapid urbanization driven by colonial expansion, which included the development of transport infrastructure like railways and river steamers, combined with social upheaval and the widespread reuse of medical needles, created a "perfect storm" that facilitated the spread of the virus from its initial localized cases to a broader population long before the disease was [clinically recognized in the 1980s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_HIV/AIDS).[^cite_note-145]

## Media

See also: [List of television stations in Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_television_stations_in_Kinshasa)



Office of the [Agence Congolaise de Presse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agence_Congolaise_de_Presse) (ACP)

Kinshasa serves as the nation's principal media hub, hosting the largest concentration of media outlets in the country. The primary languages used in media production are French and Lingala, with other indigenous languages seldom appearing.[^cite_note-Matanga-2007-171] Press freedom remains limited, with journalism subject to strict control and censorship. The 2023 [Press Freedom Index](8264708.md) rated the Democratic Republic of the Congo at 48.55%.[^cite_note-172] State-run channels generally provide minimal political content, and restrictive regulations limit [investigative journalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigative_journalism). In the early 2000s, especially during the political transition, Kinshasa's media scene grew rapidly. By 2004, the city had 23 radio stations and 26 TV stations, with the number of television broadcasters increasing dramatically to 63 by 2007.[^cite_note-Matanga-2007-171]

These outlets are primarily commercial, religious, or community-based and are often affiliated, formally or informally, with political figures or parties. For instance, Numerica TV is owned by Kibambi Shintwa and Kabeya Pindi Pasi, both of whom were candidates in the 2006 parliamentary elections. Mirador TV is owned by Michel Ladi Luya, a former Member of Parliament and newspaper publisher.[^cite_note-Matanga-2007-171][^cite_note-Welo-2016-173] Digital Congo TV was co-founded by [Croatian](69801.md) businessman Nicolas Vazonne and [Jaynet Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaynet_Kabila), twin sister of then-President [Joseph Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kabila).[^cite_note-Matanga-2007-171] Africa TV is associated with [Azarias Ruberwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azarias_Ruberwa), a former vice president and [2006 presidential candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_general_election), and [Eugène Serufuli Ngayabaseka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eug%C3%A8ne_Serufuli_Ngayabaseka), the then-governor of [North Kivu](446958.md). CCTV (*Canal Congo Télévision*) and Canal Kin are linked to [Jean-Pierre Bemba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Bemba).[^cite_note-Matanga-2007-171] While many of these channels deny formal political affiliations, their ownership structures suggest otherwise.[^cite_note-Matanga-2007-171] Not all stations are politically aligned. Neutral and independent outlets such as [Antenne A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antenne_A) and Congoweb TV contribute to a more balanced media environment. [RTNC1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio-T%C3%A9l%C3%A9vision_nationale_congolaise) remains the primary public broadcaster with nationwide reach, while other widely viewed stations include [RTGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Television_Groupe_Avenir) (*[Radio Télévision Groupe L'Avenir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Television_Groupe_Avenir)*), Digital Congo TV, Mirador TV, Congoweb TV, and Antenne A.[^cite_note-Matanga-2007-171]

Several prominent national radio and television stations are headquartered in Kinshasa. The [RTNC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio-T%C3%A9l%C3%A9vision_nationale_congolaise) operates multiple channels, RTNC1, RTNC2, RTNC3, and RTNC4, providing news, cultural programming, and public service broadcasts. The [UN](31769.md)-supported [Radio Okapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Okapi), jointly managed with [MONUSCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MONUSCO), is also based in the city and is widely regarded for its balanced reporting.[^cite_note-Welo-2016-173][^cite_note-174] Other notable broadcasters include Top Congo FM, RTGA, Radio Télévision Message de Vie (RTMV), Raga FM, and Digital Congo FM. In addition to these, Kinshasa hosts a wide array of specialized and religious broadcasters, such as Radio Télévision Armée de l'Éternel (RTAE), Radio Télévision Sentinelle (RTS), Canal Chemin de Vérité et Vie (CVV), Radio Télévision Catholique Elikya (RTCE), Radio Parole de l'Éternel (RPE), and Radio Télé Assemblée Chrétienne (RTACK).[^cite_note-Welo-2016-173][^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177] Community-based stations include Radio Shaloom Racha, Radio 7, Tam Tam Africain, Jo Dacosta FM, Afri Radio, and Mirador FM. International broadcasters such as the [BBC](19344654.md) (on 92.6 FM), [Radio France Internationale](3866201.md) (RFI), Africa Radio, [China Radio International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Radio_International), and [Euronews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronews) are also present and broadcast according to the editorial policies of their parent organizations.[^cite_note-Welo-2016-173][^cite_note-Matanga-2007-171]

The print and digital media sector is equally diverse, with the capital being home to the state-owned news agency *[Agence Congolaise de Presse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agence_Congolaise_de_Presse)* (ACP) and a range of newspapers and online platforms. Among the most prominent publications are *[L'Avenir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Avenir_\(Congolese_newspaper\)), L'Observateur*, *[Le Potentiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Potentiel)*, *Le Phare*, *Le Soft*, *La Conscience*, *[La Prospérité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Prosp%C3%A9rit%C3%A9),* and *LeCongolais*.[^cite_note-178]

## Sports



The exterior of the Stade des Martyrs, the largest sports venue by capacity in DR Congo

[Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports), especially [football](10568.md) and [martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_arts) are popular in Kinshasa. The city is home to the country's national stadium, the [Stade des Martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_des_Martyrs) (Stadium of the Martyrs). The [Vita Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Vita_Club), [Daring Club Motema Pembe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Motema_Pembe) and [AS Dragons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Dragons_\(Kinshasa\)) frequently draws large crowds, enthusiastic and sometimes rowdy, to the Stade des Martyrs. [Dojos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dojo) are popular and their owners influential.[^cite_note-Trapido2016-78]

In 1974, Kinshasa hosted [The Rumble in the Jungle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rumble_in_the_Jungle) boxing match between [Muhammad Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali) and [George Foreman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Foreman), in which Ali defeated Foreman, to regain the World [Heavyweight](591011.md) title.

Notable features of the city include the Gecamines Commercial Building (formerly SOZACOM) and [Hotel Memling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Memling); [L'ONATRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'ONATRA?action=edit\&redlink=1), the building of the Ministry of Transport; the central market; the [Limete Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limete_Tower).

## Infrastructure and housing



Road of Kinshasa City

The city's infrastructure for [running water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tap_water) and electricity is generally in bad shape.[^cite_note-179] The [electrical network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power_distribution) is in disrepair to the extent that prolonged and periodic [blackouts](296636.md) are normal, and exposed [lines](1105937.md) sometimes electrify pools of rainwater.[^cite_note-Trapido2016-78][^cite_note-Flouriot2013-93]

[Regideso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regideso), the national public company with primary responsibility for water supply in the Congo, serves Kinshasa only incompletely, and without uniform quality. Other areas are served by decentralized *Associations des Usagers des Réseau d'Eau Potable* (ASUREPs).[^cite_note-Bédécarrats2016-108] Gombe uses water at a high rate (306 liters per day per inhabitant) compared to other communes (from 71 L/d/i in Kintambo down to 2 L/d/i in Kimbanseke).[^cite_note-Flouriot2013-93]

Since 2008, the provincial government has established several technical [public services](1291782.md), collectively known as "*urban régies*", which fall under the governor's authority. These include:[^cite_note-Leganet.cd-2008b-180][^cite_note-Kinyamba-2015h-119]

The housing market has seen rising prices and [rents](342324.md) since the 1980s. Houses and apartments in the central area are expensive, with houses selling for a million dollars and apartments going for \$5000 per month. High prices have spread outward from the central area as owners and renters move out of the most expensive part of the city. [Gated communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gated_communities) and shopping malls, built with foreign capital and technical expertise, began to appear in 2006. [Urban renewal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_renewal) projects have led in some cases to violent conflict and displacement.[^cite_note-Trapido2016-78][^cite_note-183] The high prices leave incoming refugees with few options for settlement besides illegal [shantytowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shantytown) such as Pakadjuma.[^cite_note-Iazzolino2016-110] The [Cité du Fleuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_cit%C3%A9_du_Fleuve), an upmarket residential development, has had significant delays.[^cite_note-184] In 2005, 55% of households had televisions and 43% had mobile phones. 11% had refrigerators and 5% had cars.[^cite_note-Flouriot2013-93]

## Transport

### Road



The Boulevard Lumumba



A Transco Bus in 2020

Kinshasa's main roads spread from the [city center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_centre) toward the outer parts of the [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area). Lumumba Boulevard runs from [N'Djili International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'djili_International_Airport) to [N'Dolo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'Dolo_Airport). It is about 25 km long and generally consists of four [lanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lane) in each direction in certain sections. Poids Lourds Avenue passes through the industrial and riverside areas of [Limete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limete) and connects with [Boulevard du 30 Juin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevard_du_30_Juin) at 30 June Square.[^cite_note-\:6-185] It is approximately 15 km long and has two lanes in each direction between Lumumba Boulevard and the Ndolo level crossing. Luambo Makiadi Avenue, measuring 3.9 km in length and 7 m in width, connects Lumumba Boulevard with the city center. Joseph Kasa-Vubu Avenue covers about 8 km and follows a radial alignment, initially oriented north–south before curving around the city along an east–west route.[^cite_note-\:6-185] With a width of approximately 10 m, it is one of the main roads leading out of the city center. Liberation Avenue stretches for about 13 km and connects western [Gombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombe,_Kinshasa) with the southwestern parts of the metropolitan area. It has three lanes in each direction between Moleart Roundabout and Boulevard du 30 Juin and serves as an important entrance to and exit from administrative Gombe. Boulevard du 30 Juin runs east–west through Gombe and passes through its administrative *quartiers*.[^cite_note-\:6-185] At [Kintambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kintambo) Magasin, Matadi Road provides access to the outer parts of [Ngaliema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngaliema). Boulevard du 30 Juin has four lanes in each direction and runs for about 5.5 km between [Kinshasa Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gare_de_l'Est,_Kinshasa) and Socimat Roundabout. [University Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Avenue_\(Kinshasa\)) provides access to the southern parts of the city and is about 10 km long. It has one lane in each direction along its entire length. Most of these main roads follow north–south routes, creating strong connections across the city in that direction. They also tend to meet toward the northern part of the metropolitan area, while the east–west road network is less developed and works less well.[^cite_note-\:6-185] Gombe has one of the city's more developed paved road networks. Boulevard du 30 Juin was modernized during the 2000s and early 2010s, but many of the other roads are in poor condition.[^cite_note-\:6-185][^cite_note-Trapido2016-78]

Kinshasa has about 5,019 km of urban roads, of which only 546 km are paved, as well as 362 km of [national roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Road_1_\(Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo\)) and 74 km of provincial roads.[^cite_note-\:6-185] The city is located along [National Road 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Road_1_\(Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo\)), the highway between the provinces of [Kongo Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_Central) and [Haut-Katanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haut-Katanga). It connects the capital with other cities in the western and southern DRC, including [Lubumbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubumbashi).[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187] A recent complete reconstruction of the section between Kinshasa and the provinces of Kwango and Kwilu significantly improved road quality and reduced travel time from up to a week down to six hours.[^cite_note-188] Kinshasa does not have direct road connections to many provincial capitals, especially those in the north.[^cite_note-WorldBank-2016-189]

The public bus company for Kinshasa, created in 2003, is Transco (*Transport au Congo*).[^cite_note-190] Several companies operate registered [taxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxis) and [taxi-buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_taxi), identifiable by their yellow color. In addition, an app-based taxi hailing service was introduced in 2023.[^cite_note-191]

### Air

The city has two airports: [N'djili International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'djili_International_Airport) (FIH) is the main airport with connections to other African countries as well as to [Istanbul](3391396.md), [Brussels](3708.md), [Paris](22989.md) and some other destinations. [N'Dolo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'Dolo_Airport), located close to downtown, is used for domestic flights only with small turboprop aircraft. Several international airlines serve Ndjili Airport including [Kenya Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Airways), [South African Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Airways), [Ethiopian Airlines](394924.md), [Brussels Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels_Airlines), [Air France](14998012.md) and [Turkish Airlines](167925.md). An average of ten international flights depart each day from N'djili Airport.[^cite_note-192] A small number of airlines provide domestic service from Kinshasa, for example [Congo Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Airways) and [CAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FlyCAA). Both offer scheduled flights from Kinshasa to a limited number of cities inside DR Congo.[^cite_note-193]

### Rail

See also: [Railways in the Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railways_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo)



[Rolling stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_stock) near the [Kinshasa Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gare_de_l'Est,_Kinshasa)



The port of Kinshasa on the Congo River

Plans to build an urban railway system in Kinshasa called [MetroKin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetroKin) were announced in 2023. The start of service is not expected until 2026 at the earliest.[^cite_note-RG-2023-194]

The [Kinshasa central station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gare_de_l'Est,_Kinshasa) is in the riverside commune of Gombe,[^cite_note-195] and is on the [Matadi–Kinshasa Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matadi%E2%80%93Kinshasa_Railway), a line connecting it with the country's main Atlantic seaport of [Matadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matadi) and the province of Kongo Central.[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-MB-2025-197] The railway reopened in 2025 for passenger service multiple times per week, after five years of renovation.[^cite_note-Eco-198] Before the reopening, service was provided twice daily along the smaller section between Kinshasa and [Kasangulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasangulu).[^cite_note-RG-2023-194][^cite_note-MB-2025-197]

There is no rail direct connection between Kinshasa and the interior of the DRC, though it is historically connected by river transport to [Ilebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilebo), [Kasaï Province](4225839.md), which is the start of the line south to Lubumbashi and the [Zambian](34415.md) border.[^cite_note-WorldBank-2010-199][^cite_note-Log-200]

### River

Kinshasa is the major [river port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_port) of the Congo, which handles over two million tons of freight annually. Rivers are the main connection of Kinshasa with much of the country due to the fractured and poor state of the road network, and the limited extent of rail network.[^cite_note-WorldBank-2016-189] The port, called 'Le Beach Ngobila' extends for about 7 km (4 mi) along the river, comprising scores of [quays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quay) and [jetties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jetty) with hundreds of boats and barges tied up. Ferries cross the river to [Brazzaville](52710.md), a distance of about 4 km (2 mi). River transport also connects to dozens of ports upstream, such as [Kisangani](588207.md) and [Bangui](56617.md).

## Social issues



Downtown Kinshasa at night

### Crime and punishment

Since the [Second Congo War](877492.md), the city has been striving to recover from disorder, with many youth gangs living and operating from Kinshasa's poorer areas.[^cite_note-201] The U.S. State Department in 2010 informed travelers that Kinshasa and other major Congolese cities are generally safe for daytime travel, but to beware of robbers, especially in traffic jams and in areas near hotels and stores.[^cite_note-202]

Some sources say that Kinshasa is extremely dangerous, with one source giving a homicide rate of 112 per 100,000 people per year.[^cite_note-203] Another source cites a homicide rate of 12.3 per 100,000.[^cite_note-ElechiMorris2010-204] By some accounts, crime in Kinshasa is not so rampant, due to relatively good relations among residents and perhaps to the severity with which even petty crime is punished.[^cite_note-Trapido2016-78]

While the military and National Police operate their own jails in Kinshasa, the main detention facility under the jurisdiction of the local courts is the Kinshasa Penitentiary and Re-education center in Makala. This prison houses much more than its nominal capacity of 1,000 inmates. In 2024, the population of Makala Prison was reported at 15,000.[^cite_note-205] The Congolese military intelligence organization, Détection Militaire des Activités Anti-Patrie ([DEMIAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEMIAP)) operates the Ouagadougou prison in [Kintambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kintambo) commune with notorious cruelty.[^cite_note-ElechiMorris2010-204][^cite_note-206]

### Street children

In the 2010s, street children, or "Shegués", often orphaned, are subject to abuse by the police and military.[^cite_note-207] Of the estimated 20,000 children living on Kinshasa's streets, almost a quarter are beggars, some are street vendors and about a third have some kind of employment.[^cite_note-208] Some have fled from physically abusive families, notably step-parents, others were expelled from their families as they were believed to be witches,[^cite_note-209] and have become outcasts.[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212]

Street children are mainly boys,[^cite_note-213] but the percentage of girls is increasing according to UNICEF. Ndako ya Biso provides support for street children, including overnight accommodation for girls.[^cite_note-214] There are also second generation street children.[^cite_note-215]

These children have been the object of considerable outside study.[^cite_note-216]
