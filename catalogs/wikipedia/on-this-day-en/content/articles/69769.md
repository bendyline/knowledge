**Bedfordshire** ([/ˈbɛdfərdʃɪər, -ʃər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/4e/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Tesco250-Bedfordshire.wav/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Tesco250-Bedfordshire.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/4e/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Tesco250-Bedfordshire.wav/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Tesco250-Bedfordshire.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; abbreviated *Beds*) is a [ceremonial county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_County) in the [East of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_England). It is bordered by [Northamptonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northamptonshire) to the north, [Cambridgeshire](6288.md) to the north-east, [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire) to the south-east and south, and [Buckinghamshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckinghamshire) to the west. The largest settlement is [Luton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton).

The county has an area of 1,235 km<sup>2</sup> (477 sq mi) and had an estimated population of 749,943 in 2024. Luton is in the south of the county, and [Dunstable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunstable) neighbours it to the west. [Leighton Buzzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leighton_Buzzard) is in the south-west, and [Bedford](69772.md) in the centre-north. For [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_England) purposes Bedfordshire comprises three [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England) areas: [Bedford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Bedford), [Central Bedfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bedfordshire), and Luton.

The centre of Bedfordshire is a gently undulating clay plateau, out of which rises a ridge of [greensand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensand) that runs south-west to north-east and which has a distinct north-facing [escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escarpment).[^cite_note-Lincolnshire_Wolds-3][^cite_note-Lincolnshire_Wolds2-4] The south of the county, around Luton, contains part of the chalk [Chiltern Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiltern_Hills), and the north of the county part of a limestone ridge that runs between [Milton Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Keynes) and [Northampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton).[^cite_note-Lincolnshire_Wolds4-5][^cite_note-Lincolnshire_Wolds3-6] The county's highest point is at 243 m (797 ft) on the [Dunstable Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunstable_Downs), which are part of the Chilterns.[^cite_note-7] The [River Great Ouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Great_Ouse) flows through Bedfordshire, entering upstream of [Harrold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrold,_Bedfordshire) in the north-east and flowing in a circuitous but broadly southerly route to Bedford before turning north-east to exit the county upstream of [St Neots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Neots) in Cambridgeshire.

## History

Main article: [History of Bedfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bedfordshire)

The first recorded use of the name in 1011 was "Bedanfordscir", meaning the shire or county of Bedford, which itself means "Beda's ford" (river crossing).

Bedfordshire was historically divided into nine [hundreds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_\(division\)): [Barford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barford,_Bedfordshire), [Biggleswade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biggleswade_\(hundred\)), [Clifton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifton,_Bedfordshire), [Flitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flitt), [Manshead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manshead), [Redbornestoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wixams), [Stodden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stodden), [Willey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willey_\(hundred\)), [Wixamtree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wixamtree), along with the liberty and the [ancient borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_borough) of [Bedford](69772.md).

There have been several changes to the county boundary; for example, in 1897 [Kensworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensworth) and part of [Caddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caddington) were transferred from [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire) to Bedfordshire.&#x20;

## Geography



Hand-drawn map of Northampshire, Bedfordshire, Huntingdonshire, Cambridgeshire and Rutland by Christopher Saxton from 1576

The southern end of the county is on the [chalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalk) ridge known as the [Chiltern Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiltern_Hills). The remainder forms part of the broad [drainage basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) of the [River Great Ouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Great_Ouse) and its tributaries. Most of Bedfordshire's rocks are [clays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay) and [sandstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) from the [Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic) and [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) periods, with some [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone). Local clay has been used for [brick-making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick-making) of [Fletton-style bricks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fletton_brick) in the [Marston Vale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marston_Vale). [Glacial](12463.md) erosion of chalk has left hard [flint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint) nodules deposited as gravel—these have been commercially extracted in the past at pits which are now lakes: at [Priory Country Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priory_Country_Park), [Wyboston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyboston) and [Felmersham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felmersham_Gravel_Pits). The [Greensand Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedfordshire_Greensand_Ridge) is an escarpment across the county from near Leighton Buzzard to near Gamlingay in [Cambridgeshire](6288.md).

### Climate

Bedfordshire, being situated in the east of England, has a relatively dry climate for the UK with regular but generally moderate rainfall. Average annual rainfall is 608.6 millimetres (23.96 in) at Bedford. October is the wettest month, with 65.3 millimetres (2.57 in), and March the driest, with 37.3 millimetres (1.47 in).[^cite_note-metoffice.gov.uk-8]

Although temperatures are usually moderate, the county has one of the largest absolute temperature ranges in the UK – of more than 60 °C (108 °F). Average temperatures in Bedford range from a low of 1.5 °C (34.7 °F) overnight in February to a high of 22.4 °C (72.3 °F) during the day in July.[^cite_note-metoffice.gov.uk-8] The highest official temperature recorded in Bedfordshire was 39.7 °C (103.5 °F) in 2022.[^cite_note-9] The lowest official temperature recorded in Bedfordshire was −20.6 °C (−5.1 °F) in 1947.[^cite_note-10]

## Politics

### Police and Crime Commissioner

The Bedfordshire Police and Crime Commissioner is [John Tizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tizard), a member of the [Labour Party](19279158.md).[^cite_note-11]

### Local government

For local government purposes, Bedfordshire is divided into three [unitary authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority): the [boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_status_in_the_United_Kingdom) of [Bedford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Bedford) and [Luton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton), and the [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_England) of [Central Bedfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bedfordshire). Healthcare in the county is dealt with by a single Clinical Commission Group (CCG), which serves all three local authorities in the county, alongside the [City of Milton Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Milton_Keynes) in [Buckinghamshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckinghamshire).[^cite_note-12]

### Emergency services

Policing and fire and rescue services continue to be provided on a county-wide basis, with [Bedfordshire Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedfordshire_Police) governed by the [Bedfordshire Police and Crime Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedfordshire_Police_and_Crime_Commissioner) and [Bedfordshire Fire and Rescue Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedfordshire_Fire_and_Rescue_Service) governed by a Fire Authority comprising members of the three councils.[^cite_note-13] Ambulance Services are provided by the [East of England Ambulance Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_England_Ambulance_Service) NHS Trust.

## Culture

### Food

The "[Bedfordshire clanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedfordshire_clanger)" is a local dish consisting of a [suet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suet) crust pastry filled with meat in one end and a fruit preserve in the other. It was traditionally a farm labourers' meal, designed so as to produce no waste as well as two separate meals.

Chocolate Toothpaste is another local delicacy.[^cite_note-20] A [chocolate tart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chocolate_tart), Chocolate Toothpaste consists of a gritty chocolate filling (said to resemble the texture of toothpaste) within a pastry tart, commonly finished with a swirl of whipped cream on top.

## Transport

See also: [List of future transport developments in the East of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_future_transport_developments_in_the_East_of_England)

Bedfordshire lies on many of the main transport routes which link [London](17867.md) to the [Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Midlands), [Northern England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_England) and the rest of the UK.

### Roads

Two of England's six main [trunk roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trunk_roads) pass through Bedfordshire. The [A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_road_\(Great_Britain\)) London to [Edinburgh](9602.md) road (the Great North Road) runs close by Biggleswade and Sandy, and [Watling Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watling_Street), the Roman road between London and Chester, passes through Dunstable. Until it was diverted in 2017,[^cite_note-22] this was also the route of the [A5 road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A5_road_\(Great_Britain\)) between London and [Holyhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holyhead). The Bedfordshire section of the A5 now runs from junction 11a of the M1 to rejoin Watling Street between Dunstable and [Hockliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockliffe), then continues on to cross the [Buckinghamshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckinghamshire) border at the [City of Milton Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Milton_Keynes).

To these was added in 1959 the [M1 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_motorway), running from London to [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds). Running from junctions 10 to 13 in Bedfordshire, there are two junctions serving Luton (at the southern end), with another one serving Bedford and [Milton Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Keynes) (at the northern end). Between these lie two other junctions in the county; one connecting to the [A5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A5_\(road\)) and serving [Dunstable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunstable), and the other serving the town of [Flitwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flitwick). There is one motorway service station in the county: [Toddington Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toddington_services).

Former trunk roads, now local roads managed by the local highway authorities, include the [A428](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A428_road) (Cambridge-Coventry) running east–west through Bedford Borough, and the [A6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A6_road_\(England\)) from Luton to [Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle).

### Railways

Three of England's main lines pass through Bedfordshire.

The [West Coast Main Line](182538.md) has but a short section where it passes through the far west of the county, with one station at [Leighton Buzzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leighton_Buzzard_railway_station) served by [West Midlands Trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_Trains) to [London Euston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euston_railway_station) and [Northampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton_railway_station).

The [East Coast Main Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_Main_Line) has stations at [Arlesey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlesey_railway_station), [Biggleswade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biggleswade_railway_station) and [Sandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_railway_station), served by [Great Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Thameslink_Railway) services to [King's Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_King's_Cross_railway_station) and [Peterborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterborough_railway_station).

The [Midland Main Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Main_Line) serves [Luton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton_railway_station), [Luton Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton_Airport) (via [Luton DART](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton_DART) link from the station at [Luton Airport Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton_Airport_Parkway_railway_station)) and [Bedford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_railway_station), with trains to many destinations operated by [East Midlands Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Midlands_Railway) and [Thameslink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Thameslink_Railway). Intermediate stations at [Flitwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flitwick_railway_station), [Harlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlington_railway_station) and [Leagrave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leagrave_railway_station) are served by Thameslink.

There are London North Western rural services also running between Bedford and [Bletchley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bletchley_railway_station) along the [Marston Vale Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marston_Vale_line).

### Waterways

The [River Great Ouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Great_Ouse) links Bedfordshire to the [Fenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fens) waterways. As of 2004 there are plans by the [Bedford & Milton Keynes Waterway Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_&_Milton_Keynes_Waterway_Trust) to construct a [canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal) linking the Great Ouse at Bedford to the [Grand Union Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Union_Canal) at Milton Keynes, 14 miles (23 km) distant.[^cite_note-23]

### Air



[Luton Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton_Airport)

[Luton Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton_Airport) (the [fifth busiest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_airports_in_the_United_Kingdom) in the United Kingdom) has flights to many UK, European, [Middle Eastern](19323.md) and [North African](21714.md) destinations, operated largely (but not exclusively) by low-cost airlines.

## Education

The state education system for all of Bedfordshire used to be organised by Bedfordshire County Council. Unlike most of the United Kingdom, Bedfordshire County Council operated a [three-tier education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-tier_education) system arranged into lower, middle and upper schools, as recommended in the [Plowden Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plowden_Report) of 1967, although Luton continued to operate a two-tier system. The three-tier arrangement continued in the rest of the county, though in 2006 a vote was held with a view to moving to the two-tier model, but this was rejected.[^cite_note-46]

After the [2009 structural changes to local government in England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_structural_changes_to_local_government_in_England), Bedfordshire County Council was abolished, and its responsibilities for education were passed to [Bedford Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_Borough_Council) and [Central Bedfordshire Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bedfordshire_Council).

### Bedford Borough

Bedford Borough Council voted in November 2009 to change to the two-tier model in its area.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] The change was due to be introduced over a five-year period and be completed in 2015.[^cite_note-49] However, with the cancellation of the [Building Schools for the Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_Schools_for_the_Future) programme in 2010, the borough changed its proposals, and the switch proceeded on school by school basis where council funds allowed. However, as of 2020 all of Bedford Borough has a two-tier education structure apart from in the Marston Vale area (one upper school remains).

Most of the secondary schools in the area offer [sixth form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_form) courses (such as [A Levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Levels)), though [Bedford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_College,_Bedford) and [The Bedford Sixth Form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bedford_Sixth_Form) also offer a range of [further education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Further_education) courses. Additionally, [Stella Mann College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_Mann_College_of_Performing_Arts) is a private college which offers a range of further education courses relating to the [performing arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performing_arts).[^cite_note-50]

There are a number of [independent schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_schools), many of which have links to the [Harpur Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpur_Trust). These include [Bedford School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_School), [Bedford Modern School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_Modern_School) and [Bedford Girls' School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_Girls'_School).

### Central Bedfordshire



[Shuttleworth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuttleworth_College_\(Bedfordshire\))

In Central Bedfordshire, the school systems in Dunstable and Sandy have been re-organised into the two-tier model in response to parent and school demand, but elsewhere in the authority the three-tier model continues. Plans for the construction of new settlements in Marston Vale have included lower, middle and upper schools.

As well as sixth form departments in schools, the main further education providers in the district are [Central Bedfordshire College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bedfordshire_College) and [Shuttleworth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuttleworth_College_\(Bedfordshire\))[^cite_note-51]

### Luton

Luton also operates a three-tier education system, though its organisation of infant, junior and high schools mirrors the traditional transfer age into secondary education of 11 years. However, most of Luton's high schools do not offer sixth-form education. Instead, this is handled by [Luton Sixth Form College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton_Sixth_Form_College), though [Barnfield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnfield_College) and [Cardinal Newman Catholic School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Newman_Catholic_School), [Stockwood Park Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockwood_Park_Academy) and [The Chalk Hills Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Chalk_Hills_Academy) also offer a range of [further education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Further_education) courses.

### Higher education

There are two [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universities) based in the county – the [University of Bedfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bedfordshire) and [Cranfield University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranfield_University). These institutions attract students from all over the UK and abroad, as well as from Bedfordshire.

## Landmarks

### Cardington airship sheds

The enormous Cardington airship sheds are situated to the south of Bedford, near the villages of [Cardington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardington,_Bedfordshire) and Shortstown. They were originally built for the construction of large airships during World War I. Since falling out of their intended use, one has been used for many purposes including housing film sets for 'Charlie and the Chocolate Factory' and 'Batman Begins' and as a rehearsal space for [Take That](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_That), with the other having been extensively refurbished and now accommodating Hybrid Air Vehicles, a British modern airship design and manufacturing company.

### St Paul's Church Bedford

[St Paul's Church, Bedford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Paul's_Church,_Bedford) is a Church of England parish church and the Civic Church of the Borough of Bedford and the County of Bedfordshire. Located on St Paul's Square, it is a large medieval and later church of cathedral proportions and spire. St Paul's was the church of the BBC during the Second World War.

### Millbrook Proving Ground

The [Millbrook Proving Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millbrook_Proving_Ground), near Junction 13 of the M1, has 70 kilometres (43 miles) of varied vehicle test tracks.[^cite_note-52]

## Sport and leisure

Main article: [Sport in Bedfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Bedfordshire)

Bedfordshire is home to League One team [Luton Town F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton_Town_F.C.) and the [Ampthill RUFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampthill_RUFC) and [Bedford Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_Blues) rugby teams, amongst other various sporting teams.

Bedfordshire boasts a 40-mile (64  km) walk traversing the county from Leighton Buzzard at the southern endpoint and [Sandy, Bedfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy,_Bedfordshire)/[Gamlingay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamlingay) in southern Cambridgeshire to the east; this is called the [Greensand Ridge Walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensand_Ridge_Walk). For cyclists, there is a parallel route called the [Greensand Cycle Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensand_Cycle_Way) that follows minor country roads.
