**21 Air, LLC** is an all-[cargo airline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo_airline) of the United States headquartered in [Greensboro, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensboro,_North_Carolina).[^cite_note-FreightWaves-4][^cite_note-\:0-3] The airline operates [ACMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft,_Crew,_Maintenance_and_Insurance) charters with a fleet of [Boeing 757s](151995.md) and [767s](4165.md).[^cite_note-5]

## History

21 Air was founded on February 25, 2014, as a [virtual airline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_airline_\(economics\)) operating under [Dynamic Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Airlines_\(2018\)#Dynamic_Airways)' [air operator's certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_operator's_certificate).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] It was founded by Adolfo Moreno, who formerly operated an air cargo brokerage firm in Miami.[^cite_note-\:2-8] In 2015, the company had revenues of US\$12.6 million. 21 Air began operating under its own air operator's certificate in the summer of 2016, initially operating two [Boeing 767](4165.md) freighter aircraft.[^cite_note-9]

In early 2019, 21 Air operated a series of cargo flights from the United States to [Venezuela](32374.md). Venezuelan authorities reported that the cargo on one of the flights arriving in [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia,_Venezuela) included a crate of military equipment, which held guns, radio equipment, and ammunition. The origin and purpose of the weapons were not clear.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

21 Air began operating services on behalf of [DHL Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DHL_Aviation) in 2020.[^cite_note-12] The airline began operating flights for [Amazon Air](56149507.md) in 2024, with a fleet of Boeing 767 aircraft. The initial fleet of seven aircraft were leased by Amazon from Titan Aviation, which contracted 21 Air to operate them. The aircraft were formerly operated by Titan's sibling company [Atlas Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Air).[^cite_note-FreightWaves-4]

The airline changed ownership in 2021, with a 25% stake acquired by Canadian cargo airline [Cargojet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargojet), and a controlling stake held by logistics industry veteran [Jim Crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crane). Crane, who also owns the [Houston Astros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Astros) baseball team, was formerly chair of Cargojet's board of directors.[^cite_note-13]

Former [Amerijet International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerijet_International) CEO Tim Strauss served as CEO of the airline from 2024 through early 2026. He was replaced by Keith Winters, a longtime associate of Crane.[^cite_note-\:2-8] In April 2026, Cargojet sold its 25% shares of 21 Air in order to focus on its own operations.[^cite_note-Cargojet-2] Both airlines continue to operate on "select commercial opportunities".[^cite_note-14]

Following the crash of [Flight 7598](84161986.md) in September 2026, industry observers raised concerns about the safety culture of the airline, which was the subject of a whistleblower lawsuit.[^cite_note-NPRconcerns-15]

## Destinations

21 Air operates scheduled cargo services between the United States and Mexico.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] The airline began operating services to the [Dominican Republic](8060.md) and [Colombia](5222.md) for Amazon Air in 2025.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

On September 14, 2026, Amazon suspended 21 Air's [Amazon Air](56149507.md) operations, revoking their ability to haul Amazon packages, following the crash of [Flight 7598](84161986.md).[^cite_note-20]

## Fleet

As of September 2026, 21 Air operates 13 [Boeing 767](4165.md) and 5 [Boeing 757](151995.md) aircraft, some leased from DHL or Amazon affiliates.[^cite_note-21]

## Accidents and incidents

- On September 6, 2026, [21 Air Flight 7598](84161986.md), a [Boeing 767-33AER(BDSF)](4165.md#767-300F) registered N1997A operating for [Amazon Air](56149507.md) from [Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Mu%C3%B1oz_Mar%C3%ADn_International_Airport) in [San Juan, Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_Puerto_Rico), overran Runway 30 while landing at [Miami International Airport](128968.md), Florida. The aircraft struck multiple vehicles during the excursion, catching on fire in the process. Five people on the ground were killed, and another three were injured.
