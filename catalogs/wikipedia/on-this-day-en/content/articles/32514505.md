The **Malawi women's national football team**, nicknamed the **Scorchers**, represents [Malawi](19088.md) in international [women's association football](191466.md). Established in the early 2000s and controlled by the [Football Association of Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Association_of_Malawi) (FAM), the team qualified for the [Women's Africa Cup of Nations](47015413.md) for the first time in [2026](78603473.md), placing second. Its most notable achievements are winning the [2023 COSAFA Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_COSAFA_Women's_Championship) and qualifying for the 2027 [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md).

## History

### 2002–2010: Formation and early years

The Malawi women's national football team was established in the early 2000s, during a period when many African nations were forming women's teams. Malawi made its first appearance on the international stage in April 2002, participating in the inaugural [COSAFA Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COSAFA_Women's_Championship) held in [Harare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harare), Zimbabwe. In their first official match on 20 April 2002, they were defeated 8–0 by neighboring [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia_women's_national_football_team). The team recorded its first victory in the following match, defeating [Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho_women's_national_football_team) 3–0, before losing to the hosts in the final match of the tournament.

In 2004, Malawi took part in its [first qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_African_Women's_Championship_qualification) campaign for the [African Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Women's_Championship), advancing past Uganda by walkover before losing 9–0 on aggregate to [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia_women's_national_football_team). A second attempt in 2006 was unsuccessful, although the team earned its first point with a scoreless draw against [Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_women's_national_football_team) in the second leg. From that period until the 2010s, Malawi competed primarily at the regional level, participating in the COSAFA Women's Championship.

### 2011–2018: Building phase

In 2011, during the [fourth COSAFA Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_COSAFA_Women's_Championship), Malawi made history by qualifying for the semi-finals for the first time. However, losses to [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_women's_national_soccer_team) and [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania_women's_national_football_team) left the team in fourth place. The following year, Malawi participated in the [African Women's Championship qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_African_Women's_Championship_qualification) and recorded its first-ever victory in a continental qualifying match by defeating Zambia 4–2, overturning a heavy first-leg defeat. The team then went on a three-year hiatus before returning in 2015, when they played a friendly against Tanzania.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] A similar situation occurred in 2016, with only a single match played throughout the year.[^cite_note-4]

The return of the COSAFA Women's Championship in 2017, and its transition to an annual event, proved crucial in providing competitive matches for the *She-Flames*.[^cite_note-5] Although the team was eliminated in the group stage in both [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_COSAFA_Women's_Championship) and [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_COSAFA_Women's_Championship), the number of games played tripled compared to the previous two years.

### 2019–present: Breakthrough years

In 2019, Malawi participated in its first [Olympic Games qualifying tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_CAF_Women's_Olympic_qualifying_tournament), defeating [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique_women's_national_football_team) 11–1 and 3–0 across the two legs to advance to the first round.[^cite_note-6] At that [year's COSAFA Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_COSAFA_Women's_Championship), Malawi recorded two wins out of three matches, including a notable 13–0 victory against [Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comoros_women's_national_football_team).[^cite_note-7] Although the team was eliminated by [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_women's_national_football_team) in the first round of Olympic qualifying, their performance demonstrated significant progress.[^cite_note-8]

In January 2020, the team's nickname was officially changed to "the Scorchers", distinguishing them from the [men's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_national_football_team), known as "the Flames".[^cite_note-9] Increased match experience led to improved results and a narrowing gap with top Southern African sides. In 2021, Malawi reached the final of the [COSAFA Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_COSAFA_Women's_Championship), narrowly losing to Tanzania.[^cite_note-10] Later that year, the team participated in the [2022 WAFCON qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Women's_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification), facing regional rivals Zambia; although Malawi was eliminated after the second-leg loss, the results reflected continued improvement.[^cite_note-11]

In September 2023, as preparation for that year's regional tournament, Malawi played two friendly matches against [Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_women's_national_football_team), winning both 17–0, marking their largest victories to date.[^cite_note-12] During the [tournament itself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_COSAFA_Women's_Championship), Malawi went undefeated and captured their first COSAFA Women's Championship title,[^cite_note-13] significantly raising the team's profile and paving the way for more fixtures.[^cite_note-14] While they failed to defend their title in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_COSAFA_Women's_Championship),[^cite_note-15] the federation arranged additional matches, and in 2025 the team played eight international friendlies, a record for the side.[^cite_note-16] In October 2025, Malawi achieved a historic milestone by qualifying for the [Women's Africa Cup of Nations](47015413.md) for the first time, defeating [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_women's_national_football_team) over a two-leg aggregate in the final qualifying round.[^cite_note-17]

On August 9, 2026, the team were in Rabat in Morocco at the [2026 Women's Africa Cup of Nations](78603473.md).[^cite_note-18] [Temwa Chawinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temwa_Chawinga) had scored an equaliser against Ghana and [Mercy Sikelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercy_Sikelo)'s goalkeeping was solid.[^cite_note-19] [Rose Kadzere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Kadzere) scored Malawi's second goal, which won them a place in the semi-finals. More historically it earned a place for the Malawian team in the [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md). Malawi has never competed before at that level.[^cite_note-win26-20] Malawi joined Algeria, Morocco and Cameroon to qualify for the World Cup in Brazil;[^cite_note-bbc4-21] they were the lowest ranked national team to compete at either a men's or women's World Cup at FIFA rank 153.[^cite_note-22]

The team made the final of the WAFCON competition where they were beaten by Cameroon. As runners-up the team won \$750,000. Their debut run was lauded. The First Lady, [Gertrude Mutharika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertrude_Mutharika), hosted a luncheon at the [Kamuzu Presidential Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamuzu_Palace) on 20 August 2026, where she announced that each player was to receive 5 million kwacha from the President.[^cite_note-23]
