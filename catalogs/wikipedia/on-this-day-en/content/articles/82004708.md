***Fjord*** is a 2026 [legal thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_thriller) [family drama film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_drama_film)[^cite_note-CineuropaHristova-2] written, co-produced, and directed by [Cristian Mungiu](11409466.md). A co-production of Romania, France, Denmark, Finland, Norway, and Sweden, it stars [Sebastian Stan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Stan) and [Renate Reinsve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renate_Reinsve) as a [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians)-[Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegians) conservative couple facing scrutiny after moving to the wife's progressive remote Norwegian hometown. It is loosely inspired by the true story of the Bodnariu family (renamed Gheorghiu in the film) whose children were forcefully taken from them by the authorities in Norway.[^cite_note-Cannes-1][^cite_note-CineuropaHristova-2][^cite_note-3]

The film had its world premiere in the main competition of the [2026 Cannes Film Festival](82368000.md) on 18 May, where it received a 12-minute standing ovation and won the [Palme d'Or](612946.md), the [François Chalais Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Chalais_Prize), the [FIPRESCI Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_of_Film_Critics), the [Prize of the Ecumenical Jury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prize_of_the_Ecumenical_Jury), and the Prix de la Citoyenneté. It received critical acclaim, with particular praise for its performances and screenplay, and was released theatrically in Romania on 13 June.

It was also selected as the Romanian entry for the [Best International Feature Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_International_Feature_Film) at the [99th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/99th_Academy_Awards).[^cite_note-\:0-4]

## Premise

Inspired by real events, the story, set in Norway, confronts the contradictions of a society that advocates tolerance and openness to others, but which can brutally exclude those who deviate from the path laid out for them.[^cite_note-5]

The Gheorghiu family, with a Romanian father and a Norwegian mother, have moved to the mother's birthplace, a remote Norwegian village, and befriend the neighboring Halberg family. When the Gheorghius are suspected of abusing their children, their lives are thrown into chaos as they become the center of scrutiny.[^cite_note-Cannes-1][^cite_note-Neon-6][^cite_note-Norway-7]

## Production



Left to right: Reinsve, Mungiu, and Stan promoting the film at the [Cannes Film Festival](82368000.md) on 19 May 2026

The film is a co-production between Romania, Norway, Sweden, Denmark, Finland, and France. Mungiu and Tudor Reu produced the film with Romanian production company Mobra Films, alongside [Why Not Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Why_Not_Productions), Eye Eye Pictures, Snowglobe Film, Aamu Film Company, and Filmgate Films.[^cite_note-Norway-7]

### Filming

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography) began in [Hjørundfjorden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hj%C3%B8rundfjorden), Norway in March 2025. Marius Winje Brustad served as production designer, Mircea Olteanu served as film editor, and Tudor Vladimir Pandoru served as cinematographer.[^cite_note-8]

### Music

The score for the film was composed by Danish composer Kaspar Kaae. The soundtrack was released by Plaza Mayor Company in digital formats on 7 July 2026.[^cite_note-9]

## Release

In May 2025, it was reported that [Neon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon_\(company\)) had purchased distribution rights for the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and New Zealand, while French distributor Goodfellas would manage worldwide distribution, with an expected 2026 release.[^cite_note-Neon-6][^cite_note-Norway-7][^cite_note-10]

The film had its world premiere in the main competition of the [2026 Cannes Film Festival](82368000.md) on 18 May,[^cite_note-11] where it won the [Palme d'Or](612946.md).[^cite_note-Palme_d'Or-12] It was also screened in competition at the [73rd Sydney Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/73rd_Sydney_Film_Festival).[^cite_note-Nordic-13] The film also screened at the [Karlovy Vary International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60th_Karlovy_Vary_International_Film_Festival)[^cite_note-14] and Hurtigruten Nordic Film Festival, both in July 2026.[^cite_note-Nordic-13] Additionally, it was screened in the Summer Screen Programme section of the [32nd Sarajevo Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Sarajevo_Film_Festival) in August 2026.[^cite_note-15] It had its North American premiere at the [53rd Telluride Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/53rd_Telluride_Film_Festival).[^cite_note-16] It was also selected for the Special Presentations section of the [2026 Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Toronto_International_Film_Festival), the Perlak section at the [74th San Sebastián International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/74th_San_Sebasti%C3%A1n_International_Film_Festival),[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] and for the Spotlight Gala section of the [2026 New York Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_York_Film_Festival).[^cite_note-19] It will also be screened in the Gala section of [39th Tokyo International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/39th_Tokyo_International_Film_Festival) on 29 October 2026.[^cite_note-gs1\:var-20]

On 13 June 2026, *Fjord* had exclusive premiere screenings across Romania on 96 theaters, in 53 cities, and 315 screens. It had an unprecedented response from local audiences, becoming the most successful [art film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_film) premiere in the country.[^cite_note-Record-21][^cite_note-FNE-22][^cite_note-Adevărul-23] Forum Film România secured Romanian distribution.[^cite_note-24] It was released theatrically in France by [Le Pacte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Pacte) on 19 August 2026,[^cite_note-25] and will be released theatrically in the United States by Neon on 9 October 2026.[^cite_note-26] The Indian rights were acquired by Impact Films.[^cite_note-27] [Falcon Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_Pictures?action=edit\&redlink=1) acquired the Indonesian rights to the film.[^cite_note-28]

It was also selected as the Romanian entry for the [Best International Feature Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_International_Feature_Film) at the [99th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/99th_Academy_Awards),[^cite_note-\:0-4] marking Mungiu's third film selected to represent Romania, following *[4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_Months,_3_Weeks_and_2_Days)* (2007) and *[Beyond the Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond_the_Hills)* (2012).

## Reception

### Critical response

On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator) website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes), 93% of 74 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 7.8/10. The website's consensus reads: "Presenting a knotty moral dilemma and refusing to provide easy answers, Cristian Mungiu's superbly well-acted *Fjord* has a chilly surface that belies a heated humanity beneath the frost."[^cite_note-Rotten_Tomatoes-29] [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic), which uses a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean), assigned the film a score of 81 out of 100, based on 22 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".[^cite_note-Metacritic-30]

Guy Lodge of *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))* wrote that *Fjord* is "sharply attuned to the world's ever-expanding possibilities for movement, misunderstanding and conflict".[^cite_note-31] Lee Marshall of *[Screen Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_International#Screen_Daily)* considered the film to be a "thought-provoking drama about conflicting values that feels, in the end, a little bloodless and underpowered".[^cite_note-32] Pete Hammond of *[Deadline Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood)* lauded *Fjord* as "a fiercely intelligent and gripping movie that finds its power in providing no easy answers".[^cite_note-33]

[David Ehrlich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ehrlich) of *[IndieWire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndieWire)* gave the film a 'B+' rating, describing it as "a characteristically fraught and tangled drama" vivisecting the reactionary pitfalls of globalization.[^cite_note-34] [David Rooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rooney_\(critic\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* assessed the film to be "compellingly squirm-inducing, if far from the director’s best" in the bottom line.[^cite_note-35] [Robbie Collin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Collin) of *[The Telegraph](23797577.md)* rated the film 5 out of 5 stars, declaring it "a slow-burn provocation that knows exactly which buttons it is pressing".[^cite_note-36]

[Peter Bradshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw) of *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* rated the film 2 out of 5 stars, billing it as an "anticlimactic, underpowered movie".[^cite_note-37] He also criticised its win for the Palme d'Or, writing, "*Fjord* has the director's procedural mannerisms, but here they do not do real work in illuminating any very interesting truth; *Fjord* feels like a co-production contrivance, but certainly one that impressed \[the Cannes] jury".[^cite_note-38]

Marshall Shaffer of *[Slant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant_\(magazine\))* rated the film 2½ out of 4 stars, writing that the trial in the film "is far more interesting for what it reveals than how it resolves".[^cite_note-39]

According to the [Norwegian Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NRK) (NRK), the film is critical of the [Norwegian Child Welfare Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Child_Welfare_Services).[^cite_note-40]
