**Zhytomyr Oblast** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Житомирська область, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Zhytomyrska oblast*), also referred to as **Zhytomyrshchyna** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Житомирщина), is an [oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Ukraine) (province) in northwestern [Ukraine](31750.md). The [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center) of the oblast is the city of [Zhytomyr](1025103.md). Its population is approximately 1,179,032 (2022 estimate).[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-4]

## History



Flag of Zhytomyr Oblast, used for a short period between 2002 and 2003.

[^cite_note-7]

The oblast was created as part of the [Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) on September 22, 1937, out of territories of [Vinnytsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnytsia_Oblast) and [Kyiv](494884.md) oblasts as well as two [border okrugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okrugs_of_the_Ukrainian_SSR) of Kyiv Oblast – Korosten Okrug and Novohrad-Volynsky Okrug.

The oblast covers territories of the historic regions of [Polesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polesia), [Volhynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynia), and [Podolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podolia), which are reflected on the oblast's coat of arms.

Before the 18th century the larger half of the oblast belonged to the [Kyiv Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev_Voivodeship) ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Kijów*), while the smaller western half around the city of [Zviahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zviahel) belonged to the [Volyn Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynian_Voivodeship_\(1569%E2%80%931795\)). Following the [Treaty of Andrusovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Andrusovo), the city of Zhytomyr ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Zytomierz*) continued to act as an administrative center of the Kyiv Voivodeship.

Following the [second partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_partition_of_Poland), on the newly annexed territory was formed an oversized Izyaslav Vice-royalty (Russian: *namestnichestvo*) which included former Polish territories in Volhynia, Podolia, and Kyiv land and centered in [Izyaslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izyaslav). However, a couple of years later the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) annexed more territories of the Polish Kingdom during the [third partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_partition_of_Poland), contributing to the complete disappearance of the Polish statehood. The Izyaslav Vice-royalty was reformed and the territory of today's Zhytomyr Oblast predominantly ended up in the Russian [Volhynian Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynian_Governorate) and the city of [Zviahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zviahel) which was renamed as Novohrad-Volynskyi (Russian: *Novograd-Volynskiy*) became its administrative center. Later the administrative center was transferred to the bigger city of Zhytomyr (Russian: *Zhitomir*).

During the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) Russian troops [partly occupied the region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_occupation_of_Zhytomyr_Oblast).[^cite_note-7337100Zhytomyr-8] They were completely repulsed when the [Narodychi settlement hromada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narodychi_settlement_hromada?action=edit\&redlink=1) (where Russian forces had been dug in from the opening of the offensive in late February 2022) was declared liberated on 4 April 2022.[^cite_note-7337100Zhytomyr-8]

## Geography

The total area of Zhytomyr Oblast is 29,832 km<sup>2</sup> (11,518 sq mi).

Among the points of interest it is important to mention the following sites that were nominated for the [Seven Wonders of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Wonders_of_Ukraine):

- [Church of Saint Basil the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Basil_the_Great_\(Ovruch\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([Ovruch](2936682.md))

- [Stone village state preserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_village_state_preserve?action=edit\&redlink=1)

- [Korolev Memorial Astronautical Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korolev_Memorial_Astronautical_Museum)

There are 58 [monuments of national significance in Zhytomyr Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monuments_of_national_significance_in_Zhytomyr_Oblast).[^cite_note-\:2-9]

The [Museum of Ukrainian home icons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Ukrainian_home_icons), which is the only one in [Europe](9239.md), is situated in Zhytomyr Oblast. It is located in [Radomyshl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radomyshl), a small town, about 90 km (56 mi) away from [Kyiv](585629.md). The museum is the part of the [Radomysl Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radomysl_Castle) historical and cultural complex. It was founded by [Olga Bogomolets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Bogomolets).[^cite_note-10]

## Population

The current estimated population of the oblast is 1,268,903 (as of 2013).

According to the [2001 Ukrainian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Ukrainian_census), [ethnic Ukrainians](145144.md) accounted for 90.3% of the population of Zhytomyr Oblast, [ethnic Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Ukraine) for 5.0%, and [ethnic Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poles_in_Ukraine) for 3.5%.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

[Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) is the only official language on the whole territory of Zhytomyr Oblast.[^cite_note-21]

On 20 December 2018, a moratorium on the public use of Russian-language cultural products was imposed in Zhytomyr Oblast by a decision of the [Zhytomyr Oblast Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhytomyr_Oblast_Council).[^cite_note-22]

According to a poll conducted by *[Rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rating_\(sociological_group\))* from 16 November to 10 December 2018 as part of the project «Portraits of Regions», 74% of the residents of Zhytomyr Oblast believed that the Ukrainian language should be the only state language on the entire territory of Ukraine. 14% believed that Ukrainian should be the only state language, while Russian should be the second official language in some regions of the country. 9% believed that Russian should become the second state language of the country. 3% found it difficult to answer.[^cite_note-23]

On 18 November 2022, Zhytomyr Oblast Military Administration approved the «Oblast programme for the development of the Ukrainian language in all spheres of public life in Zhytomyr Oblast for 2022—2025», the main objective of which is to strengthen the positions of the Ukrainian language in various spheres of public life in the oblast.[^cite_note-24]

According to the research of the *Content Analysis Centre*, conducted from 15 August to 15 September 2024, the topic of which was the ratio of Ukrainian and Russian languages in the Ukrainian segment of [social media](5897742.md), 84.0% of posts from Zhytomyr Oblast were written in Ukrainian (76.1% in 2023, 73.5% in 2022, 24.6% in 2020), while 16.0% were written in Russian (23.9% in 2023, 26.5% in 2022, 75.4% in 2020).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

## Economy

The economy of Zhytomyr Oblast mostly deals with [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) of granite and other construction [stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_\(geology\)), [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry), [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) and various [machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinery) manufacturing.

The northern part of the province is highly affected by the [Chernobyl disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_disaster): some of the [towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town) and [raions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raion) are devastated and are included in the [Chernobyl zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zone_of_alienation), while others are prohibited from producing their own agriculture.

## Nomenclature

See also: [Romanization of Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian)

Most of Ukraine's oblasts are named after their [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(political\)) cities, officially referred to as "oblast centers" ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): обласний центр, [translit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian) *oblasnyi tsentr*). The name of each oblast is a [relative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_clause) [adjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjective), formed by adding a feminine [suffix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffix) to the name of respective center city: *Zhytomyr* is the center of the *Zhytomyrs'ka oblast'* (Zhytomyr Oblast). Most oblasts are also sometimes referred to in a feminine noun form, following the convention of traditional regional place names, ending with the suffix "-shchyna", as is the case with Zhytomyr Oblast, *Zhytomyrshchyna*.
