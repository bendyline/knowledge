**Kyushu** ([九州](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E4%B9%9D%E5%B7%9E), *Kyūshū*; pronounced [\[k̟ʲjɨ́ɨ̀.ɕɨ̀ɨ̀\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/27/Ja-Kyushu.ogg/Ja-Kyushu.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/27/Ja-Kyushu.ogg/Ja-Kyushu.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>,[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] lit. 'nine [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Japan)') is the third-largest and most southerly of [Japan's four main islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Japan#Main_islands).[^cite_note-main-islands-japan-5][^cite_note-6] In the past, it has been known as *Kyūkoku* (九国, "Nine Countries"), *Chinzei* (鎮西, "West of the Pacified Area") and *Tsukushi-no-shima* (筑紫島, "Island of Tsukushi"). The historical regional name [Saikaidō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saikaid%C5%8D) (西海道; lit. West Sea [Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_\(country_subdivision\))) referred to Kyushu and its surrounding islands. Kyushu has a land area of 36,782 square kilometres (14,202 sq mi) and a population of 12,382,121 as of 2025, a population size similar to [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea) or [Cuba](5042481.md).[^cite_note-7]

There is a theory that Kyushu was in ancient times home to its own independent dynasty, where a unique, southern-influenced culture and tradition distinct from that of [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu) flourished.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

In the 8th-century [Taihō Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taih%C5%8D_Code) reforms, [Dazaifu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dazaifu_\(government\)) was established as a special administrative term for the region.[^cite_note-10]

## Geography



Kyushu from the [International Space Station](15043.md)



Geofeatures map of Kyushu

The island is mountainous, and Japan's largest active volcano, [Mount Aso](956535.md) at 1,591 metres (5,220 ft), is on Kyushu. There are many other signs of tectonic activity, including numerous areas of [hot springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_spring). The most famous of these are in [Beppu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beppu,_%C5%8Cita), on the east shore, and around Mt. Aso in central Kyushu. The island is separated from [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu) by the [Kanmon Straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanmon_Straits). Being the nearest island to the [Asian continent](689.md),[^cite_note-11] historically it is the gateway to Japan.[^cite_note-12]

The total area is 36,782.37 km<sup>2</sup> (14,201.75 sq mi) which makes it the 37th [largest island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area) in the world.[^cite_note-island1-1] It is slightly larger than [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Taiwan) or [Vancouver Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Island).[^cite_note-thoughtco-13] The highest elevation is 1791 meters (5876 feet) on [Mount Kujū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kuj%C5%AB).[^cite_note-peaklist-14]

The name *Kyūshū* comes from the nine ancient provinces of [Saikaidō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saikaid%C5%8D) situated on the island: [Chikuzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chikuzen_Province), [Chikugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chikugo_Province), [Hizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hizen_Province), [Higo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higo_Province), [Buzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzen_Province), [Bungo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungo_Province), [Hyūga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hy%C5%ABga_Province), [Ōsumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Csumi_Province), and [Satsuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsuma_Province).[^cite_note-15]

Today's Kyushu Region (九州地方, *Kyūshū-chihō*) is a politically defined region that consists of the seven [prefectures](74517.md) on the island of Kyushu (which also includes the former [Tsushima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsushima_Province) and [Iki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iki_Province) as part of Nagasaki), plus [Okinawa Prefecture](22477.md) to the south:

## Population

Kyushu has 10.1 percent of the population of Japan.[^cite_note-honshu2-16] Most of Kyushu's population is concentrated along the northwest, in the cities of [Fukuoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka) and [Kitakyushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitakyushu), with population corridors stretching southwest into [Sasebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasebo) and [Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki) and south into [Kumamoto](6793346.md) and [Kagoshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagoshima). Except for [Oita](6793355.md) and [Miyazaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyazaki_\(city\)), the eastern seaboard is sparsely populated.

Politically, Kyushu is described as a stronghold of the [Liberal Democratic Party](164231.md).[^cite_note-veconomist-17]

Per Japanese census data,[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] the Kyushu region's population with [Ryukyu Islands](8458939.md) ([Okinawa](22477.md) and [Kagoshima](217439.md) Prefectures) has experienced a large decline since around 2000. However, the population decline in total is mild because of the relatively high birth rate of [Ryukyuans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyuans) both within the Ryukyuan lands ([Okinawa](22477.md) and [Kagoshima](217439.md)) and throughout the Kyushu region. In addition, the other prefectures in Kyushu also have exceptionally high [TFRs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate#Japan) compared to the rest of Japan.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## Environment and agriculture

Parts of Kyushu have a [subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_climate), particularly [Miyazaki Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyazaki_Prefecture) and [Kagoshima Prefecture](217439.md). Major agricultural products are [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea), [tobacco](30942.md), [sweet potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potato), and [soy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy). [Silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk) is also widely produced.

Besides the [volcanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic) area of the south, there are significant mud [hot springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_springs) in the northern part of the island, around [Beppu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beppu). The springs are the site of occurrence of certain [extremophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremophile) [microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism), which are capable of surviving in extremely hot environments.[^cite_note-22]

There are two [World Natural Heritage](44940.md) sites in Kyushu: [Yakushima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakushima) (registered in 1993) and [Amami-Ōshima Island, Tokunoshima Island, northern part of Okinawa Island, and Iriomote Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amami-%C5%8Cshima_Island,_Tokunoshima_Island,_northern_part_of_Okinawa_Island,_and_Iriomote_Island) (registered in 2021).[^cite_note-heritage-23]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Japan](15578.md)



M-V rocket launch rehearsal at USC

Kyushu's economy accounts for approximately 10% of Japan's total economic output, making it comparable to countries such as [Iran](14653.md), [Norway](21241.md), [Austria](26964606.md), the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), and [Thailand](30128.md). It is the fourth largest economic region in Japan, following the three major metropolitan areas of [Tokyo](30057.md), [Osaka](52230.md), and [Nagoya](61720.md).[^cite_note-FFG-24]

Kyushu's economy has a well-balanced industrial structure, ranging from primary industries such as agriculture, to secondary industries such as manufacturing, and tertiary industries such as retail, services, and tourism. Agricultural output in the region amounts to 1.8 trillion yen (20% share of the national total), and the region is a major domestic production center for the [automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile) and [semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor) industries. Kyushu also has a thriving healthcare industry, including medical and nursing care, and numerous research and manufacturing facilities in the fields of [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen), [solar power](13690575.md), and other renewable energies. Furthermore, [Fukuoka City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka_City), [Kitakyushu City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitakyushu_City) and [Okinawa Prefecture](22477.md) have been designated as [National Strategic Special Zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Strategic_Special_Zones?action=edit\&redlink=1), which are expected to have an economic ripple effect on the entire Kyushu region through the creation of innovation in industry and the promotion of new entrepreneurship and start-ups.[^cite_note-FFG-24]

Kyushu is a region with strong economic ties to Asia. For example, Asia accounted for 420 (77.9%) of the 539 overseas expansion cases of Kyushu-Yamaguchi companies from 2010 to 2019, and Asia accounted for 61.1% of Kyushu-Yamaguchi's total exports in 2019, 7.4 percentage points higher than the nation as a whole. As the logistics node between Japan and Asia, the ports of Hakata and Kitakyushu handle a large number of international containers. In addition, the number of cruise ship calls in 2019 was 772, with Kyushu accounting for 26.9% of the nation's total.[^cite_note-25]

Kyushu is noted for various types of [porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcelain), including Arita, Imari, Satsuma, and Karatsu. [Heavy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_industry) is concentrated in the north around Fukuoka, Kitakyushu, Nagasaki, and Oita and includes chemicals, automobiles, semiconductors, metal processing, shipbuilding, etc.[^cite_note-26] The island of [Tanegashima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanegashima) hosts the [Tanegashima Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanegashima_Space_Center), which is the largest rocket-launch complex in Japan.

## Transportation

Kyushu is linked to the larger island of [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu) by the [Kanmon Railway Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanmon_Railway_Tunnel),[^cite_note-27] which carries the non-[Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinkansen) trains of the [Kyushu Railway Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyushu_Railway_Company), and the newer [Shin-Kanmon Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin-Kanmon_Tunnel) carrying the [San'yō Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San'y%C5%8D_Shinkansen). Railways on the island are operated by the Kyushu Railway Company and [West Japan Railway Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Japan_Railway_Company), as well as a variety of smaller companies such as [Amagi Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amagi_Railway_Amagi_Line)[^cite_note-28] and [Nishitetsu Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishi-Nippon_Railroad).[^cite_note-29] [Kyushu Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyushu_Shinkansen) trains operate between major cities on the island,[^cite_note-\:0-30] such as [Fukuoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka) and [Kagoshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagoshima),[^cite_note-31] with an additional route between [Takeo-Onsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeo-Onsen_Station) and [Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki) which has been in operation since September 2022.[^cite_note-32] Kyushu is also known for its scenic train services, such as the Limited Express Yufuin no Mori and Limited Express Kawasemi Yamasemi.[^cite_note-\:0-30]

The [Kanmon Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanmon_Bridge) and [Kanmon Roadway Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanmon_Roadway_Tunnel) also connect the island with Honshu, allowing for vehicular transport between the two.[^cite_note-33] The [Kyushu Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyushu_Expressway) spans the length of the island, linking the [Higashikyushu Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higashikyushu_Expressway) and Ibusuki Skyline, connecting major cities such as [Fukuoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka) and [Kumamoto](6793346.md) along the way.[^cite_note-34] There are also many quiet country roads, including popular tourist routes such as the [Nichinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nichinan,_Miyazaki) coast road and the Aso Panorama Line in [Kumamoto Prefecture](220713.md). Bus services are available and cover 2,400 routes within Kyushu's cities, connecting many other destinations.[^cite_note-\:0-30]

Several passenger and car ferry services connect both northern and southern Kyushu with main port cities on the main island of Honshu (Kobe, Osaka, Tokyo) and [Shikoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikoku).[^cite_note-35]

The island's main larger airport is served by [Fukuoka Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka_Airport) which provides most domestic and internationals flights. There are also smaller airports across island including [Kumamoto Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumamoto_Airport), [Miyazaki Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyazaki_Airport), [Oita Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oita_Airport), [Saga Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saga_Airport), [Nagasaki Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki_Airport) [Kitakyushu Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitakyushu_Airport) and [Kagoshima Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagoshima_Airport).[^cite_note-36]

## Culture

Kyushu has maintained the strongest economic and cultural ties with [Okinawa (Ryukyu)](22477.md) from ancient times to the present. Traces of Okinawan culture can be seen throughout Kyushu, and vice versa. Okinawan musical scales are often found in local folk songs, and there are many similarities in cuisine and language. Kyushu has a traditional instrument called the *[gottan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottan)*, which resembles the *[sanshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanshin)* and *[shamisen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamisen)*. Crafts of the region include [Hakata-ori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakata-ori) (博多織), [Beppu bamboo crafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beppu_bamboo_crafts)(別府竹細工), [Kurume Kasuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurume_Kasuri) (久留米絣), and [Sathuma Kiriko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsuma_kiriko).

### People

[People in Kyushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_in_Kyushu?action=edit\&redlink=1) are known for being conservative even within Japan. In particular, men from Kyushu are often referred to as "[Kyushu danji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyushu_danji?action=edit\&redlink=1)" (九州男児), evoking an image of being wild and strong. It is said that this term has existed since ancient times, originally used to inspire and encourage soldiers. Being from Kyushu is a source of great pride for its people. Thanks to this cultural spirit, Kyushu has preserved a rich variety of unique traditional cultures that continue to thrive today.[^cite_note-37]

### Food



Hakata-ramen (元祖長浜屋)

The main features of Kyushu cuisine are the use of fresh ingredients and seasonings such as sweet soy sauce ([Kyushu soy sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyushu_soy_sauce)) and barley miso, which differ from those used in [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu).

### Musical instruments



Gottan(ゴッタン)

In Kyushu, there exists a traditional stringed instrument known as the *[gottan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottan) (ゴッタン)*, which developed independently within the region's unique cultural and geographic context. The instrument is considered to be a midpoint between the *[shamisen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamisen)* of [mainland Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu) and the *[sanshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanshin)* of [Okinawa](22477.md), incorporating characteristics of both. Structurally, it resembles the sanshin more closely—for example, it lacks the *sawari*, a buzzing mechanism found in the shamisen. This absence, along with its simplified wooden box construction and single string, aligns it with the more minimalistic design typical of southern instruments.[^cite_note-40]

Musically, the *[gottan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottan)* is sometimes used to perform melodies that reflect the Ryukyuan pentatonic scale, suggesting a subtle but notable influence from Okinawan musical traditions. While it remains a distinct and localized folk instrument of Kyushu, its sound and structure reveal traces of a broader cultural exchange within the southern Japanese archipelago, embodying a hybrid identity between the mainland and the Ryukyus.[^cite_note-41]
