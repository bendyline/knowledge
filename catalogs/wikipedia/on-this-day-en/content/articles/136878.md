**East Rutherford** is a [borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_\(New_Jersey\)) in [Bergen County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_County,_New_Jersey), in the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [New Jersey](21648.md). It is an [inner-ring suburb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_suburb) of [New York City](645042.md), located 7 miles (11 km) west of [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan). As of the [2020 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the borough's population was 10,022,[^cite_note-Census2020-9][^cite_note-LWD2020-10] an increase of 1,109 (+12.4%) from the [2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census) count of 8,913,[^cite_note-Census2010-19][^cite_note-LWD2010-20] which in turn reflected an increase of 197 (+2.3%) from the 8,716 counted in the [2000 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_census).[^cite_note-21]

Under the terms of an act of the [New Jersey Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Legislature) on April 17, 1889, a portion of the old [Union Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Township,_Bergen_County,_New_Jersey) was incorporated under the name of Boiling Springs Township.[^cite_note-Story-22] The new township took its name from a spring in the community. On March 28, 1894, the Borough of East Rutherford was created, based on the results of a referendum held the previous day, and Boiling Springs Township was dissolved.[^cite_note-Story-22] While there was no change in its borders, the name and form of government were changed.[^cite_note-Story-22][^cite_note-23] The borough was the second formed during the "[Boroughitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroughitis)" phenomenon then sweeping through Bergen County, in which 26 boroughs were formed in the county in 1894 alone.[^cite_note-24] The borough was already the site of several industries prior to its incorporation, with the number of industrial firms located in East Rutherford totaling over 50 by 1940.[^cite_note-25]

East Rutherford is the home of the [Meadowlands Sports Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowlands_Sports_Complex), which includes [Meadowlands Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowlands_Arena) and [MetLife Stadium](5476983.md). MetLife Stadium is home of the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants) and [New York Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Jets) of the [National Football League](21211.md) and hosted [Super Bowl XLVIII](4057023.md), which made East Rutherford the smallest city ever to host a [Super Bowl](27718.md). East Rutherford hosted seven matches for the [1994 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) at [Giants Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giants_Stadium) and hosted eight matches for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), including the [World Cup final](75845879.md), held at MetLife Stadium, also making the borough the smallest-ever host of any of those tournament final games.

The borough is also the site of [American Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Dream_\(shopping_mall\)), a 3,000,000-square-foot (280,000 m<sup>2</sup>) shopping center and entertainment complex that opened in October 2019.[^cite_note-26]

## Geography

According to the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the borough had a total area of 4.04 square miles (10.47 km<sup>2</sup>), including 3.71 square miles (9.61 km<sup>2</sup>) of land and 0.33 square miles (0.85 km<sup>2</sup>) of water (8.14%).[^cite_note-CensusArea-1][^cite_note-GR1-2]

East Rutherford is bounded on the north by the boroughs of [Carlstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlstadt,_New_Jersey) and [Wallington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallington,_New_Jersey) and to the south by the borough of [Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutherford,_New_Jersey) in Bergen County; by [Secaucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secaucus,_New_Jersey) in [Hudson County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_County,_New_Jersey); and by [Passaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passaic,_New_Jersey) in [Passaic County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passaic_County,_New_Jersey).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The [Passaic River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passaic_River) is the western boundary, and the [Hackensack River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hackensack_River) is the eastern boundary. The area in which East Rutherford is located is the valley of the Passaic and Hackensack rivers.

[Carlton Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlton_Hill,_New_Jersey) is an [unincorporated community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_New_Jersey#Unincorporated_communities) located within the township.[^cite_note-30]

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), East Rutherford had a population of 10,022. The median age was 37.7 years. 17.8% of residents were under the age of 18 and 14.2% of residents were 65 years of age or older. For every 100 females there were 94.5 males, and for every 100 females age 18 and over there were 93.0 males age 18 and over.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-42][^cite_note-Census2020PL-43]

100.0% of residents lived in urban areas, while 0.0% lived in rural areas.[^cite_note-Census2020DHC-44]

There were 4,306 households in East Rutherford, of which 27.5% had children under the age of 18 living in them. Of all households, 40.9% were married-couple households, 22.3% were households with a male householder and no spouse or partner present, and 28.9% were households with a female householder and no spouse or partner present. About 32.1% of all households were made up of individuals and 10.2% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-42]

There were 4,585 housing units, of which 6.1% were vacant. The homeowner vacancy rate was 1.2% and the rental vacancy rate was 5.3%.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-42][^cite_note-Census2020PL-43]

### 2010 census

The [2010 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census) counted 8,913 people, 3,792 households, and 2,226 families in the borough. The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) was 2,403.2 per square mile (927.9/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 4,018 housing units at an average density of 1,083.4 per square mile (418.3/km<sup>2</sup>). The racial makeup was 73.04% (6,510) [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_\(U.S._census\)), 4.50% (401) [Black or African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_\(U.S._census\)), 0.22% (20) [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_\(U.S._census\)), 13.93% (1,242) [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(U.S._census\)), 0.03% (3) [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_\(U.S._census\)), 5.83% (520) from [other races](273285.md), and 2.43% (217) from two or more races. [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_\(U.S._census\)) residents of any race were 17.54% (1,563) of the population.[^cite_note-Census2010-19]

Of the 3,792 households, 24.2% had children under the age of 18; 42.9% were married couples living together; 11.6% had a female householder with no husband present and 41.3% were non-families. Of all households, 33.5% were made up of individuals and 11.6% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.35 and the average family size was 3.06.[^cite_note-Census2010-19]

The age distribution showed 18.1% of the population under the age of 18, 8.6% from 18 to 24, 33.2% from 25 to 44, 26.6% from 45 to 64, and 13.5% that was 65 years of age or older. The median age was 37.8 years. For every 100 females, the population had 93.0 males. For every 100 females ages 18 and older there were 92.5 males.[^cite_note-Census2010-19]

The Census Bureau's 2006–2010 [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey) showed that (in 2010 [inflation-adjusted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation_adjustment) dollars) [median household income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_household_income) was \$62,471 (with a margin of error of +/− \$8,225) and the median family income was \$71,357 (+/− \$10,225). Males had a median income of \$57,511 (+/− \$8,669) versus \$48,502 (+/− \$2,269) for females. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the borough was \$32,467 (+/− \$2,752). About 5.9% of families and 6.5% of the population were below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line), including 4.2% of those under age 18 and 13.4% of those age 65 or over.[^cite_note-45]

[Same-sex couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_partnership) headed 19 households in 2010, a decline from the 27 counted in 2000.[^cite_note-46]

## Economy

East Rutherford is home to the [American Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Dream_\(shopping_mall\)) shopping mall, the [second largest shopping mall in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_shopping_malls_in_the_United_States) at 3,000,000 square feet (280,000 m<sup>2</sup>). Completed after more than a decade of stops and starts, the mall was constructed for an estimated \$5 million and is more than half entertainment space, including theme-park attractions and recreational facilities, with the remainder devoted to retail at more than 450 stores.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] The mall is located in [Bergen County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_County,_New_Jersey), the last county with state-mandated [Blue Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_laws_in_the_United_States#New_Jersey) which limit retail operations on Sunday, though many of the stores at American Dream are open on Sunday, as the [New Jersey Sports and Exposition Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Sports_and_Exposition_Authority) that owns the site has said that the laws don't apply at the mall.[^cite_note-49]

East Rutherford is home to the [Hudson Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Group), a retailer which operates a chain of newsstands, bookstores, [fast food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_food) restaurants, and other retail stores chiefly at [airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport) and train stations.[^cite_note-50] Contract manufacturing organization [Cambrex Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrex_Corporation) is based in East Rutherford.

The [East Rutherford Operations Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Rutherford_Operations_Center) handles currency in the area covered by the [Federal Reserve Bank of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank_of_New_York), a task that includes removing and destroying 5 million currency notes every day.[^cite_note-51]

The [Dawn Bible Students Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawn_Bible_Students_Association) moved to East Rutherford in 1944. The organization has a worldwide outreach and publishes many Bible-based books as well as *The Dawn* magazine.[^cite_note-52]

## Sports



MetLife Stadium during a match between Brazil and Morocco at the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).

East Rutherford is the home of the [Meadowlands Sports Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowlands_Sports_Complex), which includes [Meadowlands Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowlands_Arena) and [MetLife Stadium](5476983.md), and used to be the location of [Giants Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giants_Stadium). The arena is best known as the former home of the [New Jersey Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils) of the [National Hockey League](21809.md) and of the [New Jersey Nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Nets) of the [National Basketball Association](22093.md), and for hosting college basketball, arena football, concerts, and other events. Among some of the [most expensive stadiums ever constructed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_stadiums) at a stated cost of \$1.6 billion,[^cite_note-53] MetLife Stadium is home of the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants) and [New York Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Jets) of the [National Football League](21211.md) (NFL) and hosted [Super Bowl XLVIII](4057023.md), which made East Rutherford the smallest city ever to host a [Super Bowl](27718.md); it was the former home of the [New York Guardians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Guardians) of the [XFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XFL_\(2020\)).

East Rutherford had hosted seven games for the [1994 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) at [Giants Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giants_Stadium), including the semi-final game where Bulgaria lost to Italy, which would go on to lose the finals to Brazil.[^cite_note-54] East Rutherford was one of 16 venues chosen to host games for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), with eight matches including the [final](75845879.md) on July 19 at MetLife Stadium (which for the purposes of the event will be identified as New York/New Jersey Stadium).[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] The nations of Brazil, France, Germany and England, as well as several other teams played their games at MetLife Stadium.[^cite_note-57]

Giants Stadium, which hosted the Giants and Jets until 2009, was the original home of the [New York Red Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Red_Bulls) of [Major League Soccer](71802.md) from 1996 (founded as the MetroStars) until they moved in 2010 to [Sports Illustrated Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Illustrated_Stadium) in [Harrison, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison,_New_Jersey).[^cite_note-58]

East Rutherford is the only municipality with fewer than 10,000 residents to have been home to five professional sports teams simultaneously,[^cite_note-59] as well as the smallest city to host any professional sports team within its city limits, with a population of around 10,000.[^cite_note-60]

## Government

### Local government

East Rutherford is governed under the [borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_\(New_Jersey\)) form of New Jersey municipal government, which is used in 218 municipalities (of the 564) statewide, making it the most common form of government in New Jersey.[^cite_note-61] The governing body is comprised of a mayor and a borough council, with all positions elected [at-large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At-large) on a partisan basis as part of the November general election. A mayor is elected directly by the voters to a four-year term of office. The borough council includes six members elected to serve three-year terms on a staggered basis, with two seats coming up for election each year in a three-year cycle.[^cite_note-DataBook-3] The borough form of government used by East Rutherford is a "[weak mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_mayor) / strong council" government in which council members act as the legislative body with the mayor presiding at meetings and voting only in the event of a tie. The mayor can [veto](74661.md) ordinances subject to an [override](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veto_override) by a two-thirds majority vote of the council. The mayor makes committee and liaison assignments for council members, and most appointments are made by the mayor with the advice and consent of the council.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

As of 2026, East Rutherford's [Mayor](101146.md) is [Democrat](5043544.md) Jeffrey Lahullier, whose term of office ends December 31, 2027.[^cite_note-Mayor-4] The borough council members are Council President Antonio Segalini (D, 2026), Daniel Alvarez Jr. (D, 2027), George W. ## Emergency services

### Police

The East Rutherford Police Department (ERPD) serves as the primary law enforcement agency for the borough, providing comprehensive public safety, crime prevention, and emergency response services. Operating on a 24-hour basis, the department is tasked with protecting a resident population of approximately 9,000 while simultaneously managing the significant logistical and security challenges posed by the Meadowlands Sports Complex and the American Dream entertainment and retail complex. Due to the presence of these major regional venues and the borough’s strategic position at the intersection of critical transit corridors—including Route 3, Route 17, Route 120, and the New Jersey Turnpike—the ERPD maintains a high state of operational readiness to accommodate large-scale traffic management and public safety demands.

The department is structured into several specialized components, with the Patrol Division serving as its core. This division utilizes a 12-hour shift system to conduct routine community policing, rapid emergency response, and proactive traffic enforcement. Supporting these field operations is a Detective Bureau dedicated to investigative follow-up, alongside a Traffic Division that manages the complex flow of vehicles through the borough’s high-traffic areas. Administrative oversight and operational integrity are maintained through a Records Bureau and an Internal Affairs unit, ensuring that the department adheres to New Jersey Civil Service standards and professional accountability.

In addition to its localized duties, the ERPD operates a centralized Dispatch Center that provides continuous communication and emergency coordination for police, fire, and emergency medical services within the jurisdiction. Recognizing the complexity of its service area, the department frequently engages in mutual aid agreements with neighboring municipal agencies, as well as county and state law enforcement entities. This collaborative approach ensures that the borough is integrated into a broader regional security framework, allowing for the effective deployment of resources during major events or significant public safety incidents. The Chief of Police is Dennis M. Rivelli.[^cite_note-129]

### Fire

The East Rutherford Fire Department (ERFD) is an [all-volunteer fire department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volunteer_fire_department). The ERFD was organized in 1894 and consists of a [chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_Chief) and three assistant chiefs. There are three [fire stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_station). The department is staffed by eighty fully trained [firefighters](146303.md). The ERFD utilizes two [Engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_apparatus#Fire_engine), a [Ladder truck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_apparatus#Turntable_ladder), a [Heavy Rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_rescue_vehicle), and a [Quint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_apparatus#Quint_and_Quad). The ERFD also provides [emergency medical service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_medical_services) to the borough.[^cite_note-130]

## Universal Oil Products Superfund Site

A portion of land within East Rutherford along Route 17[^cite_note-\:0-138] is designated by the [Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md) as the Universal Oil Products [Superfund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfund) Site. The land was the site of several industrial facilities between 1930 and 1979,[^cite_note-139] with the operation of solvent recovery and wastewater treatment facilities between 1956 and 1971[^cite_note-\:1-140] resulting in chemical seepage into the surrounding soil, water, and marshlands. In 1983, the EPA would add a 75-acre (30 ha) plot of land to the Superfund program's [National Priorities List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Priorities_List).[^cite_note-\:0-138] Early investigations into the site showed the presence of [polychlorinated biphenyls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychlorinated_biphenyl) and [polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycyclic_aromatic_hydrocarbon) in soil samples, in addition to levels of lead totaling up to 14,100 parts per million (ppm).[^cite_note-\:1-140]

Cleanup actions began in 1990, with a plan formalized in 1993.[^cite_note-\:0-138] The plan divided the land into two portions, Operable Unit 1 and Operable Unit 2, or uplands and lowlands respectively.[^cite_note-\:1-140] Cleanup efforts were carried out through 2006, and involved the removal of more than 6,800 cubic yards (5,200 m<sup>3</sup>) of PCP contaminated soil and 7,000 cubic yards (5,400 m<sup>3</sup>) of soil contaminated with volatile organic compounds. Cleanup efforts also included the treatment of approximately 5,600,000 US gallons (21,000,000 L; 4,700,000 imp gal) of groundwater, as well as the installation of an impermeable cap over soil with lead concentration exceeding 600 ppm.[^cite_note-\:1-140]

As a result of the organization's fifth five-year review in 2021, the EPA would state the following year that no further cleanup was required at the Universal Oil Products site.[^cite_note-141] A sixth five-year review was published on March 30, 2026, stating that existing remedies were continuing to function as expected.[^cite_note-\:1-140]
