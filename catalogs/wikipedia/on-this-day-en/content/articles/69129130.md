# Unmanned aerial vehicles in the Iranian military

&#x20;In the mid-1980s, [Iran](14653.md) became interested in [unmanned aerial vehicles](58900.md) (UAVs). Iranians have since began manufacturing (UAVs). As an attack munition rather than intelligence, surveillance, or reconnaissance (ISR) platform, the first generation Ababil appears to have been deployed during the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War).[^cite_note-1] Iran's [Shahed drones](75153890.md) have been actively utilized by Russian forces in the [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md) and by Iranian forces in the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md). In response to their effectiveness, Ukraine created its own analogs of the [Shahed-136](71747103.md) in 2025 for long-range strikes. By December 2025, the U.S. military announced the development of the [LUCAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-cost_Uncrewed_Combat_Attack_System) drone, a clone of the Shahed 136, achieved through reverse engineering, and deployed a squadron in the Middle East.

## History

Iran first began using UAVs in 1985, with the [Ababil-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HESA_Ababil) and [Mohajer-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qods_Mohajer), which spied on Iraqi positions behind front-line trenches. In 1988, following [Operation Praying Mantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Praying_Mantis) where the [US Navy](20518076.md) inflicted heavy loses on Iran’s air and navy forces,[^cite_note-2] Iranian strategists realized they could not openly face the [US](3434750.md) by water. It was then that Iran started investing heavily in drones. The Iranian government has since been open about its interest in UAVs.[^cite_note-3] As well, Iran started manufacturing UAVs.[^cite_note-4] The [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Aerospace Force](5613577.md) is the primary operator of Iran’s growing fleet of UAVs, although most Iranian military services employ them.[^cite_note-5]

[Mohammad Bagheri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Bagheri_\(general\)) in September 2016 announced the development of new long-range attack drones with the capability of precision bombing which was being shared with Russia.[^cite_note-6]

Drones became a technology that Iranian engineers could develop locally as external markets closed due to sanctions or high prices.[^cite_note-7]

On 29 July 2021 US sanctions were targeted at Iran's weapons program. While the US has previously sanctioned Iran's missile programs, the new penalties will target groups that supply parts for drones and missiles to Iran.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

Also on 29 July 2021, a delta-wing drone, similar to unmanned "kamikaze" drones developed for the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md), flew into the [pilothouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilothouse) of the Merchant tanker *Mercer Street* in the [July 2021 Gulf of Oman incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2021_Gulf_of_Oman_incident). It was reported that two crewmembers (a Romanian and UK national assigned to security) were killed.[^cite_note-11] Later tests indicated that the UAV had been rigged to cause injury using a Nitrate-based explosive, [RDX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RDX).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

In October 2021 Iran unveiled its new [Kaman 22 (UAV)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaman_22_\(UAV\)), which seems to be modeled on the U.S.-made [MQ-1 Predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MQ-1_Predator) and with other features from the more advanced [MQ-9 Reaper](4769988.md). The aircraft is also reminiscent of the Chinese [CH-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CH-5) drone.[^cite_note-14]

According to former [U.S. Central Command](338482.md) chief [Gen. Kenneth McKenzie](55230452.md), the U.S. is "for the first time since the Korean War operating without complete air superiority" due to threats posed by Iranian drones.[^cite_note-15]

Issues with Iranian drones include a lack of access to modern components and parts, especially engines, lack of self-defense features for drones, problems with technical reliability, and poor weather.[^cite_note-16]

Iran's [Shahed drones](75153890.md) have been used by Russian forces during the [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md) and by Iranian forces during the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] As a response to the effectiveness of the [Shahed 136 drones](71747103.md), Ukraine developed its own Shahed-136 analogs in 2025 for long-range strikes.[^cite_note-19] In December 2025, the U.S. military said that it had developed the [LUCAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-cost_Uncrewed_Combat_Attack_System) drone, a clone of the Shahed 136 developed from the reverse engineering of the Shahed 136, and deployed a squadron in the Middle East.[^cite_note-20]
