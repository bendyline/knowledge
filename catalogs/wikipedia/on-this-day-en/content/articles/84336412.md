The **[Women's road race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCI_Road_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_road_race)** of the [2026 UCI Road World Championships](82925241.md) was a cycling event that took place on 26 September 2026 in [Montreal](7954681.md), [Canada](5042916.md). It was the 33rd edition of the championship, for which [Magdeleine Vallieres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdeleine_Vallieres) of [Canada](5042916.md) was the defending champion, having won in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UCI_Road_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_road_race).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

The race was won by Dutch rider [Demi Vollering](60239290.md), beating Polish rider [Katarzyna Niewiadoma-Phinney](40712010.md) in a sprint finish. Italian rider [Elisa Longo Borghini](37097733.md) finished third.[^cite_note-3] Vollering became the second woman to achieve a [Triple Crown of Cycling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Crown_of_Cycling), having won the [Giro d'Italia Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Giro_d'Italia_Women) and [Tour de France Femmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Tour_de_France_Femmes) earlier in the season. The 180.1 kilometres (111.9 mi) long course was the longest ever tackled by elite women at the UCI Road World Championships.[^cite_note-4]

## Course

The race covered 180.1 kilometres (111.9 mi) with 2,570 metres (8,430 ft) of total elevation gain. Starting at [Quartier DIX30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartier_DIX30) in [Brossard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brossard), the course passed through the [Montérégie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont%C3%A9r%C3%A9gie) region before crossing the [Samuel De Champlain Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel-De_Champlain_Bridge) to reach the finishing circuit in downtown Montréal.[^cite_note-uci-200days-5][^cite_note-cyclingnews-routes-6]

The circuit was 13.4 kilometres (8.3 mi) in length around [Mount Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Royal), with 269 metres (883 ft) of elevation gain per lap.[^cite_note-uci-200days-5] The circuit included the Voie Camillien-Houde climb (2.3 kilometres (1.4 mi) at 6.2% average gradient), the Chemin de la Polytechnique with sections exceeding 11% gradient, and a false flat rising along Avenue du Parc, which served as the finish line. The circuit would be tackled 8 times. The circuit was similar to that used by the [Grand Prix Cycliste de Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_Cycliste_de_Montr%C3%A9al), as well as previous cycling events in Montréal such as the [1974 UCI Road World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_UCI_Road_World_Championships) and [1976 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_at_the_1976_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_individual_road_race).[^cite_note-uci-200days-5]
