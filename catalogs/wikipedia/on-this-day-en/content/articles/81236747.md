The **2025** **Alberta teachers' strike** was a public education teachers' [strike](155134.md) and [labour dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_dispute) that took place across [Alberta](717.md), [Canada](5042916.md), and affected the [province's education system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Alberta), resulting in a suspension of classes from October 6, 2025, to October 29, 2025. It was ended after the [Notwithstanding Clause](123500.md) was invoked.&#x20;

The strike, organized by the [Alberta Teachers' Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Teachers%27_Association) (ATA), was the result of multiple disagreements with the [Government of Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Alberta) over the terms of a new [labour contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employment_contract).&#x20;

The strike was announced on September 29, 2025, after an offer by the government was declined.[^cite_note-1] The strike began on October 6, 2025, with 51,000 teachers on strike.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] Due to the strike, all public education classes were cancelled, impacting 730,000 students,[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] and 2,500 public, [separate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separate_school), and francophone schools.[^cite_note-7]&#x20;

## Background

The Alberta Teachers' Association focused on seeking better working conditions, increased pay, and [class-size reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class-size_reduction).[^cite_note-8] Many schools reported overcrowded classrooms, with some exceeding 40 students.[^cite_note-9] Underfunding for education in Alberta was also cited as a key contributor to the strike. By the time of the strike, spending per K-12 student was \$13,494 in Alberta, the lowest among all provinces in Canada.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Alberta also ranked highest in percentage of per-student funding to independent private schools.[^cite_note-13]&#x20;



At a rally, with one of the signs saying "38 kids in my class is too many," highlighting concerns about Alberta's overcrowded classrooms.

### Rallies

After the strike announcement, multiple [rallies](6787314.md) were organized across Alberta (primarily in [Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary) and [Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton)),[^cite_note-14] mainly to support educators.[^cite_note-15] A notable rally occurred on October 5, 2025, at the [Alberta Legislature Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Legislature_Building), where thousands of people attended.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]&#x20;

### Back to School Act passage

&#x20;Alberta's premier, [Danielle Smith](23078986.md), ordered teachers back to work on October 29, following the passage of the Back to School Act (Bill 2).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Because of this, teachers are not permitted to strike until August 31, 2028, when the legislation expires.[^cite_note-21]



Albertan students participating in [walkouts](2016860.md) to support teachers.

## Aftermath

Gil McGowan, president of the [Alberta Federation of Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Federation_of_Labour), held a press conference following the passing of Bill 2, saying "We will begin the process of organizing towards a potential [general strike](41138909.md)."[^cite_note-22] He was joined by leaders from the province's other major unions, forming a collective known as the Common Front. Three weeks after McGowan made the claim of organizing towards a general strike, no general strike action had been taken.&#x20;

### Student walkouts

In response to the passing of Bill 2, thousands of students in schools across the province participated in student [walkouts](2016860.md) in support of the teachers, mainly in middle and high schools.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]&#x20;
