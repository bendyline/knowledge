# Airstrike

For other uses, see [Airstrike (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airstrike_\(disambiguation\)).



A [United States Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces) [B-24 Liberator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_B-24_Liberator) after an airstrike on [Saint-Malo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Malo), [France](5843419.md) in 1944

An **airstrike**, **air strike**, or **air raid**[^cite_note-1] is an [offensive](15077993.md) operation against [ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land) or [sea surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_surface) targets carried out by [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft), such as [military balloons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_balloon), [airships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airship), [attack aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_aircraft), [bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomber), [gunships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunship), [attack helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_helicopter) and [attack drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_drone). The official definition of an airstrike includes all sorts of targets, including low-altitude air targets, but in popular usage the term is usually narrowed to a [tactical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tactics) (small-scale) [strafing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strafing), [missile](19594.md) strike or [tactical bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_bombing) on a specific [army](1376.md), [militia](20622.md) or [naval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval) position, as opposed to a larger, more generalized and indiscriminate attack against an entire area such as [carpet bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpet_bombing) and [strategic bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing).

Weapons used in an airstrike can range from [aircraft-mounted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_artillery) [direct-fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct-fire) [cannons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_cannon) and [machine guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_gun), [rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_\(weapon\)#Air-launched) and [air-to-surface missiles](1544116.md), to various types of [aerial bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_bomb) (both [unguided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unguided_bomb) and [guided](3587974.md)), [glide bombs](233100.md), [air-launched cruise missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-launched_cruise_missile), and even [directed-energy weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed-energy_weapon) such as [laser weapons](4714516.md).

In [close air support](600792.md), air strikes are usually controlled by [trained observers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_air_control) on the ground for coordination with ground troops and intelligence in a manner derived from [artillery](2508.md) tactics.

## History

### Beginnings



Rebel infantry advancing under Brazilian air attack during the 1932 [Constitutionalist Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionalist_Revolution)

The first large-scale air raid occurred during [World War I](4764461.md) in 1915, when [London](17867.md) was bombed by 15 German [Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeppelin) dirigibles at night. Since the residents of London, and many of its defenders, were asleep, a loud warning system for air raids made sense, leading to the creation of the [air raid siren](51018.md).[^cite_note-2]

### World War II



The aftermath of a September 1940 air raid on London

It was not until [World War II](32927.md) that the [Oxford English Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary) first records usage of the term "air strike",[^cite_note-3] which remained two separate words for some time thereafter. The [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) also saw the first development of [precision-guided munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition), which were fielded successfully by the Germans, and contributed to the modern sense of air "strike", a precision targeted attack as opposed to a [strafing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strafing) run or [area bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_bombing).

The importance of precision targeting cannot be overstated: by some statistics, over a hundred raids were necessary to destroy a point target in World War II; by the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), the [U.S. Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Air_Force) was able to release to media precise footage of television- or radar-guided bombs directly hitting the target without significant collateral damage (using, for example, the [LANTIRN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LANTIRN) pod). [Paul Fussell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Fussell) noted in his seminal work *[The Great War and Modern Memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_War_and_Modern_Memory)* the popular 20th century tendency to assume an errant bomb hitting a church, for example, was completely deliberate and reflective of the inherent evil of the enemy; over time, expectations for reduced collateral damage have increased to the point that developed countries engaging in war against less technologically advanced countries approach near-zero in terms of such damage.

### After World War II



A [United States Air Force](32090.md) [A-26 Invader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_A-26_Invader) airstrike on warehouses in [Wonsan](527626.md) during the [Korean War](16772.md)

In the [Malayan Emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_Emergency) of the 1950s, British and Commonwealth [Avro Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avro_Lincoln) heavy bombers, [de Havilland Vampire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Vampire) fighter jets, [Supermarine Spitfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermarine_Spitfire), [Bristol Brigands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Brigand), [de Havilland Mosquitos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Mosquito), and a host of other British aircraft were used in Malaya in operations against guerillas. However, the humid climate played havoc with the Mosquito's wooden airframe, and they were soon deployed elsewhere. This period also marked the last combat deployment of British Spitfires.

During the Vietnam War, airstrikes and their doctrine were adjusted to fit the jets, like the [North American F-100 Super Sabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_F-100_Super_Sabre), [Republic F-105 Thunderchief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_F-105_Thunderchief), [Douglas A-4 Skyhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_A-4_Skyhawk), and [McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantom II](11759.md), which were entering the U.S.A.F. and U.S.N. inventory. These aircraft could fly faster, carry more ordnance, and defend themselves better than the [F-4U Corsair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vought_F4U_Corsair) and [North American P-51 Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_P-51_Mustang) fighters that fought during the Korean War, albeit at the cost of the R\&D of the aircraft itself, the weapons, and, most important to the man on the ground, fuel and loiter time, though this situation was slightly alleviated with the introduction of aircraft like the [Cessna A-37 Dragonfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_A-37_Dragonfly), [LTV A-7 Corsair II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTV_A-7_Corsair_II), and [Lockheed AC-130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_AC-130) gunships.

Today, airstrike terminology has extended to the concept of the [strike aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_aircraft), what earlier generations of military aviators referred to as [light bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_bomber) or [attack aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_aircraft). With the near-complete air supremacy enjoyed by developed nations in undeveloped regions, fighter jets can often be modified to add strike capability in a manner less practicable in earlier generations.

Airstrikes can be carried out for strategic purposes outside of general warfare. [Operation Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Opera) was a single eight-ship Israeli airstrike against the Iraqi [Osirak nuclear reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuwaitha_Nuclear_Research_Center), criticized by world opinion but not leading to a general outbreak of war. Such an example of the [preventive strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preventive_war) has created new questions for international law.

Airstrikes, including airstrikes by drones, were extensively used during the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), [war on terror](13425800.md), [War in Afghanistan](19666611.md), [Iraq War](5043324.md), [First Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Libyan_Civil_War), [Syrian Civil War](30741795.md), [Iraqi Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Iraq_\(2013%E2%80%932017\)), [Yemeni Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Civil_War_\(2014%E2%80%93present\)), [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) and the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-4]

### Non-combatant deaths

Main articles: [Aerial bombardment and international law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_bombardment_and_international_law), [Civilian casualty ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_casualty_ratio), and [Collateral damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collateral_damage)



The wreckage of a building in [Chernivtsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernivtsi), [Ukraine](31750.md) after a Russian airstrike during the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), March 2022

Airstrike campaigns often cause the deaths of [non-combatants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-combatant), including civilians.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] [International law](8195726.md) apply the principles of *[military necessity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_necessity)*, *[distinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinction_\(law\))*, and *[proportionality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportionality_\(international_humanitarian_law\))*. These principles emphasize that an attack must be directed towards a [legitimate military target](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legitimate_military_target) and the harm caused to non-combatant targets must be proportionate to the advantage gained by such attack.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] Many modern military aircraft carry [precision-guided munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition), which military sources promote as decreasing civilian deaths.
