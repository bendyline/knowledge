The **Beechcraft 1900** is an [American](3434750.md) twin-engine [turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop) [regional airliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_airliner) manufactured by [Beechcraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft). It is also used as a [freight aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freight_aircraft) and corporate transport, and by several governmental and military organizations. With customers favoring larger [regional jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_jet), then-owner [Raytheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raytheon) ended production in October 2002.[^cite_note-GSO1-3]

Developed from the [Beechcraft Super King Air](9023483.md), the aircraft was designed to carry passengers in all weather conditions from [airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport) with relatively short [runways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runway). It is capable of flying in excess of 600 miles (970 km), although few operators use its full-fuel range.

## Development



A [Continental Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Connection) 1900D

The 1900 is Beechcraft's third regional airliner after the [Beechcraft Model 18](2045174.md) and [Beechcraft Model 99 Airliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_Model_99).

The Beechcraft 1900's design lineage began in 1949 with the [Beechcraft Model 50 Twin Bonanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_Twin_Bonanza), a 5-passenger, [reciprocating engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocating_engine) utility aircraft designed for the U.S. Army. A larger passenger cabin was added to the Twin Bonanza's airframe, and called the [Model 65 Queen Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_Queen_Air). This aircraft was, in turn, further modified by adding [turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop) engines and [cabin pressurization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabin_pressurization), and named the [Model 90 King Air](729142.md). A stretched version of the King Air was later developed and designated the [Model 200 Super King Air](9023483.md). Beechcraft developed the 1900 directly from the Super King Air, in order to provide a [pressurized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabin_pressurization) commuterliner to compete with the [Swearingen Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_Swearingen_Metroliner) and the [British Aerospace Jetstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace_Jetstream).[^cite_note-1900_p57-4]

The 1900 first flew on September 3, 1982, with [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA) certification awarded on November 22, 1983, under [Special Federal Aviation Regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Aviation_Regulations) (SFAR) 41C airworthiness standards.[^cite_note-1900_p57-4] Like the 1900, the 1900C was certified under SFAR 41C, but the later 1900D version was certified to FAR Part 23 "Commuter Category" standards.[^cite_note-ARAC-5]

The 1900 entered service in February 1984,[^cite_note-1900_p57-4] with the first ExecLiner corporate version delivered in 1985. A total of 695 Beechcraft 1900 aircraft were built. In 1991, the price of a 1900D Airliner was \$3.95 million.[^cite_note-BCAdec1991-6] With market trends favoring larger 50- to 90-seat [regional jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_jet), Raytheon ended production of the Beechcraft 1900 in October 2002. Many airlines continue to fly the 1900.

## Design



Beechcraft 1900C



The 1900 has single passenger seats either side of an aisle

Since the 1900 is derived from the King Air, all 1900s share certain characteristics with that aircraft. Cockpit controls and operations are similar to those of the King Air. The aircraft's noticeable deviations from the King Air comes from its longer fuselage and the presence of "stabilons", which are small [horizontal stabilizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_\(aeronautics\)) at the rear of the fuselage for overcoming [T-tail blanking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-tail#Disadvantages) when the aircraft is in higher angles of attack. Additional fins for yaw stability were installed on the tail for 1900Ds, which also feature a taller cabin to allow for a "stand-up cabin" with more walking headspace like conventional airliners. In the United States, the [Federal Aviation Regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Aviation_Regulations) require two pilots for passenger airline operations, but the 1900 is designed and certificated for single-pilot operation in corporate or cargo settings, as is the King Air.

The 1900 is powered by two [Pratt & Whitney Canada PT6A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Canada_PT6A) turboprop engines. The 1900 and 1900C use two PT6A-65B engines, each [flat rated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_rated) at 1,100 [shaft horsepower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsepower#Shaft_horsepower) (820 [kW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt)). The 1900D uses two PT6A-67D engines, each rated at 1,279 shaft horsepower (954 kW). The propellers are manufactured by [Hartzell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartzell_Propeller), with four blades on each propeller. The blades are made from [composite materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_material).

### Performance

At [FL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_level)230, the 1900D cruises at 280 kn (320 mph; 520 km/h) [true airspeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_airspeed) while burning 772 lb (350 kg) per hour.[^cite_note-BCAdec1991-6] Range with 19 passengers for a flight plan with [instrument flight rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_flight_rules) fuel reserves for a 100 nmi (115 mi; 185 km) alternate airport and 45-minute hold is 680 nmi (780 mi; 1,260 km).[^cite_note-BCAdec1991-6] It can take off from 3,740 ft (1,140 m) [runways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runway) at a weight of 16,950 lb (7,690 kg), at sea level and [ISA conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Standard_Atmosphere).[^cite_note-BCAdec1991-6] It can operate on grass and rough runways.

The airplane is certified to fly up to an altitude of 25,000 feet (7,600 m) above [mean sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level), with its [pressurized cabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabin_pressurization).[^cite_note-AFM-7] It is designed to operate in most weather conditions, including [icing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_icing) conditions, and it is usually equipped with [weather radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_radar) to help [pilots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_pilot) avoid severe weather.[^cite_note-AFM-7] The aircraft can be fitted with a lavatory, using space otherwise available for passenger seating and cargo storage.[^cite_note-AFM-7]

## Variants

### 1900

The original design is known simply as the Beechcraft 1900. It features two [airstair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airstair) passenger boarding doors: one near the tail of the aircraft much like the smaller King Airs, and a second at the front just behind the cockpit. It has a small cargo door near the tail for access to the baggage compartment, which is behind the passenger compartment. Only three airframes were built, with "UA" serial numbers of UA-1, UA-2, and UA-3. UA-1 and UA-2 are stored at a Beechcraft facility in [Wichita, Kansas](114932.md). UA-3, registered FAB-043, served in Bolivia until it crashed in November 2011.[^cite_note-8]

### 1900C



[Danish Air Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Air_Transport) 1900C

It quickly became clear that having two airstair doors on an aircraft holding only 19 passengers was excessive. In creating the 1900C, Beechcraft kept the front airstair, but eliminated the aft airstair door, installing an enlarged cargo door in its place. Other than the redesigned door layout, the early 1900Cs were substantially similar to the original 1900s. These were assigned serial numbers starting with the letters UB. A total of 74 UB version were built, many of which remain in service.[^cite_note-ARAC-5] Aircraft in the UA and UB series employ a bladder-type [fuel tank](1071636.md) system in the wings. Later 1900Cs use a [wet wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_wing) fuel system: entire sections of the wing are sealed off for use as fuel tanks. This design change allowed more fuel to be stored, substantially increasing the 1900C's range. The wet wing 1900Cs were assigned serial numbers beginning with "UC." These aircraft are also referred to as 1900C-1s. The wet wings proved popular, and the UC is the most common version of the low-ceiling 1900, with 174 UC airframes built.[^cite_note-ARAC-5]

Raytheon manufactured six 1900C aircraft for use by the U.S. military. These were assigned "UD" serial numbers, UD-1 through UD-6.

### 1900D



The 1900D has a larger cabin to allow passengers to walk upright inside

While the 1900C had become a popular regional airliner, Beechcraft undertook a substantial redesign of the aircraft, and in 1991 introduced a new version called the 1900D.

The 1900 and 1900C, like most 19-passenger airliners and small [business jets](247003.md), have fairly small passenger cabins, with ceilings so low that passengers (of typical male heights) cannot walk through the interior without bending forward. The 1900D was designed to remedy this by providing a "stand-up cabin", which would allow most passengers to walk upright. It is one of only two 19-seat airliners with this feature, the other being the [British Aerospace Jetstream 31/32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace_Jetstream).[^cite_note-ARAC-5]

Because the taller passenger cabin adds both weight and drag to the airplane, other elements of the 1900D were also changed. More powerful engines and modified propellers were installed, [winglets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wingtip_device) were added to reduce drag and increase the wings' efficiency, and the tail was made larger in response to the more powerful engines. The cockpit was updated with an [Electronic Flight Instrument System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Flight_Instrument_System) (EFIS). The 1900D was certified under the then-new FAR Part 23 "Commuter Category" standards, which had replaced the earlier SFAR 41C. Since the UD serial numbers were already in use by the military 1900s, the 1900D airplanes have serial numbers beginning with UE. The 1900D is the most popular version of the airliner, with 439 of the 1900D built.[^cite_note-ARAC-5]

### Super Freighter

A [supplemental type certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supplemental_type_certificate) has been awarded to Alpine Air Express to convert 1900D into cargo aircraft. The STC involved adding a second emergency exit to the cockpit and converting the interior to a cargo configuration. The 1900D can carry 900 cubic feet (25 cubic meters) of cargo, 30% more than a 1900C. Powered by PT6-67D, the super freighter has a max takeoff weight of 17,120 pounds (7,770 kilograms) and is certified under the commuter category. Due to its standup cabin, the 1900D has a large internal volume. The large volume lends the aircraft well to the cargo industry where many packages are bulky and low in density. The 1900D is able to carry nearly as much volume as an [EMB-120](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_EMB_120_Brasilia) and is certified for single pilot cargo operations. The single pilot crew and surplus of airframes allows for reduced operator costs.[^cite_note-9]

### Military C-12J

The U.S. military designation for the Beechcraft 1900C is C-12J. This is a variant of the [C-12 Huron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_C-12_Huron), which is the most common designation for military King Airs. The C-12J includes the six Beechcraft 1900s with 'UD' serial number prefixes built for the U.S. military, as well as other 1900Cs in U.S. military service.

Examples of C-12J aircraft in military service include one used for [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) [jamming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_jamming) tests at the 586th Flight Test Squadron, [Holloman Air Force Base](1092369.md), New Mexico,[^cite_note-GSO2-10][^cite_note-HAFB-11] and three based at the 459th Airlift Squadron, [Yokota Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokota_Air_Base), Japan. The U.S. Army operates both C-12J and 1900D aircraft along with other C-12 (King Air) aircraft.[^cite_note-ARAV-12]

## Accidents and incidents

- November 23, 1987: a [Ryan Air Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Air_Services) 1900 crashed on approach to the airport at Homer, Alaska. [Flight 103](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Air_Services_Flight_103) was fully loaded (all 19 seats occupied; 1437 pounds of cargo) when it took off from Kodiak. On departure at Kodiak, the aircraft displayed signs of being overloaded when it lifted off the runway, fell back and accelerated for about another 15 knots (17 mph) before it became airborne. The aircraft was approaching Homer when it was cleared for the localizer/DME approach to runway 3. The crew reported a 2-mile (3.2 km) final five minutes later. On short final the wings were seen to rock back and forth; the aircraft then dropped steeply to the ground in a rather flat attitude, struck the airport perimeter fence and slid to a stop on its belly. Both pilots and sixteen of the nineteen passengers were killed. The investigation into the crash (the first crash of a 1900) stated the probable cause as "the failure of the flight crew to properly supervise the loading of the airplane which resulted in the [center of gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_gravity_of_an_aircraft) being displaced to such an aft location that the airplane control was lost when the flaps were lowered for landing."

- May 18, 1990: a 1900C, operating for [Aerolift Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerolift_Philippines) as [Flight 075](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerolift_Philippines_Flight_075) to Surigao, took off from runway 13 at Manila's international airport. During takeoff the no. 2 engine failed. The airplane began turning to the right as the crew radioed that they were returning to the airport. With the undercarriage down and the flaps still in takeoff position the airplane impacted a house in the suburban Paranaque neighborhood. All 21 on board the aircraft and a family of four inside the house were killed making the crash the deadliest accident involving the Beechcraft 1900.

## Specifications (Beechcraft 1900D)

*Data from* Raytheon: Beechcraft 1900D Passenger Specifications and Performance[^cite_note-52]

**General characteristics**

- **Crew:** 1 (2 for airline operations)

- **Capacity:** 19 passengers and 6,356 lb (2,882 kg) useful load

- **Length:** 57 ft 8 in (17.62 m)

- **Wingspan:** 57 ft 9 in (17.64 m)

- **Height:** 15 ft 5 in (4.72 m)

- **Empty weight:** 10,874 lb (4,932 kg)

- **Gross weight:** 17,227 lb (7,814 kg)

- **Max takeoff weight:** 17,120 lb (7,764 kg)

- **Fuel capacity:** 4,458 lb (2,022 kg)

- **Fuel type**: [Jet A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_A) recommended, others usable

- **Powerplant:** 2 × [Pratt & Whitney Canada PT6A-67D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Canada_PT6) [turboprops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop), 1,279 shp (955 kW) each

**Performance**

- **Cruise speed:** 280 kn (322 mph, 518 km/h) at 20,000 ft (6,100 m)

- **Range:** 382 nmi (439 mi, 707 km) with 19 passenger payload

- **Ferry range:** 1,245 nmi (1,432 mi, 2,306 km)

- **Service ceiling:** 25,000 ft (7,620 m)

- **Rate of climb:** 2,615 ft/min (13.28 m/s)

**Avionics**\


- [Rockwell Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockwell_Collins) EFIS-84 [Electronic Flight Instrument System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Flight_Instrument_System)
