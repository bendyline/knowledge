The **General Staff of the Islamic Republic of Iran Armed Forces**[^cite_note-1] is the most senior military body of [Iranian Armed Forces](14305018.md). Its responsibility is to implement policy, monitor and coordinate activities between the [Islamic Republic of Iran Army](48259216.md) and the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md).[^cite_note-a-2]

## Function

Iran's two separate military forces, the [Islamic Republic of Iran Army](48259216.md) (Artesh) and the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC) are subordinate to the General Staff, as well as Iran's national police force, the [Police Command](5187924.md).[^cite_note-a-2][^cite_note-3]

The general staff was set up in 1989 to enhance cooperation and counterbalance the [rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interservice_rivalry) between the armed forces and is directly decreed by [Supreme Leader of Iran](36814344.md), while the [Ministry of Defence and Armed Forces Logistics](18379483.md), responsible for planning, logistics and funding of the armed forces is part of the [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Iran) under the [President of Iran](233913.md).[^cite_note-a-2][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

The position of chief of the general staff is currently held by Maj. Gen. [Ali Abdollahi](51009457.md).

## Passive Defence and Cyberwarfare

Main article: [Cyberwarfare and Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare_and_Iran)

The [National Organization for Passive Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Organization_for_Passive_Defense) also reports to the Armed Forces General Staff.

It has been reported that Iran is one of the five countries that has a cyber-army capable of conducting cyber-warfare operations. It has also been reported that Iran has immensely increased its [cyberwarfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare) capability since the post presidential election un-rest.[^cite_note-Leyne-7][^cite_note-payvand-8][^cite_note-channel4-9][^cite_note-bbc100130-10][^cite_note-Marwaha-11] In 2010, China accused the United States of having initiated a cyber war against Iran through websites such as [Twitter](9988187.md) and [YouTube](3524766.md) in addition to employing a hacker brigade for the purpose of fomenting unrest in Iran.[^cite_note-Tisdall-12][^cite_note-washingtontimes-13]

In early 2010, two new garrisons for cyberwarfare were established at [Zanjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanjan,_Iran) and [Isfahan](223149.md).[^cite_note-isfahan-14] As of 2024, Iran's cyber activities have become more sophisticated, engaging in a persistent cyber struggle with Israel that focuses on espionage, information warfare, and attempts to target critical infrastructure, although Israel maintains a technological edge.[^cite_note-15]
