**Canadians** are people identified with the country of [Canada](5042916.md). This connection may be residential, legal, historical or cultural. For most Canadians, many (or all) of these connections exist and are collectively the source of their being *Canadian*.

Canada is a multilingual and [multicultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism) society home to people of groups of many different ethnic, religious, and national origins, with the majority of the population made up of [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World) [immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Canada) and their descendants. Following the initial period of [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France) and then the much larger [British colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colonization_of_the_Americas), different waves (or peaks) of immigration and settlement of non-Indigenous peoples took place over the course of nearly two centuries and continue today. Elements of Indigenous, French, British, and more recent immigrant customs, languages, and religions have combined to form the [culture of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Canada), and thus a [Canadian identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_identity) and [Canadian values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_values). Canada has also been strongly influenced by its linguistic, geographic, and economic neighbour—the United States.

Canadian independence from the [United Kingdom](31717.md) grew gradually over the course of many years following the formation of the [Canadian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation) in 1867. The [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) and [Second World Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), in particular, gave rise to a desire among Canadians to have their country recognized as a fully-fledged, sovereign state, with a distinct citizenship. Legislative independence was established with the passage of the [Statute of Westminster, 1931](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Westminster,_1931), the [Canadian Citizenship Act, 1946](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Citizenship_Act,_1946), took effect on January 1, 1947, and full sovereignty was achieved with the [patriation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriation) of the constitution in 1982. Canada's [nationality law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_nationality_law) closely mirrored that of the United Kingdom. Legislation since the mid-20th century represents Canadians' commitment to [multilateralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilateralism) and [socioeconomic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socioeconomic_development). Nearly nine in ten (87%) Canadians were proud to identify as Canadian, with over half (61%) expressing they were very proud.[^cite_note-d680-13]

## Term

The word *Canadian* originally applied, in its French form, *Canadien*, to the colonists residing in the northern part of [New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France)[^cite_note-14]— in [Quebec](7954867.md), and [Ontario](22218.md)—during the 16th, 17th, and 18th centuries. The French colonists in Maritime Canada (New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, and Prince Edward Island), were known as [Acadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acadians).

When [Prince Edward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward,_Duke_of_Kent_and_Strathearn) (a son of King [George III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_III)) addressed, in English and French, a group of rioters at a poll in [Charlesbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlesbourg,_Quebec_City), [Lower Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Canada) (today Quebec), during the election of the [Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Lower_Canada) in June 1792,[^cite_note-Toffoli33-15] he stated, "I urge you to unanimity and concord. Let me hear no more of the odious distinction of English and French. You are all His Britannic Majesty's beloved Canadian subjects."[^cite_note-16] It was the first-known use of the term *Canadian* to mean both [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Canadian) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Canadians) settlers in [the Canadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Canadas).[^cite_note-Toffoli33-15][^cite_note-17]

## Population

See also: [Population of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_of_Canada) and [Demographics of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Canada)

As of 2010, Canadians make up 0.5% of the [world's total population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_population),[^cite_note-18] having relied upon immigration for population growth and social development.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECorneliusTsudaMartinHollifield2004100-19] Approximately 41% of current Canadians are first- or second-generation immigrants,[^cite_note-immigrantclass-20] and 20% of Canadian residents in the 2000s were not born in the country.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBybeeMcCrae200992-21] [Statistics Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Canada) projects that, by 2031, nearly one-half of Canadians above the age of 15 will be [foreign-born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_born) or have one foreign-born parent.[^cite_note-22] [Indigenous peoples](196075.md), according to the [2016 Canadian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_2016_Census), numbered at 1,673,780 or 4.9% of the country's 35,151,728 population.[^cite_note-firstnations-23]

### Original inhabitants

The [Paleo-Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indian) archeological sites at [Old Crow Flats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Crow_Flats) and [Bluefish Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluefish_Caves) are two of the oldest sites of human habitation in Canada.[^cite_note-24] The [characteristics of Indigenous societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_and_industrial_history_of_Canada#The_Stone_Age:_Fire_\(14,000_BC_%E2%80%93_AD_1600\)) included permanent settlements, agriculture, complex societal hierarchies, and trading networks.[^cite_note-25] Some of these cultures had collapsed by the time European explorers arrived in the late 15th and early 16th centuries and have only been discovered through archeological investigations.[^cite_note-26] [Indigenous peoples in present-day Canada](196075.md) include the [First Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada), [Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit), and [Métis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tis_people_in_Canada),[^cite_note-GraberKuprecht2012-27] the last being of [mixed descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed-blood) who originated in the mid-17th century when First Nations people married European settlers and their offspring subsequently developed their own identity.[^cite_note-GraberKuprecht2012-27]

### Immigration

Main articles: [Immigration to Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Canada), [Canada immigration statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_immigration_statistics), and [Annual immigration statistics of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_immigration_statistics_of_Canada)

While the first contact with Europeans and [Indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) peoples in Canada had occurred a century or more before, the first group of permanent settlers were the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people), who founded the [New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France) settlements, in present-day [Quebec](7954867.md) and [Ontario](22218.md); and [Acadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acadia), in present-day [Nova Scotia](21184.md) and [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick), during the early part of the 17th century.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHudson200215-28][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGriffiths20054-29]

Approximately 100 [Irish-born families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Quebecers) would settle the [Saint Lawrence Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Lawrence_River) by 1700, assimilating into the *Canadien* population and culture.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcGowan1999-30][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMagocsi1999736ff-31] During the 18th and 19th century; immigration westward (to the area known as [Rupert's Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert's_Land)) was carried out by "[Voyageurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyageurs)"; French settlers working for the [North West Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_Company); and by [British](19097669.md) settlers ([English](18803164.md) and [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_people)) representing the [Hudson's Bay Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson's_Bay_Company), coupled with independent entrepreneurial woodsman called [coureur des bois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coureur_des_bois).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStandford200042-32] This arrival of newcomers led to the creation of the [Métis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tis_people_\(Canada\)), an ethnic group of mixed European and [First Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada) parentage.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorrows2010134-33]

In the wake of the British [Conquest of New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_1760) in 1760 and the [Expulsion of the Acadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_the_Acadians), many families from the British colonies in [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) moved over into Nova Scotia and other colonies in Canada, where the British made farmland available to British settlers on easy terms. More settlers arrived during and after the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), when approximately 60,000 [United Empire Loyalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Empire_Loyalist) fled to [British North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_North_America), a large portion of whom settled in New Brunswick.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurrinJohnsonMcPhersonFahs2007172-34] After the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812), British (including British army regulars), Scottish, and Irish immigration was encouraged throughout Rupert's Land, [Upper Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Canada) and [Lower Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Canada).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFeltes199919-35]

Between 1815 and 1850, some 800,000 immigrants came to the colonies of British North America, mainly from the [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles) as part of the [Great Migration of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Migration_of_Canada).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarland-Jacobs2007177-36] These new arrivals included some [Gaelic-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Gaelic) [Highland Scots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Scots) displaced by the [Highland Clearances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Clearances) to Nova Scotia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECampey2008122-37] The [Great Famine of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Famine_\(Ireland\)) of the 1840s significantly increased the pace of Irish immigration to [Prince Edward Island](23071.md) and the [Province of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Canada), with over 35,000 distressed individuals landing in [Toronto](64646.md) in 1847 and 1848.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcGowan200997-38][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEElliott2004106-39] Descendants of Francophone and Anglophone northern Europeans who arrived in the 17th, 18th, and 19th centuries are often referred to as [Old Stock Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Stock_Canadians).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

Beginning in the late 1850s, the immigration of Chinese into the [Colony of Vancouver Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Vancouver_Island) and [Colony of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_British_Columbia_\(1858%E2%80%931866\)) peaked with the onset of the [Fraser Canyon Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Canyon_Gold_Rush).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHallHwang20019-42] The *[Chinese Immigration Act of 1885](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Immigration_Act_of_1885)* eventually placed a head tax on all Chinese immigrants, in hopes of discouraging Chinese immigration after completion of the [Canadian Pacific Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Pacific_Railway).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHuang2006107-43] Additionally, growing [South Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asians) immigration into British Columbia during the early 1900s[^cite_note-SinghHirap94-44] led to the [continuous journey regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_journey_regulation) act of 1908 which indirectly halted [Indian](7564733.md) immigration to Canada, as later evidenced by the infamous 1914 [*Komagata Maru* incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komagata_Maru_incident).

The [population of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_of_Canada) has consistently risen, doubling approximately every 40 years, since the establishment of the [Canadian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation) in 1867.[^cite_note-47] In the mid-to-late 19th century, Canada had a policy of assisting [immigrants from Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Canadian), including an estimated 100,000 unwanted "[Home Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Children)" from Britain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHobbsMacKechnieLavalette199933-48] [Block settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_settlement) communities were established throughout [Western Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada) between the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Some were planned and others were spontaneously created by the settlers themselves.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartens200428-49] Canada received mainly European immigrants, predominantly [Italians](385155.md), Germans, [Scandinavians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia), [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people), [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people), and [Ukrainians](145144.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDay2000124-50] Legislative restrictions on immigration (such as the continuous journey regulation and *[Chinese Immigration Act, 1923](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Immigration_Act,_1923)*) that had favoured British and other European immigrants were amended in the 1960s, opening the doors to immigrants from all parts of the world.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKsenychLiu2001407-51] While the 1950s had still seen high levels of immigration by Europeans, by the 1970s immigrants were increasingly Chinese, Indian, [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_people), [Jamaican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaicans), and [Haitian](13373.md).[^cite_note-Multicultural-52] During the late 1960s and early 1970s, Canada received many [American](19792942.md) [Vietnam War draft dissenters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draft_evasion_in_the_Vietnam_War).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKusch20012-53] Throughout the late 1980s and 1990s, Canada's growing Pacific trade brought with it a large influx of [South Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_South_Asia), who tended to settle in [British Columbia](3392.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAgnew2007182-54] Immigrants of all backgrounds tend to settle in the [major urban centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_census_metropolitan_areas_and_agglomerations_in_Canada).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilkinson1980200-55][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGood200913-56] The Canadian public, as well as the major political parties, are tolerant of immigrants.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHollifieldMartinOrrenius201411-57]

The majority of [illegal immigrants](7124412.md) come from the [southern provinces of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), with Asia as a whole, [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe), [Caribbean](18956035.md), [Africa](5334607.md), and the [Middle East](19323.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchneider2009367-58] Estimates of numbers of illegal immigrants range between 35,000 and 120,000.[^cite_note-59]

[Canadian citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_nationality_law) is typically obtained by birth in Canada or by birth or adoption abroad when at least one biological parent or adoptive parent is a Canadian citizen who was born in Canada or naturalized in Canada (and did not receive citizenship by being born outside of Canada to a Canadian citizen).[^cite_note-parent-60] It can also be granted to a [permanent resident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_resident_\(Canada\)) who lives in Canada for three out of four years and meets specific requirements.[^cite_note-rights-61] Canada [established its own nationality law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Canadian_nationality_law) in 1946, with the enactment of the *[Canadian Citizenship Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Citizenship_Act_1946)* which took effect on January 1, 1947.[^cite_note-62] The *[Immigration and Refugee Protection Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_and_Refugee_Protection_Act)* was passed by the [Parliament of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada) in 2001 as Bill C-11, which replaced the *[Immigration Act, 1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_Act,_1976)* as the primary federal legislation regulating immigration.[^cite_note-63] Prior to the conferring of legal status on Canadian citizenship, Canada's naturalization laws consisted of a multitude of Acts beginning with the *Immigration Act* of 1910.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBloemraad2006269-64]

According to [Citizenship and Immigration Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_and_Immigration_Canada), there are three main classifications for immigrants: *family class* (persons closely related to Canadian residents), *economic class* (admitted on the basis of a point system that accounts for age, health and labour-market skills required for cost effectively inducting the immigrants into Canada's labour market) and *refugee class* (those seeking protection by applying to remain in the country by way of the [Canadian immigration and refugee law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_immigration_and_refugee_law)).[^cite_note-65] In 2008, there were 65,567 immigrants in the family class, 21,860 refugees, and 149,072 economic immigrants amongst the 247,243 total immigrants to the country.[^cite_note-immigrantclass-20] Canada resettles over one in 10 of the world's [refugees](45547.md)[^cite_note-66] and has one of the highest per-capita immigration rates in the world.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZimmerman200851-67]

As of a 2010 report by the [Asia Pacific Foundation of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Pacific_Foundation_of_Canada), there were 2.8 million [Canadian citizens abroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_diaspora).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDeVoretz2011-68] This represents about 8% of the total Canadian population. Of those living abroad, the United States, Hong Kong, the United Kingdom, Taiwan, China, Lebanon, United Arab Emirates, and Australia have the largest Canadian diaspora. Canadians in the United States constitute the greatest single expatriate community at over 1 million in 2009, representing 35.8% of all Canadians abroad.[^cite_note-69] Under current [Canadian law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_law), Canada does not restrict [dual citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_citizenship), but [Passport Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passport_Canada) encourages its citizens to travel abroad on their [Canadian passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_passport) so that they can access Canadian [consular services](6770059.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGray2010302-70]

According to the [2021 Canadian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Canadian_census), over 450 "[ethnic or cultural origins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_origins_of_people_in_Canada)" were self-reported by Canadians.[^cite_note-2021newsrelease-4] The major [panethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panethnicity) origin groups in Canada are: [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Canadians) (52.5%), [North American](21139.md) (22.9%), [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Asia) (19.3%), [North American Indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) (6.1%), [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Canadians) (3.8%), [Central](6121.md) and [South American](26769.md) (2.5%), [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_people) (2.1%), [Oceanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanian_people) (0.3%), and Other (6%).[^cite_note-2021newsrelease-4][^cite_note-ethnicpopulation2021-72] Statistics Canada reports that 35.5% of the population reported multiple ethnic origins, thus the overall total is greater than 100%.[^cite_note-2021newsrelease-4][^cite_note-ethnic-73]

The country's ten largest self-reported specific ethnic or cultural origins in 2021 were [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_ethnicity)[^cite_note-Canadian-71] (accounting for 15.6 percent of the population), followed by [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Canadians) (14.7 percent), [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Canadians) (12.1 percent), [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Canadians) (12.1 percent), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Canadians) (11.0 percent), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Canadians) (8.1 percent), [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Canadians) (5.1 percent),[^cite_note-Indo-Canadian-78] [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Canadians) (4.7 percent), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Canadians) (4.3 percent), and [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Canadians) (3.5 percent).[^cite_note-ethnicpopulationordered2021-77][^cite_note-ethnicpopulation2021-72]

Of the 36.3 million people enumerated in 2021 approximately 25.4 million reported being [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people), representing 69.8 percent of the population.[^cite_note-2021newsreleaseB-2][^cite_note-Statistics_Canada_2022-79][^cite_note-populationgroups2021-80] The Indigenous population representing 5 percent or 1.8 million individuals, grew by 9.4 percent compared to the non-Indigenous population, which grew by 5.3 percent from 2016 to 2021.[^cite_note-Statistics_Canada_2022a-81] One out of every four Canadians or 26.5 percent of the population belonged to a non-White and non-Indigenous [visible minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_minority),[^cite_note-populationgroups2021-80][^cite_note-83] the largest of which in 2021 were [South Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Canadians) (2.6 million people; 7.1 percent), Chinese (1.7 million; 4.7 percent) and [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Canadians) (1.5 million; 4.3 percent).[^cite_note-Statistics_Canada_2022-79]

Between 2011 and 2016, the visible minority population rose by 18.4 percent.[^cite_note-Census_Profile,_2016_Census-84] In 1961, less than two percent of Canada's population (about 300,000 people) were members of visible minority groups.[^cite_note-85] The 2021 Census indicated that 8.3 million people, or almost one-quarter (23.0 percent) of the population reported themselves as being or having been a [landed immigrant or permanent resident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_residency_in_Canada) in Canada—above the [1921 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1921_Canadian_Census) previous record of 22.3 percent.[^cite_note-Statistics_Canada_2022b-86] In 2021 India, China, and the Philippines were the top three countries of origin for immigrants moving to Canada.[^cite_note-87]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Canada)



A 1911 political cartoon on Canada's bicultural identity showing a flag combining symbols of Britain, France and Canada; titled "The next favor. 'A flag to suit the minority.'"

[Canadian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Canada) is primarily a [Western culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture), with influences by First Nations and other cultures. It is a product of its [ethnicities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_origins_of_people_in_Canada), [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Canada), [religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Canada), [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Canada), and [legal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Canada)(s). Canada has been shaped by waves of migration that have influenced [art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_art), [cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_cuisine), [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_literature), [humour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_humour), and [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Canada).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKalman20094–7-88] Today, Canada has a diverse makeup of nationalities and constitutional protection for policies that promote [multiculturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism) rather than [cultural assimilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_assimilation).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDeRoccoChabot200813-89] In Quebec, cultural identity is strong, and many French-speaking commentators speak of a [Quebec culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Quebec) distinct from English Canadian culture.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFranklinBaun199561-90] However, as a whole, Canada is a [cultural mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_mosaic): a collection of several regional, Indigenous, and ethnic subcultures.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEnglish2004111-91][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBurgess200531-92]

Canadian government policies such as [official bilingualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_bilingualism_in_Canada); [publicly funded health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_in_Canada); [higher and more progressive taxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_taxes_in_Canada); outlawing [capital punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Canada); strong efforts to eliminate [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_Canada); strict [gun control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_politics_in_Canada); the legalizing of [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Canada), [pregnancy terminations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_Canada), [euthanasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euthanasia_in_Canada) and [cannabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_in_Canada) are social indicators of Canada's political and [cultural values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_values).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrickerWright200516-93][^cite_note-94] [American media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_media) and entertainment are popular, if not dominant, in English Canada; conversely, many Canadian cultural products and entertainers are successful in the United States and worldwide.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlackwell2005-95] The [Government of Canada](616255.md) has also influenced culture with programs, laws, and institutions. It has created [Crown corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_corporations_of_Canada) to promote Canadian culture through media, and has also tried to [protect Canadian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_cultural_protectionism) by setting legal minimums on [Canadian content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_content).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEArmstrong2010144-96]



*Monument to Multiculturalism* by Francesco Pirelli in [Toronto](64646.md); four identical sculptures are located in [Buffalo City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_City_Local_Municipality), [Changchun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changchun), [Sarajevo](26786.md), and [Sydney](27862.md)

Canadian culture has historically been influenced by [European culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Europe) and traditions, especially [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_United_Kingdom) and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_France), and by its own [Indigenous cultures](196075.md#Culture). Most of Canada's territory was inhabited and developed later than other European colonies in the Americas, with the result that themes and symbols of pioneers, trappers, and traders were important in the early development of the [Canadian identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_identity).[^cite_note-pioneer_lit-97] First Nations played a critical part in the development of [European colonies in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_colonies_and_territories_in_Canada), particularly for their role in assisting exploration of the continent during the [North American fur trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_fur_trade).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWhiteFindlay199967-98] The British conquest of New France in the mid-1700s brought a large [Francophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francophone) population under [British Imperial rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_under_British_Imperial_control), creating a need for compromise and accommodation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDufour199025-99] The new British rulers left alone much of the religious, political, and social culture of the French-speaking *[habitants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitants)*, guaranteeing through the *[Quebec Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Act)* of 1774 the right of the *Canadiens* to practise the Catholic faith and to use [French civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_France) (now [Quebec law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_law)).[^cite_note-100]

The *[Constitution Act, 1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Act,_1867)* was designed to meet the growing calls of Canadians for autonomy from British rule, while avoiding the overly strong decentralization that contributed to the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) in the United States.[^cite_note-civil_war-101] The compromises made by the [Fathers of Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fathers_of_Confederation) set Canadians on a path to [bilingualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilingualism), and this in turn contributed to an acceptance of diversity.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVaillancourtCoche200911-102][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMagocsi20023-103]

The [Canadian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Forces) and overall civilian participation in the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Canada_during_World_War_I) and [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Canada_during_World_War_II) helped to foster [Canadian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_nationalism),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENersessian2007-104][^cite_note-CIC22-105] however, in 1917 and 1944, [conscription crisis'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription_Crisis_of_1917) highlighted the considerable rift along ethnic lines between Anglophones and Francophones.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELinteauDurocherRobert1983522-106] As a result of the First and Second World Wars, the Government of Canada became more assertive and less deferential to British authority.[^cite_note-107] With the gradual loosening of political ties to the United Kingdom and the modernization of Canadian immigration policies, 20th-century immigrants with [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_of_Africa), Caribbean and [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Asia) nationalities have added to the Canadian identity and its culture.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBodvarssonVan_den_Berg2009380-108] The multiple-origins immigration pattern continues today, with the arrival of large numbers of immigrants from non-British or non-French backgrounds.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPrato200950-109]

[Multiculturalism in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism_in_Canada) was adopted as the official policy of the government during the premiership of [Pierre Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Trudeau) in the 1970s and 1980s.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuncanLey1993205-110] The Canadian government has often been described as the instigator of multicultural ideology, because of its public emphasis on the [social importance of immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_impact_of_immigration_to_Canada).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWayland1997-111] Multiculturalism is administered by the [Department of Citizenship and Immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Citizenship_and_Immigration_Canada) and reflected in the law through the *[Canadian Multiculturalism Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Multiculturalism_Act)*[^cite_note-112] and [section 27 of the *Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_27_of_the_Canadian_Charter_of_Rights_and_Freedoms).[^cite_note-113]

### Values

Main article: [Canadian values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_values)

[Canadian values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_values) are the perceived commonly shared ethical and human values of Canadians.[^cite_note-Canadian_values_Douglas_Baer_1993-114] The majority of Canadians believe they share specific values,[^cite_note-Canadian_values_d680-115][^cite_note-Canadian_values_j676-116] with a plurality identifying [human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Canada), [respect for the law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Canada) and [gender equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminism_in_Canada) as collective principles.[^cite_note-Canadian_values_u440-117][^cite_note-Canadian_values_v187-118] Canadians generally exhibit pride in [equality before the law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equality_before_the_law), fairness, [social justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_justice), freedom, and respect for others;[^cite_note-Canadian_values_h799-119] while often making personal decisions based on self-interests rather than a collective [Canadian identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_identity).[^cite_note-Canadian_values_g083-120] Tolerance and sensitivity hold significant importance in [Canada's multicultural society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism_in_Canada), as does politeness.[^cite_note-Canadian_values_g083-120][^cite_note-Canadian_values_v187-118]

### Identity

Main article: [Canadian identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_identity)

[Canadian identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_identity) refers to the unique culture, characteristics and condition of being Canadian, as well as the many symbols and expressions that set [Canada](5042916.md) and Canadians apart from other peoples and cultures of the world. Changes in demographics, history, and social interactions have led to alterations in the Canadian identity over time. This identity is not fixed; as [Canadian values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_values) evolve they impact Canadians' social integration, civic engagement, and connections with one another.[^cite_note-Canadian_identity_l552-121] In Quebec, identity is strong and there is a [French Canadian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Quebec) that is distinct from [English Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Canada) and Indigenous identities.[^cite_note-122] Nonetheless, as a whole, [Canadian multiculturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_multiculturalism), is in theory a [cultural mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_mosaic) of regional ethnic subcultures and diverse areas including [ethnic enclaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism_in_Canada#Settlement),{{multiref2|

The highest pride levels were for [Canadian history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Canada) (70%), the [armed forces](182792.md) (64%), the [health care system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Canada) (64%), and the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Canada) (63%). However, pride in Canada’s political influence was lower at 46%. Outside Quebec, pride ranged from 91% in British Columbia to 94% in Prince Edward Island, while 70% of Quebec residents felt proud. Seniors and women showed the most pride, especially among first- and second-generation immigrants, who valued both Canadian identity and achievements.[^cite_note-d680-13]

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Canada)



Freedom of religion sculpture by Marlene Hilton Moore at the McMurtry Gardens of Justice in Toronto

[^cite_note-McMurtry_Gardens_of_Justice-123]

[Religion in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Canada) encompasses a wide range of beliefs and customs that historically has been dominated by [Christianity](5211.md).[^cite_note-Religion_in_Canada_HalesLauzon2009-124][^cite_note-Religion_in_Canada_Cornelissen_2021-125] The [constitution of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Canada) refers to 'God'; however Canada has no official church and the government is officially committed to [religious pluralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_pluralism).[^cite_note-126] [Freedom of religion in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_religion_in_Canada) is a constitutionally protected right, allowing individuals to assemble and worship without limitation or interference.[^cite_note-127] Rates of religious adherence have steadily decreased since the 1960s.[^cite_note-Religion_in_Canada_Cornelissen_2021-125] After having once been central and integral to Canadian culture and daily life,[^cite_note-Religion_in_Canada_Roberts2005w-128] Canada has become a [post-Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postchristianity) state.[^cite_note-Religion_in_Canada_BramadatSeljak2009-129][^cite_note-Religion_in_Canada_Bowen2004-130][^cite_note-Religion_in_Canada_GregoryJohnston2009-131] Although the majority of Canadians consider [religion to be unimportant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Importance_of_religion_by_country) in their daily lives,[^cite_note-Religion_in_Canada_Punnett2015-132] they still believe in God.[^cite_note-Religion_in_Canada_Haskell2009-133] The practice of religion is generally considered a private matter throughout society and the state.[^cite_note-Religion_in_Canada_BoyleSheen2013-134]

### Symbols

Main article: [National symbols of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_symbols_of_Canada)

Themes of nature, pioneers, trappers, and traders played an important part in the early development of Canadian symbolism.[^cite_note-135] Modern symbols emphasize the country's geography, northern climate, lifestyles, and the Canadianization of traditional European and Indigenous symbols.[^cite_note-136] The use of the [maple leaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_leaf) as a symbol dates to the early 18th century in New France.[^cite_note-Canada_g606-137] The maple leaf is depicted on Canada's [current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Canada) and [previous flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Red_Ensign) and on the [arms of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_of_Canada).[^cite_note-Canada_symbol1-138] Canada's official tartan, known as the "[maple leaf tartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_tartans_of_Canada)", reflects the colours of the maple leaf through the seasons—green in the [spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_\(season\)), gold in the early [autumn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn), red at the first [frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frost), and brown after falling.[^cite_note-139] The arms of Canada are closely modelled after [those of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_coat_of_arms_of_the_United_Kingdom), with French and distinctive Canadian elements replacing or added to those derived from the British version.[^cite_note-140]

### Languages

Main article: [Languages of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Canada)



Approximately 98% of Canadians can speak English or French (2006)

[^cite_note-Highlights-141]

&#x20;*&#x20; English – 56.9%  English and French (Bilingual) – 16.1%   French – 21.3%  Sparsely populated area (<0.4 km<sup>2</sup> (0.15 sq mi) per person)*

A multitude of languages are used by Canadians, with [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_English) and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_French) (the [official languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language)) being the [mother tongues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language) of approximately 56% and 21% of Canadians, respectively.[^cite_note-Highlightsb-142] As of the 2016 Census, just over 7.3 million Canadians listed a non-official language as their mother tongue. Some of the most common non-official first languages include Chinese (1,227,680 first-language speakers), [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language) (501,680), Spanish (458,850), [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language) (431,385), Arabic (419,895), German (384,040), and [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language_in_Canada) (375,645).[^cite_note-Highlightsb-142] Less than one percent of Canadians (just over 250,000 individuals) can speak an [Indigenous language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Canada#Indigenous_languages). About half this number (129,865) reported using an Indigenous language on a daily basis.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGordon2005-143] Additionally, Canadians speak several [sign languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_language); the number of speakers is unknown of the most spoken ones, [American Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Sign_Language) (ASL) and [Quebec Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Sign_Language) (LSQ),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKockaertSteurs2015490-144] as it is of [Maritime Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Sign_Language) and [Plains Sign Talk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plains_Indian_Sign_Language).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrimesGrimes2000-145] There are only 47 speakers of the [Inuit Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit_Sign_Language).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchuitBakerPfau2011-146]

English and French are recognized by the [Constitution of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Canada) as official languages.[^cite_note-Languages-147] All federal government laws are thus enacted in both English and French, with government services available in both languages.[^cite_note-Languages-147] Two of Canada's territories give official status to Indigenous languages. In [Nunavut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunavut), [Inuktitut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuktitut), and [Inuinnaqtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuinnaqtun_language) are official languages, alongside the national languages of English and French, and Inuktitut is a common vehicular language in territorial government.[^cite_note-148] In the [Northwest Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territories), the *[Official Languages Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Languages_Act_\(Northwest_Territories\))* declares that there are eleven different languages: [Chipewyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dene_Suline_language), [Cree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cree_language), English, French, [Gwich'in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwich'in_language), Inuinnaqtun, Inuktitut, [Inuvialuktun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuvialuktun), [North Slavey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavey_language), [South Slavey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavey_language), and [Tłįchǫ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogrib_language).[^cite_note-149] [Multicultural media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicultural_media_in_Canada) are widely accessible across the country and offer specialty television channels, newspapers, and other publications in many minority languages.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaGanahl200662-150]

&#x20;In Canada, as elsewhere in the world of [European colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_colonialism), the frontier of European exploration and settlement tended to be a linguistically diverse and fluid place, as cultures using different languages met and interacted. The need for a common means of communication between the Indigenous inhabitants and new arrivals for the purposes of trade, and (in some cases) intermarriage, led to the development of [mixed languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_language).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWinford2003183-151] Languages like [Michif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michif_language), [Chinook Jargon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinook_Jargon), and [Bungi creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungi_creole) tended to be highly localized and were often spoken by only a small number of individuals who were frequently capable of speaking another language.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWurmMuhlhauslerTyron19961491-152] [Plains Sign Talk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plains_Sign_Talk)—which functioned originally as a trade language used to communicate internationally and across linguistic borders—reached across Canada, the United States, and into Mexico.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPfauSteinbachWoll2012540-153]
