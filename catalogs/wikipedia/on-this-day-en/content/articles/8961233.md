**Woolwich Crown Court**, or more accurately the **Crown Court at Woolwich**, is a [Crown Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Court) venue which deals with criminal cases on Belmarsh Way, [Thamesmead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thamesmead), [London](17867.md), England.

## History

In the early 1990s, the [Lord Chancellor's Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Chancellor's_Department) decided to commission a courthouse adjacent to [HM Prison Belmarsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HM_Prison_Belmarsh) so offenders did not have to be transported to court by vehicle.[^cite_note-1]

The building was designed by the [Property Services Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_Services_Agency) in the [Modernist style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_architecture), built by Hamills Construction in brick and [concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete) and was completed in 1993.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] The design involved a broadly symmetrical main frontage with an entrance block flanked by two wings, laid out a slightly different angles, facing onto Belmarsh Way. The entrance block featured a [portico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portico) formed by columns supporting a glass [canopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canopy_\(building\)) and was surmounted by a drum-like structure. The entrance block and the wings were faced in light grey cladding. Internally, the building was laid out with six courtrooms.[^cite_note-4]

Woolwich Crown Court was intended to serve as a high-security courtroom and became the preferred venue for terrorism trials. A tunnel was established linking the court to the maximum-security HM Prison Belmarsh.[^cite_note-5] This provided a secure route for bringing defendants in high-profile terrorist cases before the court. Also, armed police were deployed as necessary to provide security.[^cite_note-6] In June 1998, it was the venue for the trial and conviction of defendants charged with conspiracy to cause the [1996 Docklands bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Docklands_bombing).[^cite_note-7]

The court was subsequently the venue for [the trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21_July_2005_London_bombings_trial) and conviction, in June 2007, of the six men accused of attempting the [21 July 2005 London bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21_July_2005_London_bombings) on the London transport network,[^cite_note-8] the trial and conviction, in December 2008, of [Bilal Abdullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilal_Abdullah) in connection with the [2007 Glasgow Airport attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Glasgow_Airport_attack),[^cite_note-9] and the trial and conviction, in July 2010, of those charged with offences in connection with the [2006 transatlantic aircraft plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_transatlantic_aircraft_plot).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

The court was also the place for the trial and conviction, in January 2018, of those charged with offences in connection with the 2015 [Hatton Garden safe deposit burglary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatton_Garden_safe_deposit_burglary).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]
