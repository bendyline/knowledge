**Nadiem Anwar Makarim** (born 4 July 1984) is an Indonesian politician and businessman who was the [minister of education, culture, research, and technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education,_Culture,_Research_and_Technology_\(Indonesia\)) of Indonesia, serving from 2021 until 2024.[^cite_note-1]

Prior to entering politics, in 2010, Makarim founded [Gojek](51272668.md), Indonesia's first startup valued at over US\$10 billion.[^cite_note-2] In October 2019, he was appointed Minister of Education and Culture by [President](10011631.md) [Joko Widodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joko_Widodo) on [his second term's cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onward_Indonesia_Cabinet) and subsequently resigned from his post at Gojek.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] In 2025, Makarim was arrested in connection with the corruption case [Chromebook procurement scandal](80074870.md).[^cite_note-5] In 2026, he was convicted of corruption and sentenced to 10 years in prison; he denied wrongdoing and said he would appeal.[^cite_note-6]

## Early life and education

Nadiem Makarim was born in Singapore on 4 July 1984, to Indonesian parents, Nono Anwar Makarim and [Atika Algadri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atika_Algadri?action=edit\&redlink=1). His father is an activist, lawyer and is of mixed [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_people)-[Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Indonesians)-[Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Indonesians) descent. His maternal grandfather is [Hamid Algadri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamid_Algadri), an Indonesian independence fighter. He has two sisters, Hana Makarim, and filmmaker [Rayya Makarim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayya_Makarim?action=edit\&redlink=1). He married Franka Franklin and they have three children.[^cite_note-7]

Makarim attended high school in [Jakarta](16275.md), and then graduated from the [United World College of Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_World_College_of_Southeast_Asia) (UWC SEA) in Singapore. He attended [Brown University](4157.md) for an undergraduate degree in [International Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Relations). He completed his [MBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration) at [Harvard Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Business_School).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

## Business career



Nadiem Makarim at the [World Economic Forum](172185.md).

### Early career

After graduating from Brown University in 2006, Makarim decided to come back home to Indonesia and worked at [McKinsey & Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKinsey_&_Company). Makarim worked as a McKinsey consultant for 3 years.[^cite_note-10]

Makarim started his career at [McKinsey & Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKinsey_&_Company) as [management consultant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_consultant) in Jakarta. He left to co-found Zalora, an online fashion shop, then left [Zalora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zalora_Group) to become Chief Innovation Officer at Kartuku, a payment service provider.

### Zalora

Makarim became co-founder and managing director for [Zalora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zalora_Group) Indonesia in 2011. In 2012, Makarim made the decision to leave Zalora to focus on building his own startup, including Gojek, which at that time had 15 employees and 450 drivers. He claims to have learned enough from Zalora, which was his main goal in accepting the position in the first place. In Zalora, Makarim had the chance to build a mega startup and work with some of the best talents across the region.[^cite_note-techinasia-11]

### Kartuku

After leaving Zalora and while developing Gojek, Makarim also worked as a chief innovation officer of Kartuku.[^cite_note-12] In the early days, Kartuku didn't have any competition in cashless payment solutions in Indonesia.[^cite_note-techinasia-11] Kartuku was then acquired by Gojek to strengthen GoPay.[^cite_note-teknologi-13]

### Gojek

See also: [Gojek](51272668.md)

In 2010, Makarim created [Gojek](51272668.md), which is today a decacorn company with a valuation of over US\$10 billion.[^cite_note-14] Gojek was first established as a call centre, offering only courier delivery and two-wheeled ride-hailing services. Today, Gojek has transformed into a super app, providing more than 20 services, ranging from transportation, food delivery, groceries, massage, house cleaning, logistics to a cashless digital payment platform called GoPay.[^cite_note-teknologi-13]

Makarim often uses a motorcycle taxi, known in Indonesia as an [ojek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojek). He saw this as a business opportunity and developed it into Gojek, which was founded in 2010.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

[Gojek](51272668.md) was well received, and eventually received US\$1,3 billion funding from investors, in a 2018-round led by Alphabet Inc's [Google](1092923.md), [JD.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JD.com) Inc, and [Tencent Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencent_Holdings). It thereby became the first Indonesian [unicorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicorn_\(finance\)).[^cite_note-17] By 2019, the firm was worth up to US\$10 billion.[^cite_note-18]

On 21 October 2019, Gojek announced that Makarim would leave the company to join president-elect Jokowi's [Onward Indonesia Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onward_Indonesia_Cabinet). His position as CEO was replaced by Gojek group president Andre Soelistyo and Gojek co-founder [Kevin Aluwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Aluwi) as co-CEOs.[^cite_note-19] On the second reshuffle of the cabinet, announced at 28 April 2021, he was appointed the first holder of Minister of Education, Culture, Research, and Technology.[^cite_note-20]

### World Economic Forum

He is part of the WEF as a global shaper and has direct control over cultural matters.[^cite_note-21]

## Political career

### Cabinet minister

Under Makarim's tenure as Minister of Education and Culture, a decree prohibiting Indonesian schools from enforcing rules mandating the wearing of religious attire was signed into effect on 3 February 2021. Schools were given 30 days to repeal any rules making the wearing of religious attire compulsory before they would face sanctions. Makarim supported the ban, stating that whether a person should wear religious clothing is "an individual's right… it is not the school's decision."[^cite_note-BBC_News_2021-22]

“I joined this government at the request of President Jokowi, leaving behind a career I had spent years building,” he said. “I did it because I believed I could make a difference for Indonesian children and for the future of education in this country.”[^cite_note-23]

## Corruption case

Main article: [Chromebook procurement scandal](80074870.md)

On 4 September 2025, Makarim was arrested[^cite_note-24] after being named by the [Attorney General's Office of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General's_Office_of_Indonesia) as a new suspect in the alleged corruption case involving the [government procurement of Chromebook laptops](80074870.md).[^cite_note-DascoCNN-25]

In May 2025, the Attorney General's Office of the Republic of Indonesia opened an investigation into alleged corruption related to the procurement program for educational digitalization devices, including Chromebook laptops, at the Ministry of Education, Culture, Research, and Technology for the 2019–2023 period with a budget of nearly IDR 10 trillion. [^cite_note-26] According to him, the policy of using Chrome OS-based devices in this project came from Makarim's orders. [^cite_note-27]

Makarim himself was summoned as a witness several times. He underwent questioning on June 23, 2025, July 15, 2025, and September 4, 2025, at the Deputy Attorney General's Office for Special Crimes in South Jakarta. He explained that the procurement was in accordance with the principle of transparency, and added that the program targets schools with internet access, differing from the previous administration's administration, which targeted 3T (Disadvantaged, Frontier, and Outermost) regions.[^cite_note-28]

On September 4, 2025, the Attorney General's Office officially named Makarim a suspect with the initials NAM.[^cite_note-29] Head of the Attorney General's Office's Legal Information Center, Anang Supriatna, stated that the determination of the suspect was made based on an in-depth examination of witness statements and evidence obtained by investigators.[^cite_note-30]

On September 23, 2025, through his legal team, Makarim filed a pretrial motion challenging his suspect status. According to him, Makarim's suspect status was not supported by two valid pieces of evidence, as one of the alleged state losses in question was based on an audit by the authorized agency, namely the Financial and Development Supervisory Agency or the Supreme Audit Agency of the Republic of Indonesia. Based on this, Makarim's legal team also stated that Makarim's detention status was also invalid.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

On 30 June 2026, Makarim was convicted of corruption by an Indonesian court in connection with a school laptop procurement programme introduced during the COVID-19 pandemic while he was serving as minister of education. The court sentenced him to 10 years in prison, fined him 1 billion rupiah, and ordered him to pay 809.6 billion rupiah in restitution. The court also ruled that he would face an additional five-year prison term if he failed to pay the restitution. Makarim denied wrongdoing during the case, which was widely covered in Indonesia and abroad.[^cite_note-33]

## International organizations

With [Melinda Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melinda_Gates) and the Minister of Finance of Indonesia, [Sri Mulyani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Mulyani), Makarim served as one of the commissioners of [Pathways for Prosperity](https://www.pathwaysforprosperity.org/) for Technology and Inclusive Development that focuses on helping developing countries to adapt to various new digital innovations that change the working culture.[^cite_note-41]
