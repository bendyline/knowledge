**The Bronx** ([/brɒŋks/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*BRONKS*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is the northernmost of the five [boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroughs_of_New_York_City) of [New York City](645042.md). The Bronx has a land area of 42 square miles (109 km<sup>2</sup>) and a population of 1,472,654 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census).[^cite_note-2020CensusMap-1] It has the fourth largest area, fourth largest population, and third highest [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) of the boroughs.[^cite_note-density-7] The Bronx is the only borough located primarily on the U.S. mainland, bordering [Westchester County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westchester_County,_New_York) to its north; the [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River) and the borough of [Manhattan](45470.md), across the [Harlem River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem_River), to its west; the borough of [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens), across the [East River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_River), to its south; and [Long Island Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_Sound) to its east. The borough is coextensive with **Bronx County**, one of the 62 [counties of New York State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_in_New_York).

The Bronx is divided by the [Bronx River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_River) into a hillier section in the [west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bronx) and a flatter [eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bronx) section. East and west street names are divided by [Jerome Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Avenue). The west was [annexed](18950961.md) to New York City in 1874, and the areas east of the Bronx River in 1895.[^cite_note-ultan-8] Bronx County was separated from New York County (which had included the boroughs of Manhattan and the Bronx) in 1914.[^cite_note-courtstart-9] About a quarter of the Bronx's area is open space,[^cite_note-blooming-10] including [Woodlawn Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Cemetery_\(Bronx,_New_York\)), [Van Cortlandt Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Cortlandt_Park), [Pelham Bay Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park), the [New York Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Botanical_Garden), and the [Bronx Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Zoo) in the borough's north and center. The [Thain Family Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thain_Family_Forest) at the New York Botanical Garden is thousands of years old and is New York City's largest remaining tract of the original forest that once covered the city.[^cite_note-11] These open spaces are primarily on land reserved in the late 19th century as urban development progressed north and east from Manhattan. The Bronx is also home to [Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium) of [Major League Baseball](38776.md).

The word Bronx originated with [Jonas Bronck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Bronck), who established the first European settlement in the area as part of the [New Netherland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland) colony in 1639.[^cite_note-Faroeislands-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-Van_Rensselaer_1909_161-14] European settlers displaced the native [Lenape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenape) after 1643. In the 19th and 20th centuries, the Bronx received many immigrant and migrant groups as it was transformed into an urban community, first from European countries (especially [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Americans), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Americans), and [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe)) and later from the [Caribbean](18956035.md) region (particularly [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Ricans_in_the_United_States), [Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidadian_Americans), [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Americans), [Guyana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyanese_Americans), [Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaican_Americans), [Barbados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbadian_Americans), and the [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Americans)) and [West Africa](67393.md) (mainly [Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_Americans) and [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Americans)). [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American) migrants from the [Southern United States](179553.md) as well as [Panamanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panamanian_Americans), [Hondurans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduran_Americans), and [South Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_ethnic_groups) also established themselves in the borough in large numbers.[^cite_note-15]

The Bronx contains what had been the poorest [of all 435 U.S. congressional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_districts_of_the_United_States), [New York's 15th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York's_15th_congressional_district), until redistricting following the 2020 census.[^cite_note-16] The borough also features upper- and middle-income neighborhoods, such as [Riverdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale,_Bronx), [Fieldston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fieldston,_Bronx), [Spuyten Duyvil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spuyten_Duyvil,_Bronx), [Schuylerville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schuylerville,_Bronx), [Pelham Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay,_Bronx), [Pelham Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Gardens,_Bronx), [Morris Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Park,_Bronx), and [Country Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Club,_Bronx).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-Barone-18][^cite_note-Stat_Abst-19] Parts of the Bronx saw a steep decline in population, livable housing, and quality of life starting from the mid-to-late 1960s and lasting well into the 1980s, including a [wave of arson in the late 1970s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970s_South_Bronx_building_fires).[^cite_note-20] The [South Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bronx), in particular, experienced severe [urban decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_decay). The borough began experiencing new population growth starting in the late 1990s and continuing to the present day.[^cite_note-21]

## Etymology and naming

### Early names



A map of southern Westchester County in 1867. This, along with the southern part of the former Town of [Yonkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonkers,_New_York), became the Bronx.

The Bronx was called *_Rananchqua_*[^cite_note-NYPL-22] by the native [Siwanoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siwanoy)[^cite_note-23] band of [Lenape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenape), also known historically as *the Delawares*. Other [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) knew the Bronx as *Keskeskeck*.[^cite_note-ellis-p55-24] It was divided by the Aquahung River (now known in English as the [Bronx River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_River)).

The "Bronx" name derives from [Jonas Bronck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Bronck) (c. 1600–1643), a European settler whose precise origins are disputed. Documents indicate he was a Swedish-born immigrant from [Komstad, Norra Ljunga parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komstad) in [Småland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sm%C3%A5land), Sweden, who arrived in [New Netherland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland) during the spring of 1639.[^cite_note-Van_Rensselaer_1909_161-14][^cite_note-Hansen_1950-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-Burrows-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] Bronck became the first recorded European settler in the present-day Bronx and built a farm named "Emmaus" close to what today is the corner of Willis Avenue and 132nd Street in [Mott Haven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mott_Haven,_Bronx).[^cite_note-30]

He leased land from the [Dutch West India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_West_India_Company) on the neck of the mainland immediately north of the Dutch settlement of [New Haarlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Harlem#1637%E2%80%931866) (on [Manhattan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Island)), and bought additional tracts from the local tribes. He eventually accumulated 500 acres (200 ha) between the [Harlem River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem_River) and the Aquahung, which became known as *Bronck's River* or *the [Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_River) River*. Dutch and English settlers referred to the area as *Bronck's Land*.[^cite_note-Hansen_1950-25]

The American poet [William Bronk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Bronk) was a descendant of Pieter Bronck, probably Jonas Bronck's nephew or cousin, as there was an age difference of 16 years.[^cite_note-31] Much work on the Swedish claim has been undertaken by Brian G. Andersson, former Commissioner of New York City's Department of Records, who helped organize a 375th Anniversary celebration in Bronck's hometown in 2014.[^cite_note-32]

### Use of definite article

The Bronx is referred to with the [definite article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_\(grammar\)) as "the Bronx" or "The Bronx", both legally and colloquially.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] The "County of the Bronx" also takes "the" immediately before "Bronx" in formal references, like the coextensive "Borough of the Bronx". The [United States Postal Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Postal_Service) uses "Bronx, NY" for mailing addresses.[^cite_note-35] The definite article comes from the Bronx River and first appeared in the "Annexed District of The Bronx", created in 1874 out of part of [Westchester County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westchester_County,_New_York).[^cite_note-ny1.com-36][^cite_note-Steven_Hess_1987-37]

It was continued in the "Borough of The Bronx", created in 1898, which included a larger annexation from Westchester County in 1895.[^cite_note-38] The use of the definite article is attributed to the style of referring to rivers.[^cite_note-ny1.com-36][^cite_note-Steven_Hess_1987-37] A time-worn story purportedly explaining the use of the definite article in the borough's name says it stems from the phrase "visiting the Broncks", referring to the settler's family.[^cite_note-39]

The capitalization of the borough's name is sometimes disputed. Generally, the definite article is lowercase in place names ("the Bronx") except in some official references. The definite article is capitalized ("The Bronx") at the beginning of a sentence or in any other situation when a normally lowercase word would be capitalized.[^cite_note-40] Some people and groups refer to the borough with a capital letter at all times, such as Bronx Borough Historian [Lloyd Ultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Ultan_\(historian\)),[^cite_note-41] [The Bronx County Historical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx_County_Historical_Society), and the Bronx-based organization Great and Glorious Grand Army of The Bronx, arguing the definite article is part of the proper name.[^cite_note-WhyBronx-42][^cite_note-\:0-43] In particular, the Great and Glorious Grand Army of The Bronx is leading efforts to make the city refer to the borough with an uppercase definite article in all uses, comparing the lowercase article in the Bronx's name to "not capitalizing the 's' in 'Staten Island'".[^cite_note-\:0-43]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of the Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Bronx).



The first published book of Bronx history: *History of Bronx Borough, City of New York* by Randall Comfort

Originally, the area was part of the [Lenape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenape)'s [Lenapehoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenapehoking) territory inhabited by [Siwanoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siwanoy) of the [Wappinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wappinger) Confederacy.[^cite_note-44] European colonization of the Bronx began in 1639.[^cite_note-45] Over time, European colonists converted the area into farmlands. The Bronx was originally part of [Westchester County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westchester_County,_New_York), but it was ceded to [New York City](645042.md) in two major parts (the [West Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bronx) in 1874 and the [East Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bronx) in 1895), subsequently becoming Bronx County.

### Before 1914

See also: [List of former municipalities in New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_former_municipalities_in_New_York_City)

The Bronx's development is directly connected to its strategic location between [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) and New York ([Manhattan](45470.md)). Control over the bridges across the Harlem River plagued the period of British colonial rule. The King's Bridge, built in 1693 where [Broadway](4626.md) reached the [Spuyten Duyvil Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spuyten_Duyvil_Creek), was a possession of [Frederick Philipse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Philipse), lord of [Philipse Manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipse_Manor_Hall_State_Historic_Site).[^cite_note-CrotonHarlemFSEIS-7-12-46] Local farmers on both sides of the creek resented the tolls, and in 1759, Jacobus Dyckman and Benjamin Palmer led them in building a free bridge across the Harlem River.[^cite_note-Fordam-47] After the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), the King's Bridge toll was abolished.[^cite_note-CrotonHarlemFSEIS-7-12-46][^cite_note-Jenkins1912-48]

The territory now contained within Bronx County was originally part of [Westchester County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westchester_County,_New_York), one of the 12 original counties of the English [Province of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_New_York).[^cite_note-49] The present Bronx County was contained in the town of Westchester and parts of the towns of [Yonkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonkers,_New_York), [Eastchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastchester,_New_York), and [Pelham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham,_New_York). In 1846, a new town was created by division of Westchester, called West Farms. The town of [Morrisania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrisania,_Bronx) was created, in turn, from West Farms in 1855. In 1873, the town of [Kingsbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsbridge,_Bronx) was established within the former borders of the town of Yonkers, roughly corresponding to the modern Bronx neighborhoods of Kingsbridge, [Riverdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale,_Bronx), and [Woodlawn Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Heights,_Bronx), and included [Woodlawn Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Cemetery_\(Bronx,_New_York\)).

Among the famous people who settled in the Bronx during the 19th and early 20th centuries were author [Willa Cather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willa_Cather), tobacco merchant [Pierre Lorillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Lorillard_IV), and inventor [Jordan L. Mott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_L._Mott), who established [Mott Haven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mott_Haven) to house the workers at his iron works.[^cite_note-50]

The consolidation of the Bronx into New York City proceeded in two stages. In 1873, the state legislature annexed Kingsbridge, West Farms, and Morrisania to New York, effective in 1874; the three towns were soon abolished in the process.[^cite_note-thorne-51][^cite_note-52]

In 1895, the whole territory east of the Bronx River was annexed to the city three years before New York's consolidation with [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens), and [Staten Island](127062.md). This included the Town of Westchester, which had voted against consolidation in 1894, and parts of Eastchester and Pelham.[^cite_note-ultan-8][^cite_note-thorne-51][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-NYT1973-55] The maritime community of [City Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Island,_Bronx) voted to join the city in 1896.[^cite_note-56]

Following these two annexations, the Bronx's territory had moved from Westchester County into New York County, which already included Manhattan and the rest of pre-1874 New York City.[^cite_note-57]

On January 1, 1898, the consolidated [City of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Greater_New_York) was born, including the Bronx as one of the five distinct [boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_\(New_York_City\)). It remained part of New York County until Bronx County was created in 1914.[^cite_note-58]

On April 19, 1912, those parts of New York County which had been annexed from Westchester County in previous decades were newly constituted as Bronx County, the 62nd and last county to be created by the state, effective in 1914.[^cite_note-thorne-51][^cite_note-59] Bronx County's courts opened for business on January 2, 1914, the same day that [John P. Mitchel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_P._Mitchel) started work as [Mayor of New York City](206327.md).[^cite_note-courtstart-9] [Marble Hill, Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble_Hill,_Manhattan), was now connected to the Bronx by filling in the former waterway, but it is not part of the borough or Bronx county.[^cite_note-NYT1993-60]

### After 1914

The history of the Bronx during the 20th century may be divided into four periods: a boom period during 1900–1929, with a population growth by a factor of six from 200,000 in 1900 to 1.3 million in 1930. The [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) and post-World War II years saw a slowing of growth leading into an eventual decline. The mid to late century were hard times, as the Bronx changed during 1950–1985 from a predominantly moderate-income to a predominantly lower-income area with high rates of violent crime and poverty in some areas. The Bronx has experienced an economic and developmental resurgence starting in the late 1980s that continues into today.[^cite_note-Olmsted_1989;_Olmsted_1998-61]

#### New York City expands



The [Grand Concourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Concourse_\(Bronx\)) and [161st Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/161st_Street_\(Bronx\)) as they appeared around 1900



The [Simpson Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simpson_Street_\(IRT_White_Plains_Road_Line\)) elevated station was built in 1904. It was listed in the National Register of Historic Places in 2004.

The Bronx was a mostly rural area for many generations, with small farms supplying the city markets. In the late 19th century, it grew into a railroad suburb. Faster transportation enabled rapid population growth in the late 19th century, involving the move from horse-drawn street cars to elevated railways and the subway system, which linked to Manhattan in 1904.[^cite_note-Olmsted_1989;_Olmsted_1998-61]

The South Bronx was a manufacturing center for many years, and was noted as a center of [piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano) manufacturing in the early part of the 20th century. In 1919, the Bronx was the site of 63 piano factories employing more than 5,000 workers.[^cite_note-Piano_Workers_May_Strike-62]

Before the end of [World War I](4764461.md), the Bronx hosted the rather small [1918 World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_International_Exposition_of_Science,_Arts_and_Industries) at 177th Street and DeVoe Avenue.[^cite_note-ultan-8][^cite_note-63]

The Bronx underwent rapid urban growth after World War I. Extensions of the [New York City Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Subway) contributed to the increase in population as thousands of immigrants came to the Bronx, resulting in a major boom in residential construction.[^cite_note-64] Among these groups, many [Irish Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Americans), [Italian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Americans), and especially [Jewish Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Americans) settled here. [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_American), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans), [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_American), and other immigrants moved into the borough. As evidence of the change in population, by 1937, 592,185 Jews lived in the Bronx (43.9% of the borough's population),[^cite_note-65] while only 54,000 Jews lived in the borough in 2011. Many [synagogues](48559.md) still stand in the Bronx, but most have been converted to other uses.[^cite_note-Remembrance-66]

#### Change

[Bootleggers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rum-running) and gangs were active in the Bronx during [Prohibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_in_the_United_States), between 1920 and 1933. Irish, Italian, Jewish, and Polish gangs smuggled in most of the illegal whiskey, and the oldest sections of the borough became poverty-stricken.[^cite_note-67] Police Commissioner Richard Enright said that [speakeasies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speakeasy) provided a place for "the vicious elements, bootleggers, gamblers and their friends in all walks of life" to cooperate and to "evade the law, escape punishment for their crimes, \[and] to deter the police from doing their duty".[^cite_note-68]

In 1945, the [Bronx Marian apparitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Marian_apparitions) drew 30,000 pilgrims to the area to see the site of an alleged vision of the [Virgin Mary](73513.md).[^cite_note-69]

Between 1930 and 1960, moderate and upper income Bronxites, predominantly non-Hispanic Whites, [began to relocate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_flight) from the borough's southwestern neighborhoods. This migration has left a mostly poor African American and Hispanic, largely [Puerto Rican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Ricans_in_the_United_States), population in the West Bronx. A significant factor that shifted the racial and economic demographics was the construction of [Co-op City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-op_City,_Bronx), built to house middle-class residents in family-sized apartments. The high-rise complex played a significant role in draining middle-class residents from older tenement buildings in the borough's southern and western fringes. Most predominantly non-Hispanic White communities today [are in the eastern and northwestern sections of the borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_Bronx).[^cite_note-70]



President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter) and New York Mayor [Abraham Beame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Beame) tour the [South Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bronx) in 1977

From the mid-1960s to the early 1980s, the [quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life) changed for some Bronx residents. Historians and social scientists have suggested many factors, including the theory that [Robert Moses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Moses)' [Cross Bronx Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_Bronx_Expressway) destroyed existing residential neighborhoods and created instant slums, as put forward in [Robert Caro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Caro)'s biography *[The Power Broker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Power_Broker)*.[^cite_note-71] Another factor in the Bronx's decline may have been the development of [high-rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-rise) [housing projects](177912.md#United_States), particularly in the [South Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bronx).[^cite_note-72] Yet another factor may have been a reduction in the real estate listings and property-related financial services offered in some areas of the Bronx, such as [mortgage loans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortgage_loan) or insurance policies—a process known as [redlining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redlining). Others have suggested a "[planned shrinkage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_shrinkage)" of municipal services, such as fire-fighting.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] There was also much debate as to whether [rent control](41258426.md) laws had made it less profitable (or more costly) for landlords to maintain existing buildings with their existing tenants than to abandon or destroy those buildings.

In the 1970s, parts of the Bronx were plagued by a wave of arson. The burning of buildings was predominantly in the poorest communities, such as the South Bronx. One explanation of this event was that landlords decided to burn their low property-value buildings and take the insurance money, as it was easier for them to get insurance money than to try to refurbish a dilapidated building or sell a building in a severely distressed area.[^cite_note-76] The Bronx became identified with a high rate of poverty and unemployment, which was mainly a persistent problem in the South Bronx.[^cite_note-Gonzalez_2004-77] There were cases where tenants set fire to the building they lived in so they could qualify for emergency relocations by city social service agencies to better residences, sometimes being relocated to other parts of the city.

Out of 289 census tracts in the Bronx borough, 7 tracts lost more than 97% of their buildings to arson and abandonment between 1970 and 1980; another 44 tracts had more than 50% of their buildings meet the same fate. By the early 1980s, the Bronx was considered the most blighted urban area in the country, particularly the South Bronx which experienced a loss of 60% of the population and 40% of housing units. However, starting in the 1990s, many of the burned-out and run-down tenements were replaced by new housing units.[^cite_note-Gonzalez_2004-77]

In May 1984, [New York Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Supreme_Court) justice [Peter J. McQuillan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_J._McQuillan) ruled that [Marble Hill, Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble_Hill,_Manhattan), was simultaneously part of the Borough of Manhattan (not the Borough of the Bronx) and part of Bronx County (not New York County)[^cite_note-78] and the matter was definitively settled later that year when the [New York Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Legislature) overwhelmingly passed legislation declaring the neighborhood part of both New York County and the Borough of Manhattan and made this clarification retroactive to 1938, as reflected on the official maps of the city.[^cite_note-Bloom_1995-79][^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

### Revitalization

four-story houses along a city street

[Row houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Row_houses) on a location where there was once burnt rubble. The Bronx has since seen revitalization.

Since the late 1980s, significant development has occurred in the Bronx, first stimulated by the city's "Ten-Year Housing Plan"[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] and community members working to rebuild the social, economic and environmental infrastructure by creating [affordable housing](3531662.md). Groups affiliated with churches in the South Bronx erected the Nehemiah Homes with about 1,000 units. The grass roots organization Nos Quedamos' endeavor known as Melrose Commons[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] began to rebuild areas in the South Bronx.[^cite_note-87] The [IRT White Plains Road Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRT_White_Plains_Road_Line) ([2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)) and ​[5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)) trains) began to show an increase in riders. Chains such as [Marshalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshalls), [Staples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staples_Inc.), and [Target](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_Corporation) opened stores in the Bronx. More bank branches opened in the Bronx as a whole (rising from 106 in 1997 to 149 in 2007), although not primarily in poor or minority neighborhoods, while the Bronx still has fewer branches per person than other boroughs.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

The Bronx – All-America City sign

The Bronx – All-America City sign

In 1997, the Bronx was designated an *[All America City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_America_City)* by the [National Civic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Civic_League), acknowledging its comeback from the decline of the mid-century.[^cite_note-92] In 2006, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported that "construction cranes have become the borough's new visual metaphor, replacing the window decals of the 1980s in which pictures of potted plants and drawn curtains were placed in the windows of abandoned buildings."[^cite_note-thonx-93] The borough has experienced substantial new building construction since 2002. Between 2002 and June 2007, 33,687 new units of housing were built or were under way and \$4.8 billion has been invested in new housing. In the first six months of 2007 alone total investment in new residential development was \$965 million and 5,187 residential units were scheduled to be completed. Much of the new development is springing up in formerly vacant lots across the South Bronx.[^cite_note-94]

In addition there came a revitalization of the existing housing market in areas such as Hunts Point, the Lower Concourse, and the neighborhoods surrounding the Third Avenue Bridge as people buy apartments and renovate them.[^cite_note-95] Several boutique and chain hotels opened in the 2010s in the [South Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bronx).[^cite_note-96]

New developments are underway. The [Bronx General Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_General_Post_Office)[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] on the corner of the Grand Concourse and East 149th Street is being converted into a market place, boutiques, restaurants and office space with a USPS concession.[^cite_note-99] The [Kingsbridge Armory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsbridge_Armory), often cited as the largest armory in the world, is currently slated for redevelopment. Under consideration for future development is the construction of a platform over the [New York City Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Subway)'s [Concourse Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concourse_Yard) adjacent to [Lehman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehman_College). The construction permitted approximately 2,000,000 square feet (190,000 m<sup>2</sup>) of development and cost US\$350–500 million.[^cite_note-100]

Despite significant investment compared to the post war period, many exacerbated social problems remain including high rates of violent crime, substance abuse, overcrowding, and substandard housing conditions.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] The Bronx has the highest rate of poverty in New York City, and the greater South Bronx is the poorest area.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]

### Location and physical features

See also: [List of smaller islands in New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_smaller_islands_in_New_York_City)

According to the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Census_Bureau), Bronx County has a total area of 57 square miles (150 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 42 square miles (110 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 15 square miles (39 km<sup>2</sup>) (27%) is water.[^cite_note-GR1-107]

The Bronx is New York City's northernmost borough, New York State's southernmost mainland county and the only part of New York City that is almost entirely on the North American mainland, unlike the other four boroughs that are either islands or located on islands.[^cite_note-GR6-108] The [bedrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedrock) of the [West Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bronx) is primarily *Fordham [gneiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gneiss)*, a high-grade heavily banded [metamorphic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamorphic_rock) containing significant amounts of [pink feldspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feldspar).[^cite_note-109] Marble Hill – politically part of Manhattan but now physically attached to the Bronx – is so-called because of the formation of [Inwood marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inwood_marble) there as well as in [Inwood, Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inwood,_Manhattan), and parts of the Bronx and Westchester County.

The [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River) separates the Bronx on the west from [Alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine,_New_Jersey), [Tenafly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenafly,_New_Jersey) and [Englewood Cliffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Englewood_Cliffs,_New_Jersey) in [Bergen County, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_County,_New_Jersey). The [Harlem River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem_River) separates it from the island of [Manhattan](45470.md) to the southwest. The [East River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_River) separates it from [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens) to the southeast. To the east, [Long Island Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_Sound) separates it from [Nassau County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassau_County,_New_York) in western Long Island. Directly north of the Bronx are (from west to east) the adjoining [Westchester County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westchester_County,_New_York) communities of [Yonkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonkers,_New_York), [Mount Vernon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vernon,_New_York), [Pelham Manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Manor,_New_York) and [New Rochelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Rochelle,_New_York). There is also a short southern land boundary with [Marble Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble_Hill,_Manhattan) in the Borough of Manhattan, over the filled-in former course of the [Spuyten Duyvil Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spuyten_Duyvil_Creek); Marble Hill's postal [ZIP code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZIP_code), telephonic [area codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_codes) and fire service, however, are shared with the Bronx and not Manhattan.[^cite_note-NYT1993-60]



An aerial view of the Bronx from the east at night

The [Bronx River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_River) flows south from Westchester County through the borough, emptying into the East River; it is the only entirely [freshwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater) river in New York City.[^cite_note-nytimes-110] It separates the West Bronx from the schist of the [East Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bronx). A smaller river, the [Hutchinson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutchinson_River) (named after the religious leader [Anne Hutchinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hutchinson), killed along its banks in 1641), passes through the East Bronx and empties into [Eastchester Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastchester_Bay).

The Bronx includes several small islands in the [East River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_River) and [Long Island Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_Sound), such as [City Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Island,_New_York) and [Hart Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hart_Island_\(New_York\)). [Rikers Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rikers_Island) in the East River, home to the large jail complex for the entire city, is also part of the Bronx.[^cite_note-111]

The Bronx's highest elevation at 280 feet (85 m) is in the northwest corner, west of [Van Cortlandt Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Cortlandt_Park) and in the Chapel Farm area near the [Riverdale Country School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale_Country_School).[^cite_note-112] The opposite (southeastern) side of the Bronx has four large low peninsulas or "necks" of low-lying land that jut into the waters of the East River and were once [salt marsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_marsh): Hunt's Point, Clason's Point, Screvin's Neck and [Throggs Neck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throggs_Neck). Further up the coastline, [Rodman's Neck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodman's_Neck) lies between [Pelham Bay Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park) in the northeast and [City Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Island,_New_York). The Bronx's irregular shoreline extends for 75 square miles (194 km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-113]

Although Bronx County was the third most densely populated county in the United States in 2022 (after [Manhattan](45470.md) and [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn)),[^cite_note-CensusDensity2022-117] 7,000 acres (28 km<sup>2</sup>) of the Bronx—about one fifth of the Bronx's area, and one quarter of its land area—is given over to parkland.[^cite_note-blooming-10][^cite_note-118] The vision of a system of major Bronx parks connected by park-like thoroughfares is usually attributed to [John Mullaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mullaly).

[Woodlawn Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Cemetery_\(Bronx,_New_York\)), located on 400 acres (160 ha) and one of the largest cemeteries in New York City, sits on the western bank of the [Bronx River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_River) near [Yonkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonkers,_New_York). It opened in 1863, in what was then the town of Yonkers, at the time a rural area. Since the first burial in 1865, more than 300,000 people have been interred there.[^cite_note-119]

The borough's northern side includes the largest park in New York City—[Pelham Bay Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park), which includes [Orchard Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchard_Beach,_New_York)—and the third-largest, [Van Cortlandt Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Cortlandt_Park), which is west of Woodlawn Cemetery and borders [Yonkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonkers,_New_York).[^cite_note-Van_Cortlandt_Park_:_NYC_Parks-120] Also in the northern Bronx, [Wave Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_Hill_\(New_York\)), the former estate of [George W. Perkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Walbridge_Perkins)—known for a historic house, gardens, changing site-specific art installations and concerts—overlooks the [New Jersey Palisades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Palisades) from a promontory on the [Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River) in [Riverdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale,_Bronx).[^cite_note-Van_Cortlandt_Park_:_NYC_Parks-120]

Nearer the borough's center, and along the [Bronx River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_River), is [Bronx Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Park). Its northern end houses the [New York Botanical Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Botanical_Gardens), which preserve the last patch of the original [hemlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsuga) forest that once covered the county, and its southern end the [Bronx Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Zoo), the largest urban zoological gardens in the United States.[^cite_note-fordzoo-121] In 1904, the Chestnut Blight pathogen (*[Cryphonectria parasitica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryphonectria_parasitica)*) was found for the first time outside of [Asia](689.md), at the Bronx Zoo.[^cite_note-VanDerPlank-122] Over the next 40 years it spread throughout [eastern North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_North_America) and killed back essentially every American Chestnut (*[Castanea dentata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castanea_dentata)*), causing ecological and economic devastation.[^cite_note-VanDerPlank-122]

Just south of [Van Cortlandt Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Cortlandt_Park) is the [Jerome Park Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Park_Reservoir), surrounded by 2 miles (3 km) of stone walls and bordering several small parks in the [Bedford Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_Park,_Bronx) neighborhood; the [reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir) was built in the 1890s on the site of the former [Jerome Park Racetrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Park_Racetrack).[^cite_note-123] Further south is [Crotona Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crotona_Park), home to a 3.3-acre (1.3 ha) lake, 28 species of trees, and a large swimming pool.[^cite_note-124] The land for these parks, and many others, was bought by New York City in 1888, while land was still open and inexpensive, in anticipation of future needs and future pressures for development.[^cite_note-125]

Some of the acquired land was set aside for the [Grand Concourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Concourse_\(Bronx\)) and [Pelham Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Parkway), the first of a series of [boulevards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevard) and [parkways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkway), [thoroughfares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoroughfare) lined with trees, vegetation and greenery. Later projects included the [Bronx River Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_River_Parkway), which developed a road while restoring the riverbank and reducing pollution, [Mosholu Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosholu_Parkway) and the [Henry Hudson Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Hudson_Parkway).

In 2006, a five-year, \$220-million program of capital improvements and natural restoration in 70 Bronx parks was begun (financed by water and sewer revenues) as part of an agreement that allowed a [water filtration plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croton_Water_Filtration_Plant) under [Mosholu Golf Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosholu_Golf_Course) in Van Cortlandt Park. One major focus is on opening more of the [Bronx River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_River)'s banks and restoring them to a natural state.[^cite_note-126]

## Divisions of the Bronx

### Regional divisions



An aerial view of the Bronx, [Harlem River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem_River), [Harlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem), [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River) and [George Washington Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_Bridge)

There are two primary systems for dividing the Bronx into regions, which do not necessarily agree with one another. One system is based on the [Bronx River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_River), while the other strictly separates [South Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bronx) from the rest of the borough.

The [Bronx River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_River) divides the borough nearly in half, putting the earlier-settled, more urban, and hillier sections in the west and the newer, more suburban coastal sections in the east. It is an accurate reflection on the Bronx's history considering that the towns that existed in the area prior to annexation to the City of New York generally did not straddle the Bronx River. In addition, what is today the Bronx was annexed to New York City in two stages: areas west of the Bronx River were annexed in 1874, while areas to the east of the river were annexed in 1895.[^cite_note-WhyBronx-42]

- **[West Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bronx)**: all parts of the Bronx west of the **Bronx River** (as opposed to Jerome Avenue – this street is simply the "east-west" divider for designating numbered streets as "east" or "west". As the Bronx's numbered streets continue from Manhattan to south, on which the street numbering system is based, Jerome Avenue actually represents a longitudinal halfway point for Manhattan, not the Bronx.)

  [^cite_note-geo-128]

- **[East Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bronx)**: all parts of the Bronx east of the **Bronx River** (as opposed to Jerome Avenue)

  [^cite_note-geo-128]

  [^cite_note-129]

Under this system, the Bronx can be further divided into the following regions:

- **Northwest Bronx**: the northern half of the West Bronx; the area north of Fordham Road and west of the Bronx River

- **Southwest Bronx**: the southern half of the West Bronx; the area south of Fordham Road and west of the Bronx River

- **Northeast Bronx**: the northern half of the East Bronx; the area north of Pelham Parkway and east of the Bronx River

- **Southeast Bronx**: the southern half of the East Bronx; the area south of Pelham Parkway and east of the Bronx River

A second system divides the borough first and foremost into the following sections:

- **[North Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bronx)**: all areas not in the South Bronx (Southwest Bronx) – i.e. the Northwest Bronx, Northeast Bronx, and Southeast Bronx

- **[South Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bronx)**: the Southwest Bronx – south of Fordham Road and west of the Bronx River. This includes the areas traditionally considered part of the South Bronx.

### East Bronx

([Bronx Community Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Community_Board) 9 *\[south central]*, 10 *\[east]*, 11 *\[east central]* and 12 *\[north central]*)[^cite_note-131]



The neighborhood of [Co-op City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-op_City) is the largest cooperative housing development in the world.

East of the [Bronx River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_River), the borough is relatively flat and includes four large low peninsulas, or 'necks,' of low-lying land which jut into the waters of the East River and were once saltmarsh: Hunts Point, Clason's Point, Screvin's Neck (Castle Hill Point) and [Throgs Neck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throgs_Neck). The East Bronx has older tenement buildings, low income public housing complexes, and multifamily homes, as well as single family homes. It includes New York City's largest park: [Pelham Bay Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park) along the [Westchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westchester_County,_New_York)-Bronx border.

Neighborhoods include: [Clason's Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clason's_Point,_Bronx), [Harding Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harding_Park,_Bronx), [Soundview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soundview,_Bronx), [Castle Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Hill,_Bronx), [Parkchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkchester,_Bronx) *([Community District 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Community_Board_9))*; [Throggs Neck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throggs_Neck), [Country Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Club,_Bronx), [City Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Island,_Bronx), [Pelham Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay), [Edgewater Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgewater_Park_\(Bronx\)), [Co-op City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-op_City,_Bronx) *([Community District 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Community_Board_10))*; [Westchester Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westchester_Square,_Bronx), [Van Nest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Nest), [Pelham Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Parkway,_Bronx), [Morris Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Park,_Bronx) *([Community District 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Community_Board_11))*; [Williamsbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsbridge,_Bronx), [Eastchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastchester,_Bronx), [Baychester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baychester,_Bronx), [Edenwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edenwald,_Bronx) and [Wakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakefield,_Bronx) *([Community District 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Community_Board_12))*.

#### City Island and Hart Island

Main articles: [City Island, Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Island,_Bronx) and [Hart Island (Bronx)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hart_Island_\(Bronx\))



A sunken boat off the shore of [City Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Island,_Bronx)

([Bronx Community District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Community_Board) 10)

City Island is east of [Pelham Bay Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park) in [Long Island Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_Sound) and is known for its seafood restaurants and private waterfront homes.[^cite_note-132] City Island's single shopping street, City Island Avenue, is reminiscent of a small New England town. It is connected to [Rodman's Neck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodman's_Neck) on the mainland by the [City Island Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Island_Bridge).

East of City Island is [Hart Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hart_Island_\(New_York\)), which is uninhabited and not open to the public. It once served as a prison and now houses New York City's [potter's field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potter's_field) for unclaimed bodies.[^cite_note-133]

### West Bronx

Main article: [West Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bronx)



[Grand Concourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Concourse_\(Bronx\)) at East 165th Street in 2008

([Bronx Community Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Community_Board) 1 to 8, progressing roughly from south to northwest)

The western parts of the Bronx are hillier and are dominated by a series of parallel ridges, running south to north. The West Bronx has older apartment buildings, low income public housing complexes, multifamily homes in its lower income areas as well as larger single family homes in more affluent areas such as [Riverdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale,_Bronx) and [Fieldston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fieldston,_Bronx).[^cite_note-134] It includes New York City's third-largest park: [Van Cortlandt Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Cortlandt_Park) along the Westchester-Bronx border. The [Grand Concourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Concourse_\(Bronx\)), a wide boulevard, runs through it, north to south.

#### South Bronx

Main article: [South Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bronx)



[Morris Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Heights,_Bronx), a Bronx neighborhood of over 45,000

([Bronx Community Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Community_Board) 1 to 6 plus part of CD 7—*progressing northwards, CDs 2, 3 and 6 border the [Bronx River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_River) from its mouth to [Bronx Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Park), while 1, 4, 5 and 7 face Manhattan across the [Harlem River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem_River)*)

Like other [neighborhoods in New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_in_New_York_City), the South Bronx has no official boundaries. The name has been used to represent poverty in the Bronx and is applied to progressively more northern places so that by the 2000s, [Fordham Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_Road) was often used as a northern limit. The [Bronx River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_River) more consistently forms an eastern boundary. The South Bronx has many high-density apartment buildings, low income public housing complexes, and multi-unit homes. The South Bronx is home to the [Bronx County Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_County_Courthouse), Borough Hall, and other government buildings, as well as [Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium). The [Cross Bronx Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_Bronx_Expressway) bisects it, east to west. The South Bronx has some of the poorest neighborhoods in the country, as well as very high crime areas.

Ethnic origins in the Bronx

#### 2018 estimates

The borough's most populous racial group, White, declined from 99.3% in 1920 to 14.9% in 2018.[^cite_note-\:1-143]

The Bronx has 532,487 housing units, with a median value of \$371,800, and with an owner-occupancy rate of 19.7%, the lowest of the five boroughs. There are 495,356 households, with 2.85 persons per household. 59.3% of residents speak a language besides English at home, the highest rate of the five boroughs.

In the Bronx, the population is 7.2% under 5, 17.6% 6–18, 62.4% 19–64, and 12.8% over 65. 52.9% of the population is female. 35.3% of residents are foreign born.

The per capita income is \$19,721, while the median household income is \$36,593, both being the lowest of the five boroughs. 27.9% of residents live below the poverty line, the highest of the five boroughs.

#### 2010 census

According to the 2010 Census, 53.5% of Bronx's population was of Hispanic, Latino, or Spanish origin (they may be of any race); 30.1% non-Hispanic Black or African American, 10.9% of the population was non-Hispanic White, 3.4% non-Hispanic Asian, 1.2% of two or more races (non-Hispanic), and 0.6% from some other race (non-Hispanic).

As of 2010, 46.29% (584,463) of Bronx residents aged five and older spoke [Spanish](26825.md) at home, while 44.02% (555,767) spoke [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), 2.48% (31,361) [African languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Africa), 0.91% (11,455) [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language), 0.90% (11,355) [Italian](14708.md), 0.87% (10,946) [various Indic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages), 0.70% (8,836) [other Indo-European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages), and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language) was spoken at home by 0.50% (6,610) of the population over the age of five. In total, 55.98% (706,783) of the Bronx's population age five and older spoke a language at home other than English.[^cite_note-145] A [Garifuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garifuna_language)-speaking community from [Honduras](13394.md) and [Guatemala](17238567.md) also makes the Bronx its home.[^cite_note-146]

The Bronx is the only New York City borough with a [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) majority,[^cite_note-quickfacts-census-147] many of whom are [Puerto Ricans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rican_American) and [Dominicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_American).[^cite_note-148] According to the 2009 American Community Survey, [Black Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Americans) were the second largest [racial/ethnic](273285.md) group in the Bronx. Black people of both Hispanic and non-Hispanic origin represented over one-third (35.4%) of the Bronx's population. Black people of non-Hispanic origin made up 30.8% of the population. Over 495,200 Black people resided in the borough, of whom 87% were non-Hispanic. Over 61,000 people identified themselves as Sub-Saharan African in the survey, making up 4.4% of the population.[^cite_note-\:3-149]

Multiracial Americans are also a sizable minority in the Bronx. People of multiracial heritage number over 41,800 individuals and represent 3.0% of the population. People of mixed [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American) and [European American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Americans) heritage number over 6,850 members and form 0.5% of the population. People of mixed [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_people_of_North_America) and European heritage number over 2,450 members and form 0.2% of the population. People of mixed [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_American) and European heritage number over 880 members and form 0.1% of the population. People of mixed African American and Native American heritage number over 1,220 members and form 0.1% of the population.[^cite_note-\:3-149]

Out of all five boroughs, the Bronx has the lowest number and proportion of white residents. As of 2009, [White Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Americans) of both Hispanic and non-Hispanic origin represented over one-fifth (22.9%) of the Bronx's population, or 320,640 people. [Non-Hispanic White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hispanic_White) people accounted for one-eighth of the population (12.1%, or 168,570 12.1%). This is in contrast to a century ago, when almost all Bronx residents were white (99.3% in 1920). That share fell to about one-third by 1980 (34.4%).[^cite_note-census-150] As of 2009, White Americans of both Hispanic and non-Hispanic origin represented one-fifth (22.9%) of the Bronx's population, but counting non-Hispanic White people the proportion was under one-eighth (12.1%). The majority of the non-Hispanic European American population is of Italian and Irish descent. [People of Italian descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_American) numbered over 55,000 individuals and made up 3.9% of the population. [People of Irish descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_American) numbered over 43,500 individuals and made up 3.1% of the population. [German Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans) and [Polish Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Americans) made up 1.4% and 0.8% of the population respectively. The Bronx has the largest [Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_Americans) community in the United States.[^cite_note-151] As of 2018, non-Hispanic White people account for about one in seven residents (14.9% in 2018).[^cite_note-\:1-143]

### Population and housing



**Poverty concentrations** within the Bronx, by Census Tract

As of the 2010 census, there were 1,385,108 people living in the Bronx, a 3.9% increase since 2000.

As of the [2000 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_census),[^cite_note-GR2-142] there were 1,332,650 people, 463,212 households, and 314,984 families residing in the borough. The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) was 31,709.3 inhabitants per square mile (12,243.0 inhabitants/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 490,659 housing units at an average density of 11,674.8 units per square mile (4,507.7 units/km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-GR2-142] Census estimates place total population of Bronx county at 1,392,002 as of 2012.[^cite_note-154]

There were 463,212 households, out of which 38.1% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 31.4% were married couples living together, 30.4% had a female householder with no husband present, and 32.0% were non-families. 27.4% of all households were made up of individuals, and 9.4% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.78 and the average family size was 3.37.[^cite_note-GR2-142]

The age distribution of the population in the Bronx were as follows: 29.8% under the age of 18, 10.6% from 18 to 24, 30.7% from 25 to 44, 18.8% from 45 to 64, and 10.1% 65 years of age or older. The median age was 31 years. For every 100 females, there were 87.0 males.[^cite_note-GR2-142]

### Sports

See also: [Sports in the New York metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_the_New_York_metropolitan_area)





[Babe Ruth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babe_Ruth), often considered the greatest baseball player of all time, is seen retiring from the [Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees) in a [Pulitzer prize-winning photograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babe_Ruth_Bows_Out) in the Bronx's original [Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium_\(1923\)) ([current field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium) at right).

The Bronx is the home of the [New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees)—nicknamed "the Bronx Bombers"—of [Major League Baseball](38776.md).[^cite_note-159] The Yankees have won 27 [World Series](23489161.md) titles, more than any other team, and their roster has featured players like [Babe Ruth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babe_Ruth), [Lou Gehrig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Gehrig), [Joe DiMaggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_DiMaggio), and [Mickey Mantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Mantle).[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161]

When the team's original [Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium_\(1923\)) opened in 1923, it was the largest baseball park.[^cite_note-162] The field also hosted college football games, and was the home of two [National Football League](21211.md) teams, the [New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees_\(NFL\)) (1926–1929) and the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants) (1956–1973).[^cite_note-163] In 2008, the park was replaced with the current [Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium).[^cite_note-164]

The Bronx additionally hosts the only [Major League Soccer](71802.md) team in the five boroughs, the [New York City FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_FC), which also plays in Yankee Stadium.[^cite_note-165] Part of the [New York City Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Marathon) travels through the Bronx, including the notoriously difficult Mile 20.[^cite_note-166] From 1889 to 1904, the borough used to have a horse racing facility, the [Morris Park Racecourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Park_Racecourse).[^cite_note-167] In its later years, the course was used for [motor racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1905_Morris_Park_5): a new [land speed record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_speed_record) was reached on the track.[^cite_note-168] College teams in the Bronx include the [Fordham Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_Rams) and the [Lehman Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehman_Lightning).[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170]

### Music

See also: [Music of New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_New_York_City)



[DJ Kool Herc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Kool_Herc) in 1999

The Bronx has had a long association with music. In the early 20th century, it was a center for the evolution of [Latin jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_jazz).[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173] The [Bronx Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Opera) was established in 1967.[^cite_note-174]

In the 1970s, the Bronx was strongly associated with the development of [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop),[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176] which evolved during a decade of demographic and economic decline for the borough.[^cite_note-177] One of the genre's pioneers, [DJ Kool Herc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Kool_Herc), held parties in the community room of an apartment building at 1520 [Sedgwick Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedgwick_Avenue), where he experimented with [turntablist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turntablism) techniques such as [mixing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_mix) and [scratching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratching) of [funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk) records, as well as [rapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapping) during extended instrumentals.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179][^cite_note-PBS-180] Other significant Bronx DJs from this period include [Grandmaster Flash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandmaster_Flash) and [Afrika Bambaataa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrika_Bambaataa).[^cite_note-181] In addition, the Bronx was an important base for [drill culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drill_music), with the rise of rappers such as [Kay Flock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kay_Flock), [Sha EK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sha_EK) and many others.

### Off-Off-Broadway

Main article: [Off-Off-Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-Off-Broadway)

The Bronx is home to several [Off-Off-Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-Off-Broadway) theaters, many staging new works by immigrant playwrights from Latin America and Africa. The [Pregones Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregones_Theater), which produces Latin American work, opened a new 130-seat theater in 2005 on Walton Avenue in the South Bronx. Some artists from elsewhere in New York City have begun to converge on the area, and housing prices have nearly quadrupled in the area since 2002. However, rising prices directly correlate to a housing shortage across the city and the entire metro area.

### Arts

The [Bronx Academy of Arts and Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Academy_of_Arts_and_Dance), founded in 1998 by [Arthur Aviles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Aviles) and [Charles Rice-González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Rice-Gonz%C3%A1lez), provides dance, theatre and art workshops, festivals and performances focusing on contemporary and modern art in relation to race, gender and sexuality. It is home to the Arthur Aviles Typical Theatre, a contemporary dance company, and the Bronx Dance Coalition. The academy was formerly in the [American Bank Note Company Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Bank_Note_Company_Building) before relocating to a venue on the grounds of St. Peter's Episcopal Church.[^cite_note-182]

The [Bronx Museum of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Museum_of_the_Arts), founded in 1971, exhibits 20th century and contemporary art through its central museum space and 11,000 square feet (1,000 m<sup>2</sup>) of galleries. Many of its exhibitions are on themes of special interest to the Bronx. Its permanent collection features more than 800 works of art, primarily by artists from Africa, Asia and Latin America, including paintings, photographs, prints, drawings, and mixed media. The museum was temporarily closed in 2006 while it underwent an expansion designed by the architectural firm [Arquitectonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arquitectonica) that would double the museum's size to 33,000 square feet (3,100 m<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-183]

The Bronx has also become home to a peculiar poetic tribute in the form of the "[Heinrich Heine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Heine) Memorial", better known as the [Lorelei Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorelei_Fountain). After Heine's German birthplace of [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf) had rejected, allegedly for [antisemitic](1078.md) motives, a centennial monument to the radical [German-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-Jewish) poet (1797–1856), his incensed [German-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-American) admirers, including [Carl Schurz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Schurz), started a movement to place one instead in [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan), at [Fifth Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Avenue) and 59th Street. However, this intention was thwarted by a combination of ethnic antagonism, aesthetic controversy and political struggles over the institutional control of public art.[^cite_note-GrayNYT2007-184] In 1899, the memorial by [Ernst Gustav Herter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Gustav_Herter) was placed in [Joyce Kilmer Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyce_Kilmer_Park), near the [Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium). In 1999, it was moved to 161st Street and the Concourse.

### Maritime heritage



The [Bronx Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Zoo) is the largest zoo in New York City, and among the largest in the country.

The [peninsular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula) borough's maritime heritage is acknowledged in several ways. The City Island Historical Society and Nautical Museum occupies a former public school designed by the New York City school system's turn-of-the-last-century master architect [C. B. J. Snyder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._B._J._Snyder). The state's [Maritime College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SUNY_Maritime_College) in [Fort Schuyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Schuyler) (on the southeastern shore) houses the [Maritime Industry Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Industry_Museum).[^cite_note-185] In addition, the Harlem River is reemerging as ["Scullers' Row"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculling)[^cite_note-186] due in large part to the efforts of the Bronx River Restoration Project,[^cite_note-187] a joint public-private endeavor of the city's parks department. [Canoeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canoeing) and [kayaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayaking) on the borough's namesake river have been promoted by the Bronx River Alliance. The river is also straddled by the [New York Botanical Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Botanical_Gardens), its neighbor, the [Bronx Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Zoo), and a little further south, on the west shore, Bronx River Art Center.[^cite_note-188]

### Community celebrations

"Bronx Week", traditionally held in May, began as a one-day celebration. Begun by Bronx historian Lloyd Ultan and supported by then borough president Robert Abrams, the original one-day program was based on the "Bronx Borough Day" festival which took place in the 1920s. The following year, at the height of the decade's civil unrest, the festival was extended to a one-week event. In the 1980s the key event, the "Bronx Ball", was launched. The week includes the Bronx Week Parade as well as inductions into the "Bronx Walk of Fame".[^cite_note-189]

Various Bronx neighborhoods conduct their own community celebrations. The Arthur Avenue "Little Italy" neighborhood conducts an annual Autumn Ferragosto Festival that celebrates Italian culture.[^cite_note-190] [Hunts Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunts_Point,_Bronx) hosts an annual "Fish Parade and Summer Festival" at the start of summer.[^cite_note-191] [Edgewater Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgewater_Park_\(Bronx\)) hosts an annual "Ragamuffin" children's walk in November.[^cite_note-192] There are several events to honor the borough's veterans.[^cite_note-193] Albanian Independence Day is also observed.[^cite_note-194]

There are also parades to celebrate Dominican, Italian, and Irish heritage.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197]

### Press and broadcasting

The Bronx is home to several local newspapers and radio and television studios.

#### Newspapers

The Bronx has several local newspapers, including The Bronx Daily, *The [Bronx News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_News)*,[^cite_note-198] *[Parkchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkchester) News*, *City News*, *The [Norwood News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwood_News)*, *The [Riverdale Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale_Press)*, *[Riverdale Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale_Review)*, *The [Bronx Times Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Times_Reporter)*, and *[Co-op City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-op_City) Times*. Four non-profit news outlets, *Norwood News*, *Mount Hope Monitor*, *[Mott Haven Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mott_Haven_Herald)* and *The [Hunts Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunts_Point,_Bronx) Express* serve the borough's poorer communities. The editor and co-publisher of *The Riverdale Press*, Bernard Stein, won the [Pulitzer Prize for Editorial Writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize_for_Editorial_Writing) for his editorials about Bronx and New York City issues in 1998. (Stein graduated from the [Bronx High School of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_High_School_of_Science) in 1959.)

The Bronx once had its own daily newspaper, *[The Bronx Home News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx_Home_News)*, which started publishing on January 20, 1907, and merged into the *[New York Post](102227.md)* in 1948. It became a special section of the *Post*, sold only in the Bronx, and eventually disappeared from view.

#### Radio and television

One of New York City's major non-commercial radio broadcasters is [WFUV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WFUV), a [National Public Radio](29697232.md)-affiliated 50,000-watt station broadcasting from [Fordham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_University)'s Rose Hill campus in the Bronx. The radio station's antenna was relocated to the top of an apartment building owned by [Montefiore Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montefiore_Medical_Center), which expanded the reach of the station's signal.[^cite_note-199]

The City of New York has an official television station run by [NYC Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYC_Media) and broadcasting from [Bronx Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Community_College), and [Cablevision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cablevision) operates [News 12 The Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_12_Networks), both of which feature programming based in the Bronx. [Co-op City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-op_City) was the first area in the Bronx, and the first in New York beyond [Manhattan](45470.md), to have its own [cable television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television) provider. The local [public-access television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public-access_television) station [BronxNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BronxNet) originates from Herbert H. Lehman College, the borough's only four year CUNY school, and provides [government-access television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government-access_television) (GATV) public affairs programming in addition to programming produced by Bronx residents.[^cite_note-200]

Renovated Prow Building, part of the original [Bronx Terminal Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Terminal_Market)

Prominent shopping areas in the Bronx include [Fordham Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_Road), [Bay Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Plaza_Shopping_Center) in [Co-op City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-op_City), [The Hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hub,_Bronx), the [Riverdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale,_Bronx)/[Kingsbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsbridge,_Bronx) shopping center, and [Bruckner Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruckner_Boulevard). Shops are also concentrated on streets aligned underneath elevated railroad lines, including Westchester Avenue, [White Plains Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Plains_Road), [Jerome Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Avenue), [Southern Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Boulevard_\(Bronx\)), and [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_\(Bronx\)). The Bronx has three primary shopping centers: The Hub, Gateway Center and Southern Boulevard. The Hub–Third Avenue Business Improvement District (B.I.D.), in [The Hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hub,_Bronx), is the retail heart of the [South Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bronx), where four roads converge: East 149th Street, Willis, Melrose and Third Avenues.[^cite_note-201] It is primarily inside the neighborhood of [Melrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melrose,_Bronx) but also lines the northern border of [Mott Haven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mott_Haven,_Bronx).[^cite_note-202] The Hub has been called "the Broadway of the Bronx", being likened to [the real Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_\(Bronx\)) in Manhattan and the northwestern Bronx.[^cite_note-203] It is the site of both maximum traffic and architectural density. In configuration, it resembles a miniature [Times Square](31389.md), a spatial "bow-tie" created by the geometry of the street.[^cite_note-204] The Hub is part of [Bronx Community Board 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Community_Board_1).

The [Bronx Terminal Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Terminal_Market), in the [West Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bronx), formerly known as Gateway Center, is a shopping center that encompasses less than one million square feet of retail space, built on a 17 acres (7 ha) site that formerly held a wholesale fruit and vegetable market also named Bronx Terminal Market as well as the former [Bronx House of Detention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_House_of_Detention), south of [Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium). The \$500 million shopping center, which was completed in 2009, saw the construction of new buildings and two smaller buildings, one new and the other a renovation of an existing building that was part of the original market. The two main buildings are linked by a six-level garage for 2,600 cars. The center's design has earned it a [LEED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leadership_in_Energy_and_Environmental_Design) "Silver" designation.[^cite_note-205]

## Government and politics

### Local government

Main article: [Government of New York City](531637.md)

Since New York City's consolidation in 1898, the New York City Charter that provides for a "strong" [mayor–council system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor%E2%80%93council_government) has governed the Bronx. The centralized New York City government is responsible for public education, correctional institutions, libraries, public safety, recreational facilities, sanitation, water supply, and welfare services in the Bronx.



Bronx [borough president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_president) [Vanessa Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanessa_Gibson)

The office of [Borough President](531637.md#Borough_presidents) was created in the consolidation of 1898 to balance centralization with local authority. Each borough president had a powerful administrative role derived from having a vote on the [New York City Board of Estimate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Board_of_Estimate), which was responsible for creating and approving the city's budget and proposals for land use. In 1989 the [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md) declared the Board of Estimate unconstitutional on the grounds that [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), the most populous borough, had no greater effective representation on the Board than [Staten Island](127062.md), the least populous borough, a violation of the [Fourteenth Amendment's](31666.md) [Equal Protection Clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_Protection_Clause) pursuant to the high court's 1964 "one man, one vote" decision.[^cite_note-law\.cornell.edu-206]

Since 1990, the Borough President has acted as an advocate for the borough at the mayoral agencies, the [City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Council), the New York state government, and corporations.

Until March 1, 2009, the Borough President of the Bronx was [Adolfo Carrión Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolfo_Carri%C3%B3n_Jr.), elected as a [Democrat](5043544.md) in 2001 and 2005 before retiring early to direct the [White House Office of Urban Affairs Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Office_of_Urban_Affairs_Policy). His successor, Democratic [New York State Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Assembly) member [Rubén Díaz, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub%C3%A9n_D%C3%ADaz_Jr.) — after winning a special election on April 21, 2009, by a vote of 86.3% (29,420) on the "Bronx Unity" line to 13.3% (4,646) for the Republican district leader Anthony Ribustello on the "People First" line,[^cite_note-nytimes.com-207][^cite_note-vote.nyc.ny.us-208] — became Borough President on May 1, 2009. In 2021, Rubén Díaz's Democratic successor, [Vanessa Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanessa_Gibson) was elected (began serving in 2022) with 79.9% of the vote against 13.4% for Janell King (Republican) and 6.5% for Sammy Ravelo (Conservative).

All of the Bronx's currently elected public officials have first won the nomination of the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(U.S.\)) (in addition to any other endorsements). Local party platforms center on affordable housing, education and economic development. Controversial political issues in the Bronx include environmental issues, the cost of housing, and annexation of parkland for new [Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium).[^cite_note-209]

Since its separation from [New York County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_County) on January 1, 1914, the Bronx, has had, like each of the other 61 counties of New York State, its own criminal court system[^cite_note-courtstart-9] and [District Attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_attorney), the chief public prosecutor who is directly elected by popular vote. [Darcel D. Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darcel_D._Clark) has been the [Bronx County District Attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_County_District_Attorney) since 2016. Her predecessor was [Robert T. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_T._Johnson_\(lawyer\)), the District Attorney from 1989 to 2015. He was the first African-American District Attorney in New York State.[^cite_note-210]

The Bronx also has twelve [Community Boards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_boards_of_the_Bronx), appointed bodies that advise on land use and municipal facilities and services for local residents, businesses and institutions.

After becoming a separate county in 1914, the Bronx has supported only two Republican presidential candidates. It voted heavily for the winning Republican [Warren G. Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_G._Harding) in [1920](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_United_States_presidential_election_in_New_York), but much more narrowly on a split vote for his victorious Republican successor [Calvin Coolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Coolidge) in [1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_United_States_presidential_election_in_New_York) (Coolidge 79,562; [John W. Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_W._Davis), Dem., 72,834; [Robert La Follette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._La_Follette,_Sr.), 62,202 equally divided between the [Progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Party_\(United_States,_1924\)) and [Socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_America) lines).

Since then, the Bronx has always supported the Democratic Party's nominee for president, starting with a vote of 2–1 for the unsuccessful [Al Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Smith) in 1928, followed by four 2–1 votes for the successful [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt). (Both had been Governors of New York, but Republican former Gov. [Thomas E. Dewey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_E._Dewey) won only 28% of the Bronx's vote in 1948 against 55% for Pres. [Harry Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Truman), the winning Democrat, and 17% for [Henry A. Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_A._Wallace) of the [Progressives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Party_\(United_States,_1948\)). It was only 32 years earlier, by contrast, that another Republican former Governor who narrowly lost the Presidency, [Charles Evans Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Evans_Hughes), had won 42.6% of the Bronx's 1916 vote against Democratic President [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson)'s 49.8% and Socialist candidate [Allan Benson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_Benson)'s 7.3%.)[^cite_note-215] [Donald Trump](4848272.md) improved on the Republican Party's performance from a historic low of 8% in 2012 to 27% in 2024 over the course of his three runs for president, the highest for Republicans since [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_United_States_presidential_election_in_New_York).

### Mayoral elections

Main article: [New York City mayoral elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_mayoral_elections)

The Bronx has often shown striking differences from other boroughs in [elections for Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_mayoral_elections). The only Republican to carry the Bronx since 1914 was [Fiorello La Guardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiorello_La_Guardia) in [1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1933_New_York_City_mayoral_election), [1937](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937_New_York_City_mayoral_election), and [1941](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1941_New_York_City_mayoral_election) (and in the latter two elections, only because his 30% to 32% vote on the [American Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Labor_Party) line was added to 22% to 23% as a Republican).[^cite_note-217] The Bronx was thus the only borough not carried by the successful Republican re-election campaigns of Mayors [Rudy Giuliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudy_Giuliani) in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_New_York_City_mayoral_election) and [Michael Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bloomberg) in [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_New_York_City_mayoral_election).

The anti-war [Socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_America) campaign of [Morris Hillquit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Hillquit) in the [1917 mayoral election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1917_New_York_City_mayoral_election) won over 31% of the Bronx's vote, putting him second and well ahead of the 20% won by the incumbent pro-war Fusion Mayor [John Purroy Mitchel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Purroy_Mitchel), who came in second (ahead of Hillquit) everywhere else and outpolled Hillquit citywide by 23.2% to 21.7%. In every subsequent election through 1933, the Bronx gave the Socialist Party candidate a higher percent of the vote than any other borough.[^cite_note-218]

## Education

See also: [Education in New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_New_York_City), [List of public elementary schools in New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_public_elementary_schools_in_New_York_City), and [Category\:Charter schools in New York (state)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Charter_schools_in_New_York_\(state\))

Education in the Bronx is provided by a large number of public and private institutions, many of which draw students who live beyond the Bronx. The [New York City Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Department_of_Education) manages the borough's public noncharter schools.[^cite_note-219] In 2000, public schools enrolled nearly 280,000 of the Bronx's residents over three years old (out of 333,100 enrolled in all pre-college schools).[^cite_note-quicktable-220] There are also several public [charter schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_schools). Private schools range from elite [independent schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_school) to religiously affiliated [schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parochial_schools#United_States) run by the [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_New_York) and Jewish organizations.

A small portion of land between Pelham and Pelham Bay Park, with 35 houses, is a part of the Bronx, but is cut off from the rest of the borough due to the county boundaries; the New York City government pays for the residents' children to go to [Pelham Union Free School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Union_Free_School_District) schools, including [Pelham Memorial High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Memorial_High_School), since that is more cost effective than sending school buses to take the students to New York City schools. This arrangement has been in place since 1948.[^cite_note-gross19970506-221]

### Educational attainment

In 2000, according to the United States census, out of the nearly 800,000 people in the Bronx who were then at least 25 years old, 62.3% had graduated from high school and 14.6% held a bachelor's or higher college degree. These percentages were lower than those for New York's other boroughs, which ranged from 68.8% (Brooklyn) to 82.6% (Staten Island) for high school graduates over 24, and from 21.8% (Brooklyn) to 49.4% (Manhattan) for college graduates. (The respective state and national percentages were *\[NY]* 79.1% & 27.4% and *\[US]* 80.4% & 24.4%.)[^cite_note-222]

### Colleges and universities

See also: [List of colleges and universities in New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_New_York_City)





The Bronx hosts [Fordham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_University) and the [Albert Einstein College of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein_College_of_Medicine), among others.

In 2000, 49,442 (57.5%) of the 86,014 Bronx residents seeking college, graduate or professional degrees attended public institutions.[^cite_note-quicktable-220] Several colleges and universities are in the Bronx.

[Fordham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_University) was founded as St. John's College in 1841 by the [Diocese of New York](245234.md) as the first Catholic institution of higher education in the [northeast](431669.md). It is now officially an independent institution, but strongly embraces its [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) heritage. The 85-acre (340,000 m<sup>2</sup>) Bronx campus, known as Rose Hill, is the main campus of the university, and is among the largest within the city (other Fordham campuses are in Manhattan and Westchester County).[^cite_note-fordzoo-121]

Three campuses of the [City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_of_New_York) are in the Bronx: [Hostos Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hostos_Community_College), [Bronx Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Community_College) (occupying the former [University Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Heights,_Bronx) Campus of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University))[^cite_note-223] and Herbert H. [Lehman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehman_College) (formerly the uptown campus of [Hunter College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College)), which offers both undergraduate and graduate degrees.

The [College of Mount Saint Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Mount_Saint_Vincent) is a Catholic liberal arts college in [Riverdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale,_Bronx) under the direction of the [Sisters of Charity of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_of_New_York). Founded in 1847 as a school for girls, the academy became a degree-granting college in 1911 and began admitting men in 1974. The school serves 1,600 students. Its campus is also home to the [Academy for Jewish Religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_for_Jewish_Religion_\(New_York\)), a transdenominational rabbinical and cantorial school.

[Manhattan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_University) is a Catholic college in [Riverdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale,_Bronx) which offers undergraduate programs in the arts, business, education, engineering, and science. It also offers graduate programs in education and engineering.

[Albert Einstein College of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein_College_of_Medicine), part of the [Montefiore Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montefiore_Medical_Center), is in [Morris Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Park,_Bronx).

The coeducational and non-sectarian [Mercy University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercy_University)—with its main campus in [Dobbs Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobbs_Ferry,_New_York)—has a Bronx campus near [Westchester Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westchester_Square,_Bronx).

The [State University of New York Maritime College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_New_York_Maritime_College) in [Fort Schuyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Schuyler) ([Throggs Neck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throggs_Neck))—at the far southeastern tip of the Bronx—is the national leader in maritime education and houses the [Maritime Industry Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Industry_Museum). (Directly across [Long Island Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_Sound) is [Kings Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Point,_New_York), Long Island, home of the [United States Merchant Marine Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Merchant_Marine_Academy) and the American Merchant Marine Museum.) As of 2017, graduates from the university earned an average annual salary of \$144,000, the highest of any university graduates in the United States.[^cite_note-224]

In addition, the private, proprietary [Monroe University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe_University), focused on preparation for business and the professions, started in the Bronx in 1933 and now has a campus in [New Rochelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Rochelle,_New_York) (Westchester County) as well the Bronx's [Fordham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham,_Bronx) neighborhood.[^cite_note-225]

## Transportation

### Roads and streets



[Bronx–Whitestone Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx%E2%80%93Whitestone_Bridge)

#### Surface streets

The Bronx [street grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_grid) is irregular. Like the northernmost part of [upper Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Manhattan), the [West Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bronx)'s hilly terrain leaves a relatively free-style street grid. Much of the West Bronx's street numbering carries over from upper Manhattan, but does not match it exactly; East 132nd Street is the lowest numbered street in the Bronx. This dates from the mid-19th century when the southwestern area of [Westchester County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westchester_County,_New_York) west of the Bronx River, was incorporated into New York City and known as the Northside.

The [East Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bronx) is considerably flatter, and the street layout tends to be more regular. Only the [Wakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakefield,_Bronx) neighborhood picks up the street numbering, albeit at a misalignment due to Tremont Avenue's layout. At the same diagonal latitude, West 262nd Street in Riverdale matches East 237th Street in Wakefield.

Three major north–south thoroughfares run between Manhattan and the Bronx: [Third Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Avenue), [Park Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Avenue), and [Broadway](4626.md). Other major north–south roads include the [Grand Concourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Concourse_\(Bronx\)), [Jerome Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Avenue), [Sedgwick Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedgwick_Avenue), [Webster Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webster_Avenue), and [White Plains Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Plains_Road). Major east-west thoroughfares include [Mosholu Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosholu_Parkway), [Gun Hill Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_Hill_Road_\(Bronx\)), [Fordham Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_Road), [Pelham Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Parkway), and Tremont Avenue.

Most east–west streets are prefixed with either *East* or *West*, to indicate on which side of Jerome Avenue they lie (continuing the similar system in Manhattan, which uses [Fifth Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Avenue) as the dividing line).[^cite_note-226]

The historic [Boston Post Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Post_Road), part of the long pre-revolutionary road connecting [Boston](24437894.md) with other northeastern cities, runs east–west in some places, and sometimes northeast–southwest.

[Mosholu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosholu_Parkway) and [Pelham Parkways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Parkway), with [Bronx Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Park) between them, [Van Cortlandt Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Cortlandt_Park) to the west and [Pelham Bay Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park) to the east, are also linked by [bridle paths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridle_path).

As of the 2000 Census, approximately 61.6% of all Bronx households do not have access to a car. Citywide, the percentage of autoless households is 55%.[^cite_note-227]

## In popular culture

### Film and television

See also: [List of films set in New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_set_in_New_York_City) and [List of television shows set in New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_television_shows_set_in_New_York_City)

#### Mid-20th century

Mid-20th century movies set in the Bronx portrayed densely settled, working-class, urban culture. *[From This Day Forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_This_Day_Forward)* (1946), set in [Highbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highbridge,_Bronx), occasionally delved into Bronx life. The most notable examinations of working class Bronx life were [Paddy Chayefsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddy_Chayefsky)'s [Academy Award](324.md)-winning *[Marty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_\(film\))*[^cite_note-234] and his 1956 film *[The Catered Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Catered_Affair).* Other films that portrayed life in the Bronx are: the 1993 [Robert De Niro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_De_Niro)/[Chazz Palminteri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chazz_Palminteri) film, *[A Bronx Tale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Bronx_Tale)*, [Spike Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_Lee)'s 1999 movie *[Summer of Sam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_of_Sam)*, which focused on an [Italian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian-American) Bronx community in the 1970s, 1994's *[I Like It Like That](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Like_It_Like_That_\(film\))* which takes place in the predominantly [Puerto Rican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rican_people) neighborhood of the South Bronx, and *Doughboys*, the story of two Italian-American brothers in danger of losing their bakery thanks to one brother's gambling debts.

The Bronx's gritty urban life had worked its way into the movies even earlier, with depictions of the "[Bronx cheer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_cheer_\(gesture\))", a loud flatulent-like sound of disapproval, allegedly first made by [New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees) fans. The sound can be heard, for example, on the [Spike Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_Jones) and His City Slickers recording of "Der Fuehrer's Face" (from the 1942 [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney) [animated film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animated_film) of the [same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Fuehrer's_Face)), repeatedly lambasting [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md) with: "We'll Heil! (Bronx cheer) Heil! (Bronx cheer) Right in Der Fuehrer's Face!"[^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236]

#### Symbolism

Starting in the 1970s, the Bronx often symbolized violence, decay, and urban ruin. The wave of arson in the South Bronx in the 1960s and 1970s inspired the observation that "The Bronx is burning": in 1974 it was the title of both an editorial in *[The New York Times](30680.md)* and a [BBC](19344654.md) [documentary film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_film).[^cite_note-237] The line entered the pop-consciousness with Game Two of the [1977 World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_World_Series), when a fire broke out near [Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium_\(1923\)) as the team was playing the [Los Angeles Dodgers](18213.md). As the fire was captured on live television, announcer [Howard Cosell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Cosell) [is wrongly remembered to have said something like](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx_Is_Burning#Summary), "There it is, ladies and gentlemen: the Bronx is burning". Historians of New York City often point to Cosell's remark as an acknowledgement of both the city and the borough's decline.[^cite_note-238] A feature-length documentary film by Edwin Pagán called *Bronx Burning* chronicled what led up to the many arson-for-insurance fraud fires of the 1970s in the borough.[^cite_note-239][^cite_note-240]

Bronx gang life was depicted in the 1974 novel *The Wanderers* by Bronx native [Richard Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Price_\(writer\)) and the [1979 movie of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wanderers_\(1979_film\)). They are set in the heart of the Bronx, showing apartment life and the then-landmark Krums ice cream parlor. In the 1979 film *[The Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Warriors_\(film\))*, the eponymous gang go to a meeting in [Van Cortlandt Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Cortlandt_Park) in the Bronx, and have to fight their way out of the borough and get back to [Coney Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coney_Island) in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn). The 2005 video game adaptation features levels called Pelham, Tremont, and "Gunhill" (a play off the name [Gun Hill Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_Hill_Road_\(Bronx\))). *[A Bronx Tale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Bronx_Tale)* (1993) depicts gang activities in the [Belmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont,_Bronx) "Little Italy" section of the Bronx. This theme lends itself to the title of *[The Bronx Is Burning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx_Is_Burning)*, an eight-part [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) TV mini-series (2007) about the [New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees)' drive to winning baseball's [1977 World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_World_Series). The TV series emphasizes the team's boisterous nature, led by manager [Billy Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Martin), catcher [Thurman Munson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurman_Munson) and outfielder [Reggie Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggie_Jackson), as well as the malaise of the Bronx and New York City in general during that time, such as the blackout, the city's serious financial woes and near bankruptcy, the arson for insurance payments, and the election of [Ed Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Koch) as mayor.

The 1981 film *[Fort Apache, The Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Apache,_The_Bronx)* is another film that used the Bronx's gritty image for its storyline. The movie's title is from the nickname for the 41st Police Precinct in the South Bronx which was nicknamed "Fort Apache". Also from 1981 is the horror film *[Wolfen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfen_\(film\))* making use of the rubble of the Bronx as a home for werewolf type creatures. *[Knights of the South Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_of_the_South_Bronx)*, a true story of a teacher who worked with disadvantaged children, is another film also set in the Bronx released in 2005. The Bronx was the setting for the 1983 film *[Fuga dal Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuga_dal_Bronx)*, also known as *Bronx Warriors 2* and *Escape 2000*, an Italian B-movie best known for its appearance on the television series *[Mystery Science Theater 3000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystery_Science_Theater_3000)*. The plot revolves around a sinister construction corporation's plans to depopulate, destroy and redevelop the Bronx, and a band of rebels who are out to expose the corporation's murderous ways and save their homes. The film is memorable for its almost incessant use of the phrase, "Leave the Bronx!" Many of the movie's scenes were filmed in [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens), substituting as the Bronx. *[Rumble in the Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumble_in_the_Bronx)*, filmed in Vancouver, was a 1995 [Jackie Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Chan) [kung-fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kung-fu) film, another which popularized the Bronx to international audiences. *[Last Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Bronx)*, a 1996 Sega game played on the bad reputation of the Bronx to lend its name to an alternate version of post-Japanese bubble Tokyo, where crime and gang warfare is rampant. The 2016 [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) series *[The Get Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Get_Down)* is based on the development of hip-hop in 1977 in the South Bronx.[^cite_note-241]

### Literature

See also: [List of books set in New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_books_set_in_New_York_City)

#### Books

The Bronx has been featured significantly in fiction literature. All of the characters in [Herman Wouk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Wouk)'s [City Boy: The Adventures of Herbie Bookbinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Boy:_The_Adventures_of_Herbie_Bookbinder) (1948) live in the Bronx, and about half of the action is set there. [Kate Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Simon)'s *Bronx Primitive: Portraits of a Childhood* (1982) is directly autobiographical, a warm account of a Polish-Jewish girl in an immigrant family growing up before World War II, and living near [Arthur Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Avenue) and [Tremont Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tremont_Avenue).[^cite_note-242] In Jacob M. Appel's short story, "The Grand Concourse" (2007),[^cite_note-243] a woman who grew up in the iconic [Lewis Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Morris) Building returns to the [Morrisania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrisania) neighborhood with her adult daughter. Similarly, in [Avery Corman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avery_Corman)'s book *The Old Neighborhood* (1980),[^cite_note-244] an upper-middle class white protagonist returns to his birth neighborhood ([Fordham Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_Road) and the [Grand Concourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Concourse_\(Bronx\))), and learns that even though the folks are poor, Hispanic and African-American, they are good people.

By contrast, [Tom Wolfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Wolfe)'s *[Bonfire of the Vanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bonfire_of_the_Vanities)* (1987)[^cite_note-245] portrays a wealthy, white protagonist, Sherman McCoy, getting lost off the [Bruckner Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruckner_Expressway) in the [South Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bronx) and having an altercation with locals. A substantial piece of the last part of the book is set in the resulting riotous trial at the Bronx County Courthouse. However, times change, and in 2007, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported that "the Bronx neighborhoods near the site of Sherman's accident are now dotted with townhouses and apartments." In the same article, the Reverend [Al Sharpton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Sharpton) (whose fictional analogue in the novel is "Reverend Bacon") asserts that "twenty years later, the cynicism of *The Bonfire of the Vanities* is as out of style as Tom Wolfe's wardrobe."[^cite_note-246]

[Don DeLillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_DeLillo)'s *[Underworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underworld_\(DeLillo_novel\))* (1997) is also set in the Bronx and offers a perspective on the area from the 1950s onward.[^cite_note-247]

#### Poetry

In poetry, the Bronx has been immortalized by one of the world's shortest [couplets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Couplet):

The Bronx?\
&#x20;No Thonx\
&#x20; [Ogden Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogden_Nash), *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker)*, 1931

Nash repented 33 years after his [calumny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calumny), penning the following poem to the dean of faculty at [Bronx Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Community_College) in 1964:[^cite_note-248]

I wrote those lines, "The Bronx? No thonx";\
&#x20;I shudder to confess them.\
&#x20;\
&#x20;Now I'm an older, wiser man\
&#x20;I cry, "The Bronx? God bless them!"[^cite_note-thonx-93]

In 2016, W. R. Rodriguez published *Bronx Trilogy*—consisting of *the shoe shine parlor poems et al.*, *concrete pastures of the beautiful bronx*, and *from the banks of brook avenue*. The trilogy celebrates Bronx people, places, and events. [DeWitt Clinton High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeWitt_Clinton_High_School), [St. Mary's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mary's_Park_\(Bronx\)), and Brook Avenue are a few of the schools, parks, and streets Rodriguez uses as subjects for his poems.[^cite_note-249]

Nash's couplet "The Bronx? No Thonx" and his subsequent blessing are mentioned in *Bronx Accent: A Literary and Pictorial History of the Borough*, edited by Lloyd Ultan and Barbara Unger and published in 2000. The book, which includes the work of Yiddish poets, offers a selection from [Allen Ginsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Ginsberg)'s *[Kaddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaddish_\(poem\))*, as his Aunt Elanor and his mother, Naomi, lived near Woodlawn Cemetery. Also featured is Ruth Lisa Schecther's poem, "Bronx", which is described as a celebration of the borough's landmarks. There is a selection of works from poets such as [Sandra María Esteves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Mar%C3%ADa_Esteves), [Milton Kessler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Kessler), Joan Murray, W. R. Rodriguez, Myra Shapiro, Gayl Teller, and [Terence Wynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terence_Winch).[^cite_note-250]

"Bronx Migrations" by Michelle M. Tokarczyk is a collection that spans five decades of Tokarczyk's life in the Bronx, from her exodus in 1962 to her return in search of her childhood tenement.[^cite_note-251][^cite_note-252]

#### Bronx Memoir Project

*[Bronx Memoir Project: Vol. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Memoir_Project:_Vol._1)* is a published [anthology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthology) by the Bronx Council on the Arts and brought forth through a series of workshops meant to empower Bronx residents and shed the stigma on the Bronx's burning past.[^cite_note-NYDNI-253] The Bronx Memoir Project was created as an ongoing collaboration between the [Bronx Council on the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Council_on_the_Arts) and other [cultural institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_institution), including the Bronx [Documentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_film) Center, the [Bronx Library Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Library_Center), the (Edgar Allan) [Poe Park Visitor Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poe_Park_Visitor_Center), Mindbuilders, and other institutions and funded through a grant from the [National Endowment for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Endowment_for_the_Arts).[^cite_note-HP-254][^cite_note-AT-255] The goal was to develop and refine memoir fragments written by people of all walks of life that share a common bond residing within the Bronx.[^cite_note-HP-254]

### Theater

[Clifford Odets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Odets)'s play *[Awake and Sing!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awake_and_Sing!)* is set in 1933 in the Bronx. The play, first produced at the [Belasco Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belasco_Theatre_\(Broadway\)) in 1935, concerns a poor family living in small quarters, the struggles of the controlling parents and the aspirations of their children.[^cite_note-260]

[René Marqués](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Marqu%C3%A9s)' *[The Oxcart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Carreta)* (1959) concerns a rural Puerto Rican family who immigrate to the Bronx for a better life.[^cite_note-261]

*[A Bronx Tale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Bronx_Tale_\(play\))* is an autobiographical [one-man show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-man_show) written and performed by [Chazz Palminteri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chazz_Palminteri). It is a coming-of-age story set in the Bronx. It premiered in Los Angeles in the 1980s and then played on Off-Broadway. After a film version involving Palminteri and Robert De Niro, Palminteri performed his one-man show on Broadway and on tour in 2007.[^cite_note-262]
