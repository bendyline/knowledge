The **Boeing P-8 Poseidon** is an American [maritime patrol and reconnaissance aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_patrol_aircraft) developed and produced by [Boeing Defense, Space & Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Defense,_Space_&_Security). It was developed for the [United States Navy](20518076.md) as a derivative of the civilian [Boeing 737 Next Generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_Next_Generation) airliner.

The P-8 operates in [anti-submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) (ASW), [anti-surface warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-surface_warfare) (ASUW), and [intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance](650971.md) (ISR) roles. It is armed with [torpedoes](31424.md), [Harpoon anti-ship missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpoon_\(missile\)), and other weapons, can drop and monitor [sonobuoys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonobuoy), and can operate in conjunction with other assets, including the [Northrop Grumman MQ-4C Triton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_MQ-4C_Triton) maritime surveillance [unmanned aerial vehicle](58900.md) (UAV).

In addition to the U.S. Navy, the P-8 is also operated by the [Indian Navy](604522.md), the [Royal Australian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Air_Force), the United Kingdom's [Royal Air Force](25679.md), the [Republic of Korea Navy](2284275.md), the [Royal Norwegian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Norwegian_Air_Force), the [Royal New Zealand Air Force](190903.md) and the [German Navy](12960.md). It also has been ordered by the [Republic of Singapore Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Singapore_Air_Force), the [Royal Canadian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Air_Force) and the [Royal Danish Air Force](548819.md).

## Development

### Origins

The [Lockheed P-3 Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_P-3_Orion), a turboprop ASW aircraft, had been in service with the United States Navy (USN) since 1962.[^cite_note-USNavyFactFile-4] In the 1980s, the USN began studies for a P-3 replacement, the range and endurance of which were reduced due to increasing weight and airframe fatigue life limitations. The specification required a new aircraft to have reduced operating and support costs. In 1989, Lockheed was awarded a fixed-price contract to develop the [P-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_P-7), but this was canceled the following year.[^cite_note-5]

In 2000, a second competition for a replacement began. [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin) submitted the [Orion 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_P-3_Orion#Variants), an updated new-build version of the P-3.[^cite_note-6] Boeing's proposal was based on its [737-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_Next_Generation#737-800) airliner.[^cite_note-7] [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems) offered a new-build version of the [Nimrod MRA4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems_Nimrod_MRA4), a British jet-powered maritime patrol aircraft. BAE withdrew from the competition in October 2002, recognizing that without a production partner based in the United States, the bid was politically unrealistic.[^cite_note-8] On 14 May 2004, Boeing was selected as the winner of the *Multimission Maritime Aircraft* program.[^cite_note-boeing_20040514-9]

In June 2004, the USN awarded a development contract to Boeing.[^cite_note-10] The project was planned to be for at least 108 airframes for the USN.[^cite_note-P-8_Navy_fact_file-11] Project value is expected to be worth at least \$15 billion. [Raytheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raytheon), [Northrop Grumman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman), [Spirit AeroSystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_AeroSystems), [GE Aviation Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Aviation_Systems), [Marshall Aerospace and Defence Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Aerospace_and_Defence_Group), [CFM International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFM_International), BAE Systems, and [Marotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marotta) were the major subcontractors.[^cite_note-12] In July 2004, the USN placed an order for five aircraft, with the first flight-test aircraft to be completed in 2009.[^cite_note-P-8_Navy_fact_file-11] On 30 March 2005, it was assigned the P-8A designation.[^cite_note-13]

### Design phase and testing



The rollout of the P-8 on 30 July 2009

The P-8 is to replace the P-3.[^cite_note-14] Initially, it was equipped with legacy systems with later upgrades to incorporate newer technology. The [Government Accountability Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Office) credited the incremental approach with keeping the project on schedule and on budget. The [Naval Air Systems Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Systems_Command) (NAVAIR) deleted the requirement for the P-8A to be equipped with [magnetic anomaly detection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_anomaly_detector) (MAD) equipment as a weight reduction measure, improving endurance. A hydrocarbon sensor detects fuel vapors from diesel-powered submarines and ships.[^cite_note-15]

The P-8's first flight was on 25 April 2009.[^cite_note-first_flight-3] The second and third P-8s had flown and were in flight testing in early August 2010.[^cite_note-T3_first_flight-16] On 11 August 2010, [low-rate production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_rate_initial_production) of the P-8 was approved.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] A P-8 released [sonobuoys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonobuoy) for the first time on 15 October 2010, dropping six in three separate low-altitude passes.[^cite_note-19] In 2011, the ice detection system was found to be defective due to the use of [counterfeit components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unapproved_aircraft_part); allegedly these parts were poorly refurbished and sold to subcontractor BAE Systems as new by a Chinese supplier.[^cite_note-20]



A P-8A flying alongside a Lockheed [P-3C Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-3C_Orion), close to [Naval Air Station Patuxent River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Patuxent_River), Maryland, 2010

On 4 March 2012, the first production P-8A was delivered to the USN, flying to [Naval Air Station Jacksonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Jacksonville), Florida, for training with the Fleet Replacement Squadron (FRS), [Patrol Squadron 30 (VP-30)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VP-30).[^cite_note-FI_first_production-2] On 24 September 2012, Boeing announced a \$1.9 billion (\~\$2.56 billion in 2024) order for 11 aircraft.[^cite_note-21] On 10 June 2013, a [U.S. Department of Defense](7279897.md) (DoD) Inspector General (IG) report recommended delaying full-rate production over a lack of key data to assess if the P-8 met operational requirements; additional tests were also needed to guarantee a 25-year lifespan.[^cite_note-22] Boeing executives dismissed the report, saying that the test program was on track.[^cite_note-23] In 2013, full-rate production was delayed until the P-8 could demonstrate it can survive its 25-year lifespan without structural fatigue, overcome deficiencies, track surface ships, and perform primary missions.[^cite_note-24]

On 24 June 2013, during weapons integration testing, the P-8 achieved a milestone by firing a live [AGM-84 Harpoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpoon_\(missile\)) anti-ship missile and scored a direct hit on a low-cost modular target.[^cite_note-25] On 1 July 2013, an initial operational test and evaluation (IOT\&E) report found that the P-8A was "operationally effective, operationally suitable, and ready for fleet introduction." Six test and nine low-rate initial production aircraft had been delivered at that point.[^cite_note-ready-26] On 31 July 2013, Boeing received a \$2.04 billion contract to build 13 P-8As in the fourth low-rate initial production lot, for a fleet of 37 aircraft by the end of 2016, and long-lead parts for 16 P-8As of the first full-rate production lot.[^cite_note-27]

In January 2014, Naval Air Systems Command proceeded with full-rate production of the P-8A. Increment 1 systems include persistent [anti-submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) (ASW) capabilities and an integrated sensor suite; in 2016, Increment 2 upgrades will add multi-static active coherent acoustics, an automated identification system, and high-altitude anti-submarine weapons.[^cite_note-28] Increment 3 in 2020 shall enable "net-enabled anti-surface warfare".[^cite_note-29]

In July 2014, Fred Smith, business development director for the P-8, noted that the program had: "saved \$2.1 billion on 2004 estimates of the cost of production... the aircraft is now selling for \$150 million, down from the forecasted \$216 million".[^cite_note-30] The halving of USN orders from 16 aircraft per year down to eight in 2015 due to the expiration of the [Bipartisan Budget Act of 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipartisan_Budget_Act_of_2013) was expected to be partially offset by commercial 737 sales and P-8 export sales.[^cite_note-31] The DoD wanted to follow a program template for the P-8 similar to the [Joint Strike Fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Strike_Fighter_Program) (JSF) program, with international cooperation from prospective users.[^cite_note-32]

### Derivatives

In 2010, Boeing proposed to replace the [United States Air Force](32090.md)'s (USAF) [E-8 Joint STARS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_E-8_Joint_STARS) fleet with a modified P-8 at the same cost Northrop Grumman proposed for re-engining and upgrading the E-8s.[^cite_note-pitches-33] The proposed P-8 Airborne Ground Surveillance (AGS) would integrate an [active electronically scanned array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_electronically_scanned_array) (AESA) radar, and have ground moving target indicator (GMTI) and [synthetic aperture radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_aperture_radar) (SAR) capabilities. A key feature was a pod-mounted radar on the lower centerline of the fuselage, positioned so the engine nacelles do not obstruct its line of sight. It reuses the P-8A's Raytheon [AN/APY-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/APY-10) multi-mission surface search radar. Two aft ventral fins increase stability.[^cite_note-pitches-33][^cite_note-PI_looks_to_sell-34]

In 2013, Boeing proposed repackaging some of the P-8's systems in the smaller and less expensive [Bombardier Challenger 600 series](937178.md) business jet, as the [Boeing Maritime Surveillance Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Maritime_Surveillance_Aircraft) (MSA).[^cite_note-CASR201307-35] In 2014, Boeing also offered a JSTARS replacement based on the [737-700](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_Next_Generation#737-700), rather than the P-8's 737-800.[^cite_note-36]

## Design



US Navy pilots in the cockpit of a P-8 in 2019

The P-8 is a 737-800ERX, roughly similar to the 737-800 commercial passenger aircraft and the USN's 737-700-based [C-40 Clipper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_C-40_Clipper) transport aircraft, but with several modifications to make it suitable for its military service role. Many of the changes reflect the need for the aircraft to operate at lower altitudes and be capable of more aggressive maneuvering than a commercial aircraft. However, the changes were kept in line with the existing assembly process. Boeing 737 [fuselage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuselage) builder Spirit AeroSystems added structural strength to the airframe, while adding a short [bomb bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomb_bay) for torpedoes and other stores behind the wing.[^cite_note-p8_bravado-37]

The aircraft is assembled at the [Boeing Renton Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Renton_Factory) with the stronger wings from the 737-900.[^cite_note-Boeing_P-8A_Specs-38] The aircraft uses [raked wingtips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raked_wingtips) similar to those fitted to the [Boeing 767-400ER](4165.md#767-400ER), instead of the [blended winglets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wingtip_device#Blended_winglets) available on 737NG variants.[^cite_note-39] To combat icing on the raked wingtips, horizontal stabilizers and vertical stabilizers, the aircraft is outfitted with [electro-mechanical expulsion deicing systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_protection_system#Electro-mechanical).[^cite_note-p8_bravado-37] In the cockpit, changes were made to the flight control and alerting systems, allowing for an increased [bank angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_angle), a more responsive [autothrottle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autothrottle), and the elimination of [audible warnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_proximity_warning_system) during low-altitude operations.[^cite_note-p8_bravado-37] In order to power additional onboard electronics, the P-8 has a 180 kVA electric generator on each [CFM International CFM56](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFM_International_CFM56) engine, replacing the 90 kVA generator of civilian 737s; this required the redesigning of the nacelles and their wing mountings.[^cite_note-p8_bravado-37] The Navy says that compared to the P-3, the P-8 has a smoother flight experience, subjecting crews to less turbulence and fumes, allowing them to concentrate better on missions.[^cite_note-40]



Crew at work stations inside a P-8

After the aircraft roll off the assembly line, five operator stations (two [naval flight officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Flight_Officer) plus three enlisted Aviation Warfare Operators/naval aircrewman) are mounted in a sideways row, along the port side of the cabin. Other than one large window on each side of the forward cabin for two observers, none of the other crew stations have windows.[^cite_note-p8_bravado-37] A short bomb bay for torpedoes and other stores opens behind the wing. The P-8 is to be equipped with the High-Altitude Anti-Submarine Warfare Weapon Capability (HAAWC) Air Launch Accessory (ALA), turning a [Mark 54 torpedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_54_Lightweight_Torpedo) into a [glide bomb](233100.md) for deploying from up to 30,000 ft (9,100 m).[^cite_note-mad_drone-41]

The P-8 features the Raytheon APY-10 multi-mission surface search radar.[^cite_note-Raytheon_APY-10-42] Unlike the preceding P-3, most versions of the P-8 lack a MAD, but the P-8I is equipped with a MAD at India's request.[^cite_note-43] Various sensor data are combined via [data fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_fusion) software to track targets.[^cite_note-44] Following the cancellation of Lockheed Martin's [Aerial Common Sensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_Common_Sensor) project, Boeing proposed a signals intelligence variant of the P-8 for the USN's requirement.[^cite_note-fi_20060126-45] During the P-8A Increment 2 upgrade in 2016, the [Littoral Surveillance Radar System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_Surveillance_Radar_System) (LSRS) will be replaced by the [Advanced Airborne Sensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Airborne_Sensor) radar.[^cite_note-aas_2014-46]

In U.S. service, the P-8A is complemented by the [MQ-4C Triton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_MQ-4C_Triton) unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) which provides continuous surveillance. In January 2015, BAE Systems was awarded a contract for the USN's High Altitude ASW (HAASW) Unmanned Targeting Air System (UTAS) program to develop a sub-hunting UAV equipped with a MAD for launching from the P-8.[^cite_note-mad_drone-41]



Naval aircrewman at one of the two large observer's windows, the only crew stations with windows.

The P-8 cannot use the Navy's typical [probe and drogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probe_and_drogue) in-flight refueling method, instead using a [flying boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_boom) receptacle on the upper-forward fuselage, making it, like the USN's [E-6 Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-6_Mercury) aircraft, reliant on US Air Force (USAF) [KC-135 Stratotanker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC-135_Stratotanker), [KC-10 Extender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC-10_Extender) and [KC-46 Pegasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC-46_Pegasus) aircraft for in-flight refueling. In April 2017, the USAF [459th Air Refueling Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/459th_Air_Refueling_Wing) worked with the Naval Air Systems Command to certify operationally the P-8 for in-flight refueling.[^cite_note-P8Refueling-47] For extended endurance, the P-8 is equipped with six auxiliary fuel tanks.[^cite_note-48]

## Operational history

### United States

In February 2012, the P-8 made its mission debut during "[Bold Alligator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bold_Alligator)" 2012, an annual littoral warfare exercise.[^cite_note-49] In April 2012, it took part in [Exercise Joint Warrior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_Joint_Warrior), flying out of [RAF Lossiemouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Lossiemouth).[^cite_note-50] During [RIMPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIMPAC) 2012 in the Hawaiian area, two P-8As participated in 24 scenarios as part of Air Test and Evaluation Squadron One ([VX-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_Test_and_Evaluation_Force)) while forward deployed to [Marine Corps Base Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Base_Hawaii).[^cite_note-51] On 29 November 2013, its inaugural deployment began when six aircraft and 12 air crews of squadron [VP-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VP-16) departed its home station of NAS Jacksonville, Florida, for [Kadena Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadena_Air_Base) in Okinawa, Japan.[^cite_note-InauguralDeployment-52] This deployment was a pre-planned regional re-balancing action, but occurred shortly after China's establishment of the [East China Sea Air Defense Identification Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Defense_Identification_Zone_\(East_China_Sea\)), heightening tensions.[^cite_note-53]

During early exercises and the Japanese deployment, the P-8 reportedly suffered radar, sensor integration, and data transfer problems, leading to more testing. In 2012–3, the US government's [Director, Operational Test and Evaluation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director,_Operational_Test_and_Evaluation) (DOTE) evaluated the P-8A Increment 1, and reported that it was effective for small-area and cued ASW search, localization and attack missions, but lacked the [P-3C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-3C)'s broad-area ASW acoustic search capability; the Mk 54 torpedoes were of limited use against evasive targets. The P-8A was also effective at ASuW search, detection and classification in all-weather at short to medium ranges for all surface vessels and at longer ranges for larger vessels, yet was not effective for Intelligence, Surveillance and Reconnaissance mission due to various issues including no high-resolution SAR capability. It did have better range, speed, and reliability than older aircraft.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] DOTE concluded that it was not deployment ready.[^cite_note-56] Pentagon acquisition under-secretary [Frank Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Kendall) said of the report that, although its findings are factual, it ignored future capability upgrades for ASW and surveillance.[^cite_note-57]

A second squadron, [VP-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VP-5), completed its transition to the P-8 in August 2013.[^cite_note-InauguralDeployment-52] During mid-2014, a pair of P-8s were dispatched to [Perth, Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth,_Australia) for two months for an international search for the missing [Malaysia Airlines Flight 370](42142305.md).[^cite_note-58] On 2 October 2015, USN P-8s stationed at Naval Air Station Jacksonville, Florida, alongside U.S. Coast Guard [HC-144A Ocean Sentry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HC-144A_Ocean_Sentry), [HC-130H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_HC-130#USCG_HC-130H) and USAF Reserve [HC-130P Combat Shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_HC-130#USAF_HC-130P_Combat_Shadow) aircraft, searched the Eastern Caribbean Sea for the missing [SS El Faro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_El_Faro) cargo ship that sank on 1 October in the Category 3 [Hurricane Joaquin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Joaquin) near Crooked Island in the [Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahamas).[^cite_note-59] On 20 February 2018, a P-8 of [Patrol Squadron Eight (VP-8)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VP-8) rescued three [fishermen](242592.md) whose vessel had been adrift in the South [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) for eight days, deploying a search and rescue (SAR) kit containing supplies and communications equipment, the first time that a P-8 deployed a SAR kit in a real operation.[^cite_note-60]

On 19 August 2014, a Chinese [Shenyang J-11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenyang_J-11) fighter came within 30 feet (9.1 m) of a USN P-8A of VP-5 about 135 miles (217 km) east of [Hainan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainan_Island) while patrolling the [South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea_dispute). The J-11 flew past the P-8's nose and performed a [barrel roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrel_roll) at close proximity.[^cite_note-61] A Pentagon spokesperson said the J-11's unit had made close intercepts earlier that year.[^cite_note-62] The U.S. sent a diplomatic note to China about the behavior of the Chinese fighter groups' commander.[^cite_note-63] China stated that the claims were "totally groundless", and that the root cause was U.S. surveillance of China.[^cite_note-64] In November 2016, a Russian [Su-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su-30) fighter intercepted a P-8 over the [Black Sea](3386.md), coming within 5 feet (1.5 m) of it, forcing the P-8 through its [jet wash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_turbulence), causing "a 15-degree roll and violent turbulence".[^cite_note-65]



A US Navy P-8 landing at [Kadena AB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadena_AB)

USN P-8s routinely rotate through bases of allies.[^cite_note-66] In September 2014, the Malaysian government offered the use of bases in [East Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia) for P-8s, but no flights have yet been approved.[^cite_note-67] On 7 December 2015, P-8s were deployed to Singapore as part of a Defense Cooperation Agreement between the US and Singapore for "fighting terrorism and piracy."[^cite_note-68] China criticized the Singapore deployment as "regional militarization by the U.S."[^cite_note-69] The third detachment of two P-8s based in [Paya Lebar Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paya_Lebar_Air_Base), Singapore, participated in naval military drills with the Singapore Armed Forces (SAF) in mid 2016.[^cite_note-70]

On 20 November 2023, a USN P-8A assigned to [VP-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VP-4)[^cite_note-71] [overshot the runway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Boeing_P-8_Poseidon_runway_overrun) while landing in the rain at [MCAS Kaneohe Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MCAS_Kaneohe_Bay) and ended up in the water. None of the nine crew on board were injured.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] The USN had hopes to repair the aircraft and return it to operational status.[^cite_note-74] The aircraft was floated and pulled from the sea on 2 December 2023.[^cite_note-75] The USN later determined that restoring the aircraft was not cost-effective and subsequently scrapped it.[^cite_note-76]

A USN P-8A flew through the [Taiwan Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Strait) on 17 April 2024, asserting navigational rights amid tensions with China over Taiwan's sovereignty. This followed the first talks between US and Chinese defense chiefs since 2022, aimed at easing regional tensions.[^cite_note-77]

Boeing P-8 Poseidon patrol aircraft were also used to intercept tankers during the [United States naval blockade of Iran](82937256.md) as part of the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-wsj-78]

### India



An Indian Navy P-8I in flight

In January 2008, Boeing proposed the P-8I, a customized export variant of the P-8A, for the [Indian Navy](604522.md).[^cite_note-79] The P-8I has a version of the Raytheon APY-10 multi-mission surface search radar that meets export requirements.[^cite_note-Raytheon_export-80] It also features two components not fitted on the P-8A, a [Telephonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephonics_Corporation) APS-143 OceanEye aft radar and a MAD.[^cite_note-sp31m14-81] The P-8I also features multiple Indian-origin components which include [Bharat Electronics Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharat_Electronics_Limited) (BEL) Data Link II communication suite, BEL-developed [IFF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identification_friend_or_foe) Interrogator system, Avantel [mobile satellite service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_satellite_service) (MSS)[^cite_note-\:0-82] and [Electronics Corporation of India Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics_Corporation_of_India_Limited) (ECIL) speech secrecy system. The Data Link II will allow the P-8I to exchange tactical data between [Indian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Naval_Air_Arm) aircraft, ships and shore establishments. These systems will be integrated in Boeing Renton Factory.[^cite_note-\:1-83][^cite_note-\:2-84][^cite_note-\:3-85]

#### First contract

On 1 January 2009, India's [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(India\)) signed a US\$2.1 billion (\~\$2.98 billion in 2024) agreement with Boeing for eight P-8Is to replace the Indian Navy's aging [Tupolev Tu-142](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-142)M maritime surveillance turboprops. The contract was signed after negotiations and clearance for the same from the [Cabinet Committee on Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Committee_on_Security) (CCS). While the P-8I contract was a direct agreement with Boeing, some end-user verifications are yet to be clarified with the [US Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Government).[^cite_note-TOI090105-86] Boeing will deliver the first of eight P-8I aircraft within 48 months of contract signing.[^cite_note-\:1-83][^cite_note-\:2-84] The agreement included an additional purchase of four aircraft through [Option Clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_\(finance\)).[^cite_note-\:6-87][^cite_note-\:4-88] It was Boeing's first military sale to India and the P-8's first international customer.[^cite_note-Boeing_sell_8_P-8Is-89]

India has purchased 21 AGM-84L Harpoon Block II missiles and 32 Mark 54 Lightweight Torpedoes All-Up-Round for the P-8I.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

In April 2010, BEL delivered the first Data Link II systems to Boeing a month ahead of schedule. BEL was contracted to supply them in August 2009 and is expected to complete the delivery by late 2011.[^cite_note-\:3-85][^cite_note-93] In December 2010, BEL supplied its IFF Interrogator system to Boeing.[^cite_note-\:1-83][^cite_note-\:2-84] On 26 December 2011, Avantel delivered 12 sets of their MSS-based communication systems to Boeing, at a cost of \$1.8 million, for the Indian Navy's P-8I program. The system had been examined and approved by the [CEMILAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEMILAC).[^cite_note-\:0-82]

The production of first P-8I fuselage began on 7 December 2010 at Spirit AeroSystems facility in [Wichita, Kansas](114932.md) with the production of a bonded aluminum panel which would be fitted to the fuselages' upper lobe to support an antenna. This marked the progress of the program from the design phase to production phase.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] The fuselage was then delivered to Boeing Renton Facility for final assembly line on 29 May 2011. The installation of wings and engines followed. The final assembly begun by 21 June 2011.[^cite_note-\:6-87][^cite_note-\:5-96]

On 29 September 2011, the first P-8I completed its initial flight from [Boeing Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Field). The aircraft took off at 12\:02 pm local time and landed after a two hour and 31 minute long sortie. Multiple airborne systems checks including engine accelerations & decelerations and autopilot flight modes were conducted before the aircraft was take to an altitude of up to 41,000 ft (12,000 m). Boeing would now begin mission systems installation and checkout work on this aircraft at its Renton facility.[^cite_note-\:4-88] In July 2012, Boeing conducted another P-8I flight testing from the same airfield. The sortie was 3 hours 49 minutes long where the aircraft fulfilled all its requirements. Further flight testing would be conducted in a US Navy range in the [Neah Bay, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neah_Bay,_Washington) and a joint US-Canadian range. The first aircraft was expected to be delivered by January 2013 followed by the remaining units by 2016.[^cite_note-\:5-96]

On 19 December 2012, the first P-8I was handed over to an Indian naval team at Boeing's facility. When the first three aircraft will be delivered, all of these will flown to India at once. The delivery of the other five would be completed by 2015.[^cite_note-\:7-97] It was inducted into the Indian Navy on 15 May 2013 at [INS *Rajali*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Rajali) in [Tamil Nadu](29918.md).[^cite_note-98] The second aircraft was delivered on 15 November 2013. Meanwhile, the first aircraft completed its weapons trials by the Indian Navy which included firing a Harpoon anti-ship missile and dropping a torpedo. The aircraft's acceptance trials was still underway while the same for the second aircraft will now commence within months.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-hin16n-100]

#### Optional clause contract

In October 2010, the Defence Acquisition Council (DAC), under the Indian Ministry of Defence, approved the purchase of four additional P-8Is.[^cite_note-101] As of December 2012, the negotiations for four P-8I was underway. This would add to the capability to the highest of Indian Navy's three-tier maritime surveillance grid.[^cite_note-\:7-97] On 1 July 2016, the Cabinet Committee on Security (CCS) cleared the procurement worth around \$1.1 billion. The deal would be signed within few days while the first aircraft was to be delivered within 50 months or three years of signing the contract.[^cite_note-102] The deal was finally signed on 27 July 2016.[^cite_note-\:8-103][^cite_note-104]

The first was delivered to Indian naval air station [INS *Hansa*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Hansa), [Dabolim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dabolim), [Goa](40010153.md) on 19 November 2020,[^cite_note-105] followed by the second on 13 July 2021,[^cite_note-106] the third on 18 October 2021[^cite_note-107] and the last on 23 February 2022.[^cite_note-108] The last two aircraft arrived at the air station on 30 December 2021. The aircraft was welcomed by a [MiG-29K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-29K) formation. This marked the maiden operation of P-8I from the air station.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-\:11-110] The second squadron of P-8I based at Hansa, [INAS 316](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INAS_316) *Condors*, was commissioned on 29 March 2022. During its commissioning, the [commanding officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commanding_officer) was [Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander) Amit Mohapatra. The squadron will employ the four aircraft received as part of the Option Clause contract.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

In April 2020, DSCA approved the sale of 10 [AGM-84L Harpoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-84_Harpoon) missiles along with containers, spare and repair parts, support and test equipment for the P-8I fleet to the Indian Navy at a value of \$93 million. The possible sale of 16 aircraft-launched Mark 54 Lightweight Torpedo all up rounds and 3 Mk 54 Exercise Torpedoes for its additional P-8I fleet at a cost of \$63 million was also cleared. These sales were for the second batch of four P-8Is.[^cite_note-BBC_Ukr_Harpoon-114][^cite_note-115]

#### Second contract

In 2011, India planned to order twelve more P-8Is at a later date; in 2019, this was cut to eight to ten due to a limited budget.[^cite_note-India_induct_24-116][^cite_note-117]

On 28 November 2019, the Defence Acquisition Council (DAC) approved the procurement of six additional P-8Is.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-india-2019plan2-119] By 27 July 2020, reports revealed that Indian Ministry of Defence has sent [Letter of Request](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_of_Request) (LoR) to the US Government for the procurement at an estimated cost of \$1.8 billion. The LoR was forwarded to the [Defense Security Cooperation Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Security_Cooperation_Agency) (DSCA) and the proposal featured on a notice by the agency under the US [Foreign Military Sales](30857635.md) (FMS) program. The DSCA is expected to respond with a Letter of Acceptance (LoA) with the deal expected to be signed by early 2021.[^cite_note-120]

On 30 April 2021, the [US Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Department_of_Defense) (DoD) cleared the sale of six P-8Is and related equipment to India at an estimated cost of \$2.42 billion. The numbers were reduced from ten due to budget constraints. The related equipment includes eight Multifunctional Information Distribution System-Joint Tactical Radio Systems 5 (MIDS-JTRS 5) (6 installed, 2 spares); forty-two AN/AAR-54 Missile Warning Sensors (36 installed, 6 spares); and fourteen LN-251 with Embedded Global Positioning Systems (GPS)/Inertial Navigations Systems (EGIs) (12 installed, 2 spares). The DSCA informed about the clearance to the [US Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Congress).[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123]

By 2025, the price of the possible sale of six additional P-8Is were increased by 50% in view of the supply chain costs.[^cite_note-124] On 7 August 2025, *[Reuters](18998750.md)* quoted three Indian officials and reported that multiple planned defence procurements from the US — including the case for six additional aircraft — had been halted,[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126] though the claims were rejected by the Indian Defence Ministry as "false and fabricated" the following day.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] The US Department of Defense and Boeing officials visited India between 16 and 19 September[^cite_note-130] for detailed discussions on their current offers on the additional procurement of P-8I aircraft made to the Indian government. The US delegation included officials from the undersecretary of defence for policy, Navy International Programs Office (NIPO), Maritime Patrol and Reconnaissance Aircraft Program Office (PMA 290), and the DSCA. While the NIPO strengthens global maritime partnerships by facilitating the export and transfer of capabilities that advance U.S. strategic security interests, the PMA-290 is responsible for the acquisition, sustainment, and delivery of maritime patrol aircraft. The deal cost could range around \$4 billion.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133]

The \$3 billion deal for six P-8I aircraft is close to being sealed as of 4 February 2026. The defence ministry is expected to clear the deal shortly followed by the [Cabinet Committee on Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Committee_on_Security) which is the final approval required before the contract is signed.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] The Defence Acquisition Council (DAC) is expected to meet in the third week of February where it will take up the proposal. The deal is expected to be signed in the [FY](5752099.md)2026-27.[^cite_note-136]

The deal was granted Acceptance of Necessity (AoN) by the DAC on 12 February 2026.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138]

#### Operations

In 2014, several Indian Navy P-8Is conducted search operations for the missing Malaysia Airlines Flight 370.[^cite_note-sp31m14-81] The Indian Navy inducted the first squadron in November 2015.[^cite_note-139] The aircraft, based in INS Rajali, was deployed during the search operations of the wreckage after the [2016 Indian Air Force An-32 crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Indian_Air_Force_An-32_crash) in the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md).[^cite_note-\:8-103] P-8Is were deployed in the [2017 Doklam Standoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doklam#2017_Doklam_standoff) between the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) and China's [People's Liberation Army](66890.md). Indian Navy P-8Is also monitored [Pakistani Army](1965925.md) units during the [2019 Pulwama standoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_India%E2%80%93Pakistan_border_skirmishes).[^cite_note-140] On 4 June 2019, one of the P-8I aircraft was deployed in during the search and rescue operations following [2019 Indian Air Force An-32 crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Indian_Air_Force_An-32_crash).[^cite_note-141]

In August 2024, it was announced that the Indian Navy's P-8I fleet will be maintained by Air India Engineering Services Limited (AIESL), a subsidiary of [Air India](26457880.md), which signed a deal with [Boeing](18933266.md) for receiving training for its personnel. AIESL had already overhauled the landing gear of the entire fleet.[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144] The Indian Navy has also signed an agreement with the [Royal Australian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Air_Force) for [KC-30A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC-30A)'s to potentially refuel Indian P-8Is during long-range operations.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]

During Exercise Konkan 2025 conducted between [UK Carrier Strike Group 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Carrier_Strike_Group#Carrier_Strike_Group_25_\(Operation_Highmast\)) and [INS *Vikrant*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Vikrant_\(2013\))-led [Carrier Battle Group](7772.md#India), included an anti-submarine warfare operation. Royal Navy [Merlin Mk2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlin_Mk2) helicopters operating from [HMS *Prince of Wales* (R09)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Prince_of_Wales_\(R09\)) and [HMS *Richmond* (F239)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Richmond_\(F239\)) and Indian Navy P-8I Neptune aircraft were deployed during the operation, while an Indian submarine was the target.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148] Indian P-8Is also coordinated search and rescue efforts for the crew of [IRIS Dena](74329424.md), sunk by a US submarine off the coast of [Sri Lanka](26750.md) during the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-149]

The [Defence Research and Development Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Research_and_Development_Organisation) (DRDO) and the Indian Navy conducted four trials of its indigenous Air Droppable Container (ADC-150) from P-8I aircraft off the coast of [Goa](40010153.md) in early 2026. The ability to employ the ADC-150 from P-8I will facilitate delivery of urgent critical stores, equipment and medical assistance to vessels at sea.[^cite_note-150] On 27 March 2026, the Indian defence ministry signed a contract worth [₹](490783.md)431 [crore](121420.md) (US\$45 million) with Boeing India Defense Private Ltd, a wholly owned Indian subsidiary of Boeing for depot level inspection of the fleet. The project is under the Buy Indian category with 100% indigenous content at an in-country [MRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_maintenance) facility.[^cite_note-151]

The P-8I was deployed during [Operation Sindoor](79896622.md) and conducted Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance (ISR) operations providing inputs to the Indian Air Force and Indian Army to strike their targets. Captain Saurbabh Kumar of the [Indian Navy](604522.md) was awarded the Nao Sena Medal (Gallantry) for ISR mission and for evading the Chinese Beyond Visual Range air to air missile fired by Pakistan while on his mission. The missile is supposed to be the [PL-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PL-15) which is the only Chinese BVRAAM in operation with Pakistan.[^cite_note-152] The citation for Commander Kumar reads, "The Officer was deployed at forward airbase to undertake ISR operations. These missions were flown towards updating enemy targets of interests to IAF and IA. As the pilot in command he undertook various missions including intelligence gathering, proving critical inputs to further own operations and plans. The aircraft was experiencing bad weather and a GPS denied environment while flying over the mountainous terrain. When warning of an enemy missing closing in on the aircraft was received, he quickly reacted and outmaneuvered the enemy missile, ensuring mission accomplishment along with the safety of the crew and the aircraft."[^cite_note-153]

### Australia



An Australian P-8A in November 2020

On 20 July 2007, the [Australian Minister for Defence](593152.md) announced that the P-8A was the preferred aircraft to replace the [Royal Australian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Air_Force) fleet of Lockheed [AP-3C Orions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP-3C_Orion) in conjunction with a then yet-to-be-selected unmanned aerial vehicle. The last AP-3C was scheduled to be retired in 2018, after nearly 30 years of service.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155] In March 2009, Australia's Chief of Air Force stated that the RAAF planned to introduce the P-8A in 2016.[^cite_note-156]

In October 2012, Australia formalized its participation, committing [A\$](81215.md)73.9m (US\$81.1m) in an agreement with the USN.[^cite_note-157] In July 2013, [Air Marshal Geoff Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Brown_\(RAAF_officer\)), head of the RAAF, said Australia was considering buying more P-8As and fewer MQ-4C Triton UAVs than earlier planned.[^cite_note-158] On 21 February 2014, Prime Minister [Tony Abbott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Abbott) announced the intention to procure eight P-8As plus options for four more; entry into service is planned for 2021.[^cite_note-CT20140221-159]

In July 2014, negotiations commenced between Boeing and the US Department of Defense to integrate the AGM-84 Harpoon Block 1G anti-ship missile onto the P-8A on Australia's behalf.[^cite_note-160] In August 2014, the USN concluded an advanced acquisition contract on the first four of up to 12 P-8As to be bought by Australia, with delivery expected from 2017.[^cite_note-161] In January 2016, Australia ordered a further four P-8As.[^cite_note-162] The 2016 Defence White Paper stated that eight P-8As would be in service in the early 2020s and that 15 P-8As are planned for by the late 2020s.[^cite_note-Aust_White_Paper-163] Including support facilities, the first group of eight aircraft's total cost is estimated at \$3.6 billion (AU\$4 billion).[^cite_note-164]

The RAAF accepted its first P-8A on 27 September 2016;[^cite_note-RAAF_First_Poseidon_off_the_rank-165] it arrived in Australia on 14 November.[^cite_note-166] The RAAF had received 12 P-8As by 13 December 2019.[^cite_note-RAAF_receives_12th-167] The Australian Government approved ordering two additional aircraft on 30 December 2020. The option to acquire a 15th aircraft may not be taken up.[^cite_note-RAAF_14_ordered-168] The RAAF's fourteenth and final P-8 was delivered in May 2026.[^cite_note-14th_RAAF_P-18A_arrives-169]

According to the Australian Defence Minister, [Richard Marles](4386805.md), in May 2022 a Chinese [J-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J-16) is alleged to have flown alongside a RAAF P-8A, deploying [flares](37708.md) and [chaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaff_\(countermeasure\)). One piece of chaff is alleged to have been ingested into the P-8's engine.[^cite_note-170] On 20 October 2025 Australian Defence Minister Richard Marles disclosed that a Chinese Su-35 fighter jet had released flares "very close" to a P-8A aircraft doing routine surveillance in international airspace over the South China Sea the previous day, stating also that the plane was not damaged.[^cite_note-171]

### United Kingdom



The Royal Air Force's first P-8

In August 2012, it was reported that Boeing saw the [United Kingdom](31717.md) as a market for the P-8, following the cancellation of the [Nimrod MRA4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimrod_MRA4) in 2010.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173] On 23 November 2015, the UK announced its intention to order nine P-8s in the [Strategic Defence and Security Review 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Defence_and_Security_Review_2015), that would be based at RAF Lossiemouth in Scotland, to protect the UK's [nuclear deterrent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_deterrent) and aircraft carriers and for [search-and-rescue](172599.md) and [overland reconnaissance](20338498.md).[^cite_note-DefenceReview-174][^cite_note-175]

On 25 March 2016, the U.S. State Department approved a proposed [Foreign Military Sale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Military_Sale) to the UK for up to nine P-8s and associated support.[^cite_note-176] During an April 2016 tour of US anti-submarine capabilities, the British defense procurement minister stated that the [Royal Air Force](25679.md) would initially operate the P-8 with U.S. weapons, with the option to use British weapons later.[^cite_note-177] It was not initially clear whether the UK would have access to future ground-surveillance capabilities developed for the P-8.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179] On 11 July 2016, Boeing announced the signing of a \$3.87 billion (£3 billion) contract for nine P-8s and support infrastructure, in three production lots over ten years, with deliveries commencing in 2019.[^cite_note-180][^cite_note-2016_RAFbuy-181]

The RAF gave the aircraft the [service name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_military_aircraft_designation_systems) Poseidon MRA Mk1.[^cite_note-Royal_Air_Force_2018-182] They are operated by [120 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._120_Squadron_RAF) and [201 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._201_Squadron_RAF).[^cite_note-p8-announcement-183][^cite_note-184] The first Poseidon MRA Mk1 (*ZP801*) made its initial flight on 13 July 2019.[^cite_note-185] The UK took delivery of the first aircraft, named *Pride of Moray*, at Boeing's Seattle facility on 29 October. It arrived at [Kinloss Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinloss_Barracks) in February 2020 before moving to RAF Lossiemouth in October 2020, along with *ZP802* which was delivered on 13 March 2020.[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188] The RAF declared the P-8 had reached [initial operating capability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_operating_capability) (IOC) on 1 April 2020.[^cite_note-RAFIOC-189] The final ordered aircraft arrived at RAF Lossiemouth in January 2022.[^cite_note-190]

### New Zealand



A New Zealand P-8A during [Warbirds over Wanaka 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warbirds_over_Wanaka#2024), with an open [bomb bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomb_bay)

The [ Defence Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Defence_\(New_Zealand\)) [Chris Penks'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Penk) office also said it had no further comment beyond NZDF's statement.[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208]

### Canada

Boeing identified that the [Royal Canadian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Air_Force)'s fleet of [CP-140 Auroras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP-140_Aurora) (Canadian variant of the P-3 Orion) would begin to reach the end of their service life by 2025. In 2015, Boeing offered the [Challenger MSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Challenger_600), a smaller and cheaper aircraft based on the Bombardier Challenger 650 integrating many of the P-8's sensors and equipment, to complement but not replace the CP-140s. Boeing also offered the P-8A with modifications specific to Canadian operations to replace the Aurora.[^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225]

In 2019, Canada announced the start of a project to replace its CP-140s, valued at greater than [CA\$](101846.md)5 billion and named "Canadian Multimission Aircraft Project". The Canadian Armed Forces requirements call for a crewed, long-range platform, capable of providing [C4](7092305.md), ISR, and ASW with the ability to engage/control and to fully integrate with other ISR and ASW assets.[^cite_note-226] In 2022, Boeing officially announced it would offer the P-8A in the Canadian Multi-Mission Aircraft project[^cite_note-227] with CAE, GE Aviation Canada, IMP Aerospace & Defence, KF Aerospace, Honeywell Aerospace Canada and Raytheon Canada.[^cite_note-228] In March 2023, Canada announced the submission of a Letter of Request via the U.S. government's Foreign Military Sales program on the acquisition of up to 16 P-8As.[^cite_note-229] On 27 June 2023, the State Department approved a possible \$5.9 billion sale of up to 16 P-8As and associated equipment, pending approval by Congress.[^cite_note-230]

On 30 November 2023, Defence Minister [Bill Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Blair_\(politician\)) announced that Canada would purchase up to 16 P-8As for CA\$10.4 billion (US\$7.7 billion).[^cite_note-Canada_Buys_upto_16_P-8As-231] The cost breakdown includes CA\$8 billion for the aircraft and related equipment while another CA\$2.4 billion is for simulators, infrastructure and weapons.[^cite_note-Ottawa_Citizen_article-232] Delivery of the type is expected between 2026 and 2027, with full operational capability anticipated by 2033.[^cite_note-Canada_Buys_upto_16_P-8As-231] The fleet is to be based in Nova Scotia at [14 Wing Greenwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFB_Greenwood), and in British Columbia at [19 Wing Comox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFB_Comox).[^cite_note-Canada_Gov_Press_Release-233]

### Singapore

On 3 March 2025, Singapore's Defence Minister [Ng Eng Hen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng_Eng_Hen) announced that replacement for its Fokker 50 Maritime Patrol Aircraft is to be announced soon, with the Boeing P-8A and [Airbus C295](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_C295) under consideration.[^cite_note-234] The decision to order the P-8 was confirmed in September 2025 after a meeting between Singapore's Defence Minister [Chan Chun Sing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Chun_Sing) and [U.S. Secretary of Defense](43998.md) [Pete Hegseth](34076202.md).[^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237]

### Potential operators

#### Brazil

On 23 September 2022, Boeing's Latin America director, Tim Flood, presented the capabilities of the P-8 to the [Brazilian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Air_Force) commander, General [Carlos de Almeida Baptista Júnior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_de_Almeida_Baptista_J%C3%BAnior). According to Baptista Júnior, "the discussions have a great importance to prospect the future of FAB's maritime patrol aviation", as part of a plan to replace its P-3AMs currently in service.[^cite_note-Brazil-238][^cite_note-Brazil2-239]

#### Denmark

According to a leaked defence list in the Danish newspaper Altinget in 2023, Denmark is considering acquiring P-8s in the near future.[^cite_note-240] In June 2025, Denmark signed a cooperation agreement with Norway to lease Norwegian P-8 aircraft to meet immediate maritime patrol needs.[^cite_note-241]

On 15 September 2025, the Danish Minister of Defence Troels Lund Poulsen announced in a TV2 interview, that the government wishes to buy "a number" of P-8s worth at least DKK 10 billion.[^cite_note-242] On 29 December 2025, Denmark received approval to buy three P-8 aircraft and related equipment in a deal worth \$1.8 billion.[^cite_note-243] On 7 July 2026, the Danish Ministry of Defence confirmed plans to buy two P-8A aircraft.[^cite_note-244]

#### Italy

Italy indicated interest in purchasing P-8s, with fleet support provided by [Alitalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alitalia), in 2004.[^cite_note-avweek_20040621-245] However, in December 2008, Italy announced the purchase of four [ATR 72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_72) aircraft to replace its aging [Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breguet_Atlantic) maritime patrol fleet,[^cite_note-246] possibly as a temporary solution because Italy remained interested in the P-8.[^cite_note-247]

#### NATO

In April 2019, Boeing was reported to be in exploratory talks with various [NATO](21133.md) allies to offer the P-8 as a NATO-shared interim solution to provide European allies with its capabilities until domestic capabilities could be secured.[^cite_note-248]

#### Saudi Arabia

In 2017, Boeing announced it had signed several agreements with [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), which intends to order the P-8.[^cite_note-249] The [International Institute for Strategic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Strategic_Studies) reported in 2019 that a Saudi order for the type was still pending.[^cite_note-250]

#### Turkey

In 2016, Turkey indicated that it planned to acquire a new MMA aircraft to supplement existing assets, the P-8A being the main candidate based on the required performance.[^cite_note-251]

### Failed bids

#### Malaysia

In December 2017, the [Royal Malaysian Air Force](1137609.md) had shortlisted four aircraft types to replace its aging fleet of [Beechcraft Super King Air](9023483.md) used as maritime patrol aircraft; the [Airbus C-295](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_C-295), the P-8, [ATR 72 MP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_72#Other_versions), and the [CASA/IPTN CN-235](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASA/IPTN_CN-235).[^cite_note-252] In May 2023, Malaysia selected the ATR-72MP as its replacement aircraft.[^cite_note-253]

*Data from* *United States Navy*,[^cite_note-USN_Fact_File_P-8-277][^cite_note-278] *Boeing*,[^cite_note-Boeing_P-8A_Specs-38] and *others*[^cite_note-p8_bravado-37]

**General characteristics**

- **Crew:** Flight crew: 2; Mission crew: 7

- **Capacity:** 19,800 lb (9,000 kg)

- **Length:** 129 ft 5 in (39.47 m)

- **Wingspan:** 123 ft 6 in (37.64 m)

- **Height:** 42 ft 1 in (12.83 m)

- **Empty weight:** 138,300 lb (62,730 kg)

- **Max takeoff weight:** 189,200 lb (85,820 kg)

- **Powerplant:** 2 × [CFM56-7B27A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFM_International_CFM56) turbofans, 27,300 lbf (121 kN) thrust each

**Performance**

- **Maximum speed:** 564 mph (907 km/h, 490 kn)

- **Cruise speed:** 509 mph (815 km/h, 440 kn)

- **Combat range:** 1,383 mi (2,225 km, 1,200 nmi) radius with 4 hours on station for anti-submarine warfare mission

  [^cite_note-279]

- **Ferry range:** 5,200 mi (8,300 km, 4,500 nmi)

- **Service ceiling:** 41,000 ft (12,500 m)

**Armament**\


- Hardpoints: 11 total

- Internal bay with 5 hardpoints and 6 external hardpoints for a variety of conventional weapons, e.g. [AGM-84 Harpoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-84_Harpoon), [AGM-84H/K SLAM-ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-84H/K_SLAM-ER), [AGM-88G AARGM-ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-88_HARM#AGM-88G_AARGM-ER),

  [^cite_note-280]

  &#x20;[AGM-158C LRASM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-158C_LRASM),

  [^cite_note-281]

  &#x20;[Mark 54 torpedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_54_torpedo), [Sting Ray torpedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sting_Ray_\(torpedo\)),

  [^cite_note-282]

  &#x20;[naval mines](22102.md), [depth charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depth_charge), and the *High Altitude Anti-Submarine Warfare Weapon Capability (HAAWC) system*

  [^cite_note-283]

**Avionics**\
