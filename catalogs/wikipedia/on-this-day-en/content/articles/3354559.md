The **Ministry of Culture** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Ministère de la Culture* [\[ministɛʁ də la kyltyʁ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)) is the [ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_\(government_department\)) of the [Government of France](3398091.md) in charge of [national museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_France) and the *[monuments historiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_historique)*. Its goal is to maintain the French identity through the promotion and protection of the arts (visual, plastic, theatrical, musical, dance, architectural, literary, televisual and cinematographic) on national soil and abroad. Its budget is mainly dedicated to the management of the *[Archives Nationales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archives_Nationales_\(France\))* (six national sites and hundred decentralised storage facilities) and the regional *Maisons de la culture* (culture centres).

Its main office is in the [Palais-Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais-Royal) in the [1st arrondissement of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_arrondissement_of_Paris) on the [Rue de Valois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rue_de_Valois).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] It is headed by the Minister of Culture, a cabinet member. The incumbent is [Catherine Pégard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_P%C3%A9gard), a newspaper editor who [directed the management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Establishment_of_the_Palace,_Museum_and_National_Estate_of_Versailles) of the [Palace of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Versailles).

Deriving from the [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance) and [Burgundian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Burgundy) courts of the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance), the notion that the state had a key role to play in the sponsoring of artistic production and that the arts were linked to national prestige was found in France from at least the 16th century on. During the pre-revolutionary period, these ideas are apparent in such things as the creation of the [Académie française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acad%C3%A9mie_fran%C3%A7aise), the [Académie de peinture et de sculpture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acad%C3%A9mie_de_peinture_et_de_sculpture) and other state-sponsored institutions of artistic production, and through the cultural policies of [Louis XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV)'s minister [Jean-Baptiste Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Colbert).

The modern post of Minister of Culture was created by [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle) in 1959 and the first officeholder was the writer [André Malraux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Malraux). Malraux established the goals of the *droit à la culture* ('right to culture'), an idea which had been incorporated in the [Constitution of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_France) and the [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights) (1948), by democratising access to culture, while also achieving the [Gaullist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaullism) aim of elevating the "grandeur" ('greatness') of post-war France. To this end, he created numerous regional cultural centres throughout France and actively sponsored the arts. Malraux's artistic tastes included the modern arts and the avant-garde, but on the whole he remained conservative.



One of France's six National Archives sites operated by the Ministry of Culture, in [Pierrefitte-sur-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierrefitte-sur-Seine)

Under President [François Mitterrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Mitterrand) the Minister of Culture was [Jack Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Lang_\(French_politician\)) who showed himself to be far more open to popular cultural production, including [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz), [rock and roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll), [rap music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rap_music), [graffiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graffiti), [bande dessinées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bande_dessin%C3%A9e), [fashion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion) and [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food). His famous phrase "économie et culture, même combat" ('economy and culture: it's the same fight') is representative of his commitment to cultural democracy and to active national sponsorship and participation in cultural production. In addition to the creation of the *[Fête de la Musique](745073.md)* and overseeing the French Revolution bicentennial (1989), he was in charge of the [massive architectural program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grands_Projets_of_Fran%C3%A7ois_Mitterrand) of the [François Mitterrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Mitterrand) years (the so-called *Grands travaux*; 'Great Works') that gave permission for the building of the [Bibliothèque nationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioth%C3%A8que_nationale), the new [Louvre](17546.md), the [Arab World Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_World_Institute), the [Musée d'Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_d'Orsay), the [Opéra-Bastille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ra-Bastille), the "[Grande Arche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Arche)" of [La Défense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_D%C3%A9fense) (the Parisian business quarter), the new seat of the [French Ministry for the Economy and Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Ministry_for_the_Economy_and_Finance), the [Jean-Marie Tjibaou Cultural Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marie_Tjibaou_Cultural_Centre), and the [Cité des Sciences et de l'Industrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cit%C3%A9_des_Sciences_et_de_l'Industrie) and [Cité de la Musique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cit%C3%A9_de_la_Musique), both in the [Parc de la Villette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_de_la_Villette).

The Ministry of [Jacques Toubon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Toubon) was notable for a number of laws (the "[Toubon Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toubon_Law)") enacted for the preservation of the [French language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language), both in advertisements (all ads must include a French translation of foreign words) and on the radio (35% of songs on French radio stations must be in French), ostensibly in reaction to the presence of English.

## Names of the Ministry of Culture

Since the French constitution does not identify specific ministers (merely speaking of "the minister in charge of" this or that), each government may label each ministry as they wish, or even have a broader ministry in charge of several governmental sectors. Hence, the ministry has gone through a number of different names:

| 1959: *Ministère des Affaires culturelles* 1974: *Ministère des Affaires culturelles et de l'Environnement* 1974: *Secrétariat d'État à la Culture* 1976: *Ministère de la Culture et de l'Environnement* 1978: *Ministère de la Culture et de la Communication* 1981: *Ministère de la Culture* 1986: *Ministère de la Culture et de la Communication* 1988: *Ministère de la Culture, de la Communication, des Grands Travaux et du Bicentenaire* | 1991: *Ministère de la Culture et de la Communication* 1992: *Ministère de l'Éducation nationale et de la Culture* 1993: *Ministère de la Culture et de la Francophonie* 1995: *Ministère de la Culture* 1997: *Ministère de la Culture et de la Communication* 2017: *Ministère de la Culture* |
| --- | --- |

## Organisation

### Central administration

The Ministry of Culture is made up of a variety of internal divisions, including:

- *Direction de l'administration générale* (DAG)

- *Direction de l'architecture et du patrimoine* (DAPA) in charge of national monuments and heritage&#x20;

  - [General inventory of cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_inventory_of_cultural_heritage) maintains extensive databases of historical sites and objects via the [Base Mérimée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_M%C3%A9rim%C3%A9e) and *[monument historique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_historique)* status.

- *Direction des archives de France* (DAF) in charge of the [National Archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archives_nationales_\(France\))

- *Direction du livre et de la lecture* (DLL) in charge of [French literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_literature) and the book trade

- *Direction de la musique, de la danse, du théâtre et des spectacles* (DMDTS) in charge of [music, dance and theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_culture)

- *Direction des Musées de France* (DMF) in charge of labelled museums

The Ministry has access to one inter-ministerial division:

- *Direction du développement des médias* (DDM) in charge of developing and expanding the [French media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_of_France) (although French public television is run through the public-service company [France Télévisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_T%C3%A9l%C3%A9visions))

The Ministry also runs three "delegations" (administrative boards):

- *Délégation aux arts plastiques* (DAP) in charge of the visual and sculptural arts

- *Délégation au développement et aux affaires internationales* (DDAI) in charge of international affairs and French art

- *Délégation générale à la langue française et aux langues de France* (DGLFLF) in charge of the [French language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) and [languages of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_France)

Finally, the Ministry shares in the management of the [National Centre of Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_du_cin%C3%A9ma_et_de_l'image_anim%C3%A9e) (Centre national de la cinématographie, CNC), a public institution.[^cite_note-3]

The [Alliance française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_fran%C3%A7aise) is run by the [Ministry of Europe and Foreign Affairs](250002.md).[^cite_note-4]

### Other services

On the national level, the Ministry also runs:

- [Regional](58846.md) Cultural Affairs (Direction régionale des affaires culturelles, DRAC)

- [Departmental](38523.md) Architecture and Monuments (Services départementaux de l'architecture et du patrimoine, SDAP)

- [Departmental](38523.md) Archives under the direction of the [departmental councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departmental_councils_\(France\))

- [Centre National de la Danse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_National_de_la_Danse), institution for the study and preservation of dance

## Cultural activities

The Ministry of Culture is responsible for, or a major sponsor of, a number of annual cultural activities, including the *[Fête de la Musique](745073.md)*, the [Maison de la culture de Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maison_de_la_culture_de_Grenoble), the *[Festival d'Avignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival_d'Avignon)*, the [Public Establishment of the Palace, Museum and National Estate of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Establishment_of_the_Palace,_Museum_and_National_Estate_of_Versailles), the [Joconde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joconde_\(database\)) (online database of objects in French museums), the [Base Mérimée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_M%C3%A9rim%C3%A9e) (database of listed heritage monuments), and the [Maître d'art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma%C3%AEtre_d'art) program.
