**Qinghai**[^cite_note-7] is an inland [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China) in [Northwestern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_China). It is the largest province of China (excluding autonomous regions) by area and has the third-smallest population, with its capital and largest city being [Xining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xining).

Qinghai borders [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) on the northeast, [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) on the northwest, [Sichuan](65185.md) on the southeast, and the [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region) on the southwest. Qinghai province was established in 1928 during the period of the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)), and until 1949 was ruled by [Hui Chinese Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) warlords known as the [Ma clique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_clique). The [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language) name "Qinghai" is after [Qinghai Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinghai_Lake), the largest lake in China. In [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetic_languages), the lake is known as མཚོ སྔོན་པོ (*Mtsho-sngon-po*) or *Tso ngönpo* ("Blue Lake or Sea"). In English, it is sometimes referred to as Koko Nor, derived from the Classical Mongolian [Oirat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oirat_language) name for Qinghai Lake, ᠬᠥᠬᠡ ᠨᠠᠭᠤᠷ (*köke naɣur*); the modern Mongolian name is Хөхнуур (*Höhnuur*). Both *Tso ngönpo* and Koko Nor are names found in historic documents to describe the region.[^cite_note-Khishong-8]

Located mostly on the [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau), the province is inhabited by a number of peoples including the [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) (concentrated in the provincial capital of Xining, nearby [Haidong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haidong), and [Haixi](3472790.md)), [Tibetans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people), [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people), [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols), [Monguors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monguor_people), and [Salars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salar_people). According to the 2021 census reports, Tibetans constitute a fifth of the population of Qinghai and the Hui compose roughly a sixth of the population. There are over 37 recognized ethnic groups among Qinghai's population of 5.6 million, with [ethnic minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minorities_in_China) making up a total of 49.5% of the population.

The area of Qinghai came under the control of the [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu)-led [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) around 1724, after their defeat of [Khoshut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoshut) Mongols who previously controlled most of the area. After the [Xinhai Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinhai_Revolution) and the ensuing fall of the Qing dynasty in 1912, Qinghai came under Chinese Muslim warlord [Ma Qi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Qi)'s control until the [Northern Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Expedition) by the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) consolidated central control in 1928. In the same year, the province of Qinghai was established by the [Nationalist Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_government), with Xining as its capital.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

## History

During the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age), Qinghai was home to a diverse group of nomadic tribes closely related to other Central Asians who traditionally made a living in [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) and [husbandry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husbandry), the [Kayue culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayue_culture). The eastern part of the area of Qinghai was under the control of the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) about 2,000 years ago. It was a battleground during the [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) and subsequent [Central Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhongyuan) dynasties when they fought against successive [Tibetan tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Tibet).[^cite_note-12]

In the middle of 3rd century CE, nomadic people related to the [Mongolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol) [Xianbei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xianbei) migrated to pasture lands around the [Qinghai Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinghai_Lake) (Koko Nur) and established the [Tuyuhun Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuyuhun_Kingdom).

In the 7th century, the Tuyuhun Kingdom was attacked by both the [Tibetan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Empire) and the Tang dynasty as both sought control over the [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road) trade routes. Tibetan King [Songtsen Gampo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songtsen_Gampo) was victorious, and settled the area around Tso ngon (Lake Go, or Kokonor Lake).[^cite_note-13] Military conflicts had severely weakened the Tuyuhun kingdom and it was incorporated into the Tibetan Empire. The Tibetan Empire continued expanding beyond Tso ngon during [Trisong Detsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trisong_Detsen)'s and [Ralpacan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralpacan)'s reigns, and the empire controlled vast areas north and east of Tso ngon until 848,[^cite_note-14] which included [Xi'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an).

During the [fragmentation of the Tibetan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era_of_Fragmentation), a series of local polities emerged under the political jostling of [Western Xia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Xia) to the north and [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) to the east – from the military-rule of [Guiyi Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guiyi_Circuit), to a Tibetan tribal confederacy, and eventually [the Tibetan theocratic kingdom of Tsongkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsongkha). The Song dynasty eventually defeated the Kokonor kingdom Tsongkha in the 1070s.[^cite_note-15] During the Mongol-led [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty)'s [administrative rule of Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_under_Yuan_rule), the region comprised the headwaters of the Ma chu (Machu River, [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River)) and the Yalong ([Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze)) rivers and was known as [Amdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amdo), but apportioned to different administrative divisions than Tibet proper.[^cite_note-16]

Most of Qinghai was, for a short time in the aftermath of the Yuan dynasty's overthrow, under the control of early [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty), but later gradually lost to the [Khoshut Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoshut_Khanate) founded by the [Oirats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oirats). The [Xunhua Salar Autonomous County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xunhua_Salar_Autonomous_County) is where most [Salar people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salar_people) live in Qinghai. The Salars migrated to Qinghai from [Samarkand](51491.md) in 1370.[^cite_note-Betta-17] The chief of the four upper clans around this time was Han Pao-yuan and Ming granted him office of centurion, it was at this time the people of his four clans took Han as their surname.[^cite_note-18] The other chief Han Shan-pa of the four lower Salar clans got the same office from Ming, and his clans were the ones who took Ma as their surname.[^cite_note-19]

From 1640 to 1724, a big part of the area that is now Qinghai was under [Khoshut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoshut) Mongol control, but in 1724 it was conquered by the armies of the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty).[^cite_note-20] [Xining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xining), the capital of modern Qinghai province, began to function as the administrative center, although the city itself was then part of [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) province within the "Tibetan frontier district".[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] In 1724, 13-Article for the Effective Governing of Qinghai (Chinese\:青海善后事宜十三条) was proposed by [Nian Gengyao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nian_Gengyao) and adopted by the Central Government to gain full control of Qinghai.

Under the Qing dynasty, the governor was a viceroy of the Emperor, but local ethnic groups enjoyed significant autonomy. Many chiefs retained their traditional authority, participating in local administrations.[^cite_note-23] The [Dungan Revolt (1862–1877)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungan_Revolt_\(1862%E2%80%931877\)) devastated the [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) Muslim population of [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi), shifting the Hui center of population to [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) and Qinghai.[^cite_note-Cooke-24]<sup>: 405 </sup> Another [Dungan Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungan_Revolt_\(1895%E2%80%9396\)) broke out in Qinghai in 1895 when various Muslim ethnic groups in Qinghai and Gansu rebelled against the Qing. Following the [overthrow of the Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinhai_Revolution) in 1911, the region came under Chinese Muslim warlord [Ma Qi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Qi) control until the [Northern Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Expedition) by the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) consolidated central control in 1928.

In July–August 1912, General [Ma Fuxiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Fuxiang) was "Acting Chief Executive Officer of Kokonur" (de facto Governor of the region that later became Qinghai).[^cite_note-25] In 1928, Qinghai province was created. The Muslim warlord and General Ma Qi became military governor of Qinghai, followed by his brother [Ma Lin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Lin_\(warlord\)) and then Ma Qi's son [Ma Bufang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Bufang). In 1932 [Tibet invaded Qinghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinghai%E2%80%93Tibet_War), attempting to capture southern parts of Qinghai province, following contention in [Yushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yushu_Tibetan_Autonomous_Prefecture), Qinghai, over a monastery in 1932. The army of Ma Bufang defeated the Tibetan armies. Governor of Qinghai Ma Bufang was described as a [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideology_of_the_Communist_Party_of_China) by American journalist [John Roderick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Roderick_\(correspondent\)) and friendly compared to the other Ma Clique warlords.[^cite_note-26] Ma Bufang was reported to be good humoured and jovial in contrast to the brutal reign of [Ma Hongkui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Hongkui).[^cite_note-27] Most of eastern China was ravaged by the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War) and the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War), by contrast, Qinghai was relatively untouched.

Ma Bufang increased the prominence of the [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) and [Salar people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salar_people) in Qinghai's politics by heavily recruiting to his army from the counties in which those ethnic groups predominated.[^cite_note-Goodman-28] General Ma started a state run and controlled industrialization project, directly creating educational, medical, agricultural, and sanitation projects, run or assisted by the state. The state provided money for food and uniforms in all schools, state run or private. Roads and a theater were constructed. The state controlled all the press, no freedom was allowed for independent journalists.[^cite_note-29]

As the [1949 Chinese revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Revolution_\(1949\)) approached Qinghai, Ma Bufang abandoned his post and flew to [Hong Kong](13404.md), traveling abroad but never returning to China. On January 1, 1950, the Qinghai Province People's Government was declared, owing its allegiance to the new [People's Republic of China](5405.md). Aside from some minor adjustments to suit the geography, the PRC maintained the province's territorial integrity.[^cite_note-30] Resistance to Communist rule continued in the form of the Huis' [Kuomintang Islamic insurgency (1950–58)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuomintang_Islamic_insurgency_in_China_\(1950%E2%80%9358\)), spreading past traditionally Hui areas to the ethnic-Tibetan south.[^cite_note-Cooke-24]<sup>: 408 </sup> Although the Hui composed 15.6% of Qinghai's population in 1949, making the province the second-largest concentration of Hui after [Ningxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningxia), the state denied the Hui ethnic autonomous townships and counties that their numbers warranted under Chinese law until the 1980s.[^cite_note-Cooke-24]<sup>: 411 </sup>

## Geography

Qinghai is located on the northeastern part of the [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau). By area, it is the largest province in the People's Republic of China (excluding the [autonomous regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_regions_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China)).

The [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River) originates in the southern part of the province, while the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River) and [Mekong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong) have their sources in the southwestern part. Qinghai is separated by the [Riyue Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyue_Mountain) into pastoral and agricultural zones in the west and east.[^cite_note-Cao-31]

The [Sanjiangyuan National Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjiangyuan_National_Nature_Reserve) is located in Qinghai and contains the headwaters of the Yellow River, Yangtze River, and [Mekong River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong_River). The reserve was established to protect the headwaters of these three rivers and consists of 18 subareas, each containing three zones which are managed with differing degrees of strictness.

[Qinghai Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinghai_Lake) is the largest salt water lake in China, and the second largest in the world. Other large lakes are [Lake Hala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Hala) in the [Qilian mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qilian_Mountains), lakes [Gyaring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyaring_Lake) and [Ngoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngoring_Lake) in the headwater region of the Yellow River, [Lake Donggi Cona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donggi_Cona), and many saline and [salt lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_lake) in the western part of the province.

The [Qaidam basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaidam) lies in the northwest part of the province at an altitude between 3000 and 5000 meters above sea level. About a third of this resource rich basin is desert.

### Climate

Köppen–Geiger climate classification map at 1-km resolution for Qinghai (China) for 1991–2020

The average elevation of Qinghai is approximately 3000 m.[^cite_note-32] Mountain ranges include the [Tanggula Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanggula_Mountains) and [Kunlun Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunlun_Mountains), with the highest point being [Bukadaban Feng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukadaban_Feng) at 6860 m.[^cite_note-33] Due to the high altitude, Qinghai has quite cold winters (harsh in the highest elevations), mild summers, and a large [diurnal temperature variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diurnal_temperature_variation). Its mean annual temperature is approximately −5 to 8 °C (23 to 46 °F), with January temperatures ranging from −18 to −7 °C (0 to 19 °F) and July temperatures ranging from 15 to 21 °C (59 to 70 °F). It is also prone to heavy winds as well as [sandstorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_storm) from February to April. Significant rainfall occurs mainly in summer, while precipitation is very low in winter and spring, and is generally low enough to keep much of the province [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid) or [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid).

## Politics

Main article: [Politics of Qinghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Qinghai)

The politics of Qinghai Province in the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) are structured in a one party-government system like all other governing institutions in [mainland China](55079.md).

The [Governor of Qinghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Qinghai) is the highest-ranking official in the People's Government of Qinghai. However, in the province's dual party-government governing system, the Governor has less power than the Qinghai [Chinese Communist Party Committee Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_Secretary_of_Qinghai).

### Ethnicity

There are over 37 recognized ethnic groups among Qinghai's population of 5.6 million, with [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) population standing at 50.5% of the total population and [ethnic minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minorities_in_China) making up 49.5% of the population.[^cite_note-\:1-54] In 2010, [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people) population stood at 20.7%, [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) 16%, [Tu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monguor_people) (Monguor) 4%, with also some groups of [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Mongols), and [Salar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salar_people), all of those groups being the most populous in the province. [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) predominate in the cities of [Xining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xining), [Haidong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haidong), [Delingha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delingha) and [Golmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golmud), and elsewhere in the northeast. The Hui are concentrated in Xining, Haidong, [Minhe County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minhe_Hui_and_Tu_Autonomous_County), [Hualong County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hualong_Hui_Autonomous_County), and [Datong County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datong_Hui_and_Tu_Autonomous_County). The [Tu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tu_people) predominate in [Huzhu County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huzhu_Tu_Autonomous_County) and the Salars in [Xunhua County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xunhua_Salar_Autonomous_County); Tibetans and Mongols are sparsely distributed across the rural western part of the province.[^cite_note-Goodman-28] Of the Muslim ethnic groups in China, Qinghai has communities of Hui, Salar, [Dongxiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongxiang_people), and [Bao'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bao'an_people).[^cite_note-Betta-17] The Hui dominate the wholesale business in Qinghai.[^cite_note-55]

## Culture

Qinghai has been influenced by interactions "between Mongol and Tibetan culture, north to south, and Han Chinese and Inner Asia Muslim culture, east to west".[^cite_note-Goodman-28] The languages of Qinghai have for centuries formed a [local Sprachbund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinghai%E2%80%93Gansu_sprachbund), with [Zhongyuan Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhongyuan_Mandarin), [Amdo Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amdo_Tibetan_language), [Salar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salar_language), [Yugur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Yugur_language), and [Monguor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monguor_language) borrowing from and influencing one another.[^cite_note-Janhunen-59] In mainstream [Chinese culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_culture), Qinghai is most associated with the *[Tale of King Mu, Son of Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tale_of_King_Mu,_Son_of_Heaven)*. According to this legend, [King Mu of Zhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Mu_of_Zhou) (r. 976–922 BCE) pursued hostile [Quanrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanrong) nomads to eastern Qinghai, where the goddess [Xi Wangmu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi_Wangmu) threw the king a banquet in the [Kunlun Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunlun_Mountains).[^cite_note-60]

The main religions in Qinghai are [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism), [Islam](6037917.md) and [Chinese Folk Religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Folk_Religions). The [Dongguan Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongguan_Mosque) has been continuously operating since 1380.[^cite_note-Cooke-24]<sup>: 402 </sup> Measures of education in Qinghai are low, particularly among the ethnic minorities.[^cite_note-Goodman-28] The [yak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yak), which is native to Qinghai, is widely used in the province for transportation and its meat.[^cite_note-Cao-31]

## Economy



Oil well in [Tsaidam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsaidam) (Qaidam), Qinghai

Qinghai's economy is amongst the smallest in China. Its [nominal GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominal_GDP) in 2025 was just RMB 412 billion (US\$58 billion) and contributes to about 0.29% of the entire country's economy. Per capita GDP was RMB 69,567 (US\$9,739) (nominal), the 24th in China.[^cite_note-data2022-61]

Its heavy industry includes iron and steel production, located near its capital city of Xining. Oil and natural gas from the [Qaidam Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaidam_Basin) have also been an important contributor to the economy.[^cite_note-thechinaperspective.com-62] Salt works operate at many of the province's numerous salt lakes.

Outside of the provincial capital, Xining, most of Qinghai remains underdeveloped. Qinghai ranks second lowest in China in terms of highway length, and will require a significant expansion of its infrastructure to capitalize on the economic potential of its rich natural resources.[^cite_note-thechinaperspective.com-62]

### Economic and technological development zone

[Xining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xining) Economic & Technological Development Zone (XETDZ) was approved as state-level development zone in July 2000. It has a planned area of 4.4 km<sup>2</sup>. XETDZ lies in the east of Xining, 5 km from the city centre. Xining is located in the east of the province at the upper reaches of the [Huangshui River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huangshui_River), one of the Yellow River's branches. The city is surrounded by mountains with an average elevation of 2261 m, the highest at 4393 m. XETDZ is the first of its kind at the national level on the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau. It is established to fulfill the nation's strategy of developing the west.

XETDZ is connected to the Xining–Lanzhou Expressway and traversed by two major city thoroughfares. It is located 4 km from the main railway station and 15 km from Xining Airport, which offers flights to major domestic destinations including Beijing, Guangzhou, Shanghai, Chengdu, and Xi'an. As a regional transportation hub, Xining is served by multiple highways and two primary rail lines, the Lanzhou–Qinghai and Qinghai–Tibet railways.

It focuses on the development of following industries: chemicals based on salt lake resources, nonferrous metals, and petroleum and natural gas processing; special medicine, foods and bio-chemicals using local plateau animals and plants; new products involving ecological and environmental protection, high technology, new materials as well as information technology; and services such as logistics, banking, real estate, tourism, hotel, catering, agency and international trade.[^cite_note-63]

### Tourism



View of the [Qinghai Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinghai_Lake)

There are many tourist attractions center on [Xining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xining), the provincial seat of Qinghai.

During the hot summer months, many tourists from the hot southern and eastern parts of China travel to Xining, as the climate of Xining in July and August is quite mild and comfortable, making the city an ideal summer retreat.

[Qinghai Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinghai_Lake) (青海湖; *qīnghǎi hú*) is another tourist attraction, albeit further from Xining than [Kumbum Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumbum_Monastery) (Ta'er Si). The lake is the largest saltwater lake in China, and is also located on the "Roof of the World", the Tibetan Plateau. The lake itself lies at 3,600 m elevation. The surrounding area is made up of rolling [grasslands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassland) and populated by ethnic Tibetans. Most pre-arranged tours stop at Bird Island (鸟岛; *niǎo dǎo*). An international bicycle race takes place annually from Xining to Qinghai Lake.

### Transportation

See also: [Transport in the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_the_People's_Republic_of_China)



[China National Highway 109](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Highway_109) in Qinghai

The [Lanqing Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanqing_Railway), running between [Lanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzhou), [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) and [Xining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xining), the province's capital, was completed in 1959 and is the major transportation route in and out of the province. A continuation of the line, the [Qinghai-Tibet Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinghai-Tibet_Railway) via [Golmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golmud) and western Qinghai, has become one of the most ambitious projects in PRC history. It was completed in October 2005 and now links Tibet with the rest of China through Qinghai.

Construction on the [Golmud–Dunhuang Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golmud%E2%80%93Dunhuang_Railway), in the province's northwestern part, started in 2012.

Six [National Highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Highways) run through the province.

[Xining Caojiabao International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xining_Caojiabao_International_Airport) provides service to [Beijing](18603746.md), [Lanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzhou), [Golmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golmud) and [Delingha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delingha). Smaller regional airports, [Delingha Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delingha_Airport), [Golog Maqin Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golog_Maqin_Airport), [Huatugou Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huatugou_Airport), [Qilian Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qilian_Airport) and [Yushu Batang Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yushu_Batang_Airport), serve the province's smaller communities; plans exist for the construction of three more by 2020.[^cite_note-64]

## Telecommunications

See also: [Telecommunications industry in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_industry_in_China)

Since the [Ministry of Industry and Information Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Industry_and_Information_Technology) began its "Access to Telephones Project", Qinghai has invested 640 million yuan to provide [telephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone) access to 3,860 out of its 4,133 administrative villages. At the end of 2006, 299 towns had received [Internet access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_access). However, 6.6 percent of villages in the region still have no access to the telephone. These villages are mainly scattered in [Qingnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingnan,_Qinghai?action=edit\&redlink=1) Area, with 90 percent of them located in [Yushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yushu_Tibetan_Autonomous_Prefecture) and [Guoluo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golog_Tibetan_Autonomous_Prefecture). The average altitude of these areas exceeds 3600 meters, and the poor natural conditions hamper the establishment of [telecommunications](33094374.md) facilities in the region.

[Satellite phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_phone) have been provided to 186 remote villages in Qinghai Province as of September 14, 2007. The areas benefited were Yushu Tibetan Autonomous Prefecture and Guoluo Tibetan Autonomous Prefecture. Qinghai has recently been provided with satellite telephone access. In June 2007, [China Satcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Satcom) carried out an in-depth survey in Yushu and Guoluo, and made a special satellite phones for these areas. Two phones were provided to each village for free, and calls were charged at the rate of 0.2 RMB (about a quarter of a US cent at that time) per minute for both local and national calls, with the extra charges assumed by China Satcom. No monthly rent was charged on the satellite phone. International calls were also available.
