**Warkworth** ([Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_language): *Puhinui*)[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] is a town on the [Northland Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northland_Peninsula) in the upper [North Island](62046.md) of New Zealand. It is in the northern part of the [Auckland Region](676368.md), 64 km (40 mi) north of [Auckland](18660332.md) and 98 km (61 mi) south of [Whangārei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whang%C4%81rei), at the head of the [Mahurangi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahurangi_River).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-ac-10] [State Highway 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Highway_1_\(New_Zealand\)) runs past it.

European settler John Anderson Brown first settled at Warkworth in 1843, establishing a timber mill on the banks of the Mahurangi River. The town was established in 1853 and became a hub for timber and shipbuilding industries. The town became a hub for the Wilsons Cement Works in 1884, after the closure and with roading improvements in the 1930s it transitioned into a commercial and service hub for the surrounding rural area. Warkworth was the main settlement in [Rodney County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodney_County,_New_Zealand) and was the seat of local governance for many years.

Warkworth had a large American military presence during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War). In the 21st century Warkworth has been expanding, with the town expected to increase up to five-fold in size.

## Etymology

Warkworth was named in 1854 by early settler John Anderson Brown, who took the name from [Warkworth, Northumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warkworth,_Northumberland),[^cite_note-WarkworthNorth-11][^cite_note-RobertBrassey-12] allegedly because the Mahurangi River reminded him of the [River Coquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Coquet) at Warkworth, where a relative of Brown's worked as a school headmaster.[^cite_note-LINZ-13] The major streets of Warkworth were named after villages adjacent to Warkworth in England, or after major Northumbrian families.[^cite_note-RobertBrassey-12] For ten years prior to this, the area was known as Brown's Mill, after the John Anderson Brown sawmill,[^cite_note-WarkworthNorth-11] and until the 1870s the township was primarily known by the name Upper Mahurangi.[^cite_note-RobertBrassey-12]

The traditional [Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_language) name for Warkworth is *Puhinui* ("Big Plume"), referring to the Puhinui Waterfalls located in the township.[^cite_note-ManuhiriWarkworth-14]

## Geography



The [Mahurangi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahurangi_River) is a major waterway that flows through Warkworth



The Warkworth area was a [kauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agathis_australis)-dominated forest until the mid-19th century. Some remnant kauri trees can be found at Parry Kauri Park

The Warkworth area is at the upper navigable point of the [Mahurangi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahurangi_River), which flows south-east towards the [Mahurangi Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahurangi_Harbour).[^cite_note-RobertBrassey-12] Most of the town is located near the river, with the town centre being adjacent to the river bend. Most of Warkworth is undulating land.[^cite_note-ac-10] The wider area is predominantly uplifted [Waitemata Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waitemata_Group) sandstone.[^cite_note-RobertBrassey-12] Historically, the area was heavily forested until European settlement.[^cite_note-ManuhiriWarkworth-14] Warkworth is a lower-lying area with [mountain ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_ranges) to the north and south. [The Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dome_\(New_Zealand\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Conical Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conical_Peak), [Mount Tamahunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tamahunga) are the highest points of ranges to the north and [Moirs Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moirs_Hill?action=edit\&redlink=1) is the highest point of the range to the south.[^cite_note-ac-10]

## History

The area that became Warkworth had limited Māori occupation. The [Mahurangi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahurangi_River) was the route between the [Mahurangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahurangi_Harbour) and [Kaipara Harbours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaipara_Harbour) for the local tribes. This route was later used by Europeans.[^cite_note-RobertBrassey-12]

### Land sales and early colonial settlement



1864 advertisement for Warkworth village lots

The area that became Warkworth was part of the [Mahurangi purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahurangi) made in 1841.[^cite_note-McKinney-18] The purchase was not finalised until 1853 and land was not subdivided nor resold until then, instead the Crown offered timber licences to settlers.[^cite_note-WarkworthNorth-11]

In 1843 early settler John Anderson Brown began squatting on land along the Mahurangi River, establishing a dam and timber mill on the left bank in 1844 after purchasing a timber licence. The timber industry, later joined by shipbuilding, were the two major economic activities in the area during the mid-19th century.[^cite_note-WarkworthNorth-11] The area of Warkworth was initially a Maori reserve but in 1853 the land was sold to the Crown for £150.[^cite_note-townhall-19] In 1853 Brown was among the first land owners in the area he purchased 153 acres (62 ha) of land from the Crown. Brown subdivided part of his land on the bank of the Mahurangi river in 1854. The street layout was in a rectangular grid pattern. Growth was slow and not all lots had been sold by 1864.[^cite_note-WarkworthNorth-11][^cite_note-riverina-20] The town developed slowly, primarily around the Mahurangi River wharf, where settlers could supply goods and services for the [Auckland](18660332.md) market.[^cite_note-WarkworthNorth-11] Bridge House Lodge, established on the site of John Anderson Brown's home next door to the Warkworth Bridge, is the oldest surviving building in Warkworth.[^cite_note-21]

The Mahurangi Post Office and Library were established in 1859.[^cite_note-RobertBrassey-12] In the same year, the Mahurangi School opened, and by 1864 Henry Palmer had established a flour mill in the settlement.[^cite_note-WarkworthNorth-11][^cite_note-riverina-20] Warkworth continued to grow, becoming the largest settlement of [Rodney County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodney_County,_New_Zealand).[^cite_note-townhall-19]

Shipbuilding declined in the area during the late 1870s, and ceased in 1880.[^cite_note-RobertBrassey-12] Unlike other areas of North Auckland, [kauri gum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kauri_gum) digging did not appear to play a large part in the economy of the town. Orchards were established around Warkworth, with some continuing to operate today.[^cite_note-RobertBrassey-12] As forested land was cleared for shipbuilding the cleared land was developed for agriculture and orchards.[^cite_note-WarkworthNorth-11]

In 1864 the first public hall was constructed, remaining in use until 1911 when a replacement [town hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warkworth_Town_Hall) was constructed on the corner of Alnwick and Neville Streets. In 1936 the hall was extended due to the growth of the town.[^cite_note-townhall-19]

### Lime and concrete works



*Lime Kiln, Mahurangi River* (1873), watercolour by William Eastwood



Wilson Portland Cement Works, circa 1910

John Sullivan began Warkworth's first [roche lime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_oxide) production in 1849. Combes & Daldy were the first to produce limestone commercially, with advertisements for limestone dating back to 1850. John Southgate established a second limeworks at a site further downstream in 1857, selling it to [Nathaniel Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathaniel_Wilson_\(entrepreneur\)) in 1864.[^cite_note-WarkworthNorth-11]

Wilson became interested in cement in 1883 and formed the Wilsons Cement Works in 1884 with his brothers John and James.[^cite_note-EvolvingAuckland12.6-22][^cite_note-WarkworthNorth-11] The site became the first [portland cement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_cement) manufacturing works in the Southern Hemisphere. The company built the Warkworth Bridge in 1899.[^cite_note-WarkworthNorth-11]

By 1910 the cement company had become one of the major employers in Warkworth. In 1918 it amalgamated with the New Zealand Portland Cement Company who were based in and the operation was gradually moved to [Whangārei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whang%C4%81rei), eventually closing entirely in 1929.[^cite_note-EvolvingAuckland12.6-22] A separate limestone quarry to the northeast of Warkworth is still operational.[^cite_note-ac-10]

### World War II, developing community



Reconstructed [United States Army](32087.md) huts at the Warkworth Museum

In 1933 the road to Auckland had improved enough that the town no longer relied on the steamer service to transport goods, and the steamers stopped operating in 1937. After this time, Warkworth transitioned from a logging town into a commercial and service hub for the surrounding area.[^cite_note-RobertBrassey-12] Logging continued at Warkworth until the 1930s.[^cite_note-RobertBrassey-12]

During [World War II](32927.md), Warkworth and the surrounding area became home to over 40 camps for [United States Army](32087.md) servicemen, including the [3rd Marine Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Marine_Division), [25th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)) and the [43rd Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/43rd_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)). The first personnel to arrive were the 43rd Infantry Division in October 1942,[^cite_note-McKinney-18] and camps included the modern-day Rodney Showgrounds, and a military hospital that was constructed near Hill Street.[^cite_note-WarkworthNorth-11] The historic cement works, which had been closed since 1928, was used as demolition practice by the servicemen.[^cite_note-FieldGuide2008-23] Some of the 3rd Marine Division servicemen were stationed in Warkworth. The [Warkworth Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warkworth_Town_Hall) was used to stage entertainment for the servicemen.[^cite_note-McKinney-18][^cite_note-townhall-19] By 1944 when the United States Army had left, the [New Zealand Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Army) requisitioned many of the camps.[^cite_note-McKinney-18]

In 1971 the [Warkworth Radio Astronomical Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warkworth_Radio_Astronomical_Observatory) was established near the town, as the first major long-range telecommunication centre for satellite telephone circuits and television in New Zealand.[^cite_note-EvolvingAuckland8.3-24] By 2010, the facility had been redeveloped as an [AUT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland_University_of_Technology) [radio observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_observatory).[^cite_note-EvolvingAuckland8.3-24]

The [Warkworth & District Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warkworth_&_District_Museum), a local museum showcasing local history,[^cite_note-nzmuseums-Warkworth_Museum-25] opened in 1980.[^cite_note-Local-26]

Warkworth saw significant suburban housing growth between 2008 and 2018,[^cite_note-Urban_Form-27] with the population increasing by 61% between 2006 and 2018.[^cite_note-Census_2018-28] In 2023, the [Puhoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puhoi) to Warkworth section of the [Auckland Northern Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland_Northern_Motorway) was opened, diverting traffic away from Warkworth.[^cite_note-29] Warkworth is expected to grow from around 5,000 residents to 25,000–30,000. The town is currently zoned for 7,500 dwellings. Warkworth is the largest town between Auckland and Whangarei.[^cite_note-ac-10]

## Local government

The first local government for the township was the Upper Mahurangi Highway District, which was formed on 24 December 1864. It was one of the first highway districts to form north of the city of Auckland, and was formed to administer the road upkeep costs in the area.[^cite_note-Bloomfield-30] With the abolition of the [provinces of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_New_Zealand) in 1876, Warkworth became part of [Rodney County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodney_County,_New_Zealand).[^cite_note-Bloomfield-30] The highway district was merged with the Lower Mahurangi Highway District in 1903 and it became the Warkworth Road Board. In 1908 the road board became a town board, and in 1954 it became a town council following the Municipal Corporations Act. In 1976 Warkworth became a district community council of [Rodney County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodney_County,_New_Zealand).[^cite_note-Bloomfield-30][^cite_note-coast-31] From 1989 to 2010, Warkworth was part of [Rodney District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodney_District).

In November 2010, all city and district councils in the [Auckland Region](676368.md) were merged into a single unitary authority, [Auckland Council](22312574.md),[^cite_note-32] with Warkworth in the [Rodney Local Board area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodney_\(local_board_area\)).[^cite_note-ac-10]

Warkworth has served as the seat of local governance in the area since the Upper Mahurangi Highway Board was established in 1864. The [Rodney County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodney_County_Council) offices were initially based in Warkworth.[^cite_note-townhall-19] The [Rodney Local Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodney_Local_Board) has been based in Warkworth since 2023.[^cite_note-33]

## Education

The first school in Warkworth was the Mahurangi School, established in 1863.[^cite_note-WarkworthNorth-11]

### Mahurangi College



Mahurangi College

Mahurangi College is a co-educational secondary school, teaching students between years 7–13 (form 1–7, grade 6–12), with a roll of 1,581 students as of July 2026.[^cite_note-NZ_school_roll_data-38][^cite_note-39] It was first built on the current site in 1957, and was then called Warkworth District High School. It was renamed Mahurangi College in 1962, and had a roll of 210 students at the time.

### Warkworth School

Warkworth School is a co-educational primary (years 1–6) school with a roll of 626 students as of July 2026.[^cite_note-NZ_school_roll_data-38][^cite_note-40]

## Economy

Warkworth is both a service town for the wider area and a satellite town of Auckland. The nearby surrounds are a mix of industrial, horticulture, and agricuture.[^cite_note-ac-10]

## Sports

Mahurangi RFC is the local rugby club, formed in 1989 as an amalgamation of three local rugby clubs: Warkworth, Kaipara Flats and Omaha.[^cite_note-41]

## Morrison's Orchard

[Edward Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Morrison_\(horticulturalist\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) established the Red Bluffs Orchard near [Hepburn Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_Creek?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the 1870s on a 4 acre site. Morrison created new varieties and was a very successful orchadist; by 1914 Morrison had 250,000 trees covering 57 hectares (140 acres) — the largest orchard in New Zealand. Red Bluffs closed in 1919; however, the Morrison family established another orchard in 1935, Kenilworth. Kenilworth is a 40 acre orchard that is still operating. Both the Morrison family and the Warkworth public support retaining the orchard through the urbanisation and expansion of the town.[^cite_note-ac-10][^cite_note-42]

## Notable buildings

The Warkworth town centre has a high proportion of historic buildings including: the [Warkworth Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warkworth_Town_Hall), [Warkworth Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warkworth_Post_Office?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Broomfield House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broomfield_House_\(Warkworth\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Warkworth Masonic Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warkworth_Masonic_Hall?action=edit\&redlink=1), [St Columba's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Columba's_Church,_Warkworth?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the [Warkworth Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warkworth_Hotel?action=edit\&redlink=1). Other historic places in Warkworth include the [ruins of the Wilsons' cement works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilsons'_Cement_Works_Ruins?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Riverina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverina_\(house\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the [Wilsons' Manager's House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilsons'_Manager's_House?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-townhall-19]

## Kōwhai festival

Warkworth has a festival celebrated when [kōwhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dwhai) trees start to drop their flowers (September–October). Thousands of visitors come to see the festival which features old horse carriages, markets, and bands.[^cite_note-coast-31]

## Transportation

[State Highway 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Highway_1_\(New_Zealand\)) passed through Warkworth until an extension of the [Auckland Northern Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland_Northern_Motorway) opened in July 2023.[^cite_note-46] The new extension ends just north of Warkworth.[^cite_note-47]

Auckland Transport operatives bus services to [Snells Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snells_Beach), [Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha,_New_Zealand), [Wellsford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellsford,_New_Zealand), and to the [Hibiscus Coast busway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibiscus_Coast_busway_station). There are no local services for transport around the town itself.[^cite_note-ac-10] [InterCity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCity_\(New_Zealand\)) buses run through Warkworth from Auckland to [Kerikeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerikeri)[^cite_note-48] and Mahu City Express twice a day to Auckland.[^cite_note-49]
