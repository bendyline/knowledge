Tomoko Akane ([Japanese](15606.md): 赤根智子; born 28 June 1956)[^cite_note-2] is a Japanese jurist and current judge at the [International Criminal Court](14880.md) (ICC) for [Japan](15573.md), as well as the president of the court.[^cite_note-\:3-3]

## Professional career

After graduating from the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo),[^cite_note-4] she became a public prosecutor in 1982. She later earned a Master of Science in Criminal Justice from [Jacksonville State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville_State_University) in December 1990.[^cite_note-5] During her career, she assumed posts at different levels of the justice system.[^cite_note-\:0-6] She chose to become a public prosecutor due to the lack of opportunities the private sector provided for women and prosecutors, and because she wanted to be involved in serving justice to both victims and perpetrators.[^cite_note-\:1-7] Akane was chief prosecutor of the [Hakodate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakodate) district in [Hokkaido](58092.md) between 2010 and 2012, and became a prosecutor at the [Supreme Public Prosecutors Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Prosecutors_Office_\(Japan\)#Supreme_Public_Prosecutors_Office) in 2012.[^cite_note-\:2-8]

Akane was also a professor in criminal justice practice at both the [Chukyo University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chukyo_University) Law School and the [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University) Law School between 2005 and 2009.[^cite_note-\:0-6] In Nagoya, she was also researching in the field of criminal justice reform between 2005 and 2006.[^cite_note-9] She was the head of the International Cooperation Department (ICD) in the Japanese [Ministry of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_\(Japan\)) between 2009 and 2010.[^cite_note-\:2-8] She was involved in the activities of the [United Nations](31769.md) Asia and Far East Institute for the Prevention of Crime and the Treatment of Offenders (UNAFEI) for more than seven years[^cite_note-\:2-8] and served as the director of the UNAFEI between July 2013 and October 2014.[^cite_note-10] From 2014 to 2016, she was the director-general of the Research and Training Institute of the Ministry of Justice. From 2016 until joining the ICC, she served as Japan's ambassador for international judicial cooperation.[^cite_note-\:2-8]

### Fields involved

Akane reports to valuing the ability of judicial systems in preventing people from reoffending.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-\:2-8] During her work for the UNAFEI, she was twice deployed to Kenya, where she was involved in the training of probation officers.[^cite_note-12]

## Judge at the International Criminal Court

She was nominated as a candidate to the ICC in April 2016 by the [Government of Japan](30858300.md)[^cite_note-13] and elected as a judge of the ICC on the 4 December 2017 by the Assembly of [State Parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_parties_to_the_Rome_Statute_of_the_International_Criminal_Court) in [New York](645042.md).[^cite_note-14] She assumed her post in March 2018 for a tenure of nine years.[^cite_note-\:1-7] As a pre-trial judge at the ICC she was mainly assigned to Pre-Trial Chamber II.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

On 20 March 2023, Russia initiated a criminal investigation against Akane, [Rosario Salvatore Aitala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosario_Salvatore_Aitala) and [Sergio Gerardo Ugalde Godinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Gerardo_Ugalde_Godinez) in response to [an arrest warrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_arrest_warrants_for_Russian_figures) issued on 17 March 2023 against its president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) for the unlawful [deportations](412990.md) of [Ukrainian](31750.md) children to Russia during the [Russian-Ukrainian war](42085878.md).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] In July 2023, the [Russian Ministry of Internal Affairs](53056.md) issued a warrant for her arrest.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

In March 2024 she was elected [President of the International Criminal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_the_International_Criminal_Court) for the term 2024–2027 by the judges of the ICC.[^cite_note-\:3-3]

### U.S. sanction

On 18 August 2026, Akane was [sanctioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_sanction) by the [U.S. State Department](31975.md) in furtherance of its recently announced diplomatic campaign to dismantle the International Criminal Court "brick by brick".[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## Personal life

She is fluent in [Japanese](15606.md) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language).[^cite_note-\:0-6]
