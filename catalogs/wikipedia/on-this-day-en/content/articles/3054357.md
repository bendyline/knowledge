This article provides an overview of **speed records** in **rail transport**. It is divided into absolute records for rail vehicles and fastest connections in the timetable.

## Current speed records



An [L0 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L0_Series) trainset, holding the non-conventional train world speed record of 603 km/h (375 mph)



[TGV](57212.md) 4402 (operation *V150*) reaching 574.8 km/h (357 mph)

The world record for a conventional wheeled passenger train is held by a modified French [TGV](57212.md) high-speed (with standard equipment) code named V150, set in 2007 when it reached 574.8 km/h (357.2 mph) on a 140 km (87 mi) section of [LGV Est](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGV_Est) line, part of the [Trans-European high-speed rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-European_high-speed_rail_network).[^cite_note-1] Japan's experimental [maglev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maglev) train [L0 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L0_Series) achieved 603 km/h (375 mph) on a 42.8 km (26.6 mi) [magnetic levitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_levitation) track in 2015.[^cite_note-guardian20150421-2]

Under commercial traffic and practical conditions where trains carry passengers across from one station to another, the world records for top operating speeds of maglev and single-phase trains are held respectively by China's [Shanghai Maglev Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Maglev_Train) with a top speed of 431 km/h (268 mph) and [CR400 Fuxing Hao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuxing_Hao) at 350 km/h (220 mph). They are followed by France's [TGV Duplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TGV_Duplex), Japan's [E5 Series Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E5_Series_Shinkansen) and the German [ICE 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICE_3) (when operating in France[^cite_note-3]) which all have maximum operating speeds of 320 km/h (200 mph) for commercial services.[^cite_note-4]
